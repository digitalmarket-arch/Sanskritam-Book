# References

Canonical source list. Lessons cite entries as `[REF:key §locus]`; every `key` below is an
anchor. Entries are grouped by role. Add entries in place (keep groups alphabetized by key);
never delete a key that lessons cite.

Access notes: primary Sanskrit texts are cited by their own internal numbering
(sūtra, chapter.verse, maṇḍala.hymn.verse), which is edition-independent.

---

## Primary: grammar and phonetics

<a id="ashtadhyayi"></a>**ashtadhyayi** — Pāṇini, *Aṣṭādhyāyī*. Cited by sūtra
(adhyāya.pāda.sūtra), e.g. `[REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.1]`. Reference text: the sūtrapāṭha as
transmitted with the Kāśikāvṛtti.

<a id="mahabhashya"></a>**mahabhashya** — Patañjali, *Vyākaraṇa-Mahābhāṣya* (c. 2nd c. BCE).
Cited by āhnika and the sūtra under discussion. Ed. F. Kielhorn, 3 vols., 1880–1885.

<a id="kashika"></a>**kashika** — Jayāditya & Vāmana, *Kāśikāvṛtti* (c. 7th c. CE). Running
gloss on the Aṣṭādhyāyī; cited by sūtra.

<a id="siddhantakaumudi"></a>**siddhantakaumudi** — Bhaṭṭoji Dīkṣita, *Vaiyākaraṇa-
Siddhāntakaumudī* (17th c.). Re-ordered presentation of the Aṣṭādhyāyī; cited by sūtra or
prakaraṇa.

<a id="laghukaumudi"></a>**laghukaumudi** — Varadarāja, *Laghusiddhāntakaumudī* (17th c.).
Abridged Siddhāntakaumudī; the traditional beginner's grammar. Ed./tr. J.R. Ballantyne.

<a id="paniniyashiksha"></a>**paniniyashiksha** — *Pāṇinīya Śikṣā* (attributed to the Pāṇinian
school). Versified phonetics: articulation places, effort, the sound inventory. Cited by verse.

<a id="pratishakhya-rv"></a>**pratishakhya-rv** — *Ṛgveda-Prātiśākhya*. Vedic phonetic treatise;
cited by paṭala.sūtra.

<a id="maheshvarasutras"></a>**maheshvarasutras** — The 14 varṇa-samāmnāya sūtras
(अइउण् … हल्) prefixed to the Aṣṭādhyāyī tradition. Cited by sūtra number 1–14.

## Primary: lexica

<a id="amarakosha"></a>**amarakosha** — Amarasiṃha, *Amarakośa (Nāmaliṅgānuśāsana)*
(c. 4th–6th c. CE). Thesaurus; cited by kāṇḍa.varga.verse.

<a id="dhatupatha"></a>**dhatupatha** — *Dhātupāṭha* of the Pāṇinian tradition. Root list with
gaṇa and marker letters; cited by gaṇa and root.

## Primary: literature quoted in lessons

<a id="bhagavadgita"></a>**bhagavadgita** — *Bhagavadgītā*. Cited chapter.verse (e.g. 2.47,
10.33). Critical edition: BORI *Mahābhārata*, Bhīṣmaparvan.

<a id="brhadaranyaka"></a>**brhadaranyaka** — *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad*. Cited
adhyāya.brāhmaṇa.verse (e.g. 1.3.28 — asato mā sad gamaya). Ed./tr. Olivelle 1998.

<a id="rigveda"></a>**rigveda** — *Ṛgveda Saṃhitā*. Cited maṇḍala.hymn.verse (e.g. 3.62.10 —
the Gāyatrī/Sāvitrī mantra). Tr.: Jamison & Brereton 2014.

<a id="panchatantra"></a>**panchatantra** — *Pañcatantra*. Textus simplicior unless noted;
cited by tantra and story. Tr.: Olivelle 2006 (Oxford).

<a id="raghuvamsha"></a>**raghuvamsha** — Kālidāsa, *Raghuvaṃśa*. Cited sarga.verse (e.g. 1.1
vāgarthāv iva…).

## Modern scholarship: grammars and dictionaries

<a id="apte1890"></a>**apte1890** — V.S. Apte, *The Practical Sanskrit-English Dictionary*.
Poona 1890; revised ed. 1957–59. Cited s.v. (sub voce).

<a id="cardona1997"></a>**cardona1997** — G. Cardona, *Pāṇini: His Work and its Traditions*,
2nd ed., Motilal Banarsidass 1997. The standard modern account of the Pāṇinian system.

<a id="coulson2010"></a>**coulson2010** — M. Coulson, *Complete Sanskrit* (Teach Yourself),
rev. Gombrich & Benson, 2010. Model for modern self-study pedagogy.

<a id="goldman2019"></a>**goldman2019** — R.P. Goldman & S.J. Sutherland Goldman,
*Devavāṇīpraveśikā: An Introduction to the Sanskrit Language*, 3rd ed. UC Berkeley. Standard
university coursebook; cited by chapter.

<a id="macdonell1927"></a>**macdonell1927** — A.A. Macdonell, *A Sanskrit Grammar for
Students*, 3rd ed., Oxford 1927. Cited by paragraph §.

<a id="monierwilliams1899"></a>**monierwilliams1899** — M. Monier-Williams, *A Sanskrit-English
Dictionary*, Oxford 1899. Cited s.v.

<a id="whitney1889"></a>**whitney1889** — W.D. Whitney, *Sanskrit Grammar*, 2nd ed., Harvard
1889. Cited by paragraph § (e.g. §19 on vowel a). Reference for phonology and paradigms.

## Modern scholarship: history and linguistics

<a id="burrow2001"></a>**burrow2001** — T. Burrow, *The Sanskrit Language*, rev. ed., Motilal
Banarsidass 2001. Historical development, PIE connections.

<a id="jamison2014"></a>**jamison2014** — S. Jamison & J. Brereton, *The Rigveda: The Earliest
Religious Poetry of India*, Oxford 2014. Introduction cited for Ṛgveda dating (c. 1500–1200 BCE).

<a id="mallory2006"></a>**mallory2006** — J.P. Mallory & D.Q. Adams, *The Oxford Introduction
to Proto-Indo-European and the Proto-Indo-European World*, Oxford 2006. Cognate sets.

<a id="masica1991"></a>**masica1991** — C. Masica, *The Indo-Aryan Languages*, Cambridge 1991.
Typology incl. retroflexion cross-linguistically.

<a id="ladefoged2015"></a>**ladefoged2015** — P. Ladefoged & K. Johnson, *A Course in
Phonetics*, 7th ed., Cengage 2015. IPA and articulatory descriptions.

<a id="ohala1994"></a>**ohala1994** — M. Ohala, "Hindi", *Journal of the IPA* 24.1 (1994) —
illustration of the IPA; baseline for Indic vowel/consonant values shared with Sanskrit
pronunciation norms.

## Language-learning research (pedagogy design)

<a id="roediger2006"></a>**roediger2006** — H.L. Roediger & J.D. Karpicke, "Test-Enhanced
Learning", *Psychological Science* 17.3 (2006). Retrieval practice → section 3 (Previous
Lesson Review) and quiz design.

<a id="cepeda2006"></a>**cepeda2006** — N.J. Cepeda et al., "Distributed Practice in Verbal
Recall Tasks", *Psychological Bulletin* 132.3 (2006). Spacing → Revision/Homework design.

<a id="nation2013"></a>**nation2013** — I.S.P. Nation, *Learning Vocabulary in Another
Language*, 2nd ed., Cambridge 2013. Vocabulary load and the four strands → vocabulary tables
and production-exercise quotas.

<a id="krashen1982"></a>**krashen1982** — S. Krashen, *Principles and Practice in Second
Language Acquisition*, Pergamon 1982. Comprehensible input → graded reader design (used
critically, alongside skill-building research).

<a id="cefr2020"></a>**cefr2020** — Council of Europe, *CEFR Companion Volume*, 2020. Can-do
descriptor style. (Sanskrit has no official CEFR mapping; our levels are "CEFR-informed",
stated as such in ROADMAP.)
