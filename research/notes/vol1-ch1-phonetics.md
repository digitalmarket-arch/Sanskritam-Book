## 1. What Sanskrit is (lesson 1 material)

| # | Claim | Register | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Sanskrit is an Indo-European language, in the Indo-Aryan branch of Indo-Iranian. | FACT | [REF:burrow2001], [REF:masica1991] | Sibling of Greek/Latin lineages, not their "mother". |
| 1.2 | The oldest Sanskrit text is the Ṛgveda, composed c. 1500–1200 BCE (scholarly consensus; oral composition/transmission). | HISTORY | [REF:jamison2014 p.5] | Range phrasing mandatory; no "5,000 years". |
| 1.3 | Vedic Sanskrit and Classical Sanskrit differ (phonology incl. pitch accent, morphology incl. more verb forms, lexicon). | FACT | [REF:whitney1889 pref.], [REF:burrow2001] | Ch 1 states the distinction only. |
| 1.4 | Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī (c. 4th century BCE) describes the language in ~4,000 sūtras and effectively fixed the classical norm. | HISTORY | [REF:cardona1997] | Date is approximate; scholarly estimates range 6th–4th c. BCE — say "around the 4th century BCE, estimates vary". |
| 1.5 | संस्कृत (saṃskṛta) means "refined, perfected, put together well" — सम् + √कृ. | FACT | [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. संस्कृत] | 1.x itself taught this correctly (its p.54 quiz). |
| 1.6 | Sanskrit is one of the 22 scheduled languages of the Indian Constitution (Eighth Schedule). | FACT | Constitution of India, Eighth Schedule | Stable, verifiable fact. |
| 1.7 | Sanskrit remains in active use: daily All India Radio news bulletins, the Sudharma newspaper, "spoken Sanskrit" movements (e.g. Saṃskṛta Bhāratī), and tens of thousands of self-reported speakers in Indian censuses. | HISTORY | Census of India 2011 (24,821 reported Sanskrit as mother tongue); AIR; Samskrita Bharati | Lesson phrasing: soft, illustrative — "a small but real living community". |
| 1.8 | A large share of the technical/learned vocabulary of modern Indo-Aryan languages (Hindi, Marathi, Bengali…) is borrowed from or built on Sanskrit (tatsama). | FACT | [REF:masica1991] | Motivates learning for Hindi speakers. |
| 1.9 | English has genuine Sanskrit loanwords: yoga, karma, avatar, guru, mantra, pundit, jungle (via Hindi jaṅgal ← Skt jaṅgala), sugar (via Prakrit/Persian/Arabic ← śarkarā). | FACT | [REF:monierwilliams1899], OED etymologies | These are LOANS — contrast with cognates (1.10). |
| 1.10 | English mother/Skt mātṛ, two/dvi, three/tri, name/nāman etc. are COGNATES (shared PIE ancestry), not borrowings. | FACT | [REF:mallory2006] | The cognate-vs-loan distinction is a Ch 1 teaching point (fixes IR-033 early). |
| 1.11 | BG 10.33 "akṣarāṇām akāro 'smi" — "of syllables, I am the a". | SYMBOL | [REF:bhagavadgita 10.33] | Verified locus; quoted as literature. |

**Banned in this chapter** (EDITORIAL_GUIDELINES §3): mother-of-all-languages, 5,000-year
claims, NASA legend, Einstein quote.

## 2. The sound system (lesson 2 material)

| # | Claim | Register | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | The varṇamālā is ordered phonetically: vowels first, then stops arranged by place of articulation (back → front: velar, palatal, retroflex, dental, labial), each varga ordered voiceless / voiceless-aspirated / voiced / voiced-breathy / nasal, then semivowels, then sibilants + h. | FACT | [REF:whitney1889 §19–75], [REF:ladefoged2015] | The organizing insight of the whole volume. |
| 2.2 | The Pāṇinīya Śikṣā enumerates the places of articulation; verse 13 lists eight sthānas: chest, throat, head (mūrdhan), tongue-root, teeth, nose, lips, palate. | TRADITION | [REF:paniniyashiksha 13] | Cited as the tradition's own phonetic science. |
| 2.3 | The Śikṣā tradition counts 63 or 64 varṇas (Śikṣā v. 3); the classical teaching inventory used in this course is: 13 vowels + 33 consonants + 2 ayogavāha. | TRADITION + FACT | [REF:paniniyashiksha 3]; [REF:whitney1889 §19–75] | Resolves the 1.x "14 vs 11 vs 13" mess: we always say what we are counting. Vowel count: अ आ इ ई उ ऊ ऋ ॠ ऌ ए ऐ ओ औ = 13 (ॡ is grammar-theoretical; mentioned once as a curiosity). |
| 2.4 | Anusvāra and visarga are ayogavāha — dependent sounds, neither vowels nor consonants. | FACT | [REF:whitney1889 §70–73], traditional classification | Fixes IR-008. |
| 2.5 | The five vargas map to five places: कण्ठ्य velar, तालव्य palatal, मूर्धन्य retroflex, दन्त्य dental, ओष्ठ्य labial. | FACT | [REF:whitney1889 §39–50], [REF:macdonell1927 §3] | Terminology into GLOSSARY. |
| 2.6 | IPA is the modern international standard for writing sounds; this course gives every Sanskrit sound an IPA value (STYLE_GUIDE §2). | FACT | [REF:ladefoged2015] | Lesson 2 introduces the notation itself. |
| 2.7 | Retroflex consonants occur in many language families (Indo-Aryan, Dravidian, Swedish/Norwegian, Mandarin…). | FACT | [REF:masica1991], [REF:ladefoged2015] | Anti-IR-013 counterexample. |
| 2.8 | Devanagari is an abugida: consonant letters carry an inherent अ; other vowels attach as signs (mātrās); a vowel-killer (virāma) silences the inherent vowel. | FACT | [REF:masica1991 ch.6] | Named and previewed in L2–L3, mātrā taught in L4, virāma in v1.c05. |
| 2.9 | Devanagari descends from Brāhmī via Gupta and Nāgarī scripts; the classical language was written in many regional scripts — Devanagari is a convention, not "the Sanskrit script". | HISTORY | [REF:masica1991 ch.6] | One paragraph in L2; interesting and true. |

## 3. The sound अ (lesson 3 material)

| # | Claim | Register | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | अ = /ɐ/, a short near-open central vowel; tongue at rest, no lip rounding. | FACT | STYLE_GUIDE §2.1 decision; [REF:ohala1994] (Hindi /ə~ɐ/ baseline), [REF:ladefoged2015] | Course contract value. |
| 3.2 | The traditional description: short a is saṃvṛta "closed", vs the open (vivṛta) ā — encoded in the famous final sūtra of the Aṣṭādhyāyī, अ अ (8.4.68). | TRADITION | [REF:ashtadhyayi 8.4.68], [REF:cardona1997] | Beautiful, verified detail: the grammar's last act is to restore the real pronunciation of a. |
| 3.3 | a (with ā) is by a wide margin the most frequent vowel in Sanskrit text. | FACT | [REF:whitney1889 §22] | Whitney's frequency observation; phrased qualitatively. |
| 3.4 | Every consonant letter in Devanagari, unmarked, is read with अ (the inherent vowel). | FACT | [REF:masica1991 ch.6] | The script rule taught in L3 §15. |
| 3.5 | English speakers' typical errors: replacing /ɐ/ with /æ/ (cat) or /ɑ/ (father, too long/back); Hindi speakers': deleting final -a (dharm for dharma) per Hindi phonology. | FACT | [REF:ohala1994] (schwa deletion in Hindi); [REF:ladefoged2015] | Feeds §12 Common Mistakes + quiz distractors. |
| 3.6 | Example words beginning with अ, attested: अमृत "nectar, immortality", अग्नि "fire", अर्थ "meaning, purpose", अहम् "I". | FACT | [REF:apte1890 s.v.] | Displayed with audio; only अ क म त ल are *readable* at this point — the rest are 🔊 listen-only per EDITORIAL_GUIDELINES §14 rule. |
| 3.7 | BG 10.33 as the tradition's celebration of अ. | SYMBOL | [REF:bhagavadgita 10.33] | Never quizzed. |
| 3.8 | Helper consonants for L3: क /k/ and म /m/ — glyph + sound only; full treatment in v1.c03 (spiral). | — | course design | Enables syllable practice: कम, मक, अक, अम. |

## 4. The sound आ and mātrā ा (lesson 4 material)

| # | Claim | Register | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | आ = /ɑː/, long open back unrounded; duration ≈ 2× hrasva अ. | FACT | STYLE_GUIDE §2.1; [REF:whitney1889 §22], [REF:ladefoged2015] | Traditional measure: hrasva = 1 mātrā, dīrgha = 2 (Śikṣā/prātiśākhya doctrine). |
| 4.2 | Length is phonemic — minimal pairs exist. Verified pairs usable in Ch 1: कल "indistinct/sweet (of sound)" vs काल "time"; तल "surface, bottom" vs ताल "rhythm; palmyra"; मल "dirt, impurity" vs माला "garland" (near-pair, differs in final ā too). | FACT | [REF:apte1890 s.v. कल, काल, तल, ताल, मल, माला] | Use कल/काल and तल/ताल as the true minimal pairs. |
| 4.3 | The mātrā ा (ā-kāra sign) attaches to the right of a consonant: क + आ = का. Independent letter आ is used word-initially; the mātrā is used after consonants. | FACT | [REF:masica1991 ch.6] | THE flagship fix (IR-007). |
| 4.4 | Readable words after L4 (glyph set अ आ क म त ल + ा), all attested: मम "my", माता "mother", लता "vine", माला "garland", कला "art", कमल "lotus", तल "surface", काल "time", ताल "rhythm", अमल "spotless", कमला "Lakṣmī; splendid woman", मत "thought, opinion". | FACT | [REF:apte1890 s.v. each], [REF:monierwilliams1899] | 12 real words readable by end of Ch 1 — exceeds the 8-word can-do. |
| 4.5 | माता / mātā ~ English "mother", Latin māter, Greek mētēr — cognates from PIE *méh₂tēr. | FACT | [REF:mallory2006] | Payoff moment linking L1's cognate lesson to real reading. |
