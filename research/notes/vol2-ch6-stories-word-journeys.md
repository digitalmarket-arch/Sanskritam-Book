## 0. Chapter-wide facts and policies

| 0.1 | "The Thirsty Crow" is an Aesopic fable ("The Crow and the Pitcher", Perry 390). No version is known in the Pañcatantra, the Hitopadeśa or the Jātakas; the earliest known versions are Greek and Latin (a Greek epigram of the 1st century CE; the Latin fables of Avianus, c. 400 CE, whose moral is that cleverness beats force). Pliny the Elder (1st c. CE) passes on a report of a bird — rendered "raven" in most translations — dropping stones into an urn to raise the water. Today it is one of India's best-known children's stories (Hindi प्यासा कौआ) and appears in Sanskrit school readers. Labels that call it "a Pañcatantra story" are modern. | HISTORY | [REF:perry1952] (no. 390) | Say "no known version", never "no Indian text contains it" or "it does not come from India": the evidence supports absence of attestation, not origin. Do NOT mention the speculative Lothal-jar reading in lessons. |
| 0.2 | "The Lion and the Mouse" is an Aesopic fable (Perry 150). The same tale — a lion spares a mouse, and the mouse later frees him by gnawing through ropes — is also told inside an Egyptian narrative written in Demotic in the 2nd century CE (the "Myth of the Sun's Eye"). India's fable books have cousins of it: in Pūrṇabhadra's recension of the Pañcatantra (Book 2), mice repay a herd of elephants by gnawing through the ropes that tie them; and in the frame story of Hitopadeśa Book 1 (Mitralābha) a mouse gnaws a net to free birds. Stories like these travelled widely; scholars disagree about routes, and the course makes no claim about which came first. | HISTORY | [REF:perry1952] (no. 150); [REF:lichtheim1980]; [REF:ryder1925] ("The Mice That Set Elephants Free"); [REF:hitopadesha mitralābha] | In the Pañcatantra story the elephants are tied with ropes after a trap, not caught in nets — keep that detail right. |
| 0.3 | The 1.x crow text needs three changes to fit the toolkit and stay correct: the plural पाषाणान् becomes the singular पाषाणम् with पुनः पुनः ("again and again"); पिपासितः is the clearer word for "thirsty" (1.x तृष्णार्तः is not wrong); the moral becomes the simple बुद्धिः एव बलम्। "intelligence itself is strength" (the 1.x बुद्धिः बलात् श्रेष्ठा is acceptable Sanskrit — MW records śreṣṭha "better than" with the ablative — but needs an untaught case; show it display-only if at all). | FACT | [REF:apte1890] s.vv. घट, क्षिप्, उपरि; [REF:monierwilliams1899] s.vv. pipāsita, śreṣṭha | Endings that depend on a word's role (घटे "in the pot", घटम्, पाषाणम्) are glossed whole, the v1.c06 reading policy. |
| 0.4 | The 1.x lion text needs four changes: निद्राम् करोति ("makes sleep") is unidiomatic → स्वपिति "sleeps"; the future करिष्यामि is outside the toolkit → the mouse says माम् मुञ्च! अहम् अपि तव मित्रम्। ("Let me go! I too am your friend."); the 1.x story never has the lion catch or release the mouse → add सः मूषकम् गृह्णाति। … सिंहः हसति। सः मूषकम् मुञ्चति।; कर्तयति (1.x) is attested in classical prose but the plain present is कृन्तति "cuts" — teach कृन्तति. Moral: न कः अपि लघुः। "no one is small", with the joined form न कोऽपि लघुः previewed. | FACT | [REF:apte1890] s.vv. स्वप्, ग्रह्, मुच्, कृत्; [REF:monierwilliams1899] s.v. kṛt | स्वपिति and गृह्णाति "build their stems differently" — whole words, no class numbers; मुञ्च is an imperative chunk like वदतु. |
| 0.5 | Sandhi in the moral, described correctly: before a word beginning with अ, a final -अः becomes -ओ and the following अ disappears, marked by the avagraha ऽ: कः + अपि → कोऽपि. | FACT | [REF:whitney1889] (cite bare) | NOT "the visarga turns the preceding a into o" — the whole ending -aḥ becomes -o. |
| 0.6 | अहिंसा परमो धर्मः "non-harming is the highest dharma" occurs five times in the critical edition of the Mahābhārata (1.11.12; 3.198.69; 13.116.1; 13.116.25; 13.117.37). The popular continuation "धर्महिंसा तथैव च" ("and so is violence in the service of dharma") is NOT in the critical edition at any of these places — a modern addition passed off as scripture. Pause form: अहिंसा परमः धर्मः; one join: परमः + धर्मः → परमो धर्मः (-अः → -ओ before a voiced consonant). | HISTORY | [REF:mahabharata 1.11.12] (and the other loci) | The "धर्महिंसा" line may appear in a lesson ONLY as a documented popular misquotation, clearly labelled, never as content (EDITORIAL_GUIDELINES §3 spirit). |
| 0.7 | उद्यमेन हि सिध्यन्ति कार्याणि न मनोरथैः। न हि सुप्तस्य सिंहस्य प्रविशन्ति मुखे मृगाः॥ "Tasks succeed through effort, not through wishing; deer do not walk into the mouth of a sleeping lion." — Hitopadeśa, introduction (prastāvikā), verse 36; the verse also circulates in Pañcatantra versions. The printed verse has no joins between words (it is already in pause form); मनोरथ (मनस् + रथ, "chariot of the mind" = wish) is one word. Plurals and case forms in the verse are glossed whole — a verse to read and recite, not grammar to produce (v1.c06 śloka precedent). | HISTORY | [REF:hitopadesha prastāvikā 36]; [REF:apte1890] s.vv. उद्यम, सिध्, सुप्त | Do not cite a Pañcatantra locus (recension unverified). |

| 0.9 | The 1.x "English from Sanskrit" list, re-sorted into four honest kinds: (a) COGNATES, no arrow — mother, father, brother, new, name, three, two, tooth, know, yoke, mid/middle, night, stand, foot, widow; (b) LOANS from Sanskrit — yoga (from योग "yoking, union", NOT from युग "yoke, age"; first attested in English c. 1820); (c) English words from LATIN or GREEK whose root is cognate with a Sanskrit word — video (Latin videō), ignite (Latin ignis ~ अग्नि), serpent (Latin serpēns ~ सर्प), voice/vocal (Latin vōx ~ वाच्), dental (Latin dēns ~ दन्त), novel, triple, dual, gnosis, nocturnal, state/station, pedal/pedestrian; (d) FALSE — juggle (from Latin ioculārī "to jest", unrelated to yoke or युग).
