## 1. L1 — एकादिपञ्चान्ताः: one to five (vocabulary)

Station 0 = v2.c02.l05's +1-week. **New (3):** एक, चतुर्, षट् — wait: षट् is l02's;
l01 takes 1–5: new = एक /eːkɐ/, चतुर् /t͡ɕɐt̪uɾ/ (+ चत्वारि counting form as display);
reviews द्वि (✓v1.c05.l03), त्रि (readable; first ledger entry — **verify: त्रि was
cognate-noted at v1.c05.l03 §13 but likely not a §14 headword — if absent, count as new**),
पञ्च (✓v1.c06.l01). The counting chant एकम् द्वे त्रीणि चत्वारि पञ्च introduced as
practice (0.1 policy line). Cognate rows: एक (no clean English cognate — say so, honesty

## 4. L4 — वासराः: the week (vocabulary)

**New (~8):** वासरः "day (of the week)" + the seven: रविवासरः सोमवासरः मङ्गलवासरः
बुधवासरः गुरुवासरः शुक्रवासरः शनिवासरः — count honestly (वासरः + 7 names = 8; the
planet first-members रवि/सोम/गुरु are ledger words already; मङ्गल/बुध/शुक्र/शनि arrive
inside the compounds — note, don't separately teach). The 0.8 honest frame: names
attested, the seven-day system a documented historical import (HISTORY callout, hedged
dating). Hindi -वार forms (सोमवार) noted as the everyday descendants. Calendar drills:

## 5. L5 — पुनरावृत्तिः: the numbers & time exam (review)

Five tasks: A listening (number/day matching); B dictation (numbers + one date line);
C counting production (count objects aloud 1–10; recite the chant; digit↔name matching
at speed); D register sort — cards: "November comes from Sanskrit नव" (**reject** — THE
October-fix card: Latin's own novem; cognates only), "अष्ट and octō share one ancestor"
(FACT), "होरा was borrowed INTO Sanskrit from Greek" (FACT — direction reversed!),
"the seven-day week has always been Indian" (**reject** — historical import, honestly
dated), "नव 'nine' and नव 'new' are two words that sound alike" (FACT), "dharma went
Sanskrit→English; होरा went Greek→Sanskrit" (FACT — the both-directions card);
E the calendar task: given a month grid with Devanagari digits, answer अद्य/श्वः/ह्यः
questions and present three dates aloud. Gates v2.c04 (क्रियाः — First Verbs).
