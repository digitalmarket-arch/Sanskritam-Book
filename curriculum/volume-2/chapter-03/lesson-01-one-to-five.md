## 1. Hook

You have been able to *read* पञ्च for a whole volume — it carried the ञ्च showcase — and
to *write* ९ since the signs lesson. What you could not yet do, in the language whose
grammarians counted everything, is count. v1.c06.l01 left a promise on the record: the
names of the numbers beyond पञ्च are Volume 2 Chapter 3's business. Chapter 3 is here.
Today the first five numbers get their names — three old friends back on duty, two new
words older than every calendar they ever landed on, and one honest surprise: the word
for "one" has no clean English twin — *one* is a relative only at the root — and this
course will be the one to tell you.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — the second-oldest drawer in the vocabulary, and a promise kept.

Last chapter opened the deepest drawer of inherited words — the kin terms. The numerals
live in the drawer beside it: learned in childhood, used daily, replaced almost never —
which is why the cognate shelf can open again today with full confidence.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The stems एक, द्वि, त्रि, चतुर्, पञ्च are the numbers' citation
> names — the shapes this course teaches first and dictionaries list (पञ्च is headed by its
> full stem पञ्चन् (pañcan), whose final न् drops in compounds and in the counting form). In
> running Sanskrit, numbers *inflect*: they change dress by gender and case. The neuter
> counting recitation — एकम् द्वे त्रीणि चत्वारि पञ्च — is standard practice, real
> inflected Sanskrit learned whole. [REF:apte1890]; [REF:whitney1889]

One line of course history, stated as the salvage it is: the 1.x numbers unit (PDF
pp.30–31) taught the correct stems — the audit kept them — then attached a derivation
story about English month names that is not true. Today the stems arrive with labels on
and the shelf built honestly; the printed fix is next lesson's centerpiece. And a
smaller promise redeemed: v1.c06.l01 taught पञ्च as "a reading word that happens to be
the best count-race shout in the language" and pointed here. Here we are.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why a chant, why a ladder, and why the beat matters.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Number names are the textbook case for automaticity: a digit's
> name must arrive without translation, or counting stays a classroom trick. Timed
> retrieval of small chunks — today's beat-paced digit-name ladder — is the fluency
> strand doing exactly its job. [REF:nation2013]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spaced retrieval beats massed re-study; today's Station 0 is
> v2.c02.l05's +1-week interval landing on schedule, and this lesson sets its own
> +1-day and +1-week re-runs before it ends (§20). [REF:cepeda2006] [REF:roediger2006]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Numerals are among the most stable items of basic vocabulary —
> rarely borrowed, slowly replaced. That stability is why they preserve the systematic
> sound-correspondences that serve as evidence of shared descent, and why the numeral
> set is a classic showcase of the Indo-European family's unity. [REF:mallory2006]

The chant earns its place twice: rhythm chunks a sequence for serial recall, and the
chant is real language data — learned whole now, understood as a system later
[REF:nation2013].

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — counting, in sentences and half-sentences you fully own:

> **एकम् द्वे त्रीणि चत्वारि पञ्च।**
> (ekam dve trīṇi catvāri pañca.) "One, two, three, four, five." — एक, दो, तीन, चार, पाँच।

> **एतत् किम्? — त्रि!**
> (etat kim? — tri!) "What is this (card)? — Three!" — यह क्या है? — तीन!

> **पुष्पम् अत्र अस्ति। एकम्… द्वे… त्रीणि। — त्रीणि!**
> (puṣpam atra asti. ekam… dve… trīṇi. — trīṇi!) "A flower is here. One… two… three. —
> Three!" — फूल यहाँ है। एक… दो… तीन। — तीन!

Three moves, all old friends in new work: the chant counts while the finger points and
the count closes on its last chant word; the bare citation name answers a digit card;
and अस्ति keeps its standing job when something *is here*. No verb is needed: a digit
card answered bare — त्रि! — is this course's name-tag drill convention (§15), not a
full Sanskrit word. Pause form throughout, as always.

## 8. English Explanation

No new letter, no new sign — the standing Volume 2 luxury. The load is two ledger-new
words, three old friends back on duty, one chant, and one honest piece of bookkeeping.

### The set — five on duty

**एक** (eka) "one" — back from v1.c02.l04, the course's first number word, and the humblest headline of the day: say the whole word; the
Hindi habit clips it to "ek", and §12 is waiting. **द्वि** (dvi) "two" — back from
v1.c05.l03, where the द्व ligature made it readable. **त्रि** (tri) "three" — the
almost-old friend: mentioned in v1.c01.l01's cognate table and again in v1.c05.l03 §13,
readable for a volume, never a headword until today — so today it formally enters the
ledger (§14). **चतुर्** (catur) "four" — new, the course's first headword to *end on a
bare र्*: the tongue taps and the word stops, halanta and all. **पञ्च** (pañca) "five" — back
from v1.c06.l01, where it carried the ञ्च showcase and the promise this chapter keeps.

### Two dresses, honestly labeled

Each number owns two shapes today. The **stems** एक द्वि त्रि चतुर् पञ्च are the
citation names — what the ladder answers, what a bare answer
uses. The **chant** एकम् द्वे त्रीणि चत्वारि पञ्च is the neuter counting dress — real,
inflected Sanskrit, the standard counting recitation, learned whole as a rhythm.
Numbers change dress by the gender and role of what they count; that system is honestly
deferred to Volume 4 (§15). Notice, for free, that पञ्च keeps one shape in both rows —
noticing is allowed, legislating is not.

### The digits pair up

१ २ ३ ४ ५ have been yours since v1.c05.l05; today they stop being mute shapes and get
their names. The ladder discipline (§9 drill 4, §18 exercise 1) runs both directions —
see the digit, say the name; hear the name, write the digit — within a beat each way.
The standing policy holds: Latin digits in prose, Devanagari digits as content.

### What is deliberately not here

The title एकादिपञ्चान्ताः (ekādipañcāntāḥ) "the ones from one to five" — एक and पञ्च
wearing their joined dress at the seams: एक + आदि "beginning with one", पञ्च + अन्ताः
"ending with five"; joining rules are Volume 3's business — read it, build nothing.

Three absences, all honest. The Sanskrit word that *asks* "how many?" is next lesson's
headword — today the pointing finger and the digit card ask the question. Numeral
agreement is Volume 4's machinery; today no number ever touches a plural noun (§15's
fence). And the shelf's एक slot stays empty: no clean everyday-English cognate, and the
course says so instead of reaching (§13).

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — no new sounds; the work is full finals, dental onsets, and one word that
ends on a consonant. Audio: 🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c03l01-drill.mp3`
(drills 1–3), `planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c03l01-word-list.mp3` (three speeds +
dictation track).

**Drill 1 — the finals.** एक in slow motion: long pure ए, then क with its *short full*
अ — three beats in all, ए two and क one. Then चतुर्: the tongue taps for र् and the word ends; if any
vowel escapes after the tap, re-run. Then पञ्च: pa — ñ — ca, the middle a full
consonant on च's station, never a nasalized vowel.

**Drill 2 — the roll-call.** With the audio, fixed order, three passes (slow · natural
· timed): एक — द्वि — त्रि — चतुर् — पञ्च. The order is the memory palace; your mouth
learns it so the ladder can shuffle it later.

**Drill 3 — the chant on a beat.** एकम् द्वे त्रीणि चत्वारि पञ्च — five words, five
beats, even tempo. The long vowels of त्रीणि and चत्वारि each take their two full
counts; keep the beat honest and the quantities take care of themselves. Then chant
while pointing: five fingers, five flowers on the worksheet, one word per touch.

**Drill 4 — the ladder.** The audio flashes digit calls; you name each within a beat:
३! — त्रि! Then the reverse row: hear *catur*, write ४. Log the third pass time (§18
exercise 1) — timed retrieval, the fluency strand on duty [REF:nation2013].

## 10. IPA

No new values — every symbol has been on your chart since Volume 1.

**Word transcriptions (stems):** एक /eːkɐ/ · द्वि /d̪ʋi/ · त्रि /t̪ɾi/ · चतुर्
/t͡ɕɐt̪uɾ/ · पञ्च /pɐɲt͡ɕɐ/.

**The chant:** एकम् /eːkɐm/ · द्वे /d̪ʋeː/ · त्रीणि /t̪ɾiːɳi/ · चत्वारि /t͡ɕɐt̪ʋɑːɾi/ ·
पञ्च /pɐɲt͡ɕɐ/.

**Narrow notes.** एक opens on /eː/ — always long, a pure monophthong, never the English
"ay" glide [eɪ]; frozen-jaw test. त्रि: dental /t̪/ straight into the tap /ɾ/, one
gesture, then short /i/ — English *tree* fails it three ways (alveolar t, approximant
r, long vowel). चतुर्: opens on the palatal affricate /t͡ɕ/, carries a dental /t̪/
inside, ends on the bare tap /ɾ/ with no vowel after it; the chant form चत्वारि
[t͡ɕɐt̪.ʋɑː.ɾi] welds त्व with no seam vowel. त्रीणि hides a retroflex /ɳ/ after the
long /iː/ — tongue-tip curled, not dental न्.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — four checks; the articulators know all of this already. The word-anatomy
strip on the digit-name cards (`planned:assets/images/volume-2/v2c03l01-digit-name-cards.svg`)
marks the त्रि and चतुर् joins.

1. **The frozen-jaw check.** Mirror on एक: set the jaw for ए and hold it still through
   the vowel — any drift toward "ay" means a diphthong leaked in. Then a full short अ.
2. **The teeth check.** त्रि and the त् inside चतुर्: tongue-tip *on the teeth*, not on
   the ridge behind them. English t is the ridge; Sanskrit त् is the teeth.
3. **The full-stop check.** Say चतुर् in slow motion and freeze on the last sound: the
   tongue taps and the mouth *holds* — no vowel escapes after the tap.
4. **The tongue-body check.** पञ्च: the middle of the tongue presses the palate for ञ —
   a held consonant, nose open — then releases into च. If the nose works alone and the
   tongue never lands, Hindi पाँच has intruded (v1.c06.l01's drill).

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; these feed the quiz distractors.

1. **एक clipped to Hindi "ek".** The final अ is a real vowel and it is there. *Fix:*
   three beats — ए two, then a full /ɐ/ — the schwa-deletion discipline of Volume 1 never retires.
2. **चतुर् re-voweled to "chatura".** The halanta means what it says. *Fix:* §11 check
   3 — tap and stop, nothing after.
3. **पञ्च nasalized to "panch".** The v1.c06.l01 trap returns: Hindi पाँच replaces the
   full ञ with a nasalized vowel. *Fix:* tongue lands on च's station and holds a beat.
4. **त्रि stretched to English "tree".** Alveolar t, approximant r, long vowel — three
   errors in one syllable. *Fix:* dental त्, tap र्, short इ (§10, §11 check 2).
5. **The dress confusion.** Treating the chant forms as "the real numbers" and the
   stems as abbreviations — or the stems as correct and the chant as babytalk. *Fix:*
   both are real; different jobs (§15) — names versus counting dress.
6. **Agreement invented under enthusiasm.** Gluing endings onto numbers to make them
   "match" what is counted. *Fix:* the matching system is real and it is Volume 4's;
   today the chant counts, the bare stem answers a digit card, nothing is glued (§15's
   fence).

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — the shelf opens, one slot stays honestly empty, and a ledger debt is paid.

### The shelf — four sibling rows, no arrows

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** द्वि ~ Latin *duo* ~ English *two* · त्रि ~ Latin *trēs* ~ English
> *three* · चतुर् ~ Latin *quattuor* ~ English *four* · पञ्च ~ Greek *pénte* ~ English
> *five* — parallel descendants of the Proto-Indo-European numerals, among the most
> stable words in the family. No borrowing happened in any direction: nobody's "three"
> came from anybody else's. [REF:mallory2006]; [REF:burrow2001]

Read the shelf the way Chapter 2 taught you: no arrows between siblings. The tilde says
"same ancestor", and that is all it says.

### एक — the honestly empty slot

The shelf also teaches by what it *lacks*: एक has no clean everyday-English cognate to
put beside it. Etymological handbooks do trace एक and *one* to the same ancestral
root, but each language built its word with a different suffix — एक with a k, *one*
with an n — so they are relatives at the root, not the same inherited word the way
द्वि and *two* are [REF:mallory2006]. Printing them as a clean row would be reaching —
how the 1.x book went wrong — and honesty beats
reaching. The empty slot is itself the lesson: cognate rows are evidence, not
decoration, and where the evidence is not clean, this course says so out loud.

### त्रि — the word that was always almost here

त्रि has haunted this course since its first hour: v1.c01.l01's cognate table showed it
beside *three*, v1.c05.l03 §13 named it again beside द्वि — yet it was never a §14
headword, so the ledger never counted it. Today it formally enters, and its Notes cell
says exactly that (§14). Bookkeeping is a register too, and it gets the same honesty.

One forward line, and only one: next lesson finishes the set — six to ten — and fixes,
in print, a famous claim the 1.x course made about English month names. Bring your

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — two dresses on one rack, and a fence with a gate marked Volume 4.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** (1) The stems एक द्वि त्रि चतुर् पञ्च are citation names — the
> shapes the words are named by; dictionaries head five as पञ्चन् (pañcan). (2) Sanskrit
> numbers inflect for case, and एक to चतुर् for gender too; the chant एकम् द्वे त्रीणि
> चत्वारि पञ्च is the neuter counting dress — real, inflected Sanskrit, standard
> counting practice, learned whole. (3) The inflection system is honestly deferred: the
> course builds it in Volume 4; until then counting runs on the chant.
> [REF:apte1890]; [REF:whitney1889]

One drill convention, labeled as one: when a digit card goes up, this course accepts
the bare citation name — त्रि! — as the answer. That is a name-tag drill, not a full
Sanskrit word: a stem becomes a word only once it takes an ending, so a real count
closes on its last chant word — three flowers end on त्रीणि!, five on पञ्च!, which
happens to be a real form already.

### The two dresses

| Job | Shape | Where you use it |
|---|---|---|
| Citation name | एक · द्वि · त्रि · चतुर् · पञ्च | word lists, the digit-name ladder, bare digit-card answers (drill convention) |
| Counting dress | एकम् · द्वे · त्रीणि · चत्वारि · पञ्च | the chant; counting aloud while pointing |

Both rows are real Sanskrit. The left row names; the right row counts. पञ्च sits in
both unchanged — noticing that costs nothing; concluding a rule from it would cost
plenty, and we decline.

### The fence — what we are not building

अत्र त्रि फलानि?? — no. The moment a number touches a counted noun, Sanskrit wants the
number *dressed to match* — by gender, by case — and that machinery is exactly what
Volume 4 builds (the salvage map parks numeral gender at v4.c05). Until then the rule
is a fence, stated plainly: no number meets a plural noun in this course. The chant
counts while you point; the bare citation name answers a digit card; the frames stay
pause-form,
verbless or with अस्ति — safe ground, exactly as मम + family word was in Chapter 2.
