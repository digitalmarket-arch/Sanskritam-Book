## 1. Hook

Look at a clock. The word on its face left Greece twice. One copy traveled west, through
Latin and French, and became English *hour*. The other sailed east with Greek astronomy
and became Sanskrit होरा. This course has shown you words that share one ancestor (माता
beside *mother*) and words Sanskrit lent out (dharma, karma) — today it delivers the
arrow the last exam promised: a word Sanskrit *took in*. Around it, the seven words that
run your calendar — time itself, today, tomorrow, yesterday, and the spans from week to
year — including two of the smallest words in the ledger with the biggest workouts inside.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — plain old words, one famous traveler, and a promise kept.

The everyday time words on today's list are ordinary classical vocabulary: कालः "time",
the deictic trio अद्य, श्वः, ह्यः, the spans वर्षम्, मासः, सप्ताहः — all attested across
the classical language [REF:apte1890 s.vv.]; [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.vv.]. One of
them even carries its own arithmetic: सप्ताहः is transparently सप्त (sapta) + अह (aha) — "a seven-of-days" (§8).

The traveler is होरा.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** Indian astronomy and astrology (jyotiṣa) absorbed a body of
> Hellenistic Greek material, and with it a handful of Greek technical words entered
> Sanskrit — होरा "hour", from Greek *hṓra*, among them, attested in jyotiṣa
> literature. The dating stays deliberately coarse: the word came "with Hellenistic
> astronomy" — this course claims no precise year.
> [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. होरा]; [REF:burrow2001]

One line of course history: the 1.x curriculum spoke of borrowing in one direction only —
words leaving Sanskrit. The last exam's direction table (v2.c02.l05 §15) left exactly one
row flagged "next chapter's story" for the missing arrow. This is that story.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — pointing words, usable frames, and the schedule doing its job.

अद्य, श्वः and ह्यः are *deictics* — pointing words whose reference moves with the
speaker's day, exactly as अत्र and तत्र move with the speaker's position (v1.c05.l03; the location kit of v2.c02.l04).
श्वः names a different calendar day every time the sun rises; tonight's श्वः is
tomorrow's अद्य. That is why today's drills make you *compute* the answer off a strip or
a calendar instead of reciting a memorized one — a deictic can only be owned in use.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** New vocabulary sticks best when it arrives inside usable patterns
> and gets retrieved from them under light time pressure — the frame अद्य किम्? turns
> the trio into sentences you can say the hour you learn them, and the timed
> both-direction strip drill builds the retrieval speed a real exchange demands.
> [REF:nation2013]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spaced retrieval beats massed re-study; today's Station 0 is
> v2.c03.l02's +1-day interval landing on schedule, and this lesson's own +1-day and
> +1-week re-runs are set before it ends (§20). [REF:cepeda2006] [REF:roediger2006]

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the time words, told in sentences you fully own:

> **अद्य किम्? — अद्य प्रभातम्।**
> (adya kim? — adya prabhātam.) "What is it, today? — Today: morning." — आज क्या है? —
> आज सुबह।

> **अद्य विद्यालयः अस्ति। श्वः अपि अस्ति।**
> (adya vidyālayaḥ asti. śvaḥ api asti.) "There is school today. Tomorrow too." — आज
> विद्यालय है। कल भी है।

> **कति? — सप्त! सप्ताहः!**
> (kati? — sapta! saptāhaḥ!) "How many? — Seven! A week!" — कितने? — सात! सप्ताह!

Three old frames, no new machinery. The first rides the एतत् किम्? pattern with a time
word in front — and notice its honesty: ask it again at dusk and the right answer has
become अद्य सायम्। The second is the standing अस्ति line with अपि doing its v1.c06.l02
job. The third is lesson 2's own grammar — कति? answered bare — counting the week strip
to its one-word punchline. A time word in front, pause form throughout, and no verb
required where naming will do.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

आज हिन्दी आपकी सबसे बड़ी सहायक भी है और सबसे महीन जाल भी। सहायक इसलिए कि काल, वर्ष,
मास, सप्ताह — चारों शब्द हिन्दी में जीवित हैं; पढ़ने में कोई अक्षर नहीं रोकेगा। जाल
इसलिए कि हिन्दी का "कल" दोनों दिशाओं में चलता है — बीता हुआ कल भी, आने वाला कल भी; कौन-सा
है, यह वाक्य का बाक़ी हिस्सा बताता है। संस्कृत यह काम शब्द से ही कर देती है: बीता दिन
**ह्यः**, आने वाला दिन **श्वः**, और बीच में आज का दिन **अद्य**। इसीलिए आज की तिकड़ी का
अभ्यास दोनों दिशाओं में चलेगा — पट्टी पर दिन छूकर शब्द बोलना, और शब्द सुनकर दिन छूना —
जब तक हाथ और जीभ दोनों को दिशा याद न हो जाए।

दूसरी बात — छोटे शब्द, पूरी मेहनत। श्वः और ह्यः दिखने में दो-दो अक्षर के हैं, पर दोनों
में संयुक्ताक्षर और विसर्ग साथ-साथ काम पर हैं। श्वः में श् + व का जोड़ है — बीच में कोई
स्वर नहीं; ह्यः में ह् + य — वही ह्य जो आप पाँचवें अध्याय से पहचानते हैं। और अन्त की
हल्की साँस कभी मत छोड़िए: "श्व" अधूरा है, श्वः पूरा। ह्यः को "हयः" पढ़ा तो जोड़ के भीतर
स्वर घुस गया — धीरे-धीरे, घटक बोलकर, जोड़ पढ़िए। वर्षम् में धर्म वाला रेफ है — र ऊपर
बैठा है, पर पहले बोला जाता है — और अन्त का म् होंठ बन्द करके।

तीसरी बात — होरा की कहानी, जो आज का सबसे बड़ा तथ्य है। संस्कृत ने भी शब्द उधार लिये हैं।
होरा यूनानी भाषा से, यूनानी ज्योतिष के साथ, संस्कृत में आया — और वही यूनानी शब्द दूसरे
रास्ते से — लैटिन और फ़्रांसीसी से होकर — अंग्रेज़ी का hour बना। यानी होरा और hour
रिश्तेदार तो हैं, पर माता-mother की तरह नहीं: वहाँ कोई उधार नहीं था, एक ही पुरखे की दो

## 8. English Explanation

No new letter, no new sign — the Volume 2 luxury holds. The load is six new headwords,
two upgrades, one dissection, and one arrow drawn in the honest direction.

### The set — one container, three pointers, four spans

**कालः** (kālaḥ) "time" is the container word — time itself, the thing the rest of the
list measures, and this lesson's title; it is an upgrade, काल (kāla) in sentence dress,
readable since v1.c01.l04 (§15). The three pointers are the everyday trio:
**अद्य** (adya) "today", **श्वः** (śvaḥ) "tomorrow", **ह्यः** (hyaḥ) "yesterday" — the
words your every calendar exchange will run on. The spans climb a ladder: **होरा**
(horā) "hour", then दिनम् — yours since v2.c01.l03 — then **सप्ताहः** (saptāhaḥ) "week",
then **मासः** (māsaḥ) "month" (the other upgrade — §15), then **वर्षम्** (varṣam) "year".
Run the ladder small-to-large until the order is muscle (§9): होरा — दिनम् — सप्ताहः —
मासः — वर्षम्.

### A word with arithmetic inside

सप्ताहः is built from parts you own: **सप्त** (sapta) "seven" — last lesson's numeral —
plus **अह** (aha), the compound-final form of the old day-word अहन् (ahan): "a seven-of-days"
[REF:apte1890 s.v. सप्ताह]. You have met that day-word in disguise already: मध्याह्नः
(madhyāhnaḥ) "midday" hides a form of it after मध्य (v2.c01.l03 §8). So when the week
strip asks कति? the word itself answers: सप्त! — the numeral working *inside* a word,
which is exactly the kind of receipt this chapter collects.

### The trio at work — both directions

The trio are pointing words (§5), so the drill is physical. Direction one: a finger
lands on a day — the strip's middle slot, or a date on a real calendar — and you say the
word within a beat: अद्य! Direction two: you hear the word and your finger finds the
day: ह्यः → one slot back. The frames stay verbless or carry अस्ति, exactly as §6 runs
them: अद्य किम्? — अद्य प्रभातम्। — श्वः अपि विद्यालयः अस्ति? The day-cycle words of
v2.c01.l03 — प्रभातम्, दिनम्, सायम्, रात्रिः — are the trio's working partners all
lesson. For Hindi speakers the trio is also a repair kit: Hindi कल covers both
directions and lets context decide; Sanskrit assigns one word per direction, so the
strip drill is where the habit gets rebuilt (§12).

### The hour that sailed in

होरा is today's showcase and §13's business, so here only the shape of it: a real
Sanskrit word, at home in the astronomical literature — and a *borrowed* one, taken in
from Greek, the exact reverse of the dharma story. One preview of the fine print: English
*hour* comes from the *same* Greek word, by its own road. The full story, with the arrows
drawn, is three sections down.

### What is deliberately not here

Two machines stay honestly undelivered. No locatives — "in a week" and every such form
waits for Volume 3's case machinery; today a fronted time word does that work (अद्य
विद्यालयः अस्ति।). No plurals and no numeral agreement — so "how many hours in a day?"
cannot be *asked* yet; bare कति? plus a pointed finger carries every counting exchange,
exactly as lesson 2 taught. And the days of the week? Next lesson's whole business —
today's trio is deliberately week-name-free, so it can snap onto the seven names the
moment they arrive.

a voiceless breath.

natural · timed): होरा — दिनम् — सप्ताहः — मासः — वर्षम्. The order is the memory
palace; your mouth learns the ladder so §18's exercises come out unprompted.

**Drill 3 — the trio, both directions.** Lay out the three-slot strip (ह्यः · अद्य ·
श्वः). Round one: the audio names a slot position; touch it and say the word before the
beat runs out. Round two: the audio calls a word; your finger finds the slot in silence,
then you confirm aloud. Swap rounds until neither direction hesitates — timed retrieval
of deictics in their frames [REF:nation2013].

**Drill 4 — three speeds.** The §14 list, block by block, natural, target pace; log the
third time (§18 exercise 4). The standing law at every speed: the visarga breathed on
the *smallest* words too — śvaḥ and hyaḥ end in a real breath, and होरा keeps its ओ a
pure long vowel from first pass to fastest.

**Narrow note — होरा:** the ओ is /oː/ — long, pure, steady — never the English "go"

it is written above — /ʋɐɾʂɐm/, tongue curling back right after the tap, then the lips
closing on a full final /ɐm/.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — four checks; the articulators know all of this already. The word-anatomy
strip on the time-ladder card (`planned:assets/images/volume-2/v2c03l03-time-ladder.svg`)
marks the श्व, ह्य and र्ष joins.

1. **The one-gesture check.** Say श्वः in slow motion and freeze after श्: the tongue
   blade is still high when the lower lip rises to the teeth for व — one continuous
   gesture, no vowel between. Same test on ह्यः: the breath of ह is still flowing when
   the tongue arches for य.
2. **The breath check.** Palm before your mouth for श्वः and ह्यः: after the vowel, a
   light puff — the visarga — must touch your palm. These are the smallest words you
   own; they still earn the full exam discipline of v2.c01.l05.
3. **The curl check.** वर्षम् in slow motion: tongue tip taps for र, then curls back
   for ष — two positions, in that order, though the र is written above. Then lips
   visibly together on the final म् — the सायम् mirror test (v2.c01.l03).
4. **The rounding check.** Mirror on होरा: lips round for ओ and *hold* — no drift
   toward a "go"-style glide — then open wide for the long final आ, two full beats.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; these feed the quiz distractors.

1. **The visarga vanishes on the tiny words** — श्वः clipped to "śva", ह्यः to "hya".
   Two letters do not buy an exemption: the final breath is there. *Fix:* the §11 palm
   check; drill 1 slowly, then at speed.
2. **A vowel leaks into the joins** — ह्यः read "hayaḥ", श्वः read "śavaḥ". *Fix:* a
   conjunct has no vowel inside — ह्य is the v1.c05.l03 recognition family at work;
   build from components, ह् + य, one block.
3. **श्वः and ह्यः swapped** — tomorrow for yesterday. *Fix:* pair each with a gesture
   and keep it for life: श्वः points forward, ह्यः points back over the shoulder; then
   run §9 drill 3 in both directions until the hands stop thinking.
4. **Hindi कल imported** — one word used for both days, with context left to sort it
   out. Sanskrit assigns one word per direction. *Fix:* never translate bare "कल";
   decide the direction first, then pick श्वः or ह्यः.
5. **The direction flip on होरा** — "Greek took *hṓra* from Sanskrit", or "English
   *hour* comes from Sanskrit होरा". *Fix:* §13 — the loan ran INTO Sanskrit, and
   *hour* took its own road from the same Greek source; neither claim survives with
   the arrows drawn.
6. **होरा sung like English "o"** — the ओ diphthongized to [oʊ] — and **वर्षम्
   flattened** — the ष read as dental स. *Fix:* pure steady /oː/ (§10, §11); tap then
   curl for र्ष, the धर्म rule of v1.c05.l04.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — three stories inside today's list.

### होरा — the hour that sailed east

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** होरा "hour" is a genuine loanword INTO Sanskrit: it was borrowed
> from Greek *hṓra*, arriving with Hellenistic astronomy and astrology, and it is
> attested in the jyotiṣa literature. English *hour* goes back to the same Greek word
> by a different road — Greek to Latin to French to English. So होरा and *hour* are
> relatives by *borrowing* from one shared source — not, like माता and *mother*,
> siblings by shared descent from a common ancestor. The relation is real in both
> cases; the *kind* of relation is part of the fact.
> [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. होरा]; [REF:burrow2001]; [REF:oed s.v. hour]

Say the difference plainly, because it is the whole skill: माता and *mother* were
*inherited* — no arrow, nobody borrowed. Dharma and karma were *lent out* — arrow east.
होरा was *taken in* — arrow west, an arrow arriving FROM the west, just as dharma's
arrow arrives in English from the east: name the arrow by where it comes from. With
that third arrow the direction toolkit is complete: every direction demonstrated, and "related" has stopped being one claim and
become three. The होरा story is a gift, not an embarrassment: living languages borrow in
both directions — Sanskrit lent the world dharma and took in an hour, both on the record.

### सप्ताहः — arithmetic inside a word

सप्ताहः = सप्त (sapta) + अह (aha) — seven + day: "a seven-of-days"
[REF:apte1890 s.v. सप्ताह]. Lesson 2's numeral is not just *beside* this word on the
ledger; it is load-bearing *inside* it. The day-word अह is an old one — the same word
whose form hides at the end of मध्याह्नः "mid-day" (v2.c01.l03 §8) — so today's
dissection is a reunion, not an introduction. अह itself stays a display component, off
the ledger; the working word is the whole सप्ताहः.

### The trio — words that point

अद्य, श्वः and ह्यः never change shape (§15) — what changes is the world around them.
Tonight, while you sleep, tomorrow's श्वः quietly becomes अद्य, and today's अद्य becomes
ह्यः; the words hold still and the days walk through them. That is why the §9 strip
drill points at *positions*, not dates: the trio is a moving window, and owning it means
computing the answer fresh each day — अद्य किम्? has a different true answer every
morning, and that is the design, not a defect.

## 18. Exercises

relatives the same way होरा and *hour* are." (f) "सप्ताहः carries सप्त inside it — a
seven-of-days." Check the pair (d)/(e) before looking anything up: both claim
"relatives" — why is one kept and one rejected?

**अभ्यासः 4 · श्रुतलेखनम् (dictation — production, written).** Play the dictation track
🔊: the six new headwords plus कालः and मासः in random order, each read twice with a writing pause.

too — the श्व, ह्य, द्य and प्त joins welded, the repha hooked above ष in वर्षम्. 7/8 is
a pass; re-take tomorrow (§20).

**अभ्यासः 5 · अनुवादः (translate the day — production, written).** Write the Sanskrit,
pause form: (a) "It is morning today." (b) "Is there school tomorrow? — Yes, tomorrow
too." (c) "What is this? — A week! How many? — Seven!" (d) "It is night. Tomorrow,
morning again." Read your answers aloud as one tiny day. Self-check: every sentence is a
§6/§16/§17 line or one word-swap away from one — and nothing wears a locative.

**अभ्यासः 6 · कालसोपानम् (the span ladder — production, spoken).** Recite the ladder

वर्षम्. Then stand at the week strip and close the loop aloud: कति? — सप्त! सप्ताहः!

## 22. Teacher Notes

अध्यापकटिप्पणी

**Open with the clock, not the list.** Draw a clock on the board, write *hour* beneath
it, and ask where the word is from. Collect votes — someone will say Latin, someone
English. Then draw the two arrows out of Greek *hṓra*: west to *hour*, east to होरा (on
the cards this is the arrow from the west — name the arrow by where it comes from) —
and let the room feel the reversal: Sanskrit *received* this one. The lesson's honesty
axis lands better as a map revealed than as a disclaimer read out.

**Timing plan (45 min):** Station 0 + prompts, scores logged (7) → the hour riddle on
the board, two arrows drawn (5) → set walk-through on the time-ladder card; सप्ताहः
dissected live, सप्त + अह on the board (8) → trio strip drill in pairs, both
directions, gestures fixed — श्वः forward, ह्यः back (8) → the direction chart: three
relations walked, the (d)/(e) card pair argued (6) → word list at three speeds;
dictation launch (5) → the no-dress rail on the parade table; कालः and मासः upgrades in
one line (3) → exercises, homework, the calendar task (3).

**Board work:** a three-slot strip center-stage (ह्यः · अद्य · श्वः) with arrows, the
span ladder climbing beside it (होरा → वर्षम्), and a two-column loan ledger in the
corner: "lent out: dharma, karma →" / "taken in: ← होरा". Keep the chalk arrows honest —
every claim in this lesson is an arrow or the absence of one.

**Run the direction talk carefully.** Someone will ask "so *hour* and होरा are cognates
like माता and *mother*?" That is the teachable moment: both pairs are related, but the
*kind* differs — shared descent there (no arrows), shared source here (two borrowing
arrows out of Greek). Exercise 3's card pair (d)/(e) forces exactly this conversation —
let the room argue it before you land it; the chapter exam names the relation outright.

**Differentiation:** Hindi speakers own half the list — their work is the finals (the
visarga on tiny words, म् closed on वर्षम्) and the कल split, where instinct actively
misleads: drill श्वः/ह्यः with gestures until the direction is physical. Script-side
learners build श्वः and ह्यः from components before speed work, and should say वर्षम्
slowly against the धर्म precedent before reading it fast.

**What students find hard:** believing that two-letter words deserve full discipline
(the palm check convinces faster than argument); the deictic shift — "but yesterday you
said श्वः was *Tuesday*!" — which is the point: run the strip on consecutive days and
let the words move; the होरा arrow, which pride sometimes wants reversed — the standing
answer is that lending dharma and borrowing होरा are the same kind of fact, and both are
receipts; and keeping weekday names out of the drills — promise them tomorrow.
