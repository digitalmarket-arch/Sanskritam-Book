## 1. Hook

This chapter handed you the ten oldest words you will ever count with — and two
detective stories about direction. Your calendar's November really does contain a
"nine": it is Latin's own *novem*, cousin — not child — of Sanskrit नव. And an hour
sailed the other way: होरा came INTO Sanskrit from Greek. Today you prove all of it:
count to ten with a tongue that knows dental from retroflex, read a month written in
Devanagari digits, run अद्य-श्वः-ह्यः around a real week — and sort nine claims where
the winning answer is the honest arrow. Roughly twenty-three ledger-new words this
chapter. Time to show they count.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — one paragraph, as in every review lesson.

Last chapter's exam graded three arrows; today the toolkit completes. This chapter
crossed all four relations a pair of words can have: cognates (अष्ट ~ octō — one
ancestor, two heirs, no arrow), loans outbound (dharma → English), loans inbound
(Greek hṓra → होरा), and the subtle fourth — two languages borrowing from one source
(English *hour* and Sanskrit होरा, cousins by borrowing). It also delivered the fix
the 1.x audit demanded: the month names are Latin's own arithmetic, and the honest
version — one ancestor's numbers surviving in two calendars — is the grander story.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** The seven-day planetary week entered Indian timekeeping in
> the early centuries CE, alongside Hellenistic astronomy; the वासर day-names are
> attested in later classical and medieval texts and are today's standard Sanskrit.
> [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. वासर]; [REF:burrow2001]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spaced practice produces far more durable learning than the
> same practice massed together. [REF:cepeda2006]

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why the counting recording and the calendar grid are the real
exam.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Retrieval practice strengthens memory more than re-reading —
> the testing effect. [REF:roediger2006]

Numbers are the textbook case for **automaticity**: a digit's name must arrive
without translation, or dates, counts and times stay unusable in real speech
[REF:nation2013]. That is why task C's ladder is timed at a beat, not merely marked
right or wrong — it measures retrieval speed. Counting real objects bare (round 4)
then moves the words from recitation into *reference*: the difference between a
memorized chant and a number you actually use. The calendar task does the same for
the time words: अद्य, श्वः and ह्यः are deictics — their meaning shifts with the day
— so the grid forces you to compute today's answer, not recall yesterday's.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the exam's three frames, fully readable:

> **एकम् द्वे त्रीणि चत्वारि पञ्च षट् सप्त अष्ट नव दश।**
> (ekam dve trīṇi catvāri pañca ṣaṭ sapta aṣṭa nava daśa) "One, two, three, four,
> five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten." — एक, दो, तीन, चार, पाँच, छह, सात, आठ, नौ, दस।

> **अद्य कः वासरः? — अद्य बुधवासरः। श्वः गुरुवासरः। ह्यः मङ्गलवासरः।**
> (adya kaḥ vāsaraḥ? — adya budhavāsaraḥ. śvaḥ guruvāsaraḥ. hyaḥ maṅgalavāsaraḥ.)
> "What day is it today? — Today is Wednesday. Tomorrow, Thursday. Yesterday,
> Tuesday." — आज कौन-सा वार है? — आज बुधवार। कल (आने वाला) गुरुवार। कल (बीता) मंगलवार।

> **सप्ताहे कति वासराः??** न — two machines the course has not yet delivered
> (locative, plural). The honest frame points at the week wheel and asks bare:
> **कति? — सप्त!** (kati? — sapta!) "How many? — Seven!" — कितने? — सात!

The verbless date lines are deliberate: अद्य, श्वः and ह्यः ride the same verbless
frames that carried the family and the world — the verb machinery is next chapter's
whole business.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

परीक्षा की विधि अब जानी-पहचानी है; आज की ख़ास बातें फिर तीन हैं।

पहली — **दिशा-तालिका अब पूरी है।** पिछली परीक्षा में तीन तीर थे; आज चार सम्बन्ध हैं:
कोई तीर नहीं (अष्ट और octō — एक ही पुरखे की दो सन्तानें), पूर्व की ओर (dharma संस्कृत
से अंग्रेज़ी गया), पश्चिम से (होरा यूनानी से संस्कृत में आया), और सबसे बारीक — दोनों
उधार (अंग्रेज़ी hour और संस्कृत होरा, दोनों एक ही यूनानी शब्द से, दो अलग रास्तों से)।
कार्ड छाँटते समय पहले तीर खींचिए, फिर लेबल लगाइए। और November वाला कार्ड — याद रखिए:
novem लैटिन का अपना "नौ" है; नव उसका चचेरा भाई है, पिता नहीं।

दूसरी — **गिनती की प्रस्तुति।** कार्य C में चार चक्कर हैं: मूल रूप (एक से दश तक),
गिनती-घोष (एकम् द्वे त्रीणि…), अंक-नाम की सीढ़ी (कार्ड देखिए, एक ताल में नाम बोलिए),
और असली चीज़ें गिनना — कति? — षट्! यह पहचान की नहीं, उत्पादन की परीक्षा है: जीभ को
पता होना चाहिए कि षट् में वह मुड़ती है और दश में दाँतों पर टिकती है।

तीसरी — **दिनदर्शिका।** देवनागरी अंकों वाला महीने का ग्रिड मिलेगा। पहले "आज" का चिह्न
ढूँढ़िए, फिर तीनों प्रश्न: अद्य कः वासरः? श्वः? ह्यः? — बिना क्रिया के, सधे हुए ढाँचे
में। तीन तिथियाँ ज़ोर से पढ़िए — अंक + वार — और एक कति-प्रश्न पूछकर उसका उत्तर भी
दीजिए। हिन्दी यहाँ आपकी मदद करेगी: सोमवार सोमवासरः का ही उतरा हुआ रूप है — पर लिखते
और बोलते समय संस्कृत रूप ही चाहिए, -वार नहीं।

## 8. English Explanation

The assessment architecture — five tasks, one a recording, one a grid.

| Task | Skill | Format | Pass line |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Listening | 10 items: numbers and days by ear | 8/10 |
| B | Writing | 6-word dictation + one date line | 5/6 + line intact |

The §19 quiz (10 questions) completes it. Pass = every task at partial-or-better
(rubric §22) and quiz 7+/10. Task D is this chapter's signature: the direction table
closes. Four relations now exist — no arrow (अष्ट ~ octō), arrow east (dharma →
English), arrow west (Greek → होरा), and both-borrowed (English *hour* and Sanskrit
होरा, two loans from one Greek source) — and the cards make you draw each one
correctly, including the November card the 1.x book got wrong in print. Passing gates
v2.c04 (क्रियाः — First Verbs), where sentences finally get engines.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — exam-day checks; reference values live in the lesson tables. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c03l05-listening-test.mp3`,
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c03l05-dictation-set.mp3`.

**The retroflex spine.** षट् begins and ends curled — /ʂɐʈ/, tongue-tip back both
times; अष्ट welds ष्ट into one curled gesture held through the cluster. The ladder
collapses if षट् flattens toward dental स or ट slides toward English alveolar t.

**The chant's quantities.** त्रीणि and चत्वारि carry long vowels — two full beats
each (dīrgha ≈ 2× hrasva). The chant is a rhythm exercise as much as a number drill;
keep the beat honest and the lengths take care of themselves.

**The trio onsets.** श्वः opens on /ɕʋ/ as one block, ह्यः on /ɦj/ — breathy voice
then glide. Neither takes a seam vowel: "śa-vaḥ" and "ha-yaḥ" are the flagged habits.

**The date-line breath.** अद्य सोमवासरः। — one breath-group, the final visarga a real
voiceless breath, never dropped under exam pace.

**Warm-up before recording:** the chant once slow, the षट्-अष्ट pair five times, one
date line, one bare कति exchange.

## 10. IPA

No new values. Exam spot-checks: षट् /ʂɐʈ/ and अष्ट /ɐʂʈɐ/ are retroflex throughout —
contrast दश /d̪ɐɕɐ/ and सप्त /sɐpt̪ɐ/, dental on the teeth. त्रीणि /t̪ɾiːɳi/ hides a
retroflex ṇ after the long vowel. चत्वारि /t͡ɕɐt̪ʋɑːɾi/ opens on the palatal
affricate. श्वः /ɕʋɐh/ and ह्यः /ɦjɐh/ end in visarga /h/ — voiceless; सप्ताहः
/sɐpt̪ɑːɦɐh/ holds both h-sounds of Sanskrit in one word: medial ह /ɦ/ voiced, final
ः /h/ voiceless. होरा /ɦoːɾɑː/ keeps ओ a pure long monophthong — never English "go"
[oʊ]. अद्य /ɐd̪jɐ/ welds द्य with no seam vowel.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the exam circuit, one check per task.

- **Task A (ears):** number items land on single short words; day items end in the
  compound tail -वासरः. Catch the question's shape first, then the answer.
- **Task B (pen):** every retroflex gets its own letter (ष, ट, ण) — the spelling
  follows the tongue; the date line's final ः is written because it is breathed.
- **Task C (mouth):** set the tongue before the beat — curled for षट्/अष्ट, on the
  teeth for सप्त/दश. A repair mid-word counts as a slip; a beat of silence does not.
- **Task D (head):** draw the arrow first, label second — a card argued label-first
  is how direction errors survive.
- **Task E (eyes then mouth):** scan the whole grid before speaking; find the अद्य
  marker, count columns to श्वः and ह्यः, and plan the कति exchange in advance.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — two worked model answers before you start.

**Model counting round (task C).** The ladder deals three cards: ९ · ३ · ७. Within a
beat each: नव! त्रि! सप्त! Then the reverse row — you hear *ṣaṭ* and write ६. Then
the objects: five pencils on the table. कति? — **पञ्च!** The exam-day trap is
ambition: reaching for a verb (पञ्च सन्ति?? — the verb machinery is next chapter's)
or gluing on endings. The bare answer is the taught, correct form. Simplest correct
beats ambitious wrong — that rule *is* the course.

**Model direction sort (task D).** Card: "English *hour* and Sanskrit होरा both
descend from the same Greek word." Draw the arrows: Greek hṓra → Latin hōra → Old
French → English *hour* — a borrowing chain running west; Greek hṓra → होरा —
borrowed east with Hellenistic astronomy. Verdict: **FACT**, with the reasoning line
"two borrowings from one source — cousins by borrowing, not by shared descent."
Compare card 1: "November comes from Sanskrit नव." The arrow fails: novem is Latin's
own word for nine, counting the old Roman year's ninth month; नव ~ novem are
cognates. **Reject** — and the correction is the full answer.

## 15. Grammar

"November comes from नव" from a relative or a poster — the 1.x edition of this very
course printed it. Nobody gets to feel foolish for having believed it. Land the
correction the way the course always does: the honest story is *grander* — one
ancestor's numbers surviving in two calendars, Latin counting its year and Sanskrit
counting its own, the same inheritance held twice. The fix gives learners more to be
proud of, not less.

you land it — the winning insight is that relatedness comes in kinds, and the kind is
part of the fact.

before speeding it); श्वः vs ह्यः under pressure (pair each with a gesture — tomorrow
points forward); trusting that the chant and the stems are *both* real Sanskrit (they
are — one is inflected, and the system arrives later); and, every chapter, believing
that "reject" can be the loving answer to a patriotic card — the debrief's job is
showing the honest version is the prouder one.
