## 1. Hook

Say सोमवार. You have said it your whole life — and you have been speaking compressed
Sanskrit all along: सोम, the moon, plus वार — a वासर worn smooth by centuries of daily
use. Today the full classical forms report for duty: वासरः and the seven day-names,
रविवासरः to शनिवासरः — the sun's day to Saturn's day, real Sanskrit, today's standard.
And they carry the chapter's last honesty story: the words are Sanskrit's own, but the
seven-day week itself is a traveler — it arrived with the very astronomy that brought
last lesson's होरा. An hour and a week off one ship. Today you learn the names, read a
calendar with them, and tell their story straight.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — a set of words that is Sanskrit's own, riding a system that
sailed in.

Last lesson ended on होरा — the hour that came INTO Sanskrit with Hellenistic
astronomy. Today's vocabulary is that story's traveling companion. वासरः "day" is an
attested Sanskrit word with a long dictionary pedigree, and the seven day-names are
transparent compounds built on it: a planet word in front, वासरः behind.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** वासरः "day" and the seven compound day-names रविवासरः …
> शनिवासरः are attested Sanskrit, and they are today's standard Sanskrit names for
> the weekdays. [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. वासर] [REF:apte1890 s.v. वासर]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The everyday Hindi day-names descend from the -वासर forms:
> सोमवार, मंगलवार and their row carry वार, the worn-down वासर — regular descent down
> the Indo-Aryan line, the गृह → घर story running on a calendar. [REF:masica1991]

And the week itself? That is §13's centerpiece, told with the dating honestly hedged:
the seven-day planetary week is a documented arrival, not a timeless fixture — and
the course says so with the same calm it used for होरा. One chapter promise lands
today: the day-cycle of v2.c01 (प्रभातम्, सायम् and their hours) finally gets its
calendar, and the digits of v1.c05.l05 get a grid to live on.

> all seven names into answers you can give the hour you learn them, and the wheel's

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spaced retrieval beats massed re-study: today's Station 0 is

One more working reason: अद्य, श्वः and ह्यः are deictics — their answers move with

gives you serial order (cheap, fast, stable); the grid drill forces random access
(which day is the ९?); real calendar life asks for both, and so does the exam. That
is why §9 drills the circle and §18 drills the grid, in that order.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the week, asked and answered in sentences you fully own:

> **अद्य कः वासरः? — अद्य बुधवासरः।**
> (adya kaḥ vāsaraḥ? — adya budhavāsaraḥ.) "What day is it today? — Today is
> Wednesday." — आज कौन-सा वार है? — आज बुधवार।

> **श्वः कः वासरः? — श्वः गुरुवासरः।**
> (śvaḥ kaḥ vāsaraḥ? — śvaḥ guruvāsaraḥ.) "What day is tomorrow? — Tomorrow is
> Thursday." — आने वाला कल कौन-सा वार? — कल गुरुवार।

> **ह्यः कः वासरः? — ह्यः मङ्गलवासरः।**
> (hyaḥ kaḥ vāsaraḥ? — hyaḥ maṅgalavāsaraḥ.) "What day was yesterday? — Yesterday
> was Tuesday." — बीता कल कौन-सा वार था? — बीता कल मंगलवार।

Every line is verbless — the naming frame of अहम् रामः and मम भ्राता अर्जुनः, now
telling the date. कः is the question word for a ः-word, exactly the rule of thumb of
v1.c06.l02: वासरः wears ः, so the question wears कः. The trio अद्य, श्वः, ह्यः came
from lesson 3; today they get seven answers each.

## 8. English Explanation

No new letter, no new sign — the Volume 2 luxury holds. The load is one word, seven
compounds built on it, two conjunct welds back on duty, and one honest history.

### One word, seven compounds

**वासरः** (vāsaraḥ) "day (of the week)" is the chassis. The seven names are
transparent builds: a planet word in front, वासरः behind — **रविवासरः** (ravivāsaraḥ)
Sunday, **सोमवासरः** (somavāsaraḥ) Monday, **मङ्गलवासरः** (maṅgalavāsaraḥ) Tuesday,
**बुधवासरः** (budhavāsaraḥ) Wednesday, **गुरुवासरः** (guruvāsaraḥ) Thursday,
**शुक्रवासरः** (śukravāsaraḥ) Friday, **शनिवासरः** (śanivāsaraḥ) Saturday. The tail
never changes; all the information rides up front. One display form, glossed once and
honestly: the title **वासराः** (vāsarāḥ) "days" — वासरः in its plural dress. You can
read it; the plural system is later business, and today's frames never need it.

### The fronts — two old friends, five riders

**रवि** is your sun word from v1.c04.l01, fronting Sunday. **गुरु**, your teacher
word since the course's first hour, moonlights here as the planet Jupiter. Those two
are ledger words already. The other five fronts — **सोम** the moon's old name, with
चन्द्रः keeping the everyday job while सोम works the calendar shift, **मङ्गल**
"Mars", **बुध** "Mercury", **शुक्र** "Venus", **शनि** "Saturn" — arrive *inside*
their compounds: you read them there, their planet meanings sit in §14's notes, and
they are not separate headwords today. Honest counting, as always: वासरः + seven
names = eight new words, not twelve.

### The frames

**अद्य कः वासरः?** — **अद्य बुधवासरः।** Then the trio does the rest: श्वः कः वासरः?
ह्यः कः वासरः? Verbless naming frames, pause form, कः because वासरः is a ः-word
(v1.c06.l02's rule of thumb on calendar duty). That is the entire grammar of today;
§16 runs a whole breakfast on it.

### The grid

The worksheet's month grid wears Devanagari digits and a weekday header row. The
protocol: find the अद्य marker; count forward one for श्वः, back one for ह्यः; answer
the three questions aloud. A circled date is read as a *pairing* — day and digit:
**शुक्रवासरः — ७**, or digit-first the way the exam will ask: **सप्त —
शुक्रवासरः।** For a date beyond ten, point and let the digits speak one at a time —
on १५, say सोमवासरः and tap एक, पञ्च — the teen-names are later business, and the
exam keeps its circled dates inside १–१०, the names you own. What you never do is
invent machinery: no "fifteenth", no "on Friday". Pair, point, move on.

### The two welds

मङ्गलवासरः carries **ङ्ग** — the गङ्गा stack of v1.c06.l01, back on duty: velar
nasal seated on ग. Newspaper print often writes the convenience form मंगल with the
dot; you read both identically, and this course writes the classical stack.
शुक्रवासरः carries **क्र** — the ्र stroke of v1.c05.l04 under क. Both welds are old
skills; §9 and §12 keep them honest at speed.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — no new sounds; the work is two welds, one long आ held mid-word, and full
finals. Audio: 🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c03l04-drill.mp3` (drills 1–3),

**Drill 1 — the welds.** Build both stacks from parts, slow motion: मङ् — गल —
मङ्गल — मङ्गलवासरः; शु — क्र — शुक्र — शुक्रवासरः. Finger on the nose for ङ् — it
buzzes; the nasal hums at the ग place, then releases into ग. For क्र: क् straight
into the tap, no vowel inside.

**Drill 2 — the wheel roll-call.** With the audio, fixed order, three passes (slow ·
natural · timed): रविवासरः — सोमवासरः — मङ्गलवासरः — बुधवासरः — गुरुवासरः —
शुक्रवासरः — शनिवासरः. The order is the memory palace; then one pass starting from
*today's* day, because the wheel has no edge.

**Drill 3 — the frame ladder.** The audio names a day; you answer the trio inside the
beat: अद्य गुरुवासरः। श्वः शुक्रवासरः। ह्यः बुधवासरः। Timed retrieval of chunks —

**Drill 4 — three speeds.** The §14 list, block by block, natural, target pace; log

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; these feed the quiz
distractors.

1. **The Hindi form slipping in — सोमवार for सोमवासरः.** The descendant is real and
   honored (§4, §13); the Sanskrit frame wants the full form. *Fix:* restore the
   -वासरः tail aloud, visarga and all — the chapter exam flags this exact habit.
2. **ङ्ग broken.** Either a vowel leaks ("maṅagala") or the stack goes unrecognized.
   *Fix:* §9 drill 1, §11 check 1. Reading the convenience print मंगल as the same
   sounds is fine — the गङ्गा/गंगा note of v1.c06.l01 applies unchanged; *writing*
   stays classical in this course.
3. **क्र leaked — "śukara".** *Fix:* the ्र stroke is a weld, not a syllable; §11
   check 2.
4. **The mid-word आ clipped — "vasara".** Hindi habit shortens the unstressed middle.
   *Fix:* two beats on वा at every speed; log drill 4.
5. **Ordinal ambition.** Inventing "fifteenth" grammar for dates, or a locative "on
   Friday". *Fix:* dates are digit-and-day pairings — शुक्रवासरः — ७ — and nothing
   more yet; the machinery arrives honestly later (§15).
6. **The history flip — in both directions.** "The seven-day week has always been
   Indian" fails one way; "वासरः is a Greek loan" fails the other. *Fix:* the words
   are Sanskrit's own; the *system* is the documented import, honestly dated; the
   loanword was होरा (§13). The exam's task D cards are built from this exact line.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — three stories inside today's list.

### The honest week

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** The weekday names follow the seven-planet week — a day
> each for the sun, the moon, and the five visible planets. That system entered
> Indian usage in the early centuries CE, alongside Hellenistic astronomy — the same
> current that carried होरा into Sanskrit (v2.c03.l03). The वासर day-names
> themselves are attested from later classical and medieval texts and are today's
> standard Sanskrit. Both halves are true at once: the words are classical and
> medieval Sanskrit; the seven-day system is a documented historical import. The
> same seven-planet scheme stands behind the English names too — *Sunday*, *Monday*
> and *Saturday* wear it transparently.
> [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. वासर] [REF:burrow2001] [REF:oed s.vv. Sunday, Monday, Saturday]

Hold the pairing: lesson 3 gave you a *word* that immigrated (होरा); this lesson
gives you a *system* that immigrated, wearing home-grown words. The week and the
hour arrived with the same astronomy — one ship, two souvenirs — and neither fact
diminishes the other. "Early centuries CE" is as precise as the course will honestly
be; anyone selling you an exact year is reaching, and anyone selling you a Vedic
seven-day week is wrong in the other direction.

### The planets in front

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The seven fronts are the classical planet words: रवि "sun" ·
> सोम "moon" · मङ्गल "Mars" — as a common noun also "welfare, auspiciousness" ·
> बुध "Mercury" · गुरु "Jupiter" — the same word as your "teacher" · शुक्र "Venus" ·
> शनि "Saturn". All seven senses sit in the standard dictionaries.
> [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.vv.] [REF:apte1890]

Two of the seven are already yours: रवि and गुरु from v1.c04.l01. सोम — the moon's
older name beside everyday चन्द्रः, the world set and the week shaking hands — and
the other four you meet *inside* their compounds, which is exactly how the ledger
counts them (§14).

### वार — the worn-down वासर

Hindi did to वासर what it did to गृह: centuries of daily speech wore सोमवासरः down
to सोमवार, मङ्गलवासरः to मंगलवार, and so along the row [REF:masica1991]. The
direction is the honest kind — parent to child, *inside* Indo-Aryan, true downward —
and it hands Hindi speakers half of today's lesson for free. The work left is
restoring what wore away: the full -वासरः tail, the classical ङ्ग, the breathed
ending.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — one compound shape observed, three frames at work, and the honesty
lines.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** (1) The day-names are compounds: a planet word in front,
> वासरः behind — रवि + वासरः → रविवासरः. The front member stands in its bare stem
> shape, without its sentence dress — *observed* today; the compound system is later
> business. (2) The question अद्य कः वासरः? wears कः because वासरः is a ः-word —

> locatives, no plurals taught; the title's वासराः is a display, glossed once in §8.

### The grid protocol

Find अद्य. Count forward one column for श्वः, back one for ह्यः — the wheel wraps at
शनिवासरः the way the grid wraps at the row's edge. A circled date is a pairing, said

at the cell as you say it; the finger is part of the grammar for now.

No ordinal machinery: अद्य १५ दिनम्?? is ambition — दिनम् is yours (v2.c01.l03), but
the course has given you no "fifteenth", and gluing a digit onto it does not build

## 22. Teacher Notes

अध्यापकटिप्पणी

**Open with the riddle, not the list.** Write सोमवार on the board and ask what is
inside it. Collect guesses; then split it — सोम + वार — and rewind वार to वासर on
the board, letter by letter. The whole lesson falls out of that one unwrapping: the
full forms, the planets in front, the Hindi bridge, and the honest history.

**Timing plan (45 min):** Station 0 + prompts, scores logged (7) → the सोमवार
unwrapping on the board (5) → the wheel walk: seven names placed on a drawn circle,
planet chips in front, रवि/गुरु tagged "yours already" (8) → weld drills ङ्ग and
क्र from components (5) → frame ladder in pairs, then grid work with the अद्य marker
(8) → the honest-history talk, run as a gift (5) → word list at three speeds;
dictation launch (4) → exercises, homework, THE GRID assignment (3).

**Board work:** one large circle, seven spokes, names written at the rim as they
arrive; the constant tail -वासरः written once at the hub. Keep a two-column chip
tray — "our words" and "the arrived system": अपने शब्द / आई हुई प्रणाली — every
history claim in the room gets sorted into one of the two.

**Run the history talk as a gift, not a scolding.** Someone in the room holds "the
week is anciently Indian" from a poster or a relative — treat it exactly as the
chapter treated November: the honest version is *grander*. India received the
seven-planet week and dressed it in its own classical words so well that Hindi still
says them daily. Nobody gets to feel foolish; everybody leaves with both halves.

**Differentiation:** Hindi speakers own the system already — their work is
restoration: the -वासरः tail, the classical ङ्ग against the convenience मंगल habit,
the breathed visarga. Script-side learners drill both welds from components (§9
drill 1) before any speed work. Fast finishers get the wheel-wrap challenge: trio
answers with अद्य set to शनिवासरः.

**What students find hard:** the middle of the wheel (बुध and गुरु swap under
pressure — anchor them as Mercury-midweek and teacher-Thursday); श्वः vs ह्यः off a
grid (gesture forward and back as you ask); resisting ordinal ambition on dates (the
finger does the work the grammar has not yet earned); and hearing "import" as an
insult — the debrief's job is showing it as provenance, the same honesty the course
gave होरा.
