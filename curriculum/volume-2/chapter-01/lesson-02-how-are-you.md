## 1. Hook

Volume 1 taught you to ask questions with your voice alone — the rising tune was the only
question mark Sanskrit gave you. Today the language hands you a written one, and it turns
out to be an old friend standing in a new place: अपि, the word that has walked *behind*
since last chapter ("X too"), steps to the *front* and turns a whole sentence into a polite
question. Two words later you own the exchange classical speakers themselves used to ask
after each other — अपि कुशलम्? "Is all well?" — and the answer costs nothing, because you
simply hand the question back: कुशलम्!

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिक सन्दर्भः — etiquette old enough to have epics behind it.

Today's संज्ञा names the lesson: **कुशलप्रश्नः** (kuśalapraśnaḥ) "the well-being
question" — कुशल (kuśala) "well-being" + प्रश्नः (praśnaḥ) "question". You have read the
word family since Chapter 1 without noticing: प्रश्नाः has headed every quiz in this book.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** कुशल means "well-being, welfare" — and, as an adjective, "skilful,
> in good order". The dictionaries record the greeting idiom directly from classical
> usage: कुशलम् ते? "is it well with you?" — inquiry after kuśala as the standard
> courteous question. [REF:apte1890 s.v. कुशल]

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** The kuśala-inquiry is classical etiquette, not a modern
> pleasantry in old clothes: in epic and classical narrative convention, characters who
> meet ask after each other's well-being — the kuśala-question — before any other
> business is spoken, and the dictionaries cite the formulas from exactly such passages.
> [REF:apte1890 s.v. कुशल]

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** The traditional etymology recorded in the dictionaries
> derives कुशल from कुश (kuśa) — the sharp-edged sacred grass: the kuśala person is "one
> who gathers kuśa grass" without cutting their fingers, deftness as the root image of
> being well and doing well. A story the tradition tells about the word; the meanings
> themselves are dictionary fact. [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. कुशल]

Notice which label today wears. Last volume, विद्यालयः carried the *modern* chip — real
word, modern schoolroom convention, honestly said. अपि कुशलम्? carries the *classical*
one, worn proudly: when you ask it, you are speaking etiquette the classical language
itself used. Both labels are honors — they are different honors, and this course will
always tell you which is which.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिक व्याख्या — why the answer is the question, handed back.

"How are you?" in every language is mostly not a request for information — it is a
**routine**: a fixed social exchange learned whole, run daily, and only rarely answered
with actual news. The Sanskrit exchange makes the routine's machinery unusually visible,
because the answer *echoes* the question's key word: अपि कुशलम्? — कुशलम्! Ask with a
word, hand the same word back. One content word carries the entire exchange, which makes
today's frames nearly free to learn — and every run through them is retrieval practice,
the strongest rehearsal there is [REF:roediger2006].

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** High-frequency courtesy formulas are learned fastest as unanalyzed
> chunks — frame in, frame out — with the internal grammar unpacked later, without
> relearning. Today's exchange is four such chunks and one placement rule. [REF:nation2013]

Two design notes, honestly stated. First, we drill the neuter answer कुशलम् first and
hardest because it is the answer *every* learner can always use; the gendered forms are
real and they wait for the gender system, rather than being half-taught now. Second, one
delivery fact for §9: the exchange has a fixed melody — the question rises, the echo
settles. A question with कथम् in it does not need the rise; the word itself asks.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतव्याख्या — today's संज्ञा is **कुशलप्रश्नः** (kuśalapraśnaḥ) "the well-being
question" — कुशल (kuśala) "well-being" + प्रश्नः (praśnaḥ) "question". And the unlocked
language, at once:

अपि कुशलम्?
(api kuśalam?) "(Is) all well?" — सब कुशल तो है?

कुशलम्! सर्वम् कुशलम्!
(kuśalam! sarvam kuśalam!) "Well! All is well!" — कुशल है! सब कुशल है!

भवान् कुशली?
(bhavān kuśalī?) "Are you well, sir?" — आप कुशल हैं?

अहम् कुशली।
(aham kuśalī.) "I am well." — मैं कुशल हूँ। (पुरुष वक्ता)

One honest note: कुशली (kuśalī) is said by and of a man; the classical form for a female
speaker is कुशलिनी (kuśalinī) — shown today, taught with the gender system later in this
volume, exactly as छात्रा was mentioned and deferred in Chapter 6. The neuter कुशलम् is
every speaker's safe and fully classical answer. Nothing else about these frames is
analyzed today — they are chunks, glossed whole, like every frame this course has handed
you.

आए हैं; संस्कृत का अपि कुशलम्? ठीक वही पूछता है, और कुशल शब्द दोनों भाषाओं में एक ही है।

अब दो चेतावनियाँ। पहली: हिन्दी की आदत बीच का अ खा जाती है — कुशली को "कुश्ली" मत बोलिए;
तीन पूरे अक्षर हैं — कु-श-ली — और अन्तिम ई लम्बी। कुशलम् में भी बीच के दोनों अ पूरे बोलिए।

## 8. English Explanation

One exchange, one personal form, one new question word, and one old word with a new job.

### The exchange — classical, and proud of it

**अपि कुशलम्?** (api kuśalam?) "(Is) all well?" — the classical wellness question. Answer
by handing the word back:

| You are asked | You answer |
|---|---|
| अपि कुशलम्? | कुशलम्! "well!" |
| अपि कुशलम्? | सर्वम् कुशलम्! "all is well!" |
| अपि सर्वम् कुशलम्? | आम्! सर्वम् कुशलम्! |

The echo is the whole trick: the question's key word *is* the answer. सर्वम् (sarvam)
"all, everything" is an upgrade, not a stranger — you met the stem सर्व in Chapter 5's
repha drills and recited सर्वे भवन्तु सुखिनः at the Volume Exam; today its neuter form
goes into everyday service.

### The personal form — and the safe answer

To ask after the *person* rather than the situation: **त्वम् कुशली?** (tvam kuśalī?)
"are you well?" — to a boy or man you know; **भवान् कुशली?** (bhavān kuśalī?) "are you
well, sir?" — respectfully, to a man. A male speaker answers **अहम् कुशली** (aham
kuśalī) "I am well."

Now the honest line, kindly meant: कुशली is a *gendered* form — it fits a male speaker
or addressee. The classical form for a female speaker is **कुशलिनी** (kuśalinī) —
display only today, exactly as छात्रा was mentioned and deferred in Chapter 6; the
respectful भवती (bhavatī) for addressing a woman waits in the same room. The gender
system is this volume's later business, and we will not half-teach it now. Until then,
every speaker — boy, girl, man, woman — owns the perfect, fully classical answer:
**कुशलम्!** The neuter answers for the *situation*, so it answers for everyone. Drill it
first; reach for it whenever in doubt.

### कथम् — the kit completes

**कथम्** (katham) "how?" joins the question words you have collected since Chapter 2 —
कः का किम् कुतः (kaḥ kā kim kutaḥ) — and completes the kit. Its starter frame is
**कथम् अस्ति?** (katham asti?) "how is (it)?", asked of things you can already name:

कथम् अस्ति विद्यालयः?
(katham asti vidyālayaḥ?) "How is the school?" — विद्यालय कैसा है?

Answer with what you learned five minutes ago: सर्वम् कुशलम्! Keep कथम् for things and
situations today; asking *persons* how they are is the कुशल-exchange's job, and the two
frames divide the work cleanly.

### अपि's second job

Chapter 6 taught अपि's first job: X अपि = "X too", always behind its word. Today the
second, and the position tells you which is which — **standing first in a sentence, अपि
turns the sentence into a polite yes/no question**: अपि कुशलम्? "(is) all well?" This is
classical usage, and it is the first *written* question mark this course has given you.
Hindi speakers: front-अपि is your "क्या…?", behind-अपि is your "भी" — you have owned both
moves all along. The rising tune stays on the अपि-question (it is still a yes/no
question); the कथम्-question keeps the level tune, because the question word asks.

### The ledger

Volume 1 closed at 237 readable words; lesson 1's greeting kit brought the ledger to
about 245. Today's four — कथम्, कुशलम्, कुशली, सर्वम् — push it to roughly 249: the
250 door is next lesson's to open. Everything else in this lesson, both scenes and the
story included, is built from words you already own.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — no new sounds; the work is tune and timing. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c01l02-dialogue.mp3` (both scenes at three speeds, plus
a roleplay-gap track), 🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c01l02-word-list.mp3` (four
words + dictation track).

**Drill 1 — the two question tunes, updated.** The अपि-question is a yes/no question, so
it rises — अपि कुशलम्? — even though अपि marks it on paper; ink and voice both ask.
The कथम्-question stays level — कथम् अस्ति विद्यालयः? — the word does the asking, as
किम् and कः always have. Alternate the pair three times until the switch is automatic.

**Drill 2 — the exchange rhythm.** Rise, one beat of silence, settle: अपि कुशलम्? (rise)
— beat — कुशलम्! (fall). The falling echo is what makes the answer sound *answered*.
Three rounds solo, both roles, pause form between turns.

**Drill 3 — shadow both roles.** Play Scene 1; speak the asker's lines in the gaps.
Replay; speak the answerer's. The roleplay-gap track leaves one side silent for you.

**Drill 4 — word disciplines.** कथम्: the थ is a dental stop with a real puff — tongue
on the teeth, then breath; never the English "th" of *thin*, which lets the air leak
through. कुशली: three full beats, कु-श-ली, the middle अ intact and the final ई long.
सर्वम्: Chapter 5's repha timing — the r sounds *before* the व.

/t̪ʋɐm kuɕɐliː/ · **भवान् कुशली** /bʱɐʋɑːn̪ kuɕɐliː/ · **कथम् अस्ति विद्यालयः**

**Word transcriptions:** कथम् /kɐt̪ʰɐm/ · कुशलम् /kuɕɐlɐm/ · कुशली /kuɕɐliː/ ·
सर्वम् /sɐɾʋɐm/ · अपि /ɐpi/ · display: कुशलिनी /kuɕɐlin̪iː/.

**Narrow notes:** the थ of कथम् is [t̪ʰ] — full dental closure released with aspiration,

two beats of vowel, jaw still. सर्वम् is [sɐɾ.ʋɐm]: the tap [ɾ] lands before the
labiodental [ʋ], though the eye reads the hook above the व. Intonation (the rise on the
अपि-question) is a property of the sentence, not of any segment — it lives in §9, not in
these transcriptions.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थानम् — nothing new to place; three checks to run.

1. **The थ station.** Say कथम् slowly with a tissue before your lips: tongue tip touches
   the teeth, the air stops fully, then the puff jumps the tissue. If the tissue stirs
   *continuously*, you are leaking an English "th" — close first, then release.
2. **The श in कुशलम्.** Blade of the tongue toward the hard palate, lips relaxed — the
   sound of श, not स. Say कुशलम् and feel the tongue's station; if it slides to the
   teeth, you have said the non-word "kusalam".
3. **The held ई of कुशली.** Watch your mouth through the final beat in a mirror: lips
   and jaw frozen for two full counts. If they drift, the vowel shortened — and the
   word's shape with it.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्य-भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix. These feed the quiz distractors.

1. **English "th" in कथम्.** The fricative of *thin* for /t̪ʰ/. *Fix:* full dental
   closure, then the puff — §11 check 1. Hindi speakers already own this stop in हाथ.
2. **कुशली clipped to "kuślī".** The Hindi habit deletes the middle अ. *Fix:* three
   beats, कु-श-ली, tapped on the desk; the middle beat is a whole akṣara.
3. **अपि's jobs confused.** Reading अपि कुशलम्? as "well-being too", or trying to ask a
   question with अपि parked behind a word. *Fix:* position decides — front of the
   sentence = question; behind a word = "too". Run §18 exercise 2 until sorting is
   instant.
4. **A gendered answer from the wrong speaker.** A girl answering अहम् कुशली (or being
   made to). *Fix:* कुशलम्! is everyone's answer — fully classical, never a compromise;
   कुशलिनी is display until the gender system arrives later this volume.
5. **The flat अपि-question.** अपि कुशलम् said level sounds like a broken statement.
   *Fix:* it is a yes/no question — the rise stays, exactly as in त्वम् अपि छात्रः?
   (§9 drill 1).
6. **स for श in कुशलम्.** "kusalam" — the tongue slid forward to the स station. *Fix:*
   §11 check 2; the श of कुशल is the श of शब्द, blade high, lips soft.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — three items with something inside them.

### अपि कुशलम् — the exchange, worked

**अपि कुशलम्?** (api kuśalam?) "(Is) all well?" — the question; and its three graded
answers, all built on one word:

- कुशलम्! (kuśalam!) "Well!" — the minimal echo; every speaker's safe answer.
- सर्वम् कुशलम्! (sarvam kuśalam!) "All is well!" — the generous echo.
- आम्! सर्वम् कुशलम्! (ām! sarvam kuśalam!) "Yes! All is well!" — the warm one.

One exchange, zero grammar to analyze, and a classical label on the whole set — this is
the cheapest fluent Sanskrit you will ever buy.

### कथम् — the k that asks

**कथम्** (katham) "how?" — कथम् · /kɐt̪ʰɐm/

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Every Sanskrit question word you own begins with k — कः, का,
> किम्, कुतः, कथम् — because all are built on one ancient interrogative stem. English
> carries the same stem as the wh- of its own question words (*who, what, how*):
> Sanskrit k-questions and English wh-questions are cognates, parallel descendants of
> the Proto-Indo-European interrogative stem, not borrowings in either direction.
> [REF:mallory2006] [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. कथम्]

Its starter frame stays small on purpose: कथम् अस्ति? "how is (it)?" — कथम् अस्ति
विद्यालयः? कथम् अस्ति जलम्? Point the kit at things; the कुशल-exchange handles persons.

### सर्वम् — from the śloka to the schoolyard

**सर्वम्** (sarvam) "all, everything" — सर्व · /sɐɾʋɐm/

You have carried this stem for two chapters: सर्व in the repha drills of v1.c05.l04,
सर्वे भवन्तु सुखिनः recited — with sources and syllable counts — at the Volume Exam.
Today the neuter form clocks in for ordinary work: सर्वम् कुशलम्! The course's promise
in action — nothing you learned stays ornamental; the exam's recitation word is this
week's small talk.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — in a conversation lesson the grammar is small and sharp: one placement rule,
one completed kit, one honest boundary. No new script — that campaign closed with
Volume 1.

### अपि's two jobs

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** अपि placed after a word means "also, even" — अहम् अपि "I too".
> Standing at the head of a sentence, अपि marks a polite yes/no question — अपि कुशलम्?
> "(is) all well?" Both uses are classical, recorded side by side in the dictionaries.
> [REF:apte1890 s.v. अपि]

Position is the entire rule: **behind a word — "too"; first in the sentence — a
question.** The two jobs never collide, because they cannot occupy the same slot. Test
sentence: त्वम् अपि छात्रः? — that अपि stands *behind* त्वम्, so it means "too"; the
question there is carried by the rise alone, exactly as taught in Chapter 6.

### The question kit, complete

| Question | Asks | Tune | An answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| कः? | who? (of a ः-word) | level | रामः। छात्रः। |
| का? | who? (of an -आ word) | level | सीता। बालिका। |
| किम्? | what? | level | जलम्। मम नाम माया। |
| कुतः? | from where? | level | इतः! |
| कथम्? | how? | level | कुशलम्! सर्वम् कुशलम्! |

Two kinds of question stand *outside* the kit and rise instead: the bare question
(त्वम् कुशली?) and the अपि-question (अपि कुशलम्?). Five words, one melody rule — that
is the whole apparatus of asking, as of today.

### The gender boundary, honestly drawn

कुशली is the masculine form of its word; कुशलिनी the feminine; कुशलम् the neuter of the
plain adjective — and *why* those shapes are what they are is the gender system's story,
told properly later in this volume. Today's working rules are chunks: use कुशली of a
boy or man; if you are a male speaker, answer with it; every speaker answers safely with
कुशलम्. This mirrors Chapter 6's छात्रा handling exactly: honest mention, no
half-teaching, full seat later.

### A sandhi seam, previewed

In joined classical writing you will meet **सर्वं कुशलम्** — the म् of सर्वम् dressing
as anusvāra before the following क. That machinery is Volume 3's; this course reads and
writes pause form — सर्वम् कुशलम् — until sandhi is taught, not smuggled.

## 16. Conversation

सम्भाषणम् — the heart of the lesson, in two scenes; every word is self-read. 🔊 audio:
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c01l02-dialogue.mp3` (slow pause-form, natural speed,
and a roleplay-gap track with one side silent).

### Scene 1 — two students meet

Before class, on the school path.

— सीता: भोः! नमस्ते! त्वम् कुशली?
(bhoḥ! namaste! tvam kuśalī?) "Hey! Greetings! Are you well?" — अरे! नमस्ते! तुम कुशल
तो हो?

— रामः: आम्! अहम् कुशली! अपि कुशलम्?
(ām! aham kuśalī! api kuśalam?) "Yes! I am well! Is all well (with you)?" — हाँ! मैं
कुशल हूँ! सब कुशल तो है?

— सीता: कुशलम्! सर्वम् कुशलम्!
(kuśalam! sarvam kuśalam!) "Well! All is well!" — कुशल है! सब कुशल है!

— बालकः *(running up)*: नमस्ते! अहम् अपि कुशली!
(namaste! aham api kuśalī!) "Greetings! I too am well!" — नमस्ते! मैं भी कुशल हूँ!

Watch the pattern do the teaching. सीता asks रामः the personal form — he is a boy, so
कुशली fits him. रामः asks her back the *neuter* way — the form that is safe for every
addressee — and she answers with the neuter echo. And the बालकः, uninvited as ever,
reprises Chapter 6's अपि: parked behind अहम्, it means "too" — nobody asked, which is
the joke.

### Scene 2 — the आचार्यः asks the class

The room settles; the teacher looks up.

— आचार्यः: नमस्ते! भोः! अपि सर्वम् कुशलम्?
(namaste! bhoḥ! api sarvam kuśalam?) "Greetings! Ho, everyone! Is all well?" — नमस्ते!
अरे भई! क्या सब कुशल है?

— *(the whole class, together)*: आम्! सर्वम् कुशलम्!
(ām! sarvam kuśalam!) "Yes! All is well!" — हाँ! सब कुशल है!

— आचार्यः *(to Māyā, the new girl)*: अपि कुशलम्?
(api kuśalam?) "(And you —) is all well?" — सब कुशल तो है?

— माया: आम्! कुशलम्!
(ām! kuśalam!) "Yes! (All is) well!" — हाँ! कुशल है!

— आचार्यः *(smiling)*: सर्वम् कुशलम्!
(sarvam kuśalam!) "All is well!" — सब कुशल है!

Delivery notes: the अपि-questions rise; the echoes settle — run the melody before the
meaning. Pause form between turns, one full beat. And notice माया: a female speaker,
answering perfectly with the neuter कुशलम् — that is not a workaround, that is the
teaching. The English stage directions are scaffolding, not Sanskrit; swap roles and run
both scenes until each side is yours.

## 17. Story

कथा — कुशलम् · All Is Well. Six lines; each line: Devanagari, IAST, English, Hindi.

1. माया अत्र अस्ति।
   (māyā atra asti.) "Māyā is here." — माया यहाँ है।
2. सीता वदति — नमस्ते! अपि कुशलम्?
   (sītā vadati — namaste! api kuśalam?) "Sītā says: Greetings! Is all well?" — सीता
   कहती है — नमस्ते! सब कुशल तो है?
3. माया वदति — कुशलम्! सर्वम् कुशलम्!
   (māyā vadati — kuśalam! sarvam kuśalam!) "Māyā says: Well! All is well!" — माया कहती
   है — कुशल है! सब कुशल है!
4. रामः वदति — अहम् अपि कुशली!
   (rāmaḥ vadati — aham api kuśalī!) "Rāma says: I too am well!" — राम कहता है — मैं भी
   कुशल हूँ!
5. माया वदति — कथम् अस्ति विद्यालयः?
   (māyā vadati — katham asti vidyālayaḥ?) "Māyā says: How is the school?" — माया कहती
   है — विद्यालय कैसा है?
6. सीता हसति — विद्यालयः अपि कुशली! सर्वम् कुशलम्!
   (sītā hasati — vidyālayaḥ api kuśalī! sarvam kuśalam!) "Sītā laughs: The school is
   well too! All is well!" — सीता हँसती है — विद्यालय भी कुशल है! सब कुशल है!

Line 5 is कथम् doing its plain work on a thing — the new girl asking about her new
school. Line 6 is grammar wearing a grin: विद्यालयः is a ः-word, masculine, so the
personal कुशली fits it perfectly — and the joke is a building being asked after like a
friend. माया, who could not read one letter on her first page in this course, now runs
the whole exchange in her second week of Volume 2. The cast keeps growing up with you.

## 18. Exercises

अभ्यासाः — six exercises; 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6 are production tasks. Do them in order.

**अभ्यासः 1 · कुशलमाला (the exchange chain — production).** Aloud, both roles, three
rounds: अपि कुशलम्? — कुशलम्! · अपि कुशलम्? — सर्वम् कुशलम्! · अपि सर्वम् कुशलम्? —
आम्! सर्वम् कुशलम्! Then the personal round: त्वम् कुशली? — आम्! अहम् कुशली! (male
speakers answer as themselves; everyone else is acting — see exercise 3). Finish with
the everyone-round: each answer in the room is कुशलम्! Rise on every question, settle
on every echo.

**अभ्यासः 2 · अपि-विवेकः (the two-jobs sort — written).** For each sentence, write J1
("too") or J2 (question) and translate: (a) अपि कुशलम्? (b) अहम् अपि कुशली। (c) त्वम्
अपि छात्रः? (d) अपि सर्वम् कुशलम्? (e) माया अपि अत्र अस्ति। Self-check with §15's
position rule — and mind the trap in (c).

**अभ्यासः 3 · अभिनयः (roleplay — production).** Print the worksheet ✍
(`planned:assets/worksheets/volume-2/v2c01l02-roleplay.pdf`). With a partner, perform
Scene 1 and Scene 2 from the role cards — each partner sees only their own lines. Swap
and repeat. The कुशली lines belong to male characters; if you play रामः, you say them
whoever you are — that is acting, and the card says so. Solo mode: the roleplay-gap
track 🔊 leaves one side silent for you.

**अभ्यासः 4 · कुशलवार्ता (record yourself — production).** Record the full exchange in
one take, both roles: नमस्ते! अपि कुशलम्? — कुशलम्! सर्वम् कुशलम्! Play it back against
§9: did the question rise? did the echo settle? is the श of कुशलम् a श? Keep the
recording — lesson 5's role-card exam asks for this exchange again, faster and inside a
longer ladder.

**अभ्यासः 5 · कथम्-क्रीडा (the how-game — production).** Point at three things you can
name — the worksheet's picture cards (घटः, जलम्, विद्यालयः) or the real room — and ask
aloud: कथम् अस्ति घटः? कथम् अस्ति जलम्? कथम् अस्ति विद्यालयः? Answer every time from
today's kit: सर्वम् कुशलम्! Keep the tune level on the question — कथम् does the asking.

**अभ्यासः 6 · श्रुतलेखनम् (dictation — production).** Play the dictation track 🔊 (in
the word-list audio): six lines from §16 in random order, each read twice with a writing
pause. Write them in Devanagari, question marks and daṇḍas included. Self-check against
§16: every म् and ः in place? the र्व of सर्वम् hooked correctly? 5/6 is a pass; re-take
tomorrow either way (§20).

speakers cover before pair work exposes them; go around with individual अपि कुशलम्?
questions only after two full chorus rounds. Teacher plays आचार्यः; hand the role to a
student on the second pass.

**Board work for the two jobs.** Write अहम् अपि कुशली and अपि कुशलम्? one above the
other; circle अपि in both; draw the arrow back to अहम् in the first and the question
arc over the second. Position, not meaning, is the discriminator — students who verbalize
the rule ("front asks, behind adds") sort fastest.

**Differentiation:** Hindi speakers own कुशल, थ, and both of अपि's jobs through क्या/भी
— move them fast to melody and the कु-श-ली beat discipline. English-habit learners rise
on yes/no questions natively — their work is the dental थ (tissue test) and resisting
"also" word-order habits in the sort. Watch everyone on the level tune of कथम्-questions;
English habit wants to rise there too.
