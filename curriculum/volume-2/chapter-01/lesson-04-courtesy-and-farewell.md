## 1. Hook

Some words in this lesson are as old as the Veda. One is younger than the radio. By the
end of today you will not be able to tell them apart by *feel* — they all run smoothly in
a Sanskrit sentence — and you will not need to, because you will know which is which by
*fact*. Today the courtesy kit completes: thank you, please, forgive me, no, sir,
farewell, see you again. Every word taught proudly. Every word labeled truthfully. That
double act is this volume's signature move, and this is its hardest, best lesson.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिक सन्दर्भः — how a language says thank you.

Here is an honest surprise: classical Sanskrit literature, for all its politeness, does
not run on a single all-purpose "thank you". Gratitude was spoken through its occasions —
blessings returned, favor acknowledged, the giver praised. One classical formula survives
in daily use among paṇḍits to this day: अनुगृहीतः अस्मि "I am obliged, favored" — you
will meet it below as a display. The everyday one-word thanks that modern spoken Sanskrit
uses — धन्यवादः — is built from fully classical parts and is attested as a word, but its
career as the standing reply to every kindness is a **modern convention** of the living
language.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** धन्यवादः is an attested compound — धन्यः "blessed, fortunate" +
> वादः "speech": "an utterance of 'well done', an expression of thanks or
> congratulation". Its use as the everyday all-purpose "thank you" is modern
> spoken-Sanskrit convention. Both statements are true; the course teaches the word
> *and* its label. [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. dhanyavāda]

That is not a flaw in the word. Living languages grow conveniences — Hindi's धन्यवाद and
शुक्रिया, English's own "goodbye" (once "God be with ye"). The label is the respect.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिक व्याख्या — why courtesy words are the best chunk-learning there is.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** High-frequency social formulas — thanks, apologies, farewells —
> are the classic case for chunk learning: fixed forms, instantly usable, produced
> whole under time pressure precisely when grammar-building would be slowest.
> [REF:nation2013]

Courtesy runs on reflex: the moment someone hands you a book is not the moment to
conjugate. So today's nine words are drilled as reflexes — stimulus and reply
(§9, §18) — and every run through the scenes is retrieval practice, the strongest
rehearsal there is [REF:roediger2006]. One structural note rides along: today you also
meet न, the plain negative — not a chunk but a genuine little rule (it stands before
what it negates), and the smallest one this volume will teach for a while.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतव्याख्या — the lesson's idea in the simplest possible Sanskrit.

धन्यवादः, श्रीमान्!
(dhanyavādaḥ, śrīmān!) "Thank you, sir!" — धन्यवाद, श्रीमान्!

क्षमस्व! — भद्रम्, भद्रम्!
(kṣamasva! — bhadram, bhadram!) "Forgive me! — It's fine, all is well!" — क्षमा करें! —
कोई बात नहीं!

स्वस्ति! पुनः मिलामः!
(svasti! punaḥ milāmaḥ!) "Farewell — we'll meet again!" — अच्छा, फिर मिलेंगे!

Three exchanges, three registers of time: a modern convention, a classical imperative,
and a farewell pair with one foot in the Veda and one in the present. मिलामः is glossed
whole — "we meet" — like every verb so far; its machinery is Volume 2's later business.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी व्याख्या

आज का पाठ आपकी हिन्दी के सबसे नज़दीक बैठता है — और ठीक इसीलिए सबसे सावधानी माँगता है।

पहली बात: धन्यवाद और कृपया आपकी रोज़ की हिन्दी हैं — और दोनों संस्कृत में भी ठीक वैसे ही
चलते हैं: धन्यवादः, कृपया। पर आज की असली सीख उच्चारण नहीं, **लेबल** है। धन्यवादः शब्द
पुराना है (धन्यः + वादः — "धन्य हो" कहना), पर हर बात पर "थैंक यू" की तरह धन्यवादः कहना
आधुनिक बोलचाल की रीति है — शास्त्रीय ग्रन्थ ऐसा नहीं करते थे। कृपया भी यही कहानी: कृपा
शब्द शास्त्रीय, "प्लीज़" वाला कृपया आधुनिक व्यवहार। यह जानना शब्दों का अपमान नहीं,
सम्मान है — जैसा है, वैसा कहना।

दूसरी बात: क्षमस्व। हिन्दी "क्षमा करें/करो" दो शब्दों से बनाती है; संस्कृत का क्षमस्व एक
ही शब्द में पूरा अनुरोध है — और यह रूप शास्त्रीय है, पश्य की तरह पूरा-का-पूरा सीखिए,
तोड़िए मत। उत्तर में भद्रम् — "कोई बात नहीं, सब भला है।"

तीसरी बात: न। हिन्दी का "नहीं" प्रायः वाक्य के अन्त की ओर झुकता है — "ठीक नहीं है"।
संस्कृत का न जिसे नकारता है, उसके **आगे** खड़ा होता है: न कुशलम् — "ठीक नहीं"; न अस्ति —
"नहीं है"। और लिखावट की झलक: बोलचाल में न + अस्ति जुड़कर नास्ति बन जाता है — वही
"नास्तिक" वाला नास्ति! जुड़ने का यह नियम तीसरे खण्ड का विषय है; आज विराम-रूप में न अस्ति
पढ़िए, और नास्ति को देखकर पहचानिए भर।

और विदाई की जोड़ी याद रखिए: स्वस्ति वेदों जितना पुराना आशीर्वाद है; पुनः मिलामः ("फिर
मिलेंगे") आज की बोलचाल का प्यारा रिवाज़। दोनों साथ चलते हैं — यही इस अध्याय की तस्वीर है।

## 8. English Explanation

Nine words, four moves — thank, apologize, negate, take leave — and the label on every
one of them.

### Thanks — the modern convention, worn honestly

**धन्यवादः** (dhanyavādaḥ) "thank you" — the compound §4 unpacked: धन्यः "blessed" +
वादः "a saying". Use it exactly as you use Hindi धन्यवाद; know, as its label, that the
everyday-thanks career is modern spoken usage. Reply with **भद्रम्** — "all is well" —
lesson 1's courtesy word doing its second job. And the classical display, recognition
only: **अनुगृहीतः अस्मि** "I am obliged" — in connected writing अनुगृहीतोऽस्मि, wearing
the avagraha you met in Chapter 5; the join is sandhi, Volume 3's subject.

**कृपया** (kṛpayā) "please" — literally "with compassion", from **कृपा** (kṛpā)
"compassion", a fully classical noun. The please-particle use is a modern convention
shared with Hindi. One honest line for the curious: classical politeness ran through
address and verb-forms rather than a fixed "please" — you will meet that machinery in
its season.

### Apology — the classical chunk

**क्षमस्व** (kṣamasva) "forgive (me)" — a classical imperative of √क्षम् (the root
behind Chapter 5's क्षमा "patience, forgiveness"), taught **whole**, the पश्य precedent:
no conjugation talk, just the word, entire, at the moment of need. [REF:apte1890 s.v.
क्षम्] Reply: भद्रम्! or the double भद्रम्, भद्रम्! — "it's fine, it's fine."

### No — the smallest rule in the volume

**न** (na) "not" — the plain classical negative, and it obeys one placement rule:
**न stands before what it negates.** न अस्ति "(it) is not"; न कुशलम् "not well";
न, श्रीमान् "no, sir". In connected speech and writing न + अस्ति fuse into नास्ति —
you have seen that word inside Hindi नास्तिक "unbeliever, the one who says नास्ति".
Today read the pause form न अस्ति; recognize नास्ति as its joined preview (Volume 3).

### Sir — the classical honorific

**श्रीमान्** (śrīmān) "sir; possessed of śrī" — the classical honorific built on श्री
(v1.c05.l03), still the standard respectful address (and modern Hindi's श्रीमान् too).
Use it with भवान्-level respect: धन्यवादः, श्रीमान्! It ends in the bare न् — Law 5 on
duty since Chapter 5.

### Farewell — one foot in the Veda, one in the present

**स्वस्ति** (svasti — lesson 1) is the classical goodbye-blessing. Beside it today:
**पुनः** (punaḥ) "again" — classical adverb — and **मिलामः** (milāmaḥ) "we meet",
glossed whole. The chunk **पुनः मिलामः** "we'll meet again — see you!" is a **modern
spoken formula**, warmly standard in today's Sanskrit classrooms; its joined spelling
पुनर्मिलामः shows the visarga changing shape at the seam — noticed, previewed, deferred
(Volume 3). The full farewell round: स्वस्ति! पुनः मिलामः! — a Vedic blessing and a
living promise, side by side. And **धन्यः** (dhanyaḥ) "blessed, fortunate" — the
classical adjective inside धन्यवादः — joins the ledger in its own right: धन्यः बालकः "a
fortunate boy".

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — reflex drills: courtesy must come out faster than thought. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c01l04-dialogue.mp3` (both scenes + the reflex-pair
track), 🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c01l04-word-list.mp3` (nine words +
dictation track).

**Drill 1 — the reflex pairs.** The audio gives a stimulus, you give the reply before
the beat runs out: someone hands you a book → धन्यवादः! · someone thanks you → भद्रम्! ·
you bump a desk → क्षमस्व! · someone apologizes → भद्रम्, भद्रम्! · someone leaves →
स्वस्ति! पुनः मिलामः! Three rounds, faster each time.

**Drill 2 — the ध discipline.** धन्यवादः opens on breathy ध /d̪ʱ/ and its second
syllable welds न्य — dha-nya-vā-daḥ, four beats, the final visarga a clean breath.
Never "dhanyavād": the ः is part of the word.

**Drill 3 — the न placement chant.** Call and response: अस्ति? — न अस्ति! · कुशलम्? —
न कुशलम्! · छात्रः? — न, अहम् आचार्यः! The न lands *first*, every time.

**Drill 4 — the farewell round.** With the audio, pause form: स्वस्ति! — पुनः मिलामः! —
शुभरात्रिः! Then once at natural pace. The र्मि of the joined पुनर्मिलामः appears on
the recording once, labeled, so your ear files the preview.

## 10. IPA

No new values. **Word transcriptions:** धन्यवादः /d̪ʱɐn̪jɐʋɑːd̪ɐh/ · कृपया /kr̩pɐjɑː/ ·
कृपा /kr̩pɑː/ · क्षमस्व /kʂɐmɐsʋɐ/ · न /n̪ɐ/ · श्रीमान् /ɕɾiːmɑːn̪/ · पुनः /pun̪ɐh/ ·
मिलामः /milɑːmɐh/ · धन्यः /d̪ʱɐn̪jɐh/.

**Narrow notes:** धन्यवादः carries two traps in one word — the breathy onset [d̪ʱ] and
the न्य weld [n̪j]; say dha-nya slowly before speeding. क्षमस्व ends on the स्व cluster
[sʋ] — one onset, no vowel inside, exactly as in स्वस्ति and स्वागतम्. कृपया begins on
the syllabic vowel [kr̩] — the ऋ of संस्कृतम्, not "kri" or "kru". श्रीमान् ends on the
dental nasal [n̪] held closed — no echo vowel. And पुनः ends on the visarga [h] — in
pause form it stays a breath; the r it becomes in पुनर्मिलामः is the preview, not
today's reading.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थानम् — three checks, mirror at hand.

1. **The breathy start.** Hand before the lips on धन्यवादः and धन्यः: a warm puff rides
   the very first consonant. No puff = द, a different word's beginning.
2. **The क्ष seam.** क्षमस्व opens on the Chapter 5 ligature क्ष — back closure released
   into the curled hiss, one beat, then मस्व with its own स्व seam. Jaw still through
   both clusters.
3. **The final breaths.** धन्यवादः, पुनः, धन्यः — each ends in a visarga: vowel, then a
   short voiceless breath, throat open, no new vowel after. The word is over when the
   breath is.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्य-भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix. These feed the quiz distractors.

1. **"धन्यवाद" without the visarga.** The Hindi form imported whole. *Fix:* the Sanskrit
   word wears ः — धन्यवादः, four beats and a breath; drill 2 until it is reflex.
2. **Presenting धन्यवादः as ancient idiom.** The opposite of the course's label. *Fix:*
   both truths in one breath — attested word, modern everyday-thanks convention (§4).
   The exam's register cards test exactly this.
3. **क्षमस्व dismantled.** "क्षम" + something, or क्षमा used where the request is meant.
   *Fix:* the chunk is one word; क्षमा is the noun "forgiveness", क्षमस्व the plea
   "forgive me" — related, not interchangeable (§8).
4. **न after its word.** "कुशलम् न" on the Hindi pattern. *Fix:* the chant (§9 drill 3)
   — न stands before what it negates: न कुशलम्.
5. **कृपया sprinkled everywhere.** English "please"-habit transplanted. *Fix:* one
   honest line — Sanskrit courtesy lives mostly in address (श्रीमान्, भवान्) and tone;
   कृपया is available, labeled, and best used sparingly.
6. **The farewell halves swapped in the label sort.** स्वस्ति tagged modern, पुनः
   मिलामः tagged Vedic. *Fix:* §15's table, recited — the blessing is ancient, the
   see-you is living convention; the pair's whole charm is the contrast.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — three words with something inside them.

### धन्यवादः — a thank-you built from "blessed"

**धन्यवादः** (dhanyavādaḥ) "thank you" — धन्यवाद · /d̪ʱɐn̪jɐʋɑːd̪ɐh/

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** धन्यः "blessed, fortunate" derives from धन "wealth" — the
> fortunate one as the one who has been given much. A धन्यवादः is literally "a saying
> of 'blessed!'" — praise of the giver standing in for thanks.
> [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. धन्य]

So the modern convention has a beautiful classical skeleton: when you say धन्यवादः, you
are — by etymology — calling your benefactor blessed.

### नास्ति — the preview hiding in plain Hindi

**न अस्ति** (na asti) "is not" — नहीं है · /n̪ɐ ɐst̪i/

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** In connected writing न + अस्ति regularly fuse to नास्ति — the
> form inside Hindi नास्तिक "one who says 'it is not', an unbeliever" and its partner
> आस्तिक "one who says 'it is'". The fusion is vowel sandhi, Volume 3's subject; the
> pause form न अस्ति is today's reading. [REF:apte1890 s.v. नास्तिक]

### स्वस्ति — the farewell with a Vedic passport

**स्वस्ति** (svasti) "well-being; farewell" — कल्याण हो · /sʋɐst̪i/

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** स्वस्ति is transparent Sanskrit: सु "well" + अस्ति "it is" —
> "it is well" — attested from the Ṛgveda onward as blessing and salutation. The
> everyday svastika sign takes its name from this word ("mark of well-being") — a fact
> of etymology; the sign's later history in Europe is a separate, modern story.
> [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. svasti]

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — one small rule, one keystone table.

### The negation rule

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** न negates what follows it: न अस्ति "(it) is not", न कुशलम् "not
> well", न बालकः "not a boy". In pause form the two words stand apart; connected
> language fuses न + अस्ति into नास्ति (vowel sandhi — Volume 3). This is the volume's
> first syntax rule proper, and it is one line long. [REF:apte1890 s.v. न]

### The keystone: the chapter's label table

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Classical idiom (attested use in classical/Vedic language):
> नमस्ते and नमस्कारः (formation and greeting-use both old) · स्वागतम् · स्वस्ति ·
> भद्रम् ते · क्षमस्व · the कुशल-exchange · भोः · श्रीमान् · पुनः (the adverb).
> **Modern spoken convention (word or use):** धन्यवादः as everyday thanks · कृपया as
> "please" · सुप्रभातम् as a morning greeting (the word itself classical) ·
> शुभरात्रिः · the पुनः मिलामः farewell formula. Every row is a word the course
> teaches for real use; the label states what the evidence supports — nothing more,
> nothing less. [REF:monierwilliams1899]; [REF:apte1890]

Lesson 5's task D sorts by this table. Learn it as content: it is the chapter's actual
grammar — the grammar of honesty.

### Address forms, used whole

श्रीमान् and भोः are used today as fixed address chunks (भोः! श्रीमान्!) — the calling
forms of nouns (सम्बोधन) are a real system, and it arrives with the case machinery in
Volume 3. Until then: the chunks, whole, exactly as names have been used all course.

## 16. Conversation

सम्भाषणम् — two scenes; every word self-read. 🔊 audio:
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c01l04-dialogue.mp3` (slow, natural, and reflex-gap
tracks).

### Scene 1 — the dropped book

— भोः! एतत् पुस्तकम् तत्र!
(bhoḥ! etat pustakam tatra!) "Hey — the book, there!" — अरे! वह पुस्तक वहाँ!

— क्षमस्व, श्रीमान्!
(kṣamasva, śrīmān!) "Forgive me, sir!" — क्षमा करें, श्रीमान्!

— भद्रम्, भद्रम्! एतत् तव पुस्तकम्।
(bhadram, bhadram! etat tava pustakam.) "It's fine! Here is your book." — कोई बात नहीं!
यह तुम्हारी पुस्तक।

— धन्यवादः, श्रीमान्!
(dhanyavādaḥ, śrīmān!) "Thank you, sir!" — धन्यवाद, श्रीमान्!

### Scene 2 — the parting

— सायम् अस्ति। अहम् चलामि।
(sāyam asti. aham calāmi.) "It is evening. I'm off." — शाम हो गई। मैं चलता हूँ।

— अपि सर्वम् कुशलम्?
(api sarvam kuśalam?) "All well?" — सब ठीक?

— आम्, सर्वम् कुशलम्। न, न — सर्वम् एव कुशलम्!
(ām, sarvam kuśalam. na, na — sarvam eva kuśalam!) "Yes, all well. No, no — everything
is *quite* well!" — हाँ, सब ठीक। नहीं-नहीं — सब बिलकुल ठीक!

— स्वस्ति! पुनः मिलामः!
(svasti! punaḥ milāmaḥ!) "Farewell — we'll meet again!" — अच्छा! फिर मिलेंगे!

— स्वस्ति! शुभरात्रिः!
(svasti! śubharātriḥ!) "Farewell — good night!" — अच्छा! शुभ रात्रि!

Housekeeping: चलामि "I'm off" is the first-person of चलति, glossed whole on the
वदामि/पठामि pattern — a warm, standard leave-taking use. एव (v1.c06.l04's verse word)
does its everyday job — "quite, indeed". And hear the labels working in one scene:
क्षमस्व (classical) answered, धन्यवादः (modern) offered, स्वस्ति (Vedic) beside
शुभरात्रिः (modern) at the door. That mix *is* living Sanskrit.

## 17. Story

कथा — विदाई · The Parting. Six lines; each line: Devanagari, IAST, English, Hindi.

1. सायम् अस्ति। छात्राः चलन्ति।
   (sāyam asti. chātrāḥ calanti.) "It is evening. The students are leaving." — शाम है।
   छात्र चल पड़े हैं।
2. बालकः वदति — श्रीमान्! धन्यवादः!
   (bālakaḥ vadati — śrīmān! dhanyavādaḥ!) "The boy says: Sir — thank you!" — बालक कहता
   है — श्रीमान्! धन्यवाद!
3. आचार्यः वदति — भद्रम्! त्वम् धन्यः छात्रः!
   (ācāryaḥ vadati — bhadram! tvam dhanyaḥ chātraḥ!) "The teacher says: Bless you — you
   are a fortunate student!" — आचार्य कहते हैं — कल्याण हो! तुम धन्य छात्र हो!
4. माया वदति — क्षमस्व! मम पुस्तकम् अत्र न अस्ति!
   (māyā vadati — kṣamasva! mama pustakam atra na asti!) "Māyā says: Excuse me — my book
   is not here!" — माया कहती है — क्षमा करें! मेरी पुस्तक यहाँ नहीं है!
5. सीता हसति — तव पुस्तकम्? एतत् अस्ति!
   (sītā hasati — tava pustakam? etat asti!) "Sītā laughs: Your book? Here it is!" —
   सीता हँसती है — तुम्हारी पुस्तक? यह रही!
6. सर्वे वदन्ति — स्वस्ति! पुनः मिलामः! शुभरात्रिः!
   (sarve vadanti — svasti! punaḥ milāmaḥ! śubharātriḥ!) "All say: Farewell! We'll meet
   again — good night!" — सब कहते हैं — अच्छा! फिर मिलेंगे! शुभ रात्रि!

Two honest notes: छात्राः "students" and the verbs चलन्ति/वदन्ति "they walk/say" are
plural forms, glossed whole — your eye meets them here as chunks; the plural system is
this volume's later business (सर्वे you already know from the maṅgala śloka). Line 4 is
the negation rule earning its keep on its first day.

## 18. Exercises

अभ्यासाः — six exercises; 1, 3, 4 and 5 are production tasks. Do them in order.

**अभ्यासः 1 · प्रतिवचनम् (the reflex pairs — production).** Run §9 drill 1 with the

word, correct label in mind.

**अभ्यासः 2 · लेबल-विभागः (sort the kit — written).** Two columns on paper: शास्त्रीय ·

(as greeting) · भद्रम् ते · पुनः मिलामः · श्रीमान्. Check against §15's table; note the
two rows where a classical *word* carries a modern *use* — those are the both-things-true
cases.

**अभ्यासः 3 · अभिनयः (the scenes — production).** Perform §16's two scenes with a
partner from role cards ✍ (`planned:assets/worksheets/volume-2/v2c01l04-roleplay.pdf`),
swap, repeat. Solo: the gap track. Pause form between turns; the reply words at reflex
speed.

**अभ्यासः 4 · निषेध-अभ्यासः (negation drill — production).** Turn each into its negation

पुस्तकम् न (careful — where does न go? Fix it: एतत् मम पुस्तकम् न अस्ति। or न, एतत् मम
पुस्तकम् न।) · अहम् छात्रः → न, अहम् आचार्यः! Then read Hindi नास्तिक aloud and explain
its Sanskrit insides to yourself in one line.

**अभ्यासः 5 · श्रुतलेखनम् (dictation — production).** The dictation track 🔊: eight of
today's words in random order, twice each; then one full exchange line — क्षमस्व,

**अभ्यासः 6 · तालिका-पाठः (the keystone recital).** Recite §15's label table from
memory, both columns, out loud — then teach it to someone in two minutes: "these words
are old idiom; these are today's polite conventions; all of them are real Sanskrit."
The teach-back is lesson 5's task D in rehearsal.

## 22. Teacher Notes

शिक्षक-टिप्पणी

**The label conversation is the lesson.** Run §4 as a discussion, not a lecture: ask the
room how *they* say thank you in their languages, then unveil the honest history —
classical Sanskrit had richer, occasion-bound gratitude, and the living language grew a
convenience. If a student says "so धन्यवादः is fake?", the correction is the course's
whole philosophy: attested word, real use, honest label — living languages grow, and
saying so honors them. Never let the label sound like a demotion.

**Timing plan (45 min):** §3 retrieval, written (5) → §4 discussion + the धन्यवादः
unpacking (7) → the nine words with reflex-pair intro (10) → न and the placement chant
(5) → §16 scenes performed twice (8) → the keystone table built live on the board, class
sorting each word by shout (7) → homework briefing incl. the label hunt (3).

**Drill the replies harder than the openers.** Everyone learns धन्यवादः; the mark of
real courtesy is the instant भद्रम् coming back. Grade the pairs, not the words.

**Heritage learners:** they own धन्यवाद and कृपया natively — their work is the visarga
(धन्यवादः, not धन्यवाद) and the labels themselves; deputize them for the नास्तिक X-ray,
which delights every Hindi speaker who sees it for the first time. Watch English-habit
learners on कृपया-overuse (§12 mistake 5) and on the breathy ध.

**What students find hard:** believing both halves of a both-things-true label (the
सुप्रभातम् precedent from lesson 3 helps — same structure); न before its word against
the Hindi habit; and keeping क्षमस्व whole under pressure — the reflex drill, not
analysis, is the cure.
