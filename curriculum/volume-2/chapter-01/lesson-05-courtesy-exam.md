## 1. Hook

Five lessons ago you owned exactly one greeting. Today you own a protocol: you can open
a conversation at the right hour, ask after someone's well-being, thank them, apologize
to them, and take your leave — some thirty new words, and not one of them decoration.
So today's exam is not a word list. It is **one conversation**, run from first नमस्ते
to last स्वस्ति — first from a script, then, for the finale, improvised cold from a
role card. And between the speaking tasks sits the sort this chapter was built for:
nine claims about these words, where the winning answer is the honest label.

## 3. Previous Lesson Review

पुनरावृत्तिः — four stations, one per lesson, plus Station 0. Answer from memory
*before* opening any key [REF:roediger2006].

### Station 0 — the chapter's own intervals converge

The four lessons' spacing schedules — the +1-day items of lessons 3–4, the +1-week
items of lessons 1–2 — all come due (near enough) today [REF:cepeda2006]. Two
compressed items, two minutes; log the scores next to the old ones.

1. Dictation row, one word per lesson, self-read or partner-read: अञ्जलिः · कुशलम् ·
   रात्रिः · धन्यवादः — every visarga written, the ञ्ज welded.
2. Exchange re-run, aloud and cold, both roles: अपि कुशलम्? — कुशलम्! सर्वम् कुशलम्! —
   then close it: स्वस्ति! पुनः मिलामः!

### Station 1 — नमस्कारः: the greeting kit (lesson 1)

1. The all-purpose openers नमस्ते and नमस्कारः — say both, then dissect नमस्ते: which
   piece means "homage", which "to you"?
2. भोः — what does it do in a sentence, and to whom would you say it?
3. अञ्जलिः: the word and the joined-palms gesture — which of the two is a dictionary
   fact, and which register holds the gesture?

<details><summary>उत्तराणि · Station 1 key</summary>

1. नमः "homage" + ते "to you" — the join inside the word is a sandhi result, learned
   whole for now. → l01 §8, §15.
2. A classical address particle — "O!, hey" — calling for attention, to anyone
   addressed directly. → l01 §8.
3. The word अञ्जलिः (añjaliḥ) "the two hands joined" is FACT — an attested classical
   word; the gesture itself is cultural practice, TRADITION register, never assessed.
   → l01 §8, §15.
</details>

### Station 2 — कुशलप्रश्नः: the wellness exchange (lesson 2)

1. Run the exchange from memory, both turns — then give the extended answer with
   सर्वम्.
2. कथम् — gloss it, and name two question words it joined in the kit.
3. कुशली — who says it of himself, and what did the lesson honestly display beside it
   for a female speaker?

<details><summary>उत्तराणि · Station 2 key</summary>

1. अपि कुशलम्? — कुशलम्! — extended: सर्वम् कुशलम्! "All is well!" → l02 §16.
2. कथम् (katham) "how?" — beside किम्, कः, का (and कुतः) from Volume 1. → l02 §8.
3. A man says कुशली (kuśalī) of himself; the feminine कुशलिनी (kuśalinī) was shown as
   display — the gender system is taught later in Volume 2. → l02 §8.
</details>

### Station 3 — दिनयात्रा: the day cycle, with labels (lesson 3)

1. The day's four stops — recite in order with glosses: dawn, day, evening, night.
2. Which greeting opens the morning, which closes the day — and which usage label does
   each wear?
3. शुभम् standing alone — what does it wish, and where did शुभ first appear?

<details><summary>उत्तराणि · Station 3 key</summary>

1. प्रभातम् (prabhātam) "dawn" · दिनम् (dinam) "day" · सायम् (sāyam) "evening" ·
   रात्रिः (rātriḥ) "night". → l03 §14.
2. सुप्रभातम् opens, शुभरात्रिः closes — both wear the **modern spoken** label as
   greetings; सुप्रभातम् the word is itself classical. → l03 §8, §15.
3. A freestanding wish — "may it be well"; शुभ has been readable since v1.c04.l02.
   → l03 §8.
</details>

### Station 4 — शिष्टाचारः: courtesy and farewell, with labels (lesson 4)

1. Thank, then apologize — the two words, each with its label.
2. Negate: turn अस्ति into "is not" in pause form — and say what the joined form
   नास्ति previews.
3. The farewell pair स्वस्ति! पुनः मिलामः! — which half is classical idiom, which
   modern formula?

<details><summary>उत्तराणि · Station 4 key</summary>

1. धन्यवादः — attested word, modern everyday-thanks convention; क्षमस्व — classical
   form, taught as a whole chunk. → l04 §8, §15.
2. न अस्ति (na asti) — pause form; नास्ति previews vowel sandhi, Volume 3's subject.
   → l04 §15.
3. स्वस्ति is classical (Vedic onward); पुनः मिलामः is a modern spoken formula — its
   joined form पुनर्मिलामः previews visarga sandhi. → l04 §8, §15.
</details>

Score all fourteen prompts (Station 0's two included): 12+ clean → proceed to the
exam. Below that, re-study the weakest station today and assess tomorrow — the extra
spacing helps, it does not hurt [REF:cepeda2006].

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — one paragraph, as in every review lesson.

The kit you are examined on today spans the language's whole recorded life. Some of its
formulas have done this work for as long as Sanskrit has been written down; others were
coined or repurposed by the modern spoken-Sanskrit movement — and the chapter's central
discipline has been to say which is which, word by word, without banning anything.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** स्वस्ति has served as a blessing-and-farewell word from the Vedic
> corpus onward; धन्यवादः is an attested compound whose use as the everyday "thank
> you" is a modern spoken-Sanskrit convention. Both are Sanskrit; they wear different
> labels. [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. svasti] [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. dhanyavāda]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spaced practice produces far more durable learning than the same
> practice massed together. [REF:cepeda2006]

The four lessons' schedules were built to converge here — Station 0 just collected
them — and today opens this chapter's own clock: its +1-week re-runs land inside
Chapter 2's calendar.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why the exam is a conversation, not a word list.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Retrieval practice strengthens memory more than re-reading — the
> testing effect. [REF:roediger2006]

Courtesy formulas are learned, stored and retrieved as chunks — whole units whose
fluency comes from use in situations, not from analysis [REF:nation2013]. So the exam
rehearses the situation itself: task C runs the five moves as one continuous
performance, and task E removes the script entirely — a role card supplies the
situation, and the kit must assemble the conversation on the spot. That is the highest
form of retrieval this course has yet asked for, and it is exactly what Chapter 2's
dialogues will assume. One more design note: knowing a word includes knowing *when* it
is used — register is part of vocabulary knowledge, not decoration [REF:nation2013].
A learner who says धन्यवादः beautifully but calls it classical idiom knows the word
incompletely. That is why task D, the register sort, is scored as seriously as the
speaking tasks: the label is part of the meaning.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the exam's spine, every word yours:

> **परीक्षा अत्र अस्ति।**
> (parīkṣā atra asti.) "The exam is here." — परीक्षा यहाँ है।

The five moves, in one breath:

> **सुप्रभातम्, श्रीमान्! — अपि कुशलम्? — धन्यवादः! — क्षमस्व! — स्वस्ति! पुनः मिलामः!**
> (suprabhātam, śrīmān! — api kuśalam? — dhanyavādaḥ! — kṣamasva! — svasti! punaḥ
> milāmaḥ!) "Good morning, sir! — All well? — Thank you! — Forgive me! — Farewell, we
> meet again!" — सुप्रभात, श्रीमान्! — सब कुशल? — धन्यवाद! — क्षमा कीजिए! — अच्छा,
> फिर मिलेंगे!

Greet, ask, thank, apologize, part: five moves, one conversation — pause form
throughout, every visarga sounded, and the labels carried silently in your head.

## 8. English Explanation

First the exam's reference sheet, then the architecture.

### What the chapter certifies

By the end of today you will have demonstrated every Chapter 1 can-do: a complete
courtesy exchange in pause form; the right greeting for the hour, with its label; plain
negation with न; and the role-card improvisation. The keystone reference for the whole
exam is lesson 4's label table (l04 §15) — reprinted compactly in §15 below. Keep it
in your head, not on your desk: task D is closed-book.

### The assessment architecture

| Task | Skill | Format | Pass line |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Hearing courtesy | 10 audio items: hear a line, choose the apt reply | 8/10 |
| B | Writing | dictation: 8 courtesy words + one exchange line | 7/8 + the line clean |
| C | THE ROLEPLAY LADDER | greet → wellness → thank → apologize → farewell, one continuous two-role recording | all five moves, both roles, pause form |
| D | REGISTER SORT | 9 fresh claim cards, keep-or-reject with one line of reasoning | 7/9 with reasoning |
| E | THE IMPROVISED CONVERSATION | role card → a complete courtesy conversation, recorded; plus the V1 micro-check | conversation complete + micro-check clean |

The §19 quiz (10 questions) complements the tasks: §18 proves you can *do*; §19 proves
you know *what is so*. Pass = every task at partial-or-better (rubric in §22) and quiz
7+/10. Two tasks are this chapter's signature. **D is the intellectual centerpiece** —
the claim cards this chapter was built for, where the honest label is the full answer
and "reject" always comes with the correction attached. **E is the practical
centerpiece** — the first time this course asks you to speak Sanskrit *without a
script*: a persona, a situation, and the kit, recorded. E also carries the Volume 1
maintenance micro-check (one varṇamālā row from memory, one conjunct build-and-dissect
pair), because a writing system stays owned only if it stays visited. This gate opens
Chapter 2 — परिवारः जगच्च (parivāraḥ jagacca), Family & the World.

### How the listening task thinks

Task A's items are not echoes. Some lines mirror back (नमस्ते is answered with नमस्ते
or नमस्कारः; सुप्रभातम् with सुप्रभातम्); some lines *answer* (अपि कुशलम्? wants
कुशलम्!, and क्षमस्व wants the reassurance भद्रम् — "it is well"). Choosing aptly means
hearing which kind of line you were given. The time-of-day pairs add the third
dimension: सुप्रभातम् returned at nightfall is a wrong answer even though it is a real
greeting.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — nothing new to articulate; everything to keep honest at speed. The scoring
references for tasks A, B and C. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c01l05-roleplay-model.mp3`.

**The visarga finale.** धन्यवादः, नमस्कारः, भोः, मिलामः — the final ः is a light
voiceless breath, and it is *there*. The Hindi habit clips it (धन्यवाद); the exam does
not. Feel the puff on the back of your hand.

**The full final म्.** सुप्रभातम् ends with closed lips: -tam, never "suprabhāt".
Same discipline as ever, since Volume 1: no schwa deletion, no clipped finals.

**The palatal weld.** अञ्जलिः runs ञ्ज at one station — nasal and affricate both at
the palate, exactly like v1.c06's ञ्च. If the nasal drifts dental, re-run the ñ drill.

**Long vowels at full length.** श्रीमान् — both syllables long, two beats each; मिलामः
holds -ला-. Dropping length is the fastest way to sound rushed.

**Pure ए and ओ.** ते and भोः take the long pure monophthongs — never English "tay",
never "bhow".

**Pause form, audibly.** न अस्ति keeps a real gap; पुनः मिलामः stays two words. The
joined forms are Volume 3's business — today they would cost you dictation marks.

**Warm-up before recording C:** one visarga word (धन्यवादः), one weld (अञ्जलिः), one
cluster opener (क्षमस्व), one long-vowel word (श्रीमान्) — then the five-move line of
§6 once, slowly.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the consolidated self-diagnosis circuit; these are the scoring rubric for
tasks B and C. Every speaking error today should be caught by one of them.

- **Breath check (visarga):** hold a hand before your lips at the end of धन्यवादः — a
  small voiceless puff must land on it. Nothing felt = the Hindi clip. → l04 §9.
- **Close check (final म्):** सुप्रभातम् must end with your lips together. Mirror test:
  if your mouth is open on the last sound, the म् went missing. → l03 §9.
- **Palate check (ञ्ज):** say अञ्जलिः slowly — the tongue stays at the palatal station
  across the seam; no dental detour into "an-jaliḥ" with an English n. → l01 §9.
- **Duration check:** count beats through श्रीमान् — long, long. Two short syllables
  mean the honorific shrank. → l04 §9.
- **Glide check (ए ओ):** watch your lips in the mirror through ते and भोः — they must
  not move once the vowel starts. Movement = an English diphthong crept in. → v1.c02 §9.
- **Space check (pause form):** न अस्ति — a real silence between the words. If they
  merge, you have improvised sandhi a volume early. → l04 §15.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — two worked model answers before you start.

**Model listening item (task A).** You hear: *क्षमस्व!* Printed options: भद्रम्! ·
सुप्रभातम्! · शुभरात्रिः! First decide what kind of line you were given: क्षमस्व is not
a greeting, so no greeting can answer it — it is an apology, and an apology seeks
reassurance. भद्रम् — "it is well, all good" — is the classical courtesy reply (the
chunk भद्रम् ते "blessings on you" from lesson 1). Mirror-lines echo; asking-lines get
answers; only भद्रम् answers. That decision — echo or answer? — is the whole skill of
task A.

**Model register card (task D).** Card: "धन्यवादः is how classical texts said
thank-you." Both halves of your knowledge activate. Yes: धन्यवादः is a real, attested
compound — it stands in the classical lexicon [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v.
dhanyavāda]. But no: classical texts typically express gratitude otherwise — lesson 4
displayed अनुगृहीतोऽस्मि "I am obliged" as the classical route — and the everyday
"thank you" use of धन्यवादः belongs to modern spoken Sanskrit. So the verdict is
**reject**, and the reasoning is the honest label said in full: *attested word, modern
convention*. Notice what the card is testing: not whether you love the word (you may;
it is a fine word), but whether you can hold two true things at once and label them.
That is the chapter's discipline in one sentence.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — the chapter's law, final form: the label system, the negative, and the
seams.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** A word's attestation and a usage's attestation are separate
> facts. धन्यवादः the word stands in the classical lexicon; धन्यवादः as the everyday
> "thank you" is a modern spoken convention. This course records both facts, formula
> by formula. [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. dhanyavāda] [REF:apte1890 s.v. क्षम्]

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** Everyday South Asian courtesy practice joins the palms —
> the añjali — alongside नमस्ते. The gesture is cultural practice, named by the
> classical word अञ्जलिः; it is respected, modeled in this course's illustrations, and
> never part of the word's grammar or of any assessment.

### The keystone: the label table (compact reprint of l04 §15)

| Formula | IAST | Label | The honest line |
|---|---|---|---|
| नमस्ते · नमस्कारः | namaste · namaskāraḥ | classical formation · living everyday use | नमः + ते, नमः + कारः; the all-purpose greetings of spoken Sanskrit |
| स्वागतम् | svāgatam | classical idiom | word and welcoming use both attested |
| अपि कुशलम्? — कुशलम्! | api kuśalam? — kuśalam! | classical idiom | the kuśala-inquiry is standard classical etiquette |
| स्वस्ति · भद्रम् ते | svasti · bhadram te | classical idiom | blessing-and-farewell formulas, Vedic and epic |
| क्षमस्व | kṣamasva | classical form | imperative of √क्षम्, taught as a whole chunk |
| धन्यवादः | dhanyavādaḥ | attested word · modern everyday-thanks | classical texts thank otherwise (अनुगृहीतोऽस्मि — display only) |
| सुप्रभातम् | suprabhātam | classical word · modern greeting-use | the both-things-true row |
| शुभरात्रिः | śubharātriḥ | modern convention | a "good night" calque; labeled, taught, used |
| कृपया | kṛpayā | modern convention | shared with Hindi |
| पुनः मिलामः | punaḥ milāmaḥ | modern formula | transparent formation; joined form previews sandhi |

### The negative, in pause form

न before the word it denies, both words intact: न अस्ति "is not" · न कुशलम् "not
well". That is the whole rule for now — no fusion, no movement.

### The seams, honestly parked

Three joined forms crossed this chapter as previews, never as targets: **नास्ति** (न +
अस्ति), **पुनर्मिलामः** (पुनः + मिलामः), and the display-only **अनुगृहीतोऽस्मि**. Even
नमस्ते hides one: the join of नमः + ते inside it is itself a sandhi result, learned
whole today and explained in Volume 3. The exam asks for pause form everywhere; the
previews are assessed only as *previews* (task D card 9, quiz Q10).

## 16. Conversation

सम्भाषणम् — task C's target: the five-move ladder as one continuous scene, both roles.
🔊 model at three speeds: `planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c01l05-roleplay-model.mp3`.
Cast: a छात्रः at the school door; the आचार्यः within. A book is about to slip.

**Move 1 — greet:**

— सुप्रभातम्, श्रीमान्!
(suprabhātam, śrīmān!) "Good morning, sir!" — सुप्रभात, श्रीमान्!

— सुप्रभातम्! स्वागतम्, भोः!
(suprabhātam! svāgatam, bhoḥ!) "Good morning — welcome!" — सुप्रभात! स्वागत है!

**Move 2 — the wellness exchange:**

— अपि कुशलम्?
(api kuśalam?) "All well?" — सब कुशल?

— कुशलम्! सर्वम् कुशलम्!
(kuśalam! sarvam kuśalam!) "Well — all is well!" — कुशल! सब कुशल!

**Move 3 — thank:**

— धन्यवादः, श्रीमान्!
(dhanyavādaḥ, śrīmān!) "Thank you, sir!" — धन्यवाद, श्रीमान्!

**Move 4 — the slip, and the apology:**

— क्षमस्व, भोः!
(kṣamasva, bhoḥ!) "Forgive me!" — क्षमा कीजिए!

— भद्रम्! भद्रम्!
(bhadram! bhadram!) "It is well — all good!" — कोई बात नहीं!

**Move 5 — farewell:**

— स्वस्ति! पुनः मिलामः!
(svasti! punaḥ milāmaḥ!) "Farewell — we meet again!" — अच्छा, फिर मिलेंगे!

— स्वस्ति! शुभम्!
(svasti! śubham!) "Farewell — be well!" — नमस्ते! शुभ हो!

Housekeeping: every word is readable, writable and labeled; nothing is borrowed. In
task C you perform *both* roles — switch voices, keep the pauses honest.

## 22. Teacher Notes

अध्यापकटिप्पणी

**Format:** stations (§3, 10 min including Station 0) → tasks A–B (10 min) → task C
(6 min, recorded in pairs) → task D (8 min, discussion protected) → task E (8 min,
protected) → quiz and debrief (3 min; the quiz moves to homework if time runs short,
never task E).

**Rubric:**

| Task | Full | Partial | Fail signal → remediation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (10) | 8–10 | 6–7 | hour-mismatch misses → l03 day-cycle drill; echo-vs-answer confusion → §13 model item |
| B (8+line) | 7–8 + line clean | 5–6 | visarga dropped → §12 row 1, §11 breath check; final म् clipped → row 2; sandhi smuggled → row 6 |
| C (5 moves ×2 runs) | all moves, both roles, both runs, pause form | one move prompted or scripted run only | a move skipped → §16 skeleton card; merged words → §11 space check |
| D (9) | 7–9 reasoned | 5–6 | labels blurred → §13 walk-through, then the §15 table re-derived aloud |
| E (conversation + micro-check) | complete conversation + clean micro-check | conversation with one prompt, or micro-check slip | freeze → one open-book dry run, then closed; micro-check misses → v1.c04 row cards, v1.c05 conjunct drill |

**Pass** = all tasks partial+, quiz 7+/10 → Chapter 2 (परिवारः जगच्च). One weak task →
targeted retake tomorrow. Two+ → repeat the weak lessons; frame as scheduling, not
failure.

**Running the label discussion (task D):** the cards are conversations, not gotchas.
Let a learner object "but everyone says धन्यवादः!" — and agree, warmly, because that
is the point: the word is real, the convention is alive, and the honest label honors
the living language rather than policing it. Nothing in this course gets banned;
everything gets named. Card 5 is the crown: both things are true at once, and learners
who can hold that pair have learned the chapter's deepest skill. Card 9 rewards the
learner who remembers that previews are promises, not content — praise the phrase
"taught in Volume 3" wherever it appears in a reasoning line.

**Running the improvisation exam (task E) kindly:** this is the course's first
unscripted speech. Announce the scoring out loud before anyone speaks: *moves
completed, not eloquence* — hesitation costs nothing, a missing farewell does. Allow
one restart without comment. Offer the partner option to anyone who freezes solo; the
skeleton card (five move-names) may sit face-up for the dry run and face-down for the
scored run. Record only the second attempt. Keep the room small and the tone light —
the exam should feel like the first real conversation, because it is.

**Heritage learners:** Hindi hands them धन्यवाद, कृपया and the शुभ words — deputize
them for glosses. Their genuine tests: the visarga finale (धन्यवादः, not धन्यवाद),
the full final म् (सुप्रभातम्), pause form under fluency pressure, and taking the
labels seriously — a fluent speaker's "we just say it this way" is exactly the
instinct task D asks them to examine, not abandon.

**What students find hard:** improvising while recorded (rehearse the skeleton, not
sentences); the both-things-true card (two truths feel like a contradiction until
said aloud); keeping सुप्रभातम् out of the evening under pressure; and भोः feeling
archaic — model it warmly and often, it is classical and friendly.
