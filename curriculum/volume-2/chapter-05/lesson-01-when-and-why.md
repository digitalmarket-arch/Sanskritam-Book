## 1. Hook

Your Sanskrit owns three hundred and eight words and seven ways to ask. You can ask who,
what, where, from where, how and how many — and you still cannot ask the two questions a
real conversation keeps coming back to: *when?* and *why?* Today both arrive, each with
its answer: *now* for the first, *because* for the second, and one small verb that turns
going into coming. By tonight the question kit is complete — nine askers — and two of
them turn out to share an ancestor with English *who* and *what*.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — five classical words, and the fix this lesson is.

Question words turn statements into talk. Sanskrit's are a tight family, every one
starting with क, and today the family the course has collected since Volume 1 is whole.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** All five of today's words are attested classical Sanskrit, glossed
> here as the dictionaries give them: कदा "when?", इदानीम् "now", किमर्थम् "why?, for
> what purpose?", यतः "because, since" (also "from which"), आगच्छति "comes". No modern
> usage label is needed for any of them. [REF:apte1890 s.vv.]; [REF:monierwilliams1899
> s.vv. kadā, yatas]

One line of course history, stated as the fix it is. The 1.x course's Conversation unit
listed both question words, and listed them correctly — the audit kept the words. What
it did not do was give the learner a frame for the answer, a word on how किमर्थम् is
built, or a usage label on anything in the unit. In 2.0 every asker comes with the kind
of answer it wants, and every chunk of this chapter carries an honest label. The 1.x
book's own sample question — a when-question about coming — returns in §6, in pause
form, as the first frame of the lesson.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why a question word is only half a tool, and why nine of them are
drilled with their answers.

A question word on its own does nothing. कदा earns its keep only inside an exchange —
someone asks, someone answers with a time — so the unit you learn today is the pair,
not the word.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Conversational ability grows fastest when frequent chunks and
> frames are learned whole and used at once, with analysis following later; and words
> learned together in a tight set of close relatives (a kit of question words, say) can
> interfere with one another unless each is tied to a distinct use. [REF:nation2013]

That second point shapes today's drills. Nine words that all begin with क and all ask
something blur fast as a list, so each asker is bound to the *kind* of answer it wants
(§15's table, §18 exercise 2). The wheel is for recall; the answer is for meaning.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spaced retrieval beats massed re-study: today's Station 0 is the
> verb exam's +1-week interval landing on schedule, and this lesson's own +1-day re-run
> is booked before it ends (§20). [REF:cepeda2006] [REF:roediger2006]

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the two new questions at work, in sentences you fully own:

> **त्वम् कदा आगच्छसि? — अहम् इदानीम् आगच्छामि।**
> (tvam kadā āgacchasi? — aham idānīm āgacchāmi.) "When are you coming? — I am coming
> now." — तुम कब आ रहे हो? — मैं अभी आ रहा हूँ।

> **सः कदा आगच्छति? — सः श्वः आगच्छति।**
> (saḥ kadā āgacchati? — saḥ śvaḥ āgacchati.) "When is he coming? — He is coming
> tomorrow." — वह कब आ रहा है? — वह कल आ रहा है।

> **किमर्थम् त्वम् संस्कृतम् पठसि? — यतः अहम् छात्रः अस्मि।**
> (kimartham tvam saṃskṛtam paṭhasi? — yataḥ aham chātraḥ asmi.) "Why do you study
> Sanskrit? — Because I am a student." — तुम संस्कृत क्यों पढ़ते हो? — क्योंकि मैं
> छात्र हूँ।

Three frames, one shape: the asker stands near the front, the verb at the end, pause
form between words. The when-answer can be a whole sentence or a bare time word (कदा? —
इदानीम्।); the why-answer opens with यतः and gives the reason in a sentence of its own.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

अब तक आपके पास सात प्रश्न-शब्द थे — कः, का, किम्, कुतः, कथम्, कुत्र, कति। इनसे आप
"कौन", "क्या", "कहाँ से", "कैसे", "कहाँ" और "कितने" पूछ सकते थे। पर बातचीत के दो सबसे
ज़रूरी सवाल बाक़ी थे — "कब?" और "क्यों?"। आज दोनों आ रहे हैं। कदा का अर्थ है "कब"।
किमर्थम् का अर्थ है "क्यों" — शब्दशः "किस प्रयोजन से", "किसलिए"। किमर्थम् के भीतर
किम् और अर्थम् जुड़े दिखते हैं, पर आज इसे एक पूरा शब्द मानकर सीखिए; इसके भीतर का
व्याकरण आगे की बात है। हिन्दी बोलने वालों के लिए एक ख़ास सावधानी: हिन्दी में "अर्थ"
प्रायः "मतलब" होता है, इसलिए किमर्थम् को "क्या मतलब?" समझ लेने का ख़तरा है। यहाँ अर्थ
का मतलब है प्रयोजन, उद्देश्य — किमर्थम् सीधा "क्यों" है।

उत्तर के दो शब्द भी आज मिलते हैं। इदानीम् यानी "अब, इस समय" — कदा? का सबसे सीधा
उत्तर। इसके दो साथी आपके पास पहले से हैं: अद्य "आज" और श्वः "आने वाला कल"। और यतः
यानी "क्योंकि" — किमर्थम्? का उत्तर यतः से शुरू होता है: किमर्थम् त्वम् संस्कृतम्
पठसि? — यतः अहम् छात्रः अस्मि। "तुम संस्कृत क्यों पढ़ते हो? — क्योंकि मैं छात्र हूँ।"
बिल्कुल हिन्दी जैसा जोड़ा: प्रश्न में "क्यों", उत्तर में "क्योंकि"।

आज की क्रिया है आगच्छति — "आता है"। इसे ध्यान से देखिए: यह गच्छति "जाता है" ही है,
बस आगे आ जुड़ गया है। संस्कृत में ऐसे छोटे अंश, जो क्रिया के आगे लगकर उसका अर्थ
मोड़ देते हैं, उपसर्ग कहलाते हैं — यानी क्रिया के आगे जुड़ने वाला अंश। यहाँ आ ने
"जाने" को "आने" में बदल दिया। हिन्दी में "आना" और "जाना" दो अलग क्रियाएँ हैं; संस्कृत
एक ही क्रिया से दोनों दिशाएँ बना लेती है। उपसर्गों की पूरी व्यवस्था आगे के खण्डों का
विषय है — आज केवल यही एक। अच्छी ख़बर यह कि रूप पिछले अध्याय के नियम से ही बनते हैं:
आगच्छामि, आगच्छसि, आगच्छति। और ये रूप नए शब्द नहीं गिने जाते — शब्द-कोश में केवल
आगच्छति जुड़ता है।

उच्चारण में तीन चौकियाँ: आगच्छति का पहला आ पूरी दो मात्राओं का — इसे छोटा किया तो आना

## 8. English Explanation

No new letter, no new sign — today is vocabulary: two askers, two answers, and one verb
that comes instead of going.

### The two askers

**कदा** (kadā) "when?" — the eighth asker. Two blocks, क-दा, and the final आ held for two
full beats; a clipped *kada* sounds unfinished. It asks for a time, and the course
already owns three answers: **इदानीम्** "now" (new today), **अद्य** "today" and **श्वः**
"tomorrow" (both v2.c03.l03). A bare answer is complete, correct Sanskrit — कदा? — श्वः।
— the same bare-answer rule कति taught in v2.c03.l02.

**किमर्थम्** (kimartham) "why?" — the ninth, literally "for what purpose?". You can see
the owned asker किम् "what?" (v1.c05.l01) at its front, and अर्थम् behind it. अर्थ is a
broad word in the dictionaries — aim, purpose, reason, thing, wealth, meaning
[REF:apte1890 s.v. अर्थः] — and inside किमर्थम् it is *purpose*: "for what?". The word
is learned whole today. How its म् does that job is grammar for a later volume, and the
course says so rather than half-teaching it.

### The two answers

**इदानीम्** (idānīm) "now" — a classical time word that never changes its shape, like
अद्य and श्वः: no dress to put on, nothing to agree with. Three beats, the middle two
long: इ-दा-नीम्.

**यतः** (yataḥ) "because, since" — it opens the reason: यतः अहम् छात्रः अस्मि। "because
I am a student." You have met its family. In v1.c02.l06, कुतः "from where?" asked and
इतः "from here" answered; ततः "then" ran that lesson's story. यतः is the family's
*pointing* member, the same ask/point pairing v2.c02.l04 drew between कुत्र, which
asks, and यत्र, which points. The dictionaries give it both jobs — "from which" and
"because, since" [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. yatas] — and today you use the second. A memory hook, not a
history claim: a reason is where a thing comes from.

### आगच्छति — going, turned around

**आगच्छति** (āgacchati) "comes" is **आ** (ā) in front of **गच्छति** (gacchati) "goes",
the bank verb of v2.c04.l01. The little आ turns the direction round: going away becomes
coming here. That is the whole trick, and it is a real one — Sanskrit builds many verbs
by putting a short prefix in front of a verb it already has.

The forms come free, on the Chapter 4 pattern: **आगच्छामि** "I come" (the stretch held),
**आगच्छसि** "you come", **आगच्छति** "he/she comes"; the polite भवान् takes the he-form
(भवान् कदा आगच्छति?), as v2.c04.l03 taught. Ledger rule 4: the verb enters once, as
आगच्छति. One honest line, and only one: these front pieces (उपसर्ग in the tradition's
term) are a whole system — many prefixes, many verbs, some shifting meaning far more
than "go" to "come" — and that system is later volumes' business. Today: one prefix.

### The frames — and one honest line about tomorrow

**त्वम् कदा आगच्छसि? — अहम् इदानीम् आगच्छामि।** — or **श्वः आगच्छामि।** "I'm coming
tomorrow." The verb exam told you -ति marks person, not time, and that stays true: in
श्वः आगच्छामि the ending marks only the person, the time word does the pointing, and
Sanskrit, like English *I'm coming tomorrow*, lets the present stand for a near, settled
future (§15). What the present cannot do is reach back: ह्यः आगच्छामि is not "I came
yesterday" — that needs a past tense, which is later business.

**किमर्थम् त्वम् संस्कृतम् पठसि? — यतः अहम् छात्रः अस्मि।** Your real reason may need
words you do not own yet; say the nearest true thing you *can* say — यतः मम माता
संस्कृतम् जानाति। "because my mother knows Sanskrit" is as honest a reason as any. With
कदा and किमर्थम् the kit closes at nine, every asker beginning with क — §13 shows how far
back that क goes, and §15 gives each asker its answer.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — no new sounds; the work is a long gate, a repha, a long-vowel ladder and the
kit on a beat. Audio: 🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c05l01-drill.mp3` (drills
1–3), `planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c05l01-word-list.mp3` (word list, three speeds,
the kit wheel and the dictation track).

**Drill 1 — the long gate.** Climb with the audio: आ — आग — आगच्छ — आगच्छति. Then the
contrast pair, five times: गच्छति — आगच्छति. The only difference is a long आ in front,
so the आ must be unmistakable: two full beats, mouth open, before the knot. Inside, the
च्छ is the knot from v2.c04.l01 — seal, hold, one release.

**Drill 2 — the repha.** अ — अर् — अर्थ — अर्थम् — किमर्थम्. The र is a single tap that
leads straight into थ, a dental stop with a real puff of breath — never English *th*.
Then say the whole word on four even beats, कि-म-र्थ-म्, every अ full; the Hindi habit
"kimarth" drops a syllable Sanskrit keeps.

**Drill 3 — the long-vowel ladder.** इ — इदा — इदानी — इदानीम्, then कदा on its own,
three times, holding the final आ. Short, long, long in the first word; short, long in the
second. Dental द and न in both: tongue on the teeth.

**Drill 4 — the kit wheel.** With the audio, fixed order, three passes (slow · natural ·
timed): कः — का — किम् — कुतः — कथम् — कुत्र — कति — कदा — किमर्थम्. Visargas breathed,
the का held long, किम् and कथम् closed on the lips. Log the timed pass: it is tomorrow's
Station 0 baseline (§20).

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — four checks; the articulators know all of this already. The coming-going card
(`planned:assets/images/volume-2/v2c05l01-coming-going-card.svg`) marks the long आ gate.

1. **The gate check (आगच्छति).** Mouth wide open for the opening आ, held for two beats you
   could count; only then does the tongue rise for ग. If the jaw barely drops, the gate
   was clipped.
2. **The knot check (आगच्छति).** As in v2.c04.l01: tongue-body sealed against the
   palate, a held beat, ONE release with a puff you can feel on your palm.
3. **The tap-and-puff check (किमर्थम्).** Tongue tip flicks the ridge once for र, then
   lands on the upper teeth for थ — palm in front of the lips, and the puff must be there.
   Tongue *between* the teeth means the English *th* got in.
4. **The tooth check (कदा, इदानीम्, यतः).** Every द, न and त today is dental: tongue tip
   on the back of the upper front teeth, never on the ridge behind them.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; these feed the quiz distractors.

1. **"किमर्थम् = what does it mean?"** The Hindi sense of अर्थ, "meaning", takes over;
   inside किमर्थम्, अर्थ is *purpose*. *Fix:* §8 — learn it whole: "for what purpose".
2. **The asker mix-up.** Answering कदा? with a place (अत्र) or कुत्र? with a time (श्वः):
   nine k-words blur as a list. *Fix:* §15's answer table, §18 exercise 2.
3. **यतः and कुतः swapped.** Both end in -तः; कुतः *asks* "from where?", यतः *gives* the
   reason — "कुतः अहम् छात्रः अस्मि" is no reason. *Fix:* §8's ask/point pair.
4. **The gate clipped: "agacchati".** The opening आ shortens and the word loses the
   prefix that makes it "come". *Fix:* §9 drill 1's contrast pair गच्छति / आगच्छति.
5. **Person mismatch, new verb.** अहम् आगच्छति, त्वम् आगच्छामि — Chapter 4's flagged
   error. *Fix:* v2.c04.l02 §15 — the ending IS the person.
6. **The present pushed into yesterday: ह्यः आगच्छामि for "I came yesterday".** The
   present carries a near future with a time word (श्वः आगच्छामि), never a past. *Fix:*
   §15's honesty line — इदानीम्, अद्य, श्वः until the past tense arrives.
7. **Clipped finals: "kimarth", "yat", "idānim".** Hindi-rhythm schwa loss, a dropped
   visarga, a shortened ई. *Fix:* §9 drills 2–3 and §10's narrow notes.
8. **The cognate reach, both directions.** "English *who* comes from Sanskrit कः" draws
   an arrow that does not exist; "कदा and *when* are a matched pair" claims more than the
   evidence gives. *Fix:* §13 — siblings, no arrows; the stem is shared, and only two
   rows are paired word for word.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — the cognate shelf: one ancestor, three branches, two safe rows, and the
Hindi k-words beside them. No arrows anywhere today; everything on this shelf is
siblings.

Inside Sanskrit, all nine askers start with क because they are one family: the grammars
build them on one interrogative stem, appearing as क-, कि- or कु- [REF:whitney1889].
That is why the kit chants so easily — and why §5 warns that it blurs so easily.

### Outside Sanskrit — the \*kʷ- family

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The k- question words of Sanskrit (कः, किम्, कदा, कुत्र, कथम्,
> कति), the English wh-words (*who, what, when, where*) and the Latin qu-words (*quis,
> quid, quando*) continue one Proto-Indo-European interrogative stem, reconstructed as
> \*kʷo- beside \*kʷi-. Each branch reshaped the old \*kʷ- sound by regular rules of its
> own — k- in these Sanskrit words, qu- in Latin, an older hw- that English now spells
> wh-. Shared inheritance: nobody borrowed in any direction. [REF:mallory2006]

| Sanskrit | Latin | English | What the row claims |
|---|---|---|---|
| कः (kaḥ) "who?" | *quis* "who?" | *who* | same stem, same job — a safe row |
| किम् (kim) "what?" | *quid* "what?" | *what* | same stem, same job — a safe row |

Read even the safe rows honestly. The stem is the shared part; each language put its own
endings on it, and the stem itself came in two shapes, \*kʷo- and \*kʷi-, which the
languages did not always use for the same word. So the rows match in stem and in job,
not letter for letter. And the rest of the family stops at the stem: कदा and *when*,
कुत्र and *where*, कथम् and *how* all carry it, but they are not word-for-word matches,
and this shelf does not pair them.

### Hindi's k-words — the same stem, inherited

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Hindi's question words — कौन, क्या, कब, कहाँ, कैसे, कितना — carry
> the same inherited k- stem, handed down through Middle Indo-Aryan. [REF:masica1991]

The claim is the stem, not a pedigree for each word: which Sanskrit form stands behind
which Hindi word is a question the shelf does not guess at. A Hindi speaker asking कब?,
a Sanskrit speaker asking कदा? and an English speaker asking *when?* are all using a k-
they inherited — the last one reshaped into wh-.

**What the shelf refuses:** "English *who* comes from Sanskrit कः" (no arrow — siblings,
not parent and child); "Sanskrit took its question words from Latin" (no arrow the other
way either); "कदा and *when* are an exact pair" (stem-sharers — the shelf pairs only कः
and किम्). Two safe rows, one stem statement, three refusals; the chapter exam's task D
tests exactly this.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — no new machinery: one kit completed, one prefix met, two frames, and the
honesty lines that keep them safe.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** (1) The nine askers ask for different kinds of answer — a person, a
> thing, a source, a manner, a place, a number, a time, a purpose — and an answer may
> stand bare (कदा? — श्वः।). (2) आगच्छति is आ + गच्छति: the prefix turns "goes" into
> "comes", and the verb takes the present singular endings exactly as गच्छति does —
> आगच्छामि, आगच्छसि, आगच्छति. (3) A reason is given in a clause opened by यतः
> "because": किमर्थम्? — यतः अहम् छात्रः अस्मि — a full sentence of its own. (4) The
> present form, with a time word, can state a near and settled future (श्वः आगच्छामि
> "I am coming tomorrow"); the ending still marks the person, not the time, and it
> cannot state a past.
> [REF:apte1890 s.v. गम् with आ]; [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. yatas]; [REF:whitney1889]

| का | who? (of an -आ word) | माया। | v1.c06.l02 — the कः/का pair (kit chunk; first met in v1.c04.l02's का भाषा?) |

| कथम् | how? | कुशलम्। | v2.c01.l02 |
| कुत्र | where? | अत्र। | v2.c02.l04 |
| कति | how many? | पञ्च। | v1.c02.l01 |
| कदा | when? | इदानीम्। · श्वः। | **today** |
| किमर्थम् | why? — for what purpose? | यतः … | **today** |

Recite the left column as the wheel; learn each row as a pair. The answer column is what
keeps nine look-alike words apart (§5).

   namaste!" — देखो — गुरुजी आते हैं! वे कहते हैं — नमस्ते!

## 18. Exercises

अभ्यासाः — seven exercises; 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6 are production tasks. Do them in order. All

**अभ्यासः 1 · प्रश्नचक्रम् · the kit wheel (timed, spoken/recorded).** One recorded take:

**अभ्यासः 2 · उत्तरमेलनम् · asker to answer (matching, then aloud).** Nine answer cards,

**अभ्यासः 3 · रूपनिर्माणम् · the three persons of आगच्छति — production, spoken + written.**

आगच्छसि · आगच्छामि · आगच्छति.

**अभ्यासः 4 · श्रुतलेखनम् · dictation (production, written).** Play the dictation track
🔊: the five headwords in random order, each read twice, then one frame sentence, twice.

welded, the repha sitting on थ, both long vowels of इदानीम्, the visarga of यतः. 5/5 plus

Father come?" (b) "Father is coming tomorrow." (c) "I am coming now." (d) "Why do you

coming?" Read your six aloud as one exchange.

**अभ्यासः 6 · साक्षात्कारः · the when/why interview (production, spoken/recorded).** In

किमर्थम् — and answer your partner's four, truthfully, in owned words only. Suggested
openers: त्वम् कदा विद्यालयम् आगच्छसि? · त्वम् कदा गृहम् गच्छसि? · किमर्थम् त्वम्
संस्कृतम् पठसि? · किमर्थम् त्वम् हससि? If your true reason needs words you do not own,
give the nearest true reason you can say. Swap roles; record one full round.

**अभ्यासः 7 · अङ्कनम् · the shelf sort.** Keep or reject, one reasoning line each:
(a) "कः, Latin *quis* and English *who* share one ancestor — the PIE interrogative
stem." (b) "English *who* comes from Sanskrit कः." (c) "किम् and Latin *quid* are a safe
row: same stem, same job." (d) "कदा and English *when* are an exact word-for-word pair."
(e) "Hindi's कौन, क्या and कब carry the same inherited k- stem." (f) "Sanskrit borrowed
its question words from Latin." Before checking: which two cards fail for the *same*
reason — and what is that reason?

## 22. Teacher Notes

अध्यापकटिप्पणी

**Open with the gap, not the list.** After Station 0, ask the room to play back one of
their मम दिनम् sentences and then ask them, in English, "When? Why?" They cannot answer
in Sanskrit — yet. Write कदा? and किमर्थम्? on the board at that moment. The lesson's
thesis — two questions turn a report into a conversation — lands better shown than
announced.

**Timing plan (45 min):** Station 0 + prompts, scores logged (8) → the five words on
cards; the आ arrow turning गच्छति round (8) → pronunciation: gate, repha, ladder (6) →
the nine-asker wheel and the answer match, exercise 2 in pairs (7) → when/why
interviews, exercise 6 (8) → the shelf: two safe rows, the stem, the refusals (5) →
dictation launch, homework, and the +1-day booking read aloud (3).

**Board work:** a wheel of nine spokes, seven labels already on (the kit as of Chapter
3), two blank spokes filled live with कदा and किमर्थम्. Under the wheel, a strip with
\*kʷ- in the middle and three arms — Sanskrit k-, Latin qu-, English wh- — and NO arrow
between the arms. Beside it, two arrows on one card: गच्छति → away, आगच्छति → here.

**Three predictable questions.** *Other prefixes?* (A Hindi speaker will reach for आगमन.)
Yes, a real and big system — later volumes' business; one prefix today, honestly
labelled. *Is कदा ~ when a pair too?* Honor the instinct, hold the line: they share the
stem, and the shelf pairs only the two rows it can defend — "the evidence stops here" is
part of the method. *So ह्यः आगच्छामि?* Expect it the moment श्वः आगच्छामि works: the
present can lean forward with a time word, never back; the past is coming, later.

**Differentiation:** Hindi speakers own the meanings at once (कब, अब, क्यों, क्योंकि,
आता है) — their work is the अर्थ trap (§12 mistake 1), the schwa-clipped "kimarth",
and the कल ambiguity: Sanskrit splits it into श्वः and ह्यः, and only one of them works
today. English speakers need the dental द/न/त, the puffed थ with no English *th*, and the long
gate of आगच्छति.

**What students find hard:** the kit blur under speed (slow the wheel, answer-match
before timing); the long आ gate (the contrast pair, out loud, until a partner can hear
which one you said); producing a *true* reason with a small vocabulary (model the
nearest-true move — यतः मम माता संस्कृतम् जानाति is a real reason); and accepting that
किमर्थम्'s insides stay closed today.

  inherited k.

  [pun̪ɐh milɑːmɐh]!
