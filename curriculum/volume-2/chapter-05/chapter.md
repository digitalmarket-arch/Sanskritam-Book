# Chapter 5 — संवादः · Daily Conversation

Four chapters built the parts: greetings, family and world, numbers and time, and the
present-tense verb in all three persons. This chapter puts them to work in the
conversations a learner actually needs. It completes the question kit with कदा "when?"
and किमर्थम् "why?", nine askers that share one ancestor with English *who, what, when*.
It hands over the classroom-survival phrases that keep any Sanskrit conversation alive
(अहम् न अवगच्छामि। भवान् पुनः वदतु।), the language of wanting and liking, and a market
role-play with bare-number prices. Every chunk is labelled honestly. The 1.x course
printed मह्यम् रोचते as "I like" with no grammar, and this chapter explains it: literally
"it pleases me", so the liked thing does the pleasing. The 1.x list of bare adjectives is
deferred, because adjective agreement is Volume 3's subject, and the chapter says so.

## Goals

- The full question kit (nine askers) with answer frames, and the *kʷ- cognate shelf
- Classroom-survival chunks: not understanding, asking again, asking for slower speech
- Wanting (इच्छति) and liking (मह्यम् / तुभ्यम् रोचते), each built honestly from its chunk
- A market conversation with bare-number prices and courtesy throughout
- An unscripted conversation that uses all of Volume 2 so far

## Can-do statements

By the end of this chapter the learner can:

- [ ] Ask and answer when/why questions (कदा? किमर्थम्? — यतः …)
- [ ] Keep a conversation going when lost: न अवगच्छामि · पुनः वदतु · मन्दम् वदतु
- [ ] Say what they want and what they like, and ask others
- [ ] Buy something at a market stall, asking the price and saying "enough"
- [ ] Read 20 ledger-new words (328 cumulative)
- [ ] Hold the role-card conversation and the unscripted round of the chapter exam
