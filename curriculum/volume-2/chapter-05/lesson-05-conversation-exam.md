## 1. Hook

In Chapter 1 you learned to greet someone. This chapter taught you to keep talking to
them: the last two askers (कदा "when?" and किमर्थम् "why?"), the four chunks that rescue
any Sanskrit conversation when you get lost, the honest build of liking (फलम् मह्यम्
रोचते: the fruit pleases *you*), and a market stall where the price comes back as a
bare number. Today none of it is recited. You draw a role card, a real person talks to
you, and you answer, and once, on purpose, they talk too fast. Twenty ledger-new words
this chapter, 328 in all, and the first exam whose centerpiece nobody can script.

### Station 1 — कदा? किमर्थम्? (lesson 1)

1. Recite the nine askers from memory, each with its gloss.
2. Answer truthfully, aloud: त्वम् कदा आगच्छसि? Then: किमर्थम् त्वम् संस्कृतम् पठसि?
3. आगच्छति: what does the आ do? And what exactly does the course claim about कः,
   Latin *quis* and English *who*?

<details><summary>उत्तराणि · Station 1 key</summary>

1. कः "who? (a man or boy)" · का "who? (a woman or girl)" · किम् "what?" · कुतः "from
   where?" · कथम् "how?" · कुत्र "where?" · कति "how many?" · कदा "when?" · किमर्थम्
   "why? (for what purpose)". → l01 §15.
2. E.g. अहम् इदानीम् आगच्छामि। (or अहम् श्वः आगच्छामि।) Then यतः … with your own reason
   in owned words, e.g. यतः संस्कृतम् मह्यम् रोचते। → l01 §15, §16.
3. आ + गच्छति: the prefix turns "goes" into "comes", and आगच्छामि, आगच्छसि follow the
   c04 pattern. The claim is that कः ~ *quis* ~ *who* continue one inherited
   interrogative stem: shared descent, no borrowing. → l01 §13, §15.
</details>

### Station 2 — अवगच्छामि (lesson 2)

1. The survival chain, in order, from memory: four steps.
2. वदतु looks like a relative of वदति. What kind of form is it, and which "you" does it
   go with?
3. Label two chunks: अहम् न अवगच्छामि, and कृपया.

<details><summary>उत्तराणि · Station 2 key</summary>

1. अहम् न अवगच्छामि। → भवान् पुनः वदतु। → कृपया मन्दम् वदतु। → अस्तु! → l02 §15.
2. A command-mood (imperative) chunk, "may (you) say", so "please say", learned whole
   and used with the polite भवान्. The present you-form is वदसि. The mood itself is
   Volume 3/4 business. → l02 §15.
3. अहम् न अवगच्छामि: the form is classical and the classroom use is modern-common, so
   both things are true. कृपया as the everyday "please": a modern convention shared
   with Hindi, labeled since Chapter 1. → l02 §14; v2.c01.l04 §14.
</details>

### Station 3 — इच्छामि (lesson 3)

1. किम् इच्छसि? Answer truthfully with an object from the ledger.
2. Say "I like the fruit". Then give its literal reading.
3. Which Hindi construction builds liking the same way?

<details><summary>उत्तराणि · Station 3 key</summary>

1. E.g. अहम् जलम् इच्छामि। · अहम् फलम् इच्छामि। The c04 pattern: इच्छामि इच्छसि
   इच्छति. → l03 §15.
2. फलम् मह्यम् रोचते। Literally "the fruit pleases me": the liked thing is the subject,
   and मह्यम् "to me" names the liker. → l03 §15.
3. मुझे … पसंद है (मुझे फल पसंद है). The liker stands in a "to me" form and the liked
   thing is the subject. → l03 §15.
</details>

### Station 4 — आपणः (lesson 4)

1. Point at a price-tag card and ask its price. Then answer it the course's way.

3. Say and gloss the three quantity answers, and say why this chapter teaches no

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — one paragraph, as in every review lesson.

Last chapter's exam closed the Volume 2 chapters 1–4 arc: courtesy, the world, numbers
and time, the verb. This chapter used all of it at once, and it was also a repair. The
1.x course had a Chapter 5 "Conversation" unit (question words, responses, shopping and
travel). Its words were largely right, but it never explained the grammar, gave its
chunks no usage labels, and printed a list of bare adjective stems with nothing said
about agreement. The 2.0 chapter kept the useful core. It explained the construction
that most needed it (मह्यम् रोचते, literally "it pleases me"), labeled every chunk
classical or modern, and deferred the adjectives to Volume 3 with the reason stated.
The exam is held to the same standard: it tests only the chunks the chapter delivered.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The Sanskrit k-question words (कः, किम्, कदा, कुत्र, कथम्, कति),
> the English wh-words (*who, what, when, where*) and the Latin qu-words (*quis, quid,
> quando*) continue one Proto-Indo-European interrogative stem, reconstructed
> \*kʷo- and \*kʷi-. They share a descent, and neither side borrowed from the other.
> The course pairs individual words only on its two safe rows: कः ~ *quis* ~ *who* and
> किम् ~ *quid* ~ *what*. [REF:mallory2006]; [REF:burrow2001]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spaced practice produces far more durable learning than the
> same practice massed together. [REF:cepeda2006]

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why task C is improvised, and why task E has no script at all.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Retrieval practice strengthens memory more than re-reading —
> the testing effect. [REF:roediger2006]

A recited dialogue tests memory for a text. A conversation tests whether the chunks
arrive when another person needs them. This chapter drilled functional chunks first and
analyzed them later [REF:nation2013]: कियत् मूल्यम्?, अहम् न अवगच्छामि, मह्यम् रोचते.
The exam asks for them the way life will, out of order and prompted by someone else's
question. That is why task C runs from role cards rather than a script, and why the
examiner speeds up once on purpose: the rescue chain is only owned once it works under
pressure.

Task A trains the listening move underneath every conversation: catch the asker first,
because the asker announces the kind of answer wanted (कदा a time, किमर्थम् a reason,
कियत् a price). Task D checks the boundary knowledge. English builds liking from the
liker (*I like*). Sanskrit and Hindi build it from the liked thing (फलम् मह्यम् रोचते ·
मुझे फल पसंद है). That is a difference in how languages are built, not a quirk. And the
k-askers are inherited siblings of *who* and *quis*. Hindi's कौन, क्या, कब, कहाँ carry
the same inherited k- stem [REF:masica1991].

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the exam's four frames, fully readable:

> **त्वम् कदा आगच्छसि? — अहम् इदानीम् आगच्छामि।**
> (tvam kadā āgacchasi? — aham idānīm āgacchāmi.) "When are you coming? — I am coming
> now." — तुम कब आ रहे हो? — मैं अभी आ रहा हूँ।

> **किमर्थम् त्वम् संस्कृतम् पठसि? — यतः संस्कृतम् मह्यम् रोचते।**
> (kimartham tvam saṃskṛtam paṭhasi? — yataḥ saṃskṛtam mahyam rocate.) "Why do you study
> Sanskrit? — Because Sanskrit pleases me." — तुम संस्कृत क्यों पढ़ते हो? — क्योंकि मुझे
> संस्कृत पसंद है।

> **कियत् मूल्यम्? — दश। — बहु! — अष्ट। — अस्तु। कृपया फलम् ददातु।**
> (kiyat mūlyam? — daśa. — bahu! — aṣṭa. — astu. kṛpayā phalam dadātu.) "How much is
> it? — Ten. — That's a lot! — Eight. — All right. Please give (me) the fruit." — कितने
> का है? — दस। — बहुत है! — आठ। — ठीक है। कृपया फल दीजिए।

> **अहम् न अवगच्छामि। भवान् पुनः वदतु। कृपया मन्दम् वदतु। … अस्तु!**
> (aham na avagacchāmi. bhavān punaḥ vadatu. kṛpayā mandam vadatu. … astu!) "I don't
> understand. Please say it again. Please speak slowly. … All right!" — मैं नहीं समझा।
> आप फिर से कहिए। कृपया धीरे बोलिए। … ठीक है!

> **"I like sweet mangoes"?** Not yet. "Sweet" is a describing word, and those agree
> with their nouns in gender, number and case. "Mangoes" is a plural. Both wait for
> Volume 3. The honest frame uses what you own: **फलम् मह्यम् रोचते।** (phalam mahyam
> rocate.) "I like the fruit (the fruit pleases me)." — मुझे फल पसंद है।

The exam's grammar fits in one line: the asker names the kind of answer, and the chunk
carries the grammar. Everything behind the chunks is named, deferred, and not tested:
the "to me" form of मह्यम्, the mood of वदतु, the ending family of रोचते, and the prefix
system behind आगच्छति.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

परीक्षा की विधि अब जानी-पहचानी है; आज की मुख्य बातें तीन हैं।

पहली — **कार्य C ही इस अध्याय की असली परीक्षा है।** पिछले पाठ के गृहकार्य में आपने दो

निभाएँगे; दूसरा परीक्षक स्वयं निभाएगा, और बाज़ार वाले हिस्से में वही दुकानदार बनेगा।

बहुत तेज़ बोलेगा। उस क्षण चुप हो जाना ग़लती है। सही चाल है: अहम् न अवगच्छामि। भवान् पुनः
वदतु।

दूसरी — **"मह्यम् रोचते" का सच।** अंग्रेज़ी में पसंद करने वाला कर्ता होता है: I like
fruit। संस्कृत में बनावट उलटी है: फलम् मह्यम् रोचते, यानी "फल मुझे भाता है"। जो चीज़ पसंद
है, वही वाक्य का कर्ता है, और पसंद करने वाला "मुझे" वाले रूप (मह्यम्) में खड़ा है। हिन्दी
बोलने वाले इसे तुरन्त पहचान लेंगे, क्योंकि "मुझे फल पसंद है" ठीक इसी ढंग से बनता है।
इसलिए "अहम् फलम् रोचते" ग़लत है, और यही इस अध्याय की सबसे आम भूल है।

खींचिए।

## 8. English Explanation

The assessment has five tasks. Two are recorded, and one has no script at all.

| Task | Skill | Format | Pass line |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Listening | 12 heard questions: name the asker and the kind of answer it wants | 10/12 |
| B | Writing | 6-chunk dictation + one full exchange | 5/6 + exchange intact |
| C | THE ROLE-CARD CONVERSATION | seven moves, improvised from your two cards, one recorded take | all seven moves; bare price; the rush rescued |
| D | Honesty sort | 9 claim cards, FACT or reject, one reasoning line each | 7/9 with reasoning |
| E | THE UNSCRIPTED ROUND | six examiner questions, six different askers, owned answers | 5/6 answers fitting their askers |

The §19 quiz (10 questions) completes it. Pass = every task at partial-or-better
(rubric §22) and quiz 7+/10.

Task C is the centerpiece. Your two role cards, filled in for l04's homework, become the
two people in the conversation. You play one and the examiner plays the other, then
runs the stall. Seven moves must happen, in order, and only your cards are prepared, not
your sentences. The examiner rushes one line on purpose, and the move that scores is the
rescue: अहम् न अवगच्छामि। भवान् पुनः वदतु। Freezing is the only failing answer. The
price must come back as a bare number, and liking must be built the Sanskrit way, from
the liked thing.

Task E strips the cards away: six questions, each from a different asker, none seen in
advance. The skill is the one task A trains. Catch the asker, know what kind of answer
it wants, and give the simplest true answer you own.

This closes Volume 2's first five chapters: courtesy, the world, numbers and time,
verbs, and now conversation. Passing gates v2.c06 (कथाः शब्दयात्राश्च — Stories & Word
Journeys), which is being written next.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — exam-day checks; reference values live in the lesson tables. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c05l05-listening-test.mp3`,
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c05l05-dictation-set.mp3`.

**The asker does the asking.** Every question in this exam carries an asker (कदा,
किमर्थम्, कियत्, कुत्र), and the word itself makes the question, as कथम् did in
Chapter 1. Say the asker clearly and in full, because it is the word your partner's ear
is listening for.

**The welded च्छ.** आगच्छति, अवगच्छामि, इच्छामि: one welded conjunct with the aspirated
half released. A single plain च is a different word shape.

**The y-clusters.** मह्यम् and तुभ्यम् glide straight into य with no seam vowel. A
*mahiyam* creeping in is the flagged habit. मूल्यम् and पर्याप्तम् work the same way.

**रोचते's pure vowels.** रो is a pure long ओ and the final ते a pure long ए. Neither is
English "go" or "hey".

**The imperative tails.** वदतु, अस्तु, ददातु end on a dental त with a short, clean उ:
tongue on the teeth.

**Pause form at every seam.** कियत् मूल्यम्, पुनः वदतु, यतः अहम्: finish each word before
the next begins. Joined forms are Volume 3's sandhi, and an open seam is never wrong.
Hindi speakers: keep every final म् and every visarga sounded (मूल्यम्, not *mūly*).

**Warm-up before recording:** the nine askers once at pace; the survival chain twice
(slow, then fast); one price exchange.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the exam circuit, one check per task.

- **Task A (ears):** the question is always the same: which asker, and what does it
  want? The asker usually comes first, so catch the opening word and the rest of the
  sentence confirms it.
- **Task B (pen):** every sound gets its letter: च्छ welded, ह्य and भ्य with no vowel
  between, the र्थ of किमर्थम्, the visarga of पुनः and यतः, every final म्.
- **Task C (mouth):** plan the seven moves before the take, not the sentences. Know
  which chunk opens each move (नमस्ते · मम नाम · कदा/किमर्थम् · इच्छामि/रोचते · कियत् ·
  न अवगच्छामि · धन्यवादः), then let the conversation fill the rest.
- **Task D (head):** say the rule aloud first ("the liked thing is the subject"), then
  judge the card against it. Deciding the verdict first is how an overreach gets past.
- **Task E (ears then mouth):** hear the asker, name its answer-kind silently (time,
  place, reason…), then answer. A beat of silence is fine. Silence that lasts is not:
  use the chain.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — two worked model answers before you start.

**Model role-card conversation (task C).** The learner draws the card **अर्जुनः**
(wants फलम्, likes संस्कृतम्). The examiner plays the second card, **रामः** (wants जलम्,
likes पुस्तकम्), then steps behind the stall.

— नमस्ते! अपि कुशलम्?
(namaste! api kuśalam?) "Hello! All well?" — नमस्ते! सब कुशल है?

— कुशलम्! सर्वम् कुशलम्। मम नाम अर्जुनः। अहम् छात्रः अस्मि।
(kuśalam! sarvam kuśalam. mama nāma arjunaḥ. aham chātraḥ asmi.) "Well! All is well.
My name is Arjuna. I am a student." — कुशल है! सब कुशल है। मेरा नाम अर्जुन है। मैं छात्र
हूँ।

— मम नाम रामः। एषः कः?
(mama nāma rāmaḥ. eṣaḥ kaḥ?) "My name is Rāma. Who is this?" — मेरा नाम राम है। यह
कौन है?

— एषः मम भ्राता।
(eṣaḥ mama bhrātā.) "This is my brother." (pointing at a photo) — यह मेरा भाई है।

— त्वम् कदा पठसि?
(tvam kadā paṭhasi?) "When do you study?" — तुम कब पढ़ते हो?

— अहम् सायम् पठामि। अहम् संस्कृतम् पठामि।
(aham sāyam paṭhāmi. aham saṃskṛtam paṭhāmi.) "I study in the evening. I study
Sanskrit." — मैं शाम को पढ़ता हूँ। मैं संस्कृत पढ़ता हूँ।

— किमर्थम्?
(kimartham?) "Why?" — क्यों?

— यतः संस्कृतम् मह्यम् रोचते! त्वम् कदा पठसि?
(yataḥ saṃskṛtam mahyam rocate! tvam kadā paṭhasi?) "Because Sanskrit pleases me! When
do you study?" — क्योंकि मुझे संस्कृत पसंद है! तुम कब पढ़ते हो?

— अहम् इदानीम् पठामि। पुस्तकम् मह्यम् रोचते। किम् इच्छसि?
(aham idānīm paṭhāmi. pustakam mahyam rocate. kim icchasi?) "I study right now. I like
the book. What do you want?" — मैं अभी पढ़ता हूँ। मुझे किताब पसंद है। तुम क्या चाहते
हो?

— अहम् फलम् इच्छामि। त्वम् किम् इच्छसि?
(aham phalam icchāmi. tvam kim icchasi?) "I want fruit. What do you want?" — मैं फल
चाहता हूँ। तुम क्या चाहते हो?

— अहम् जलम् इच्छामि। (The examiner steps behind the stall.)
(aham jalam icchāmi.) "I want water." — मैं पानी चाहता हूँ।

— कियत् मूल्यम्? — दश। — बहु! — अष्ट। — अस्तु। कृपया फलम् ददातु।
(kiyat mūlyam? — daśa. — bahu! — aṣṭa. — astu. kṛpayā phalam dadātu.) "How much? — Ten.
— That's a lot! — Eight. — All right. Please give (me) the fruit." — कितने का है? — दस।
— बहुत है! — आठ। — ठीक है। कृपया फल दीजिए।

— (rushed, the examiner's one deliberate rush) क्षीरम् अपि इच्छसि?

— अहम् न अवगच्छामि। भवान् पुनः वदतु। कृपया मन्दम् वदतु।
(aham na avagacchāmi. bhavān punaḥ vadatu. kṛpayā mandam vadatu.) "I don't understand.
Please say it again. Please speak slowly." — मैं नहीं समझा। आप फिर से कहिए। कृपया धीरे
बोलिए।

— क्षीरम् … अपि … इच्छसि?
(kṣīram … api … icchasi?) "Milk … too … do you want?" — दूध … भी … चाहिए?

— अस्तु! अल्पम्। … पर्याप्तम्। धन्यवादः!
(astu! alpam. … paryāptam. dhanyavādaḥ!) "Got it! A little. … Enough. Thank you!" —
ठीक है! थोड़ा-सा। … बस, काफ़ी है। धन्यवाद!

— स्वस्ति! — स्वस्ति!
(svasti! — svasti!) "Farewell! — Farewell!" — कल्याण हो! — कल्याण हो!

All seven moves happened, and every line is present singular in pause form. The price
came back bare, liking came from the liked thing, and the rush was rescued in two
chunks. Nothing in it was memorized as a text.

**Model unscripted round (task E).** Six askers, six answers, each of the simplest true
kind:

1. तव नाम किम्? — मम नाम अर्जुनः। (किम् wants a thing, here a name.)
2. तव भ्राता कः? — एषः मम भ्राता। (कः wants a person, so point.)
3. तव गृहम् कुत्र अस्ति? — मम गृहम् तत्र अस्ति। (कुत्र wants a place.)
4. त्वम् कदा आगच्छसि? — अहम् श्वः आगच्छामि। (कदा wants a time.)
5. किमर्थम् त्वम् संस्कृतम् पठसि? — यतः अहम् छात्रः अस्मि। (किमर्थम् wants a reason.)
6. (A price tag reading ७) कियत् मूल्यम्? — सप्त। (कियत् wants a price, bare.)

Had question 3 come too fast, अहम् न अवगच्छामि। भवान् पुनः वदतु। and then the answer
would have scored in full. The chain is part of the answer, not an escape from it.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — the chapter's rules restated on one page.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** **The askers:** कः का किम् कुतः कथम् कुत्र कति कदा किमर्थम्, nine
> question words, each naming the kind of answer it wants, plus कियत् "how much?" for
> amounts and prices. किमर्थम् "why? (for what purpose)" is taught whole, and its answer
> opens with यतः "because". **The prefix line:** आगच्छति "comes" = आ + गच्छति,
> conjugated by the c04 pattern (आगच्छामि, आगच्छसि); prefixes as a system are later
> business. **The imperative chunks:** वदतु "please say", ददातु "please give" and अस्तु
> "so be it, all right" are command-mood forms learned whole. वदतु and ददातु are used
> with the polite भवान्, and the mood itself is Volume 3/4's. **The liking build:**
> फलम् मह्यम् रोचते "I like the fruit" is literally "the fruit pleases me". The liked
> thing is the subject, the liker stands in a "to/for" form (मह्यम् "to me", तुभ्यम्
> "to you"), and रोचते carries a different ending family. The cases and that ending
> family are Volume 3/4's. **The stall:** prices are answered with bare numbers
> (कियत् मूल्यम्? — दश।), and बहु, अल्पम्, पर्याप्तम् are fixed answers. **The fences:**
> present singular, pause form, and no describing words yet (they agree in gender,
> number and case — Volume 3). [REF:whitney1889]; [REF:apte1890]

(phalam mahyam rocate. parīkṣā mahyam na rocate!) "I like fruit. The exam does not
please me!" — मुझे फल पसंद है। परीक्षा मुझे पसंद नहीं!

— शुभम्! स्वस्ति!
(śubham! svasti!) "All the best! Farewell!" — शुभ हो! कल्याण हो!

Housekeeping: परीक्षा remains a display word (its formal ledger entry comes with the
school set, per v2.c03.l05 §16). न stands before what it negates, Chapter 1's placement
rule, so it is मह्यम् न रोचते. And न अवगच्छामि does social work here as well as
classroom work: it is the honest "I don't get it" of two friends.

## 17. Story

कथा — production, not consumption: **the role-card dress rehearsal.** Take the two
cards you filled in for l04's homework and run the conversation twice. First run: card
one in your hand, card two in your partner's (or voice both people yourself, aloud).
Second run: the cards swapped. Take the first run slowly with every seam open, and the
second at natural pace.

Then retell what happened in three to five he-form or she-form sentences. This step
points ahead to Chapter 6's stories. Worked retelling, for comparison after your
attempt: **अर्जुनः फलम् इच्छति। सः वदति — कियत् मूल्यम्? रामः वदति — दश। अर्जुनः न
अवगच्छति। सः वदति — भवान् पुनः वदतु। अर्जुनः हसति।**

You will notice one gap while retelling. "Arjuna likes fruit" will not go into the
he-form yet. You own two liking chunks, फलम् मह्यम् रोचते and फलम् तुभ्यम् रोचते. "To
him" is another form of the same family, not yet delivered. Keep liking in the I- and
you-chunks, and retell wanting instead: सः फलम् इच्छति। (Retrieval plus recombination
is the strongest rehearsal pair there is [REF:roediger2006].)

## 18. Exercises

अभ्यासाः — the five exam tasks. A and B score against §19's key; C and E against the
rubric (§22); D against the key with reasoning.

**कार्यम् A · श्रवणपरीक्षा (listening — asker and answer-kind, 12 items).** The audio
plays twelve questions, each twice, at natural pace. For each, write two things: the
asker you heard (कदा, किमर्थम्, …) and the kind of answer it wants, from this list:
person · thing · origin · manner · place · number · time · reason · price. You do not
answer the questions; you name what they ask for.

**कार्यम् B · श्रुतलेखनम् (dictation — production).** Six chunks spanning the four
lessons, each read twice in pause form, then one full exchange (question and answer),
twice. Weld every conjunct (च्छ, र्थ, ह्य, भ्य, र्या), keep every visarga and final
म्, and write each word whole with the seam open. 5/6 + exchange intact.

**कार्यम् C · संवादः (THE ROLE-CARD CONVERSATION — recorded, production).** The
centerpiece. Bring the two role cards you filled in for l04's homework (v2.c05.l04 §21).
Each says who the person is, what they want and what they like. Shuffle them face-down.
The examiner draws one for you to play and takes the other, and at the market moment
runs the stall with a price-tag card. Record one continuous take of seven moves, in
this order:

1. **Greet:** नमस्ते! or सुप्रभातम्! · अपि कुशलम्? — कुशलम्।
2. **Introduce:** your card's name (मम नाम …।), then one family line with the pointing
   frame (एषः मम भ्राता। · एषा मम माता।) at a photo or a sketch. A line about yourself
   (अहम् छात्रः अस्मि।) is welcome.
3. **When and why:** answer one कदा question and one किमर्थम् question (the reason
   opens with यतः), and ask one of them back.
4. **Want and like:** say what your card wants (अहम् … इच्छामि।) and likes (… मह्यम्
   रोचते।), and ask your partner one: किम् इच्छसि? or किम् तुभ्यम् रोचते?
5. **Buy at the market:** ask the price (कियत् मूल्यम्?), take the bare-number answer,
   react if you like (बहु!), accept (अस्तु।), ask for the item (कृपया … ददातु।), and
   close with पर्याप्तम्।
6. **Keep it alive once:** at some point the examiner rushes one line on purpose.
   Rescue it with अहम् न अवगच्छामि। भवान् पुनः वदतु। (and, if needed, कृपया मन्दम्
   वदतु।), then carry on: अस्तु!
7. **Farewell:** धन्यवादः। · स्वस्ति! (any Chapter 1 farewell counts).

No script and no notes in hand: the cards say *who*, not *what to say*. Keep to pause
form, the present singular and bare-number prices. Simplest correct beats ambitious
wrong.

**कार्यम् D · श्रेणीविभागः (honesty sort — 9 cards).** Mark each card FACT or reject,
with one reasoning line:

1. "मह्यम् रोचते literally means 'I like'."
2. "कः, Latin *quis* and English *who* continue one ancestral stem."
3. "Sanskrit borrowed its question words from Latin."
4. "अहम् न अवगच्छामि is classical grammar in a modern classroom use."
5. "वदतु is the you-form of वदति."
6. "आगच्छति 'comes' is गच्छति 'goes' with the prefix आ in front."
7. "At the stall, a price is answered with a bare number: कियत् मूल्यम्? — दश।"
8. "कृपया as the everyday word for 'please' is a classical usage."
9. "Hindi मुझे … पसंद है builds liking the way मह्यम् रोचते does: the liker in a 'to
   me' form, the liked thing as the subject."

**कार्यम् E · प्रश्नोत्तरम् (THE UNSCRIPTED ROUND — recorded, production).** The
examiner shuffles the asker deck, sets कुतः aside (a listening-only asker today, since
its fuller answers need machinery not yet delivered), and draws six cards. You hear six
questions, each with a different asker, none seen in advance. Answer each in owned
language: present singular, pause form, and a bare number where a number is asked. If
you lose a question, one survival chunk is legal (अहम् न अवगच्छामि। भवान् पुनः
वदतु।), and then you answer the repeat. A reason you own beats a reason you cannot
build: यतः अहम् छात्रः अस्मि। is a full-credit answer. Finish with the maintenance
micro-check: recite the nine askers in order and the survival chain once.

## 22. Teacher Notes

अध्यापकटिप्पणी

**Format:** stations (§3, 10 min) → exam block (§18, 30 min) → debrief (5 min). In the
exam block, tasks C and E are recorded one learner at a time, about five minutes each,
in a quiet corner or staggered turns. The rest of the room works A, B and D. D runs well
in pairs who argue before writing.

**Rubric:**

| Task | Full | Partial | Fail signal → remediation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (12) | 10–12 | 8–9 | कुत्र/कुतः or कति/कियत् swaps → l01 §15 asker table re-heard with the §10 minimal pairs; blank answer-kinds → §15 table recited, asker → kind |
| B (6+1) | 5–6 + exchange intact | 4, or exchange with one slip | seam vowel in ह्य/भ्य, च्छ split → l03 §14 and l01 §14 rows re-copied; dropped visarga or final म् → §9 pause-form check |
| C (7 moves) | all seven moves unprompted; bare price; the rush rescued; ≤2 slips | one move prompted or missing, or 3–4 slips | liking built from अहम् → l03 §15; price dressed up → l04 §15; froze at the rush → l02 §15 chain drilled at speed; a move skipped → the §18 move list as a cue card, re-run tomorrow |
| D (9) | 7–9 reasoned | 5–6 | cards 1, 9 → l03 §15; cards 2–3 → l01 §13; cards 4, 8 → l02 §14 labels; card 5 → l02 §15; cards 6–7 → l01 §15, l04 §15 |
| E (6) | six answers, each fitting its asker | 4–5 | asker misheard → l01 §15 and a task A re-run; plurals or describing words → §15 fences; silence → l02 §15, the chain is legal |

Pass = every task at partial-or-better and quiz 7+/10. A task below partial re-runs
tomorrow, after its remediation.

**Run task C as a conversation, not an interrogation.** Play the second card warmly,
as a person with their own wants. Rush exactly one line, at normal volume but at speed,
and then slow down generously when asked. The learner who says अहम् न अवगच्छामि at that
moment has done the chapter's most important thing, so praise it on the recording. Do
not prompt the seven moves unless the learner stalls for several seconds; a prompted
move scores partial, not zero.

**The honesty cards do the chapter's deepest work.** Cards 1, 3, 5 and 8 each reject a
tempting simplification: "I like" as the meaning, borrowing as the story, वदतु as a
person form, कृपया as ancient courtesy. Card 4 is the course's favorite kind of card,
where both things are true. Land each rejection the way the course always does: a claim
that is exactly true is worth more than a bigger one that is not.

**On gender and courtesy.** The chunk was learned with भवान्. With a woman examiner, the
learner may say भवती पुनः वदतु (display-only since Chapter 1). Accept it, never require
it, and never mark भवान् wrong in this exam.

**What students find hard:** building liking the Sanskrit way under pressure (have them
whisper "the fruit pleases me" before speaking); hearing कुत्र and कुतः apart at speed;
trusting that a rescue is a success, not an admission; and keeping prices bare when
English and Hindi both want a currency word. Redirect ambition gently. The learner who
reaches for "I like sweet mangoes" is not wrong to want it: describing words and plurals
are coming in Volume 3, and say so.
