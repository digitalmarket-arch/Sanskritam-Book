## 1. Hook

In Chapter 1 you learned to greet someone. This chapter taught you to keep talking: the
last two askers (कदा, किमर्थम्), the chain that rescues any conversation when you get
lost, the honest build of liking (फलम् मह्यम् रोचते: the fruit pleases *you*), and a
market stall where the price comes back as a bare number. Today none of it is recited.
You draw a role card, a real person talks to you, and once, on purpose, they talk too
fast. Twenty ledger-new words this chapter, 328 in all.

### Station 1 — कदा? किमर्थम्? (lesson 1)

1. Recite the nine askers in order, each with its gloss.
2. Answer truthfully, aloud: त्वम् कदा आगच्छसि? Then: किमर्थम् त्वम् संस्कृतम् पठसि?
3. What does the आ do in आगच्छति? And what exactly does the course claim about कः,
   Latin *quis* and English *who*?

<details><summary>उत्तराणि · Station 1 key</summary>

1. कः "who? (m.)" · का "who? (f.)" · किम् "what?" · कुतः "from where?" · कथम् "how?" ·
   कुत्र "where?" · कति "how many?" · कदा "when?" · किमर्थम् "why? (for what purpose)".
   → l01 §15.
2. E.g. अहम् इदानीम् आगच्छामि। (or bare: श्वः।) Then यतः … with a reason in owned words,
   e.g. यतः अहम् छात्रः अस्मि। → l01 §15.
3. The prefix turns "goes" into "comes"; आगच्छामि, आगच्छसि follow the c04 pattern. The
   claim: कः ~ *quis* ~ *who* continue one inherited stem. They share a descent, and
   nobody borrowed. → l01 §13, §15.
</details>

### Station 2 — अवगच्छामि (lesson 2)

1. Run the survival chain from memory, and say who answers अस्तु.
2. What kind of form is वदतु, and which "you" does it go with?
3. Label two chunks: अहम् न अवगच्छामि, and कृपया.

<details><summary>उत्तराणि · Station 2 key</summary>

1. अहम् न अवगच्छामि। → भवान् पुनः वदतु। → कृपया मन्दम् वदतु। The speaker grants the
   request with अस्तु! and repeats; you close with आम्! अवगच्छामि। धन्यवादः। → l02 §8.
2. A request-mood (imperative) chunk, learned whole and used with the polite भवान्. It
   is NOT the you-form of वदति, which is वदसि. → l02 §15.
3. अहम् न अवगच्छामि: classical form, modern-common classroom use (both things true).
   कृपया: the form is classical ("with compassion"); its "please" use is modern spoken
   convention. → l02 §15.
</details>

### Station 3 — इच्छामि (lesson 3)

1. किम् इच्छसि? Answer truthfully with an object from the ledger.
2. Say "I like the fruit". Then give its literal reading.
3. Which Hindi construction builds liking the same way, and which Hindi build must stay
   out of the wanting frame?

<details><summary>उत्तराणि · Station 3 key</summary>

1. E.g. अहम् जलम् इच्छामि। The c04 pattern: इच्छामि इच्छसि इच्छति. → l03 §15.
2. फलम् मह्यम् रोचते। Literally "the fruit pleases me": the liked thing is the subject,
   and मह्यम् "to me" names the liker. → l03 §15.
3. मुझे फल पसंद है builds liking the same way. मुझे पानी चाहिए must not leak into
   wanting, which is अहम् जलम् इच्छामि। → l03 §5, §12.
</details>

### Station 4 — आपणः (lesson 4)

1. Point at a price-tag card, ask its price, and answer it the course's way.
2. Ask for the fruit politely with ददातु, then say it again to a woman at the stall.
3. Say and gloss the three quantity answers, and say why they stand alone.

<details><summary>उत्तराणि · Station 4 key</summary>

1. कियत् मूल्यम्? — दश। A bare number, the c03 policy. रूप्यकम् stays display-only.
   → l04 §15.
2. भवान् फलम् ददातु। To a woman, with no pronoun: कृपया फलम् ददातु। (भवती is display
   only.) → l04 §8.
3. बहु "much, a lot" · अल्पम् "a little" · पर्याप्तम् "enough". Describing words agree
   with their nouns in gender, number and case, so these stay one-word answers until
   Volume 3. → l04 §15.
</details>

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — one paragraph, as in every review lesson.

Last chapter's exam closed the arc of courtesy, the world, numbers and time, and the
verb. This chapter spent all of it at once, and it was also a repair. The 1.x course
had a "Conversation" unit (question words, responses, shopping), with mostly correct
words but no grammar explained, no usage labels, and a list of bare adjective stems with
nothing said about agreement. The 2.0 chapter kept the useful core, explained मह्यम्
रोचते ("it pleases me"), labeled every chunk, and deferred the adjectives to Volume 3
with the reason stated. The exam tests only what the chapter delivered.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The Sanskrit k-question words (कः, किम्, कदा, कुत्र, कथम्, कति),
> the English wh-words (*who, what, when, where*) and the Latin qu-words (*quis, quid,
> quando*) continue one Proto-Indo-European interrogative stem, reconstructed as \*kʷo-
> beside \*kʷi-. They share a descent; neither side borrowed. Word pairs are drawn only
> on the two safe rows: कः ~ *quis* ~ *who* and किम् ~ *quid* ~ *what*.
> [REF:mallory2006]; [REF:burrow2001]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spaced practice produces far more durable learning than the
> same practice massed together. [REF:cepeda2006]

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why task C is improvised, and why task E has no script at all.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Retrieval practice strengthens memory more than re-reading —
> the testing effect. [REF:roediger2006]

A recited dialogue tests memory for a text. A conversation tests whether the chunks
arrive when another person needs them. The chapter taught its chunks whole and analyzed
them later [REF:nation2013], so the exam asks for them the way life will: out of order,
prompted by someone else. That is why task C runs from role cards rather than a script,
and why the examiner rushes one line on purpose. A rescue chain is owned only once it
works under pressure.

Task A trains the move underneath every conversation: catch the asker first, because it
announces the kind of answer wanted. Task D checks the boundary knowledge. English seats
the person who likes as the subject (*I like*); Sanskrit and Hindi seat the liked thing
there (फलम् मह्यम् रोचते · मुझे फल पसंद है). And the k-askers share one inherited stem
with *who* and *quis*; Hindi's कौन, क्या, कब carry the same k- stem [REF:masica1991].

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the exam's four frames, fully readable:

> **त्वम् कदा आगच्छसि? — अहम् इदानीम् आगच्छामि।**
> (tvam kadā āgacchasi? — aham idānīm āgacchāmi.) "When are you coming? — I am coming
> now." — तुम कब आ रहे हो? — मैं अभी आ रहा हूँ।

> **किमर्थम् त्वम् संस्कृतम् पठसि? — यतः संस्कृतम् मह्यम् रोचते।**
> (kimartham tvam saṃskṛtam paṭhasi? — yataḥ saṃskṛtam mahyam rocate.) "Why do you study
> Sanskrit? — Because Sanskrit pleases me." — तुम संस्कृत क्यों पढ़ते हो? — क्योंकि मुझे
> संस्कृत पसंद है।

> **कियत् मूल्यम्? — दश। — बहु! अष्ट? — नव। — आम्, नव। भवान् फलम् ददातु।**
> (kiyat mūlyam? — daśa. — bahu! aṣṭa? — nava. — ām, nava. bhavān phalam dadātu.) "How
> much is it? — Ten. — That's a lot! Eight? — Nine. — Yes, nine. Please give (me) a
> fruit." — कितने का है? — दस। — बहुत है! आठ? — नौ। — हाँ, नौ। कृपया फल दीजिए।

> **अहम् न अवगच्छामि। भवान् पुनः वदतु। — अस्तु! … — आम्! अवगच्छामि।**
> (aham na avagacchāmi. bhavān punaḥ vadatu. — astu! … — ām! avagacchāmi.) "I don't
> understand. Please say it again. — All right! … — Yes! I understand." — मैं नहीं
> समझा। आप फिर से कहिए। — ठीक है! … — हाँ! समझ गया।

> **"I like sweet mangoes"?** Not yet. Describing words agree with their nouns in
> gender, number and case, and "mangoes" is a plural; both wait for Volume 3. The honest
> frame uses what you own: **फलम् मह्यम् रोचते।** (phalam mahyam rocate.) "I like the
> fruit." — मुझे फल पसंद है।

The exam's grammar fits in one line: the asker names the kind of answer, and the chunk
carries the grammar. What stands behind the chunks (the "to me" form, the request mood,
the second ending family, the prefix system) is named, deferred, and not tested.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

परीक्षा की विधि अब जानी-पहचानी है; आज की मुख्य बातें तीन हैं।

पहली — **कार्य C ही इस अध्याय की असली परीक्षा है।** पिछले पाठ के गृहकार्य में आपने दो
भूमिका-कार्ड भरे थे: एक अपने लिए, एक किसी पात्र के लिए। हर कार्ड पर तीन पंक्तियाँ हैं —
वह कौन है (नाम और परिवार की एक पंक्ति), क्या चाहता है, और उसे क्या पसंद है। परीक्षक
दोनों कार्ड उलटकर फेंटेगा। एक कार्ड की भूमिका आप निभाएँगे; दूसरी परीक्षक, जो बाज़ार
वाले हिस्से में दुकानदार भी बनेगा। सात चरण हैं: अभिवादन → परिचय → कब और क्यों → चाह और
पसंद → ख़रीदारी, दाम केवल संख्या में → एक बार बातचीत बचाना → विदाई। कोई लिखी हुई पटकथा
नहीं होगी। परीक्षक एक बार जान-बूझकर बहुत तेज़ बोलेगा। उस क्षण चुप हो जाना ही एकमात्र
ग़लती है; सही चाल है — अहम् न अवगच्छामि। भवान् पुनः वदतु।

दूसरी — **"मह्यम् रोचते" का सच।** अंग्रेज़ी में पसंद करने वाला कर्ता होता है: I like
fruit। संस्कृत में बनावट उलटी है: फलम् मह्यम् रोचते, यानी "फल मुझे भाता है"। जो चीज़ पसंद
है, वही कर्ता है, और पसंद करने वाला "मुझे" वाले रूप (मह्यम्) में बैठा है। "मुझे फल पसंद
है" ठीक इसी ढंग से बनता है। इसलिए "अहम् फलम् रोचामि" ग़लत है — यही अध्याय की सबसे आम भूल
है। और उलटी दिशा में भी सावधान: "मुझे पानी चाहिए" वाला ढाँचा चाहने पर मत लगाइए; वहाँ
सीधा अहम् जलम् इच्छामि ही सही है।

तीसरी — **कार्ड-छँटाई और बिना तैयारी का दौर।** नौ कथन हैं; हर कार्ड पर निर्णय (तथ्य या
अस्वीकार) और एक तर्क-पंक्ति। "वदतु, वदति का तुम-रूप है" — अस्वीकार: तुम-रूप वदसि है,
और वदतु अनुरोध का रूप है जो भवान् के साथ चलता है। "संस्कृत ने प्रश्नवाचक शब्द लैटिन से
उधार लिए" — अस्वीकार: कः, quis और who एक ही पूर्वज-मूल के वंशज हैं। कार्य E में परीक्षक
नौ प्रश्नवाचक शब्दों में से छह से प्रश्न पूछेगा। पहले वह शब्द पकड़िए — वही बताता है कि
उत्तर में समय चाहिए, कारण, स्थान या संख्या। सरल और सही उत्तर, महत्वाकांक्षी और ग़लत
उत्तर से हमेशा बेहतर है।

## 8. English Explanation

Five tasks: two recorded, one with no script at all.

| Task | Skill | Format | Pass line |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Listening | 12 heard questions: name the asker and the kind of answer it wants | 10/12 |
| B | Writing | 6-chunk dictation + one full exchange | 5/6 + exchange intact |
| C | THE ROLE-CARD CONVERSATION | seven moves, improvised from your two cards, one recorded take | all seven moves; bare price; the rush rescued |
| D | Honesty sort | 9 claim cards, FACT or reject, one reasoning line each | 7/9 with reasoning |
| E | THE UNSCRIPTED ROUND | six questions from six of the nine askers, owned answers | 5/6 answers fitting their askers |

The §19 quiz (10 questions) completes it. Pass = every task at partial-or-better
(rubric §22) and quiz 7+/10.

Task C is the centerpiece. The two cards you filled in for l04's homework become the two
people in the conversation: you play one, the examiner plays the other and then keeps
the stall. Seven moves must happen in order. Only the cards are prepared, never the
sentences. The examiner rushes one line, and the move that scores is the rescue; the
only failing answer is silence. The price comes back bare, and liking is built from the
liked thing.

Task E removes the cards: six questions, each from a different asker of the nine, none
seen in advance. Catch the asker, know what kind of answer it wants, and give the
simplest true answer you own. Passing gates v2.c06 (कथाः शब्दयात्राश्च — Stories & Word
Journeys), which is being written next.

**The asker does the asking.** कदा, किमर्थम्, कुत्र, कियत् make the question by
themselves, as कथम् did in Chapter 1. Say the asker clearly and in full: it is the word
your partner's ear is waiting for.

**Welds without seam vowels.** च्छ in आगच्छति, अवगच्छामि, इच्छामि; ह्य and भ्य in मह्यम्
and तुभ्यम् (a *mahiyam* is the flagged habit); ल्य and र्य in मूल्यम् and पर्याप्तम्.

**रोचते's pure vowels.** रो is a pure long ओ and ते a pure long ए, never English "go"
or "hey". The request tails वदतु, अस्तु, ददातु end on a dental त and a short, clean उ.

**Pause form at every seam.** कियत् मूल्यम्, पुनः वदतु, यतः अहम्: finish each word
before the next begins. Hindi speakers: sound every final म् and every visarga.

**Warm-up before recording:** the nine askers once at pace; the survival chain twice,
slow then fast; one price exchange from a tag.

The askers open on /k/: कदा /kɐd̪ɑː/ · कुत्र /kut̪ɾɐ/ · कुतः /kut̪ɐh/ · कति /kɐt̪i/ ·

or a breath /h/ after it) and कति from कियत् (the glide /j/). किमर्थम् /kimɐɾt̪ʰɐm/
puts an aspirated dental /t̪ʰ/ after the tap. The y-clusters glide with no seam vowel:
मह्यम् /mɐɦjɐm/ · तुभ्यम् /t̪ubʱjɐm/ · मूल्यम् /muːljɐm/ · पर्याप्तम् /pɐɾjɑːpt̪ɐm/.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the exam circuit, one check per task.

- **Task A (ears):** which asker, and what does it want? Catch the opening word; the
  rest of the sentence only confirms it.
- **Task B (pen):** every sound gets its letter: च्छ welded, ह्य and भ्य with no vowel
  between, the र्थ of किमर्थम्, the visarga of पुनः and यतः, every final म्.
- **Task C (mouth):** plan the seven moves, not the sentences. Know the chunk that opens
  each move (नमस्ते · मम नाम · कदा/किमर्थम् · इच्छामि/रोचते · कियत् · न अवगच्छामि ·
  धन्यवादः), then let the conversation fill in the rest.
- **Task D (head):** say the rule aloud first ("the liked thing is the subject"), then
  judge the card against it.
- **Task E (ears then mouth):** hear the asker, name its answer-kind silently, then
  answer. A beat of silence is fine; a long one is not. Use the chain.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — two worked model answers before you start.

**Model role-card conversation (task C).** The learner draws the card for **अर्जुनः**
(family line: एषः मम भ्राता। · wants फलम् · likes संस्कृतम्). The examiner plays the
character card **रामः**, then keeps the stall, where the price tag reads १०.

1. **Greet.** — नमस्ते! अपि कुशलम्? — कुशलम्! सर्वम् कुशलम्।
2. **Introduce.** — मम नाम रामः। तव नाम किम्? — मम नाम अर्जुनः। अहम् छात्रः अस्मि।
   एषः मम भ्राता। (pointing at a photo)
3. **When and why.** — त्वम् कदा पठसि? — अहम् सायम् पठामि। — किमर्थम्? — यतः संस्कृतम्
   मह्यम् रोचते! त्वम् कदा पठसि? — अहम् इदानीम् पठामि।
4. **Want and like.** — किम् इच्छसि? — अहम् फलम् इच्छामि। किम् तुभ्यम् रोचते? — पुस्तकम्
   मह्यम् रोचते।
5. **Buy.** — कियत् मूल्यम्? — दश। — बहु! अष्ट?
6. **Keep it alive.** (rushed) — न नव। — अहम् न अवगच्छामि। भवान् पुनः वदतु। — अस्तु!
   न … नव। — आम्, नव। भवान् फलम् ददातु। — क्षीरम् अपि इच्छसि? — अल्पम्। … पर्याप्तम्!
7. **Farewell.** — धन्यवादः! — भद्रम्। स्वस्ति! — स्वस्ति!

In English: "Hello! All well? — Well, all is well. — My name is Rāma; what's yours? —
I'm Arjuna, a student, and this is my brother. — When do you study? — In the evening. —
Why? — Because I like Sanskrit! When do you study? — Right now. — What do you want? —
Fruit. What do you like? — I like the book. — How much? — Ten. — That's a lot! Eight? —
No, nine (rushed). — I don't understand; please say it again. — All right! No … nine.
— Yes, nine; please give me a fruit. — Milk too? — A little … enough! Thank you! — My
pleasure. Farewell! — Farewell!"

All seven moves happened in the present singular and in pause form. The price came back
bare, liking came from the liked thing, the speaker granted the rescue with अस्तु, and
the stall moves follow l04 §8's play. The learner uses त्वम् for small talk and भवान्
for the requests; l02 §8 makes भवान् … वदतु right with anyone.

**Model unscripted round (task E).** Six of the nine askers, six of the simplest true
answers:

1. तव नाम किम्? — मम नाम अर्जुनः। (किम् wants a thing, here a name.)
2. तव भ्राता कः? — एषः मम भ्राता। (कः wants a person, so point.)
3. तव गृहम् कुत्र अस्ति? — मम गृहम् तत्र अस्ति। (कुत्र wants a place.)
4. त्वम् कदा आगच्छसि? — अहम् श्वः आगच्छामि। (कदा wants a time.)
5. किमर्थम् त्वम् संस्कृतम् पठसि? — यतः अहम् छात्रः अस्मि। (किमर्थम् wants a purpose.)
6. (five fruit cards) कति? — पञ्च। (कति wants a number, bare.)

Had question 3 come too fast, अहम् न अवगच्छामि। भवान् पुनः वदतु। and then the answer
would still have scored in full. The chain is part of the answer.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — the chapter's rules restated on one page.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** **The askers:** कः का किम् कुतः कथम् कुत्र कति कदा किमर्थम्,
> nine question words, each asking for its own kind of answer; an answer may stand bare.
> कियत् "how much?" asks an amount, and with मूल्यम् makes the price question. **The
> reason:** किमर्थम् is taught whole, and the answer opens with यतः "because". **The
> prefix:** आगच्छति = आ + गच्छति, built by the c04 pattern; prefixes as a system come
> later. **The request chunks:** वदतु, ददातु and अस्तु are imperatives (the request
> mood), learned whole; वदतु and ददातु go with the polite भवान्, and अस्तु is the
> speaker's "all right". **The liking build:** फलम् मह्यम् रोचते is literally "the fruit
> pleases me": the liked thing is the subject, the liker stands in a to/for form (मह्यम्,
> तुभ्यम्), and रोचते wears the second ending family, the middle voice. **The stall:**
> prices are bare numbers; बहु, अल्पम्, पर्याप्तम् are one-word answers. **The fences:**
> present singular, pause form, no describing words (they agree in gender, number and
> case — Volume 3). [REF:whitney1889]; [REF:apte1890]

| Asker | Asks for | An owned answer |
|---|---|---|
| कः · का | a person (m. · f.) | एषः मम भ्राता। · एषा मम माता। |
| किम् | a thing | अहम् फलम् इच्छामि। · मम नाम …। |
| कुतः | a source (from where?) | इतः। |
| कथम् | a manner | सर्वम् कुशलम्। |
| कुत्र | a place | मम गृहम् तत्र अस्ति। |
| कति | a number (a count) | पञ्च। |
| कदा | a time | इदानीम्। · अहम् श्वः आगच्छामि। |
| किमर्थम् | a purpose (a reason) | यतः … |
| कियत् (the stall's own asker, outside the nine) | an amount, a price | दश। |

| Chunk | Label |
|---|---|
| अहम् न अवगच्छामि। | classical form, modern-common classroom use |
| भवान् पुनः वदतु। · भवान् फलम् ददातु। · अस्तु! | classical request-mood chunks |
| कृपया · धन्यवादः | classical insides, modern spoken convention as "please" and "thank you" |
| रूप्यकम् | modern word for the currency; display only, never in the answer |

To a woman, drop the pronoun (कृपया पुनः वदतु। · कृपया क्षीरम् ददातु।), l04 §8's mirror
rule. भवती (bhavatī) is display-only: accepted when used, never required.

## 16. Conversation

सम्भाषणम् — the exam-morning scene; every word self-read. 🔊 in the listening-test
audio.

— सुप्रभातम्! त्वम् इदानीम् आगच्छसि?
(suprabhātam! tvam idānīm āgacchasi?) "Good morning! You're only arriving now?" —
सुप्रभात! तुम अभी आ रहे हो?

— आम्! अहम् इदानीम् आगच्छामि। परीक्षा अद्य अस्ति!
(ām! aham idānīm āgacchāmi. parīkṣā adya asti!) "Yes! I'm arriving now. The exam is
today!" — हाँ! मैं अभी आ रहा हूँ। परीक्षा आज है!

— (very fast, the words run together) किमर्थम् त्वम् हससि?

— अहम् न अवगच्छामि। भवान् पुनः वदतु।
(aham na avagacchāmi. bhavān punaḥ vadatu.) "I don't understand. Say it again,
please." — मैं नहीं समझा। फिर से कहिए।

— अस्तु! किमर्थम् … त्वम् … हससि?
(astu! kimartham … tvam … hasasi?) "All right! Why … are you … laughing?" — ठीक है!
तुम … क्यों … हँस रहे हो?

— यतः परीक्षा मह्यम् रोचते!
(yataḥ parīkṣā mahyam rocate!) "Because the exam pleases me!" — क्योंकि मुझे परीक्षा
पसंद है!

— सत्यम्? फलम् मह्यम् रोचते। परीक्षा मह्यम् न रोचते!
(satyam? phalam mahyam rocate. parīkṣā mahyam na rocate!) "Really? I like fruit. The
exam does not please me!" — सच? मुझे फल पसंद है। परीक्षा मुझे पसंद नहीं!

— शुभम्! स्वस्ति!
(śubham! svasti!) "All the best! Farewell!" — शुभ हो! कल्याण हो!

Housekeeping: परीक्षा remains a display word (its ledger entry comes with the school
set, per v2.c03.l05 §16). The rescue runs exactly as l02 §8 built it: the friend grants
it with अस्तु and repeats slowly, and भवान् … वदतु is right even between friends. न
stands just before the verb, so it is मह्यम् न रोचते (l03 §15).

## 18. Exercises

अभ्यासाः — the five exam tasks. A and B score against §19's key; C and E against the
rubric (§22); D against the key with reasoning.

**कार्यम् A · श्रवणपरीक्षा (listening — asker and answer-kind, 12 items).** The audio
plays twelve questions, each twice, at natural pace. For each, write the asker you heard
and the kind of answer it wants: person · thing · source · manner · place · number ·
time · purpose · amount. You name what each question asks for; you do not answer it.

**कार्यम् B · श्रुतलेखनम् (dictation — production).** Six chunks spanning the four
lessons, each twice in pause form, then one full exchange, twice. Weld every conjunct
(च्छ, र्थ, ह्य, भ्य, र्या), keep every visarga and final म्, and leave each seam open.
5/6 + exchange intact.

**कार्यम् C · संवादः (THE ROLE-CARD CONVERSATION — recorded, production).** The
centerpiece. Bring the two role cards from l04's homework (v2.c05.l04 §21): one for
yourself and one for a character, each with three lines (who: a name and one family
line; what they want; what they like). The examiner shuffles them face-down and deals
you one; read it once, then lay it face-down. The examiner plays the other card, then
keeps the stall with l04's price-tag cards. Record one continuous take of seven moves,
in order:

1. **Greet:** नमस्ते! or सुप्रभातम्! · अपि कुशलम्? — कुशलम्।
2. **Introduce:** the card's name (मम नाम …।) and its family line (एषः मम भ्राता। ·
   एषा मम माता।), pointing at a photo or a sketch.
3. **When and why:** answer one कदा question and one किमर्थम् question (the reason opens
   with यतः), and ask one of them back.
4. **Want and like:** say what the card wants (अहम् … इच्छामि।) and likes (… मह्यम्
   रोचते।), and ask your partner one of them (किम् इच्छसि? · किम् तुभ्यम् रोचते?).
5. **Buy at the market:** कियत् मूल्यम्?, a bare-number answer, a reaction (आम्, or बहु!
   with a bare counter-offer), the request (भवान् … ददातु।), and पर्याप्तम् to close.
6. **Keep it alive once:** the examiner rushes one line, anywhere in the take. Rescue it:
   अहम् न अवगच्छामि। भवान् पुनः वदतु। (and, if needed, कृपया मन्दम् वदतु।). The examiner
   answers अस्तु! and repeats; confirm (आम्! अवगच्छामि।) and carry on.
7. **Farewell:** धन्यवादः। · स्वस्ति! (any Chapter 1 farewell counts).

Keep to pause form, the present singular and bare-number prices. Simplest correct beats
ambitious wrong.

**कार्यम् D · श्रेणीविभागः (honesty sort — 9 cards).** FACT or reject, with one
reasoning line each:

1. "मह्यम् रोचते literally means 'I like'."
2. "कः, Latin *quis* and English *who* continue one ancestral stem."
3. "Sanskrit borrowed its question words from Latin."
4. "अहम् न अवगच्छामि is classical grammar in a modern classroom use."
5. "वदतु is the you-form of वदति."
6. "आगच्छति 'comes' is गच्छति 'goes' with the prefix आ in front."
7. "At the stall, a price is answered with a bare number: कियत् मूल्यम्? — दश।"
8. "कृपया, as the everyday word for 'please', is a classical usage."
9. "Hindi मुझे … पसंद है builds liking the way मह्यम् रोचते does: the liker in a 'to me'
   form, the liked thing as the subject."

**कार्यम् E · प्रश्नोत्तरम् (THE UNSCRIPTED ROUND — recorded, production).** The
examiner shuffles the nine-asker deck, draws six cards, and asks six questions, none
seen in advance. Answer each in owned language: present singular, pause form, a bare
number where a number is asked. One survival chunk per question is legal (अहम् न
अवगच्छामि। भवान् पुनः वदतु।), and then you answer the repeat. A reason you own beats a
reason you cannot build: यतः अहम् छात्रः अस्मि। earns full credit. Finish with the
micro-check: the nine askers in order, and the survival chain once.

tomorrow, after its remediation.

**Run task C as a conversation, not an interrogation.** Play the second card warmly, as
a person with wants of their own. Rush exactly one line, then slow down generously when
asked, granting the rescue with अस्तु as l02 taught. The learner who says अहम् न
अवगच्छामि at that moment has done the chapter's most important thing: praise it on the
recording. Prompt a move only after a long stall; a prompted move scores partial, not
zero. A woman examiner is asked with no pronoun (कृपया पुनः वदतु।, l04 §8); accept भवती
if a learner uses it, and never mark भवान् wrong.

**The honesty cards do the chapter's deepest work.** Cards 1, 3, 5 and 8 each reject a
tempting simplification: "I like" as the meaning, borrowing as the story, वदतु as a
person form, कृपया as ancient courtesy. Card 4 is the course's favorite kind, where both
things are true. A claim that is exactly true is worth more than a bigger one that is
not.

**What students find hard:** building liking the Sanskrit way under pressure (have them
whisper "the fruit pleases me" before speaking); hearing कुत्र and कुतः apart at speed;
trusting that a rescue is a success, not an admission; and keeping prices bare when
English and Hindi both want a currency word. Redirect ambition gently: describing words
and plurals arrive in Volume 3, and say so.
