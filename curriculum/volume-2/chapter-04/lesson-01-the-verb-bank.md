## 1. Hook

Your Sanskrit owns two hundred and ninety-seven words — and almost all of them are *things*: a
boy, a fruit, a river, a school, a week of days. For a volume and three chapters the
things have mostly stood still, animated by a handful of hard-working verbs. Today seven
engines arrive at once: *goes*, *eats*, *drinks*, *sees*, *hears*, *writes*, *knows* — and
with the three verbs you already own, the course's ten-verb bank opens for business. By
tonight, everything in your word-stock can do something.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — ten everyday verbs, and the fix this chapter is.

Every language keeps a small set of verbs that do most of the day's work — go, eat,
drink, see, hear, say, know. Sanskrit is no different: today's bank is not textbook
filler but the high-frequency core of the classical language, the verbs that run its
stories, its dialogues and its grammar books' own example sentences.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** All ten bank verbs are attested, high-frequency classical Sanskrit,
> met here in their present-tense "he/she/it" forms and glossed whole: गच्छति "goes",
> खादति "eats", पिबति "drinks", पश्यति "sees", शृणोति "hears", लिखति "writes", जानाति
> "knows" — beside the course's own पठति, वदति, हसति. [REF:apte1890 s.vv.]

One line of course history, stated as the fix it is: the 1.x book printed this same
ten-verb bank, and printed it correctly — the audit kept the words. What the audit
rejected was what came next: all ten tense-and-mood systems dumped at once, with no prior
production practice. In 2.0 those systems wait for Volumes 3–4, said once and honestly,
and this chapter does instead what 1.x skipped — deep, spoken practice of the present
singular you already half-own. The chapter you are starting *is* the correction.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why verbs change everything, and why today's drills are built the
way they are.

A noun names; a verb *asserts*. The moment खादति lands in your stock, बालकः and फलम् stop
being a list and become an event — someone is eating something. That is why vocabulary
work on verbs pays sentence-sized dividends immediately.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** New vocabulary sticks best when it arrives inside usable patterns
> and gets retrieved from them, not memorized as bare lists — the frame बालकः ___ खादति
> turns every verb on today's list into a sentence you can say the hour you learn it.
> [REF:nation2013]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spaced retrieval beats massed re-study; today's Station 0 is
> v2.c03.l05's +1-week interval landing exactly on schedule, and this lesson's own +1-day
> re-run is booked before it ends (§20). [REF:cepeda2006] [REF:roediger2006]

Two of today's drills push past recognition on purpose. The act-and-say rounds bind each
verb to its meaning through the body — you drink while saying पिबति — so the word stops
routing through English at all; and the timed bank recitation trains automaticity, the
fluency strand's standing demand: a verb you must translate before using is a verb you do
not yet own [REF:nation2013].

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the bank at work, in sentences you fully own:

> **बालकः फलम् खादति।**
> (bālakaḥ phalam khādati.) "The boy eats a fruit." — बालक फल खाता है।

> **सीता जलम् पिबति।**
> (sītā jalam pibati.) "Sītā drinks water." — सीता जल पीती है।

> **छात्रः लिखति।**
> (chātraḥ likhati.) "The student writes." — छात्र लिखता है।

Two frames, both old friends. With an object: the doer, the thing in its म् dress, the
verb at the end — exactly the shape of सः पुस्तकम् पठति from the first passage. Without
one: doer plus verb, and the sentence is complete — छात्रः लिखति needs nothing more.
Pause form between words, every word whole, as always.

## 8. English Explanation

No new letter, no new sign — and no new grammar either, honestly: today is pure
vocabulary, seven whole words of the most useful kind there is.

### The seven, one by one

**गच्छति** (gacchati) "goes" — the word with the longest waiting list in the course. You
have *heard* it since v1.c03.l02, where it rode the audio as an ear-word because its च्छ
knot needed Chapter 5's conjuncts. Then came the first passage (v1.c06.l03) — and the
course deliberately kept गच्छति out of it: the walking line was written सः विद्यालयम्
चलति, with the certified चलति, because a word that has never had a vocabulary row does
not get smuggled into a passage. Today the wait ends: गच्छति enters the ledger as a
headword, and the sentence the old 1.x passage always wanted — सः विद्यालयम् गच्छति। —
becomes fully legal Sanskrit in your mouth.

**खादति** (khādati) "eats" and **पिबति** (pibati) "drinks" — the table pair. Both read
with letters you have owned since Volume 1; the work is the long खा and the crisp, full
पि. They pair naturally with the stock: फलम् खादति, जलम् पिबति.

**पश्यति** (paśyati) "sees" — an old friend's promotion. The chunk पश्य "look!" has been
on glue duty since v1.c01.l04, always glossed whole, always an imperative island. Today
that imperative-only chunk status retires: पश्यति is the verb proper — "he/she sees" —
and पश्य stays in service as the little "look!" it always was. Nothing you learned is
un-learned; one word has simply grown up.

**शृणोति** (śṛṇoti) "hears" and **जानाति** (jānāti) "knows" — the two distinguished
guests. Real, everyday, indispensable verbs — and the two that build their stems
differently from the rest of the bank (§15 says this honestly in one line). You learn
them exactly as you learned अस्ति: whole, correct, and without machinery.

**लिखति** (likhati) "writes" — the student's verb. छात्रः लिखति is this chapter's quiet
self-portrait: it is what you will be doing in §18.

### The three veterans — and three more back on duty

The bank's other three members are already yours: **पठति** "reads" (v1.c06.l03), **वदति**
"speaks" (v1.c04.l01) and **हसति** "laughs" (v1.c04.l03) — review rows today, full bank
members from now on. And three old engines return in sentence work without joining the
bank: **चलति** "moves" (v1.c03.l02 — the course's first readable verb), **भवति** "is,
becomes" (v1.c04.l01) and **वहति** "carries, flows" (v1.c04.l03). Ten in the bank, three
on the bench, and अस्ति still holding every location sentence it ever held.

### The frames

Nothing new to learn here — only new engines in old chassis: **बालकः फलम् खादति।** —
**सीता जलम् पिबति।** — **छात्रः लिखति।** The doer first, the thing in its म् dress
glossed whole (the policy since फलम् and जलम् first dressed up), the verb at the end,
pause form throughout. Singular only, "he/she/it" only; your own "I" sentences keep to
the owned पठामि and वदामि. Simplest correct beats ambitious wrong — today as every day.

One tease, honestly unspoiled: tomorrow the course's first grammar lesson names a pattern
you have been using for three chapters. Today, stock the bank.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — no new sounds; the work is one knot, one double curl, and full finals.
Audio: 🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c04l01-drill.mp3` (drills 1–3),
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c04l01-verb-bank.mp3` (roll-call, three speeds +
dictation track).

**Drill 1 — the knot.** Climb with the audio: ग — गच् — गच्छ — गच्छति. The च्छ is one
gesture: tongue-body seal, a held beat, one release with the puff riding it. If a vowel
sneaks in ("gacha-chhati") or the hold vanishes ("gachati"), slow down and re-climb.

**Drill 2 — the double curl.** शृणोति in slow motion: श — शृ — शृणो — शृणोति. The tongue
curls for the ृ (syllabic, no helper vowel), uncurls, then curls again for ण, and the ओ
lands long and pure. Three passes, each slower than feels natural.

**Drill 3 — the sibilant check.** Alternate सा — शा — पश्य — पश्यति. The श of पश्यति is
the palatal hush /ɕ/, never plain स; the श्य block is welded, no seam vowel.

**Drill 4 — the bank roll-call.** With the audio, fixed order, three passes (slow ·
natural · timed): गच्छति — खादति — पिबति — पश्यति — शृणोति — लिखति — जानाति, then the
veterans पठति — वदति — हसति. The order is the memory palace; your mouth learns it so §18
exercise 3's timed take comes out unprompted. Log the third pass time.

**Narrow note — the गच्छ knot:** /t͡ɕt͡ɕʰ/ is the palatal affricate doubled — one long
closure, a single release, the aspiration only on the release. Two separate च-sounds with
a vowel between them is the flagged error.

**Narrow note — शृणोति:** syllabic /r̩/ for the ृ (a vowel-slot filled by r itself, as in
every ऋ word since v1.c02.l03), retroflex /ɳ/ mid-word, and /oː/ a pure long
monophthong — never English "go" [oʊ].

**Narrow note — जानाति:** two full /ɑː/ beats around a dental /n̪/ — the Hindi-rhythm
clip "jānti" loses a syllable Sanskrit keeps.

**Narrow note — the sentence subjects:** बालकः /bɑːlɐkɐh/ and छात्रः /t͡ɕʰɑːt̪ɾɐh/ end in
the voiceless breath /h/ — the visarga discipline never retires.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — four checks; the articulators know all of this already. The word-anatomy
strip on the action cards (`planned:assets/images/volume-2/v2c04l01-action-cards.svg`)
marks the गच्छ and शृ joins.

1. **The knot check (गच्छति).** Say it in slow motion and freeze inside च्छ: tongue-body
   sealed against the palate, a held beat, then ONE release with a puff you can feel on
   your palm. Two releases mean the knot slipped.
2. **The curl check (शृणोति).** The tongue tip curls twice — once for ृ (no vowel before
   the r), once for ण — and the lips round for a long pure ओ. "Shri-no-ti" means the
   first curl leaked a vowel; re-run the v1.c02.l03 ऋ drill.
3. **The puff check (खादति, लिखति).** Both words carry ख — full contact plus real
   aspiration, felt on the palm. A scraped, fricative "kh" (the Hindi ख़ habit) has lost
   the contact.
4. **The final-vowel check.** Every bank verb ends in a short, *full* इ — mirror test:
   the smile position holds through the last beat. Clipped finals are the standing enemy
   at every speed.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; these feed the quiz distractors.

1. **The knot simplified: "gachati".** The held च्छ collapses to a single छ. *Fix:* §9
   drill 1 — seal, hold, one release (v1.c05.l02's conjunct mechanism).
2. **खादति scraped: "k͟hādati" with the Hindi ख़.** The ख of खादति is a stop with breath,
   not a friction sound — and the खा holds two full beats. *Fix:* §11 check 3.
3. **पश्यति flattened: "pasyati".** The palatal श /ɕ/ slides to plain स. *Fix:* §9
   drill 3 — सा vs शा until the hush is automatic.
4. **शृणोति re-voweled: "shrinoti".** The ृ gains a helper vowel, the ण flattens to न,
   the ओ shortens or glides. *Fix:* §9 drill 2 — double curl, pure long ओ.
5. **जानाति clipped: "jānti", "jānati".** The Hindi जानता rhythm steals the second
   syllable's length. *Fix:* §10 — two full आ beats, always.
6. **Over-reach: inventing forms.** Gluing endings onto today's verbs to say "I eat" or
   "you go". The bank is "he/she/it" forms, whole; your I-lines keep to the owned पठामि
   and वदामि. Simplest correct beats ambitious wrong. *Fix:* §15's honesty lines.
7. **The cognate reach: "गच्छति is English *go*".** It is not — the resemblance is
   coincidence, and resemblance alone proves nothing. *Fix:* §13's shelf — honesty about
   absence is part of the method, and the chapter exam tests it.

## 13. Examples

### जानाति and the knowing family — a promise kept

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Sanskrit जानाति "knows", English *know* and Greek *gnōsis* are

have read for a volume and the English and Greek cousins that share its ancestor.

### पिबति and Latin bibit — one ancestor, two drinkers

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Sanskrit पिबति "drinks" and Latin *bibit* "drinks" are siblings —
> parallel descendants of one Proto-Indo-European verb; neither borrowed a drop from the
> other. [REF:mallory2006]

Both words even inherited their doubled look from that one ancestor — how the ancestor
was built is a Volume 4 story, and we are not opening it today. What you carry out the
door is the honest line: one grandparent, two heirs, no arrows.

### लिखति — and the honest blank

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** लिखति "writes" has no clean English cognate — and the course says
> so rather than reaching for one. And the reach the chapter exam will test you on:
> गच्छति and English *go* are NOT related. They sound alike; resemblance alone proves
> nothing — relatedness is established by systematic correspondences, not by echoes.

This is the same discipline एक taught in v2.c03.l01: a shelf with an honest blank is
worth more than a shelf stuffed with look-alikes. Two real siblings, one blank, one
refused reach — that is today's complete shelf, and every claim on it survives checking.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — old frames, new engines, and the honesty lines that keep today safe.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** (1) Every verb on today's list is a whole word, glossed whole — the
> same honest device the course has used since रामः वदति; no analysis of the word's
> insides happens today. (2) The sentence frames are the owned ones: doer + verb (छात्रः
> लिखति), or doer + thing-in-म्-dress + verb (बालकः फलम् खादति) — objects glossed whole,
> no case teaching, pause form throughout. (3) Today's forms are singular "he/she/it"
> only; first-person lines keep to the owned पठामि and वदामि. (4) शृणोति and जानाति build
> their stems differently from the rest of the bank — both are taught whole, and their
> other forms will arrive as whole words too. [REF:apte1890]; [REF:whitney1889]

| Frame | Example | Note |
|---|---|---|
| doer + verb | छात्रः लिखति। · परिवारः हसति। | complete as it stands — no object needed |

## 22. Teacher Notes

अध्यापकटिप्पणी

**Open with the frozen scene, not the list.** Put five noun cards on the board — बालकः,
फलम्, जलम्, सीता, छात्रः — and ask the room to make something *happen* with them. They
can't (one चलति and an अस्ति only go so far). Then deal the seven verb cards one at a
time and watch the board come alive. The lesson's thesis — verbs turn a word-stock into a
world — lands better demonstrated than announced.

**Timing plan (45 min):** Station 0 + prompts, scores logged (8) → bank walk-through on
the action cards; गच्छति's and पश्यति's comeback stories told honestly (9) →
pronunciation knots: गच्छ, शृणो, पश्य drills (6) → frame work: matching grid in pairs,
sentences aloud (7) → act-and-say, two passes (6) → the shelf: जानाति, पिबति, the लिखति
blank, the गच्छति-go refusal (4) → timed recitation + dictation launch (4) → homework and
the +1-day booking read aloud (1).

**Board work:** a bank vault drawn as a 2×5 grid — seven boxes opened today (cards pinned
in as they arrive), three veterans' boxes already stamped. Keep a side tray labeled "on
the bench": चलति, भवति, वहति — in sentences, not in the bank.

**Someone will spot the matching tails.** Mid-roll-call a learner will notice all ten
verbs end alike and ask. Congratulate the noticing, write nothing, and park it: that
observation is tomorrow's entire lesson, and it lands harder if today only collects the
evidence. Do not name the pattern today — not even as a hint.

**Run the shelf with the refusal front and center.** The गच्छति-go card is the teachable
moment: let the room vote cognate/not before you land it. The winning insight is the
method — resemblance alone proves nothing; the honest blank beside लिखति makes the same
point from the other side. Both cards return on the chapter exam.

**Differentiation:** Hindi speakers own half the bank by instinct (खाता, पीता, लिखता,
जानता) — their work is the finals (every -ति full), the खा length, the ण curl, and
resisting the "it's just Hindi" shortcut that erases exactly the differences the course
teaches. Script-side learners drill गच्छ from components (ग + च् + छ, v1.c05.l02) before
speed work.

**What students find hard:** the च्छ hold under time pressure (slow the roll-call before
timing it); शृ without a helper vowel (the v1.c02.l03 ऋ drill transfers whole); the urge
to say "I eat" the hour they learn खादति — honor the ambition, point at पठामि/वदामि, and
promise the machinery is days, not volumes, away; and accepting that शृणोति and जानाति
get no explanation today — "builds its stem differently" is the whole truth on offer, and
saying it plainly beats a half-taught system.
