## 1. Hook

For two lessons you have been loading a toolbox: ten verbs in lesson 1, the person pattern
that runs them in lesson 2. Today one two-word question turns the toolbox into conversation.
किम् करोषि? — "what are you doing?" Ask it of anyone, and every verb you own becomes somebody's
answer: अहम् पठामि। अहम् लिखामि। अहम् फलम् खादामि। The question costs you one new word — and
that word turns out to be hiding inside the name of the language itself. You have been saying
a कृ word since your very first lesson: संस्कृतम्.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — the busiest verb in the language, and the course's own name.

Every language keeps one overworked verb that does everything nobody else does — English
*do*, Hindi करना. Sanskrit's is कृ (kṛ), and today you meet it on duty: करोति "does, makes".

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** कृ "to do, to make" is among the highest-frequency verbs of classical
> Sanskrit; the dictionaries give it one of their longest entries — it does, makes,
> prepares, performs, and builds hundreds of idioms (प्रश्नम् करोति "asks a question",
> अन्नम् करोति "prepares food"). Its present singular runs करोमि, करोषि, करोति.
> [REF:apte1890 s.v. कृ]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The language's own name carries this verb: संस्कृतम् (saṃskṛtam) is
> built on सम् + कृ — "put together, polished, perfected". When you said the course's
> first word, you were already saying a कृ word. [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. संस्कृत]

One line of course history, honestly told: the 1.x curriculum shipped करोति inside a full
कृ paradigm dump that the audit flagged as its worst sequencing error. 2.0 hands you today
exactly three whole words and one living question — the paradigm takes its proper seat in
Volume 4, and this lesson says so out loud.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why one question teaches ten verbs, and what the real work is.

"What are you doing?" is a conversational workhorse in every language — the question that
can always be asked, of anyone, about anything. That is precisely why it earns its place as
this chapter's engine: every time it is asked, some verb must be *retrieved and produced*,
and every honest answer is one more rep on the pattern lesson 2 named.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Question-answer frames are learned fastest as chunks — frame in,
> frame out — and every run through a question-answer exchange is retrieval practice, the
> strongest rehearsal there is. Today's engine is two chunks and one swap.
> [REF:nation2013] [REF:roediger2006]

The swap is the real work, so name it: when you *hear* करोषि, the truthful answer comes
back in करोमि — the verb form changes hands with the speaker. Hindi and English both do
this ("तुम क्या कर रहे हो?" — "मैं पढ़ रहा हूँ"), so the habit exists; today it must learn
to run on Sanskrit endings under time pressure. We drill the swap, not just the forms.

Two honest design notes. First, करोति is taught as three whole words, not as a pattern —
everyday "do"-verbs are irregular in language after language (English *do/does/did*, *go/
went*), and pretending otherwise manufactures errors. Second, the question itself stays
level-tuned: किम् does the asking, exactly as the question-kit rule of v2.c01.l02 said.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — no new संज्ञा today; the lesson's name is its own first sentence. The
unlocked language, at once:

> **किम् त्वम् करोषि?**
> (kim tvam karoṣi?) "What are you doing?" — तुम क्या कर रहे हो?

> **अहम् पठामि।**
> (aham paṭhāmi.) "I am reading." — मैं पढ़ रहा हूँ / पढ़ रही हूँ।

> **किम् सः करोति? — सः लिखति।**
> (kim saḥ karoti? — saḥ likhati.) "What is he doing? — He is writing." — वह क्या कर रहा
> है? — वह लिख रहा है।

> **भवान् किम् करोति?**
> (bhavān kim karoti?) "What are you doing, sir?" — आप क्या कर रहे हैं?

> **माता अन्नम् करोति। अर्जुनः क्षीरम् पिबति।**
> (mātā annam karoti. arjunaḥ kṣīram pibati.) "Mother is making food. Arjuna is drinking
> milk." — माता भोजन बना रही हैं। अर्जुन दूध पी रहा है।

Everything here is built from yesterday's pattern plus three whole words: करोमि, करोषि,
करोति — one family, learned whole, like अस्मि-असि-अस्ति before it.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

आज की कुंजी आपके पास बचपन से है: "क्या कर रहे हो?" संस्कृत ठीक यही दो शब्दों में पूछती है —
किम् करोषि? किम् आपका पुराना मित्र है ("क्या"), और करोषि का अर्थ है "तुम करते हो / कर रहे
हो"। त्वम् जोड़कर पूरा साँचा बनता है: किम् त्वम् करोषि? उत्तर में वही होता है जो हिन्दी में
होता है — व्यक्ति बदल जाता है। प्रश्न में "तुम", उत्तर में "मैं": सुना करोषि, कहा करोमि।
अहम् पठामि। अहम् लिखामि। अहम् फलम् खादामि। जो भी सच हो, वही कहिए — यह पाठ सच बोलने का
अभ्यास है। और तीसरे व्यक्ति की ख़बर देनी हो तो: किम् सः करोति? — सः लिखति। किम् सा करोति?
— सा पठति।

अब आज की सबसे ज़रूरी ईमानदार बात। करोमि-करोषि-करोति तीन पूरे शब्द हैं — इन्हें कल के
गच्छ-साँचे में मत ढालिए। गच्छति की तरह "करति" या "करसि" गढ़ने का मन करेगा; मत गढ़िए। यह
शब्द अपना आधार अलग ढंग से बनाता है — पूरी व्यवस्था खण्ड 4 में आएगी, और तब तक यही एक पंक्ति
पूरा सच है: पूरा शब्द पढ़िए, पूरा शब्द बोलिए। इसी में -षि/-सि की बात भी आ जाती है: करोषि
में ष है, गच्छसि में स — करोषि का ष कृ की अपनी आदत है। कोई नियम मत खोजिए; शब्द को पूरा
पहचानिए। और ओ सदा लम्बा: क-रो-ति, तीनों रूपों में।

दो बातें और। पहली — आदर का रूप: भवान् किम् करोति? (आप क्या कर रहे हैं?)। ध्यान दीजिए:
भवान् के साथ करोति-रूप चलता है — वही रूप जो सः के साथ। क्यों, यह आगे की कथा है; आज के लिए
यह एक ईमानदार पंक्ति भर है, और उत्तर फिर भी मैं-रूप में ही आता है: अहम् पठामि। दूसरी — दो
नए शब्द काम पर: अन्नम् (भोजन) और क्षीरम् (दूध; हिन्दी की "खीर" इसी शब्द-परिवार की याद

पिबति — ठीक वैसे ही जैसे फलम् खादति चलता आया है। कोई विभक्ति-पाठ नहीं; शब्द पूरे-के-पूरे।
और हाँ — कल की सबसे बड़ी चूक आज भी घात में है: "अहम् गच्छति" मत कहिए; अहम् के साथ -मि।

## 8. English Explanation

One question, three whole words, one polite surprise, and two words for the lunch table.

### The family — three whole words, one honest line

**करोति** (karoti) "does, makes" is today's headword — and its family comes with it:
**करोमि** (karomi) "I do", **करोषि** (karoṣi) "you do". Line them up and you can *see*
yesterday's endings waving from the windows — -मि, -षि, -ति — but do not reach for the
गच्छ pattern: this word builds its stem differently, and the honest move is to learn the
row whole, exactly as you learned अस्मि-असि-अस्ति. No "करति", no "करसि" — those are
pattern-forced inventions, and §12 is waiting for them. The system behind कृ's shapes is
Volume 4's story, told properly there; today three whole words buy you the best question
in the language.

### The engine — ask in the you-form, answer in the I-form

**किम् त्वम् करोषि?** (kim tvam karoṣi?) "What are you doing?" — and the answer swaps
persons, because now *you* are speaking about *yourself*:

| You are asked | You answer, truthfully |
|---|---|
| किम् त्वम् करोषि? | अहम् पठामि। "I am reading." |
| किम् त्वम् करोषि? | अहम् लिखामि। "I am writing." |
| किम् त्वम् करोषि? | अहम् फलम् खादामि। "I am eating a fruit." |
| किम् त्वम् करोषि? | अहम् क्षीरम् पिबामि। "I am drinking milk." |

The swap is the entire art: hear करोषि, answer करोमि-family — hear the you-form, answer in
the I-form. And the truthfulness is not decoration; it is the drill. An answer you *mean*
is retrieved, not recited.

### The reporter round — किम् सः करोति?

Point the same question at a third person and both halves ride the he-form: **किम् सः
करोति?** — **सः लिखति।** · **किम् सा करोति?** — **सा पठति।** No swap this time: you ask
about him, you answer about him. The dialogue now reaches everyone in the room — you, me,
and the person by the window.

### The engine generalizes — the whole bank answers

Here is the payoff of owning a *pattern* rather than a list: swap करोषि for any bank verb
and the question machine keeps running. किम् त्वम् खादसि? "what are you eating?" — अहम्
अन्नम् खादामि। किम् त्वम् पश्यसि? "what do you see?" — अहम् विद्यालयम् पश्यामि। किम्
त्वम् शृणोषि? "what do you hear?" (शृणोषि — a whole word from lesson 2, keeping its own
ष). Ten verbs, one question word, two persons to swap — that is a conversation engine, and
§16 runs it at full speed.

### भवान् — the polite variant, with one honest surprise

To ask a man respectfully: **भवान् किम् करोति?** (bhavān kim karoti?) "What are you doing,
sir?" Look closely: भवान् walks with **करोति** — the same form सः takes, not the त्वम्
form. That is not a typo; it is how the respectful "you" behaves, and *why* is a later
story. Today it is one honest, chunk-level line — exactly how भवान् itself has been
handled since v1.c05, and how भवान् कुशली? worked in v2.c01.l02. The answer, as always,
comes back truthfully in the I-form: अहम् पठामि।

### Objects at work — अन्नम् and क्षीरम्

Two new words earn their keep at the lunch table: **अन्नम्** (annam) "food" and
**क्षीरम्** (kṣīram) "milk". They slot into the object position you have used since फलम्
खादति and जलम् पिबति (v1.c06's policy, unchanged): the object wears म्, the sentence is
glossed whole, and no case is taught — अन्नम् खादति। क्षीरम् पिबति। फलम् खादामि। And one
sentence to savor: **माता अन्नम् करोति।** "Mother is *making* food" — करोति doing its
second job, *makes*, with an object of its own.

### The ledger

Lesson 1 brought the bank and the ledger to 312; lesson 2 named the pattern, added असि,
and reached 313. Today's three — करोति, अन्नम्, क्षीरम् — bring it to **316**. करोमि and
करोषि are not separate ledger words: they are the same word करोति, learned as whole-word
chunks of one family. Everything else in this lesson is yours already.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — no new sounds; the work is one retroflex discipline, one long vowel, and the
swap at speed. Audio: 🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c04l03-dialogue.mp3` (both
scenes at three speeds, plus a roleplay-gap track), 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c04l03-word-list.mp3` (the family row + the new words,
plus the dictation track).

**Drill 1 — the chunk row, three speeds.** करोमि — करोषि — करोति, slow, natural, timed;
three passes each. The ओ is long and pure in all three — क-रो-मि, never the English "go"
glide. Then both directions with the audio: hear "I" → say करोमि; hear करोषि → say "you".

**Drill 2 — the ष/स ping-pong.** Alternate करोषि — गच्छसि — करोषि — गच्छसि, three rounds.
Feel the tongue move: curled back for the ष of करोषि, forward to the teeth for the स of
गच्छसि. Then let शृणोषि join the ष side. No rule today — each word keeps its own habit;
your mouth learns the words whole.

**Drill 3 — the swap under the clock.** The audio asks किम् त्वम् करोषि? — answer
truthfully in the I-form before the beat runs out. Second round: reporter calls — किम् सः
करोति? — answer in the he-form with the picture card in front of you. The tune stays level
on every question: किम् does the asking (§3 prompt 5).

**Drill 4 — the object words.** अन्नम्: the double न् is held — अन्-नम्, one beat of
closed tongue-on-teeth before the release; "अनम्" is a different (non-)word. क्षीरम्: the
क्ष weld from v1.c05.l03 — क् straight into ष with no vowel leaking ("kashīram" means a
vowel got in) — then two full beats of ई.

## 10. IPA

No new symbols — new speed over known values. The frames in pause form:

**किम् त्वम् करोषि** /kim t̪ʋɐm kɐɾoːʂi/ · **अहम् पठामि** /ɐɦɐm pɐʈʰɑːmi/ · **भवान् किम्
करोति** /bʱɐʋɑːn̪ kim kɐɾoːt̪i/ · **माता अन्नम् करोति** /mɑːt̪ɑː ɐn̪n̪ɐm kɐɾoːt̪i/.

**Word transcriptions:** करोति /kɐɾoːt̪i/ · करोमि /kɐɾoːmi/ · करोषि /kɐɾoːʂi/ · अन्नम्
/ɐn̪n̪ɐm/ · क्षीरम् /kʂiːɾɐm/.

**Narrow note — the ष of करोषि:** /ʂ/ is the retroflex sibilant — tongue tip curled back
toward the dome — against the /s/ of गच्छसि /gɐt͡ɕt͡ɕʰɐsi/ at the teeth. The two endings
differ in exactly one articulator position; the drill is §9's ping-pong, the policy is
"read the whole word".

**Narrow note — the ओ of the family:** /oː/ — always long, pure monophthong, in all three
forms: /kɐɾoːmi/, /kɐɾoːʂi/, /kɐɾoːt̪i/. The English "go" diphthong [oʊ] is the standing
trap (STYLE_GUIDE value since Volume 1).

**Narrow note — अन्नम्:** the geminate /n̪n̪/ holds through a full beat — [ɐn̪.n̪ɐm] —
two syllables sharing one long closure. Shortening it to /ɐn̪ɐm/ changes the word.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — three checks; every articulator already knows its station.

1. **The ष curl check.** Say करोषि in slow motion and freeze on षि: the tongue *tip* is
   curled up and back toward the dome of the mouth, not resting at the teeth. Now say
   गच्छसि and feel the tip drop forward to the teeth for स. If both words feel identical
   in the mouth, the ष has slid forward — re-run §9 drill 2.
2. **The अन्नम् hold.** Tongue on the teeth for न् — and *stay* there one full beat before
   releasing into the second न. A mirror shows nothing moving during the hold; that
   stillness is the double consonant.
3. **The क्ष weld.** Say क्षीरम् from a full stop: back of the tongue closes for क्, and
   the release lands *directly* on the curled-back ष — no अ between. Then two beats of ई,
   lips spread and frozen, and a final म् with lips visibly closed.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; these feed the quiz distractors.

1. **The -षि/-सि confusion.** Writing or reading करोसि, or "correcting" गच्छसि to गच्छषि
   because करोषि looked authoritative. *Fix:* the ष is कृ's own habit; read the whole
   word. करोषि keeps its ष, गच्छसि keeps its स, शृणोषि keeps its ष — each word whole, no
   rule hunted today (§8, §9 drill 2).
2. **The person mismatch: अहम् गच्छति।** THE flagged error, carried straight from lesson
   2 — and today's dialogue is where it strikes, because the question hands you a -षि
   form and your mouth wants to echo a third-person -ति. *Fix:* अहम् takes -मि, always:
   अहम् गच्छामि। अहम् करोमि। Run §18 exercise 2 until the swap is automatic.
3. **Answering about yourself in the he-form.** किम् त्वम् करोषि? — "पठति।" No: that
   says *he* reads. You are not the reporter of your own life; you are the speaker.
   *Fix:* truthful I-form, every time — अहम् पठामि। (§8, the swap).
4. **भवान् with the त्वम्-form.** भवान् करोषि? mixes the respectful "you" with the
   familiar form. *Fix:* भवान् walks with the करोति-form — भवान् किम् करोति? One honest
   chunk; no paradigm (§8, §15).
5. **The short or glided ओ.** "kuh-ro-ti" with a clipped o, or the English "go" glide.
   *Fix:* /oː/ — long, pure, in all three family forms (§10); tap the two beats.
6. **Regularized inventions: करति, करसि, करामि.** The pattern lesson makes the hand want
   to conjugate everything. *Fix:* this word is learned whole — करोमि, करोषि, करोति; if
   you catch yourself building it, stop and *say the row* instead (§9 drill 1).

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — three items with something inside them.

### करोति — the honest empty shelf

**करोति** (karoti) "does, makes" — करोति · /kɐɾoːt̪i/

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The cognate shelf stays honest by staying empty when it is empty:
> करोति offers no everyday-English cognate for this course's shelf — nothing like
> जानाति's *know*. जानाति (jānāti) itself, from lesson 1, remains the chapter's one
> shelf verb: Sanskrit जानाति and English *know* are parallel descendants of one ancient
> root — siblings by shared descent, borrowed by no one. [REF:mallory2006]

Honesty beats reaching, as always: a shelf that only holds real siblings is a shelf you
can trust. करोति's riches are elsewhere — in what it *does* (§4): it is the verb inside
संस्कृतम् (saṃskṛtam) itself, and the busiest worker in the dictionary.

### माता अन्नम् करोति — the verb's second job

**माता अन्नम् करोति।** (mātā annam karoti.) "Mother is making food." — माता भोजन बना रही
हैं।

The question-word किम् asked "what are you *doing*?" — but करोति with an object means
*makes, prepares*. One verb, two jobs, both classical ([REF:apte1890 s.v. कृ] lists
अन्नम् करोति "prepares food" among its working idioms). The doing-question and the
making-sentence will sit side by side in §16's kitchen.

### क्षीरम् पिबति — objects that earn their keep

**अर्जुनः क्षीरम् पिबति।** (arjunaḥ kṣīram pibati.) "Arjuna is drinking milk." — अर्जुन
दूध पी रहा है।

The new words slot into the oldest object frame you own: फलम् खादति (v1.c06), जलम्
पिबति, पुस्तकम् पठति — and now अन्नम् खादामि, क्षीरम् पिबामि. Observation, not law: the
object wears म्. The machinery behind that dress is later business; the sentences are
safe ground now, glossed whole, exactly as they have been for a volume.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — in a conversation lesson the grammar is small and sharp: one swap, one honest
row, one polite chunk, one observed dress.

### The engine's one rule — the person swap

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** (1) A question asked in the you-form (-सि/-षि) is answered about
> oneself in the I-form (-मि): किम् त्वम् करोषि? — अहम् पठामि। A question asked about a
> third person stays in the he-form on both sides: किम् सः करोति? — सः लिखति। (2) The
> laṭ present covers both English presents — "you do" and "you are doing"; context
> decides, and today's Hindi gloss क्या कर रहे हो? renders the live, right-now use.
> [REF:whitney1889]

| You hear | You say | Why |
|---|---|---|
| किम् त्वम् करोषि? | अहम् पठामि। | asked in the you-form → answer in the I-form |
| किम् सः करोति? | सः लिखति। | asked about him → answered about him |
| भवान् किम् करोति? | अहम् पठामि। | polite question in — truthful I-form out |

### The करोति row — whole words, one honest line

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** करोति builds its present stem differently from the गच्छति pattern;
> its singular row is therefore learned as three whole words — करोमि, करोषि, करोति —
> exactly as शृणोति and जानाति were handled in lessons 1–2. The endings -मि/-षि/-ति are
> visible at the back of each word; the stem system that explains the rest is Volume 4's
> business, and nothing taught today will need unlearning there. [REF:apte1890 s.v. कृ]

The working discipline: *recognize* the endings, *produce* the whole words. करति and
करसि do not exist; the row does.

### भवान् — one honest chunk-level line

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** भवान्, the respectful "you", takes the same verb form as सः — the
> करोति-form: भवान् किम् करोति? भवान् गच्छति? This is regular classical usage, stated
> today at chunk level only; the system behind it is later business. [REF:whitney1889]

This is the भवान् precedent running true: a polite chunk since v1.c05.l01, a wellness
question in v2.c01.l02 (भवान् कुशली?), a doing-question today — each time one honest
line, never a half-taught paradigm.

### Objects at work — observed, not legislated

Policy unchanged since v1.c06: the object wears म् and the sentence is glossed whole —
अन्नम् खादति। क्षीरम् पिबति। फलम् खादामि। माता अन्नम् करोति। You may *observe* that the
dress repeats; you may not yet extract a law, and no case is taught. When the case system
arrives (Volume 3), it will be built on exactly these safe sentences.

## 16. Conversation

सम्भाषणम् — the heart of the lesson, in two scenes; every word is self-read. 🔊 audio:
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c04l03-dialogue.mp3` (slow pause-form, natural speed,
and a roleplay-gap track with one side silent).

### Scene 1 — the kitchen question

Evening at home; माया wanders in, curious as ever.

— माया: भोः! किम् त्वम् करोषि?
(bhoḥ! kim tvam karoṣi?) "Hey! What are you doing?" — अरे! तुम क्या कर रहे हो?

— अर्जुनः: अहम् क्षीरम् पिबामि। किम् त्वम् करोषि?
(aham kṣīram pibāmi. kim tvam karoṣi?) "I am drinking milk. What are YOU doing?" — मैं
दूध पी रहा हूँ। तुम क्या कर रही हो?

— माया: अहम् पुस्तकम् पठामि। माता किम् करोति?
(aham pustakam paṭhāmi. mātā kim karoti?) "I am reading a book. What is Mother doing?" —
मैं पुस्तक पढ़ रही हूँ। माता क्या कर रही हैं?

— अर्जुनः: माता अन्नम् करोति!
(mātā annam karoti!) "Mother is making food!" — माता भोजन बना रही हैं!

— माया: आम्! अहम् अपि अन्नम् खादामि!
(ām! aham api annam khādāmi!) "Yes! I'm eating food too!" — हाँ! मैं भी खाना खाती हूँ!

Watch the engine do the teaching. माया asks in the you-form; अर्जुनः answers in the
I-form and hands the question straight back — the swap, twice, in four turns. The
reporter round follows (माता किम् करोति?) with no swap at all. And करोति works both its
jobs in one line: माता *makes* food; माया, uninvited as ever, plans to *do* something
about that — the old अपि joke riding today's verb.

### Scene 2 — the classroom dare

The room settles; the आचार्यः looks up from his notebook.

— आचार्यः: माया! किम् त्वम् करोषि?
(māyā! kim tvam karoṣi?) "Māyā! What are you doing?" — माया! तुम क्या कर रही हो?

— माया: अहम् लिखामि।
(aham likhāmi.) "I am writing." — मैं लिख रही हूँ।

— आचार्यः: रामः किम् करोति?
(rāmaḥ kim karoti?) "What is Rāma doing?" — राम क्या कर रहा है?

— माया: सः पश्यति! सः शृणोति!
(saḥ paśyati! saḥ śṛṇoti!) "He is watching! He is listening!" — वह देख रहा है! वह सुन
रहा है!

— रामः *(bravely, very politely)*: भवान् किम् करोति?
(bhavān kim karoti?) "And what are YOU doing, sir?" — और आप क्या कर रहे हैं?

— आचार्यः *(smiling)*: अहम् अपि लिखामि। सर्वम् पश्यामि!
(aham api likhāmi. sarvam paśyāmi!) "I too am writing. I see everything!" — मैं भी लिख
रहा हूँ। मैं सब देख रहा हूँ!

Delivery notes: every किम् question keeps the level tune — the word asks (v2.c01.l02's
rule, on duty). Pause form between turns, one full beat. And notice the dare: रामः asks
the teacher with भवान् and the करोति-form, exactly as taught — polite question in,
truthful I-form out. The English stage directions are scaffolding, not Sanskrit; swap
roles and run both scenes until each side is yours.

## 17. Story

कथा — किम् करोति? · What Is Everyone Doing? Nine lines; each line: Devanagari, IAST,
English, Hindi.

1. सायम् अस्ति। माया गृहम् गच्छति।
   (sāyam asti. māyā gṛham gacchati.) "It is evening. Māyā goes home." — शाम है। माया घर
   जाती है।
2. पिता पुस्तकम् पठति। माता अन्नम् करोति।
   (pitā pustakam paṭhati. mātā annam karoti.) "Father is reading a book. Mother is
   making food." — पिता पुस्तक पढ़ रहे हैं। माता भोजन बना रही हैं।
3. माया वदति — भोः! किम् त्वम् करोषि?
   (māyā vadati — bhoḥ! kim tvam karoṣi?) "Māyā says: Hey! What are you doing?" — माया
   कहती है — अरे! तुम क्या कर रहे हो?
4. अर्जुनः वदति — अहम् क्षीरम् पिबामि!
   (arjunaḥ vadati — aham kṣīram pibāmi!) "Arjuna says: I am drinking milk!" — अर्जुन
   कहता है — मैं दूध पी रहा हूँ!
5. माया वदति — कमला किम् करोति?
   (māyā vadati — kamalā kim karoti?) "Māyā says: What is Kamalā doing?" — माया कहती है
   — कमला क्या कर रही है?
6. कमला हसति — अहम् लिखामि। किम् त्वम् करोषि?
   (kamalā hasati — aham likhāmi. kim tvam karoṣi?) "Kamalā laughs: I am writing. What
   are YOU doing?" — कमला हँसती है — मैं लिख रही हूँ। तुम क्या कर रही हो?
7. माया फलम् खादति। सा वदति — अहम् फलम् खादामि!
   (māyā phalam khādati. sā vadati — aham phalam khādāmi!) "Māyā is eating a fruit. She
   says: I am eating a fruit!" — माया फल खा रही है। वह कहती है — मैं फल खा रही हूँ!
8. माता वदति — माया सर्वम् पश्यति! सा किम् करोति?
   (mātā vadati — māyā sarvam paśyati! sā kim karoti?) "Mother says: Māyā watches
   everything! What does SHE do?" — माता कहती हैं — माया सब देखती है! वह क्या करती है?
9. माया हसति — अहम् प्रश्नम् करोमि!
   (māyā hasati — aham praśnam karomi!) "Māyā laughs: I make questions!" — माया हँसती है
   — मैं प्रश्न करती हूँ!

Line 9 is the lesson grinning at itself: प्रश्नाः has headed every quiz in this book
since Chapter 1, and प्रश्नम् करोति "makes a question — asks" is a working कृ idiom (§4).
Asked what she *does*, the course's great question-asker answers with perfect truth in a
perfect I-form — the engine, the swap, and the new verb, all in three words. The whole
household speaks today's lesson: every person of the pattern appears, and nobody's verb
form slips.

## 22. Teacher Notes

अध्यापकटिप्पणी

**This is a speaking lesson — the question must travel.** The reading load is three
words; the win condition is the question moving around the room until every student has
asked it and answered it truthfully many times. Do not let the hour become a lecture
about an exchange; the exchange is the hour.

**Timing plan (45 min):** Station 0, scores logged (6) → hook: संस्कृतम् has कृ inside;
the family row on the board, whole-word framing (5) → chunk-row chorus, three speeds,
both directions (4) → the engine: swap demonstrated live — teacher asks, students answer
truthfully; board arrows करोषि → करोमि (6) → doing-chain in pairs, bank verbs rotating
(7) → reporter round on picture cards (5) → the ष/स ping-pong + object words अन्नम्/
क्षीरम् with the double-न् hold (5) → Scene 2 as a class performance; the भवान् dare
staged deliberately (4) → dictation/recording launch + homework briefing (3).

**Board work.** Three columns: अहम् करोमि · त्वम् करोषि · सः करोति — circle the endings,
then draw ONE arrow from करोषि (heard) to करोमि (said) and label it "the swap". Keep a
standing card in the corner all hour: अहम् … -मि — the lesson-2 flagged error will try to
return the moment attention moves to the new verb.

**Stage the भवान् dare — do not let it happen by accident.** Brief one confident student
to ask you भवान् किम् करोति? mid-activity. Answer truthfully in the I-form, then freeze
the moment on the board: भवान् took the करोति-form, and the answer still came back in
-मि. Script the honesty line exactly as the course prints it: भवान् walks with the
करोति-form; why is a later story; today it is one honest line. Never improvise a
paradigm, and keep the words "class five/eight/nine" out of the room — "builds its stem
differently" is the approved phrasing.

**Insist on truthful answers.** The drill collapses into recitation if students answer
अहम् पठामि by reflex while plainly doing something else. Hand out action cards (drink,
write, look out the window) and make the answer match the action — retrieval needs a real
choice. A student who answers about themselves in the -ति form repeats the swap arrow on
the board, aloud, once.

**Differentiation:** Hindi speakers own the frame outright — क्या कर रहे हो? maps
word-for-word — so move them fast to the two mouth disciplines: the ष of करोषि (curl
check, §11) and the long pure ओ. English-habit learners rise on questions by reflex; hold
them to the level किम् tune, and watch their ओ for the "go" glide. Everyone: the double
न् of अन्नम् needs one deliberate held beat before it feels normal.

**What students find hard:** answering about themselves in the he-form (the reporter
round contaminates the swap — alternate the two activities deliberately); the urge to
regularize (करति/करसि — send them to the chunk row, not to a rule); भवान् + करोषि from
politeness-logic ("respect should get the you-form!") — the honest line, stated calmly,
settles it; and hearing -षि vs -सि at speed — the ping-pong drill with eyes closed helps
the ear take over from the page.
