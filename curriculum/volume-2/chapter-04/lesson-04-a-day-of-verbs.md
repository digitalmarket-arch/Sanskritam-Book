## 1. Hook

Twelve sentences, one boy, one ordinary day — and, hidden in the middle of it, a question
addressed to you. Three lessons gave you ten verbs, the three endings that steer them, and
the question that turns them into talk. Today they stop being drills and start telling a
story. अर्जुनः walks to school with गच्छति at last (the course's first passage had to make
do with चलति), mutters to himself in the I-form, eats, heads home, drinks his milk — and
somewhere between lunch and dusk the text turns round and looks straight at the reader. No
new word anywhere. The only thing you must not miss is the last syllable of every verb:
it tells you who is doing what.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — where a twelve-line day sits in the course, and what its title says.

Every reading lesson so far has climbed one rung. Volume 1 ended its script work with an
eight-sentence passage about one schoolboy, told almost entirely in one person (v1.c06.l03).
Volume 2 then spent three chapters filling the shelves — greetings, family, numbers and
time — and this chapter gave the verbs their names. Today's passage is the first text in
the course that *needs* the chapter's pattern to be read correctly: three persons share
one page, and only the verb endings keep them apart.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** क्रिया (kriyā) means "action, doing" and, in the grammarians' usage,
> "a verb"; कथा (kathā) means "story, tale, talk". Both are ordinary classical words.
> [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.vv. क्रिया, कथा]

The title क्रियाकथा is the two put side by side — "a verb-story", a story made of
actions. It is this lesson's own transparent label, not the name of a traditional genre,
and the course reads it as one whole compound today: क्रिया + कथा, glossed once, built
nothing from. The chapter's own title, क्रियाः, is the first word's plural — again
read-only; plurals belong to Volume 3.

One line of course history: the 1.x curriculum never asked a learner to read connected
Sanskrit that used the verb pattern — its verb tables arrived without texts to read them
in. This passage is that missing step, placed exactly where it belongs: after the pattern
is owned, before the exam asks you to narrate your own day with it.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why one passage, read many times, at rising speeds.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Fluency development is a distinct strand of language learning:
> timed re-reading of *known* material builds automatic word recognition, and it works
> best when the text contains little or nothing new. [REF:nation2013]

That is why today's passage holds zero new words. Every item is yours already; the work
is speed of recognition — and, this time, speed of *person*-recognition. A reader who
decodes लिखामि letter by letter knows it means "write" long before noticing the -मि that
says *I*. Repeated timed reading moves that last syllable from something you work out to
something you simply see.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Answering questions from memory strengthens what was read more than
> re-reading alone does — the testing effect. [REF:roediger2006]

So the lesson's loop is fixed: read, close the book, answer (§16, §18 exercise 1), check,
re-read faster. And the schedule keeps working after the bell: lesson 3's +1-day station
ran in §3, and this lesson's own +1-day lands at the chapter exam (§20) [REF:cepeda2006].

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the lesson's idea in three tiny lines you fully own:

> **सः गच्छति। अहम् गच्छामि। त्वम् गच्छसि।**
> (saḥ gacchati. aham gacchāmi. tvam gacchasi.) "He goes. I go. You go." — वह जाता
> है। मैं जाता/जाती हूँ। तुम जाते/जाती हो।

> **अर्जुनः वदति — अहम् जानामि!**
> (arjunaḥ vadati — aham jānāmi!) "Arjuna says: I know!" — अर्जुन कहता है — मैं जानता
> हूँ!

> **त्वम् किम् करोषि?**
> (tvam kim karoṣi?) "What are you doing?" — तुम क्या कर रहे/रही हो?

The first line is the chapter's pattern; the second is how a story lets a *he* say *I*;
the third is the passage turning toward the reader. Same verbs, three people — and the
last syllable of each verb tells you which one is speaking or being spoken to.

## 8. English Explanation

Nothing new in the script, nothing new in the ledger. What is new is the *load*: a text
in which three different people hold the verb, and the reader has to keep them apart at
speed.

### Three persons, one text — the design point

Read the passage once and count who acts. अर्जुनः — the boy — carries almost every line
in the **he-form**: गच्छति, पठति, खादति, पिबति. Twice the boy talks to himself, and the
story quotes him in his own **I-form**: अहम् लिखामि, अहम् जानामि. Once, between lunch
and evening, the story stops narrating and turns to the reader in the **you-form**: त्वम्
किम् करोषि? All three persons of the chapter's pattern appear in one connected text, and
that is the whole design: lesson 2 named -मि, -सि and -ति as a table; today you meet them
the way real texts deliver them — mixed, unannounced, at reading pace. Nothing on the
page labels who is speaking. The ending does.

### The quote frame — how a *he* says *I*

Lines 5 and 6 open with a speaking verb and a dash: सः वदति — अहम् लिखामि। "He says:
I am writing." The dash opens the boy's own words, so the अहम् inside them is अर्जुनः,
not you — the same device the course has used since v1.c04.l01's story (कविः वदति — जय!).
Line 6 swaps in a different opener, सः हसति — "he laughs:" — and the same rule holds.
Spot the dash, switch the speaker, read the ending.

### The reader-question — the text looks up

Line 8, त्वम् किम् करोषि?, is the only sentence not about अर्जुनः. The -षि of करोषि is
the you-form of lesson 3's whole-word row, and nobody in the story is being addressed —
so it is addressed to *you*. Notice the order: lesson 3 asked किम् त्वम् करोषि?; the
passage fronts त्वम् — "and you — what are you doing?" Both orders are good Sanskrit;
fronting simply puts the weight on the person. The honest answer is never about Arjuna:
it is अहम् … in your own I-form, and it is true.

### The verbs on duty

Eight of the ten bank verbs work today — गच्छति, पठति, वदति, हसति, लिखति (as लिखामि),
जानाति (as जानामि), खादति, पिबति — plus करोति's you-form करोषि and the frame verb
अस्ति. पश्यति and शृणोति sit this passage out; §18 exercise 5 invites them back. Two
reminders from the chapter: जानामि is a whole-word row (जानाति builds its stem
differently — l01, l02), and करोषि is a whole word from lesson 3's chunk row; you
*recognize* their endings, you do not build them.

### The objects — whole words, as always

विद्यालयम्, पुस्तकम्, संस्कृतम्, अन्नम्, गृहम्, क्षीरम्: every object in the passage
wears म्, and every one is glossed whole — "to school", "a book", "food", "home",
"milk". The ending system that explains the म् is Volume 3's subject (v1.c06.l03 §15);
today nothing about it is taught, and nothing needs to be.

### The female mirror

Retell the story for a girl and only two things change: the name (बालिका, or माया) and
the pointer — सा in place of सः, the pair met in v1.c06.l03. Every verb stays exactly as
it is: बालिका विद्यालयम् गच्छति। सा वदति — अहम् लिखामि। The Sanskrit present verb
marks the person, not whether the person is a girl or a boy (§15). Only the Hindi gloss
changes — जाता becomes जाती.

### The text

Thirty-five words, twelve sentences, every one already yours. Read it once now, slowly;
§9 gives you the speeds, and §17 walks it line by line.

> प्रभातम् अस्ति। अर्जुनः विद्यालयम् गच्छति। सः पुस्तकम् पठति। सः संस्कृतम् पठति।
> सः वदति — अहम् लिखामि। सः हसति — अहम् जानामि! सः अन्नम् खादति। त्वम् किम् करोषि?
> सायम् अस्ति। सः गृहम् गच्छति। सः क्षीरम् पिबति। रात्रिः अस्ति।

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — the fluency protocol: one passage, three speeds, one target time, every
ending heard. Audio: 🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c04l04-passage-three-speeds.mp3`

**Drill 1 — the tail ping-pong.** Before the text, the endings alone, loud and clean:
लिखामि — लिखति — लिखामि — लिखति; then जानामि — जानाति; then करोषि — करोति. The I-form
has two signals, a stretched आ *and* a closing म — make both audible. If लिखामि and
लिखति start to sound alike at speed, slow down here first.

**Speed 1 — भागशः (bhāgaśaḥ) — slow: block by block, ≈ 100 seconds.** Every akṣara gets its beat:
प्र-भा-तम् अ-स्ति। Accuracy only. Once with the audio, once alone.

**Speed 2 — वाक्यशः (vākyaśaḥ) — natural: sentence pace, ≈ 60 seconds.** Whole words, whole
sentences; breathe at each daṇḍa. Give the quoted words in lines 5–6 a slightly
different voice — the boy talking to himself — and let line 8 land as a real question to
a real listener. This is the speed the chapter exam re-times (§20).

**Speed 3 — द्रुतपाठः (drutapāṭhaḥ) — timed: reading pace, target under 45 seconds.** The same bar the
first passage set in Volume 1 — for half again as many words. It counts only with **zero
clipped finals and zero swapped endings**: every final म् closed, every visarga breathed,
every -मि still a -मि. A fast read with "likhati" for लिखामि scores nothing; re-time.

**Drill 2 — the level question.** त्वम् किम् करोषि? is a question-word question, so it
keeps the level tune — the word किम् does the asking (v2.c01.l02's rule, on duty in
v2.c04.l03). The same holds for every कः/किम्/कुत्र/कति question in §16. Only yes/no
questions rise.

**Drill 3 — the trap read.** The audio reads the passage once with four endings
silently swapped. Follow along in the book and raise a hand (or tick the margin) at each
swap. This is §12 mistake 1, trained as a listening reflex; §18 exercise 4 runs it on
paper.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — four checks, mirror in hand; three of them map straight onto the persons.

1. **The I-form closes your lips (-मि).** लिखामि, जानामि: watch the mirror — the lips
   meet on म right before the final इ. If your lips never close, you have read -ति.
2. **The he-form touches your teeth (-ति).** गच्छति, पठति, खादति, पिबति: tongue tip on
   the back of the upper teeth for त — no lip contact at all.
3. **This passage's you-form curls (करोषि).** Tongue tip curled back toward the dome for
   ष, then the इ. Lips stay open. That ष is this word's own: the regular you-tail -सि
   keeps the tongue at the teeth, beside -ति (§10).
4. **The finals hold (म्, ः).** विद्यालयम्, पुस्तकम्, अन्नम्, गृहम्, क्षीरम् end with
   lips closed and *staying* closed; अर्जुनः, सः, रात्रिः end on a short clean breath. At
   speed 3 these are the first to go — mirror-check them on the timed run.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real reading-speed errors, each with its fix. These feed the quiz
distractors.

1. **Person endings skimmed — -मि read as -ति.** At speed the eye grabs the verb's front
   (लिख-) and guesses the tail. अहम् लिखामि comes out "likhati", and "I am writing"
   silently becomes "he writes" — the doer has changed. *Fix:* §9 drill 1 (the tail
   ping-pong), §9 drill 3 and §18 exercise 4 (catch the planted swaps). The rule for
   every timed run: the ending is read, not guessed — a swapped ending scores the run
   zero.
2. **The quote frame missed.** Reading सः वदति — अहम् लिखामि and taking अहम् to be the
   reader. *Fix:* the dash opens the boy's own words (§8); the I inside a quote belongs
   to whoever वदति or हसति. Comprehension check: कः लिखति? — अर्जुनः लिखति।
3. **The reader-question answered about Arjuna.** त्वम् किम् करोषि? answered "सः अन्नम्
   खादति". *Fix:* -षि is *you*; the answer is about you, in the I-form: अहम् …
4. **Person mismatch in the answer.** अहम् पठति — the chapter's flagged error (v2.c04.l03
   §12), which loves exactly this moment. *Fix:* अहम् takes -मि, always: अहम् पठामि।
5. **The plural reach.** Trying to tell the day for two — "Arjuna and Māyā go home" — by
   inventing an ending, or by putting two doers on one singular verb (अर्जुनः माया च
   गच्छति). *Fix:* dual and plural verb forms exist and are deferred (Volume 3); this
   course reads and writes the singular only. Two doers get two sentences: अर्जुनः गृहम्
   गच्छति। माया अपि गृहम् गच्छति।
6. **The mirror overreach.** Retelling for a girl and "feminizing" the verb on the Hindi
   model (लिखता → लिखती), as if Sanskrit लिखामि had to change. *Fix:* §15 — the Sanskrit
   present verb does not mark gender; only सः → सा and the name change.
7. **Speed strips the finals.** "vidyālay", "pustak", "sa" for सः. *Fix:* the target time
   counts only with every final intact (§9 speed 3); drop one speed, rebuild, re-time.

### सः संस्कृतम् पठति। — the he-form, the story's engine

(saḥ saṃskṛtam paṭhati.) "He reads Sanskrit." — वह संस्कृत पढ़ता है।

Line 4 is line 6 of the course's first passage, word for word (v1.c06.l03 §17) — the
first text quoted inside the second. पठति /pɐʈʰɐt̪i/: the -ति lands on the teeth, and
the doer is the सः who has meant अर्जुनः since line 3.

### सः वदति — अहम् लिखामि। — two persons in one sentence

(saḥ vadati — aham likhāmi.) "He says: 'I am writing.'" — वह कहता है — मैं लिख रहा
हूँ।

वदति is the story talking *about* him; लिखामि is the boy talking about *himself*. The
dash is the hinge. Report it back from outside and the ending swings home to the
he-form: अर्जुनः लिखति। — exactly the move §16's teacher asks for.

### सः हसति — अहम् जानामि! — the shelf's verb, first person

(saḥ hasati — aham jānāmi!) "He laughs: 'I know!'" — वह हँसता है — मैं जानता हूँ!

जानामि is a whole-word row (जानाति builds its stem differently — v2.c04.l01–l02); read
its ending, never build it. One recall line and no more: जानाति already has its shelf —
जानाति, English *know* and Greek *gnṓsis* are siblings by descent, nobody borrowed
(v2.c04.l01 §13). No other word in the passage gets a cognate line today, because none
has one this course has verified — honesty beats reaching.

### त्वम् किम् करोषि? — the you-form, pointed outward

(tvam kim karoṣi?) "What are you doing?" — तुम क्या कर रहे/रही हो?

The only line nobody in the story is asked. Its answer is yours, in the I-form, and true
— for example **अहम् क्षीरम् पिबामि।** (aham kṣīram pibāmi.) "I am drinking milk." —
मैं दूध पी रहा/रही हूँ। Answering in the he-form, or about Arjuna, is §12 mistakes 3–4.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — a reading lesson's grammar: no new rule, four things to notice, one fence.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** (1) In the present singular the ending names the person: -मि "I",
> -सि "you" (करोषि writes it -षि), -ति "he/she/it". Within one text the person can change
> from sentence to sentence, and the ending is the reader's one reliable signal of who
> acts. (2) The Sanskrit present verb marks person, not gender: अहम् लिखामि is said
> alike by a girl and a boy; सः and सा carry the difference. (3) Because the ending
> already carries the person, Sanskrit texts often leave the subject pronoun out —
> जानामि! alone means "I know!". (4) Classical prose closes quoted words with the
> particle इति. [REF:whitney1889]

### What that means for today

- **Read the tail before you move on.** Point (1) is the lesson: a skimmed ending is a
  changed doer (§12 mistake 1).
- **The mirror is cheap.** Point (2) is why the girl's version of the story changes two
  words per line at most — the name and the pointer — and no verb at all.
- **The pronouns are training wheels.** Point (3) is a preview, read-only: this course
  keeps अहम्, त्वम्, सः in every sentence for now, and real texts will take them away.
  When they do, the ending will be all you have — which is why today's drills train it.
- **The dash is ours.** Point (4) is the honest footnote to lines 5–6: the dash that
  opens the boy's words is the course's pause-form stand-in. The classical closer is
  इति, which you have read as "thus, the quoting word" since v1.c02.l01; quotations with
  इति arrive with real texts.

### The fence — what the passage never does

| The passage does | It never does | Where that lives |
|---|---|---|
| one doer per verb, singular | two doers on one verb; dual or plural forms | Volume 3 (laṭ in full) |
| the present tense only | yesterday's or tomorrow's actions | Volumes 3–4 |
| objects glossed whole (विद्यालयम्, गृहम्) | case endings explained | Volume 3 |
| जानामि, करोषि as whole words | building them from stems | Volume 4 |

Simplest correct beats ambitious wrong — the rule the chapter exam's task E is built on.

reader-question come to life: asked in the you-form, answered truthfully in the I-form —
with माया borrowing अर्जुनः's own triumph, अपि and all.

> (bālikā vidyālayam gacchati. sā vadati — aham likhāmi.) "A girl goes to school. She

three words to follow her; the Sanskrit changed none of its verbs (§15).

v2.c01.l03, each with अस्ति, holding the story upright. Lines 2–4 set the thread: अर्जुनः
is named once and becomes सः, and line 4 quietly quotes the course's first passage.
Lines 5–6 hand the verb to the boy himself — **the I-form, inside the dash**. Line 7
returns to the he-form for lunch. Then line 8 breaks the frame, the way the first
passage's eighth line did — but where that line was a statement (*I too read
Sanskrit*), this one is a question, and its -षि points straight off the page: **the
you-form, addressed to the reader**. Lines 9–12 take the boy home to his milk and the
day to its night, back in **the he-form**. The boy in 3sg, his self-talk in 1sg, the
reader-question in 2sg: every person of the pattern, one day, one page.

## 22. Teacher Notes

अध्यापकटिप्पणी

**Protect line 8.** On the board and in the first choral read, present lines 1–7 only.
Work the thread, the quotes, the questions — then write line 8 alone on a clean board and
let the room read it cold. Let the question hang a beat, then go round the room: every
student answers truthfully, in the I-form, at once. Staged, the turn is felt; unstaged,
it is one more line.

**Timing plan (45 min):** Station 0 + prompts, scores logged (6) → first read at slow
speed, lines 1–7, choral then pairs (7) → the person board: three columns सः / अहम् /
त्वम्, verbs sorted live from the text (6) → LINE 8 staged; the room answers (4) → §16
question-answer in pairs, book closed, roles swapping (7) → natural and first timed runs,
logged; the trap read (8) → mirror story, one oral round (4) → homework: the मम दिनम्
seed list walked through once (3).

**Board work:** the three-column person board — सः · -ति / अहम् · -मि / त्वम् · -सि —
with every passage verb pinned under its column (करोषि goes under -सि as a whole word
that keeps its own ष), and a clock strip प्रभातम् → सायम् → रात्रिः above the text. Leave
the board up through the timed runs; students who swap an ending should be able to look
up and see where it belongs.

**Hold the fence.** Someone will want to tell the day "with my friends" or say "we go
home". Do not open the plural. Give the honest line — plural forms exist and wait for
Volume 3 — and the working move: two doers, two sentences, अपि doing the joining
(अर्जुनः गृहम् गच्छति। माया अपि गृहम् गच्छति।). Log who asked; they are your Volume 3
readers.

**Differentiation:** Hindi-literate learners read the text nearly on sight; their work
is the gender reflex (the mirror, §12 mistake 6) and the finals at speed 3 — put them on
exercise 5 and on policing "likhati"-type swaps in their pair. Script-newcomers need the
slow speed twice before any timing and the paper swap-hunt (exercise 4) before the audio
trap read; their win today is one clean natural-speed run.

**What students find hard:** hearing -मि against -ति at speed (§11's lip check works
faster than explanation); keeping the quoted I apart from their own I; answering line 8
about themselves rather than about Arjuna; and, at homework, resisting the past tense in
मम दिनम्. Check every draft for person agreement before exam day — and cut ambition back
to the present singular rather than adding grammar to rescue it.
