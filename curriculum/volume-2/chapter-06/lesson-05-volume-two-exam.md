## 1. Hook

Volume 2 began with one question: अपि कुशलम्? Six chapters later you can count, name the
day, conjugate a verb, ask why, buy something at a stall, and read two whole stories. You
also know where those stories come from, which is more than the 1.x book knew. Today all
of it counts at once. You hear a story, read another aloud, sort ten claims the volume
taught you to check, and then tell a story yourself in Sanskrit you own. 360 words.
Pass, and Volume 2 is complete.

## 3. Previous Lesson Review

पुनरावृत्तिः — four stations plus Station 0. Answer from memory *before* opening any
key [REF:roediger2006].

### Station 0 — l04's one-day interval

The four lessons' +1-day and +1-week schedules converge today [REF:cepeda2006]. This
station runs exactly what l04 §20 booked: one item, three minutes, logged beside your l04
sort.

1. Ten word pairs sorted cold into the four kinds (cognate / loan / Latin route / false):
   tooth ~ दन्तः · dental ~ दन्तः · yoga ~ योगः · juggle ~ युग "yoke; age" · foot ~
   पादः · karma ~ कर्म · widow ~ विधवा · serpent ~ सर्पः · middle ~ मध्यम् · video ~ √विद्
   "know".

<details><summary>उत्तराणि · Station 0 key</summary>

**Cognate** (shared descent, no arrow): tooth ~ दन्तः · foot ~ पादः · widow ~ विधवा ·
middle ~ मध्यम्. **Loan** (English took the word itself from Sanskrit): yoga ← योगः
"yoking, union" (not युग) · karma ← कर्म. **Latin route** (an English word from Latin
whose root is cognate with the Sanskrit one): dental (Latin *dēns*) · serpent (Latin
*serpēns*) · video (Latin *videō* "I see"). **False:** juggle (from Latin *ioculārī* "to
jest"), unrelated to युग. 8+/10 clean → on to Station 1. → l04 §13; notes §0.9.
</details>

### Station 1 — पिपासितः काकः (lesson 1)

1. Tell the crow story's first five sentences from memory, in pause form.
2. Give the moral and its gloss. What does एव do in it?
3. Where is the story known from? One honest line, and the one claim the course refuses
   to make.

<details><summary>उत्तराणि · Station 1 key</summary>

1. एकः काकः अस्ति। सः पिपासितः अस्ति। सः जलम् इच्छति। सः एकम् घटम् पश्यति। घटे अल्पम्
   जलम् अस्ति। (घटे "in the pot" is glossed whole.) → l01 §17.
2. बुद्धिः एव बलम्। "Intelligence itself is strength." एव "itself, alone" puts weight
   on the word before it, as in Volume 1's सत्यम् एव जयते. → l01 §17.
3. It is an Aesopic fable ("The Crow and the Pitcher", Perry 390); its earliest known
   versions are Greek and Latin, and no version is known in the Pañcatantra, the
   Hitopadeśa or the Jātakas. Refused: "it does not come from India". The evidence shows
   that no version is attested, and says nothing about origin. → l01 §4; §4 below.
</details>

### Station 2 — सिंहः मूषकः च (lesson 2)

1. मुञ्चति and मुञ्च: gloss both, and say which one is a request.
2. Say the moral in pause form, then joined, and describe the seam in one sentence.
3. Name the Indian cousin of this story, and the claim the course does not make.

<details><summary>उत्तराणि · Station 2 key</summary>

1. मुञ्चति "releases, lets go" (the 3sg headword); मुञ्च "let go!", a request (imperative)
   chunk learned whole, like वदतु, and counted as its own word (STYLE_GUIDE §8 rule 4).
   → l02 §14, §15.
2. न कः अपि लघुः। → न कोऽपि लघुः। Before a word beginning with अ, the whole ending -अः
   becomes -ओ, and the following अ disappears, marked by the avagraha ऽ. → l02 §15.
3. In Pūrṇabhadra's recension of the Pañcatantra (Book 2), mice repay a herd of
   elephants by gnawing through the ropes that tie them. The course makes no claim about
   which story came first. → l02 §4; §4 below.
</details>

### Station 3 — सुभाषितानि (lesson 3)

1. Recite अहिंसा परमः धर्मः in pause form, then as the epic joins it, and name the seam.
2. Which popular continuation gets attached to this line, and what is its status?
3. Finish the first line of उद्यमेन हि सिध्यन्ति…, gloss it, and give its locus.

<details><summary>उत्तराणि · Station 3 key</summary>

1. अहिंसा परमो धर्मः: परमः + धर्मः → परमो धर्मः, because -अः becomes -ओ before a voiced
   consonant. → l03 §8, §15.
2. "धर्महिंसा तथैव च". It is not in the critical edition of the Mahābhārata at any of the
   line's five places; it is a documented modern misquotation, never scripture. → l03 §4.
3. उद्यमेन हि सिध्यन्ति कार्याणि न मनोरथैः। "Tasks succeed through effort, not through
   wishing." Hitopadeśa, introduction (prastāvikā), verse 36. → l03 §8.
</details>

### Station 4 — शब्दयात्राः (lesson 4)

1. विमानम्: what is it built from, and what is wrong with the 1.x gloss of मान?
2. दूरदर्शनम्: gloss each part and give the word its label.
3. In one line: why is *yoga* "from Sanskrit" while *video* is not?

<details><summary>उत्तराणि · Station 4 key</summary>

1. वि + √मा "measure out, traverse". The 1.x book glossed मान as a vehicle, which it is
   not. विमानम् is a classical word (a measuring-out, then the gods' aerial car in epic
   poetry) given the modern sense "aeroplane". → l04 §13, §14.
2. दूर "far" + दर्शन "seeing": a modern calque on *tele-vision*, labelled modern.
   → l04 §14.
3. *Yoga* is a loan: English took the word itself from Sanskrit योग. *Video* is Latin
   *videō* "I see", whose root is cognate with Sanskrit √विद् "know". English got it from
   Latin, and Sanskrit lent nothing. → l04 §13.
</details>

Score: Station 0 at 8+/10, then 10+ of the twelve prompts in Stations 1–4 clean →
proceed to the exam. Below that, re-study the weakest station today and sit the exam
tomorrow. The extra day is spacing, and it helps [REF:cepeda2006].

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — one paragraph, as in every review lesson, then the evidence the
exam leans on.

Volume 1 ended with the script complete and three texts honestly labelled. Volume 2
began with a greeting and ends with stories, and in between it ran one discipline on
every chapter: say how each thing is known. Chapter 1 labelled each courtesy formula
classical or modern-spoken. Chapter 2 drew the kinship cognates with no arrow between
the siblings. Chapter 3 corrected the month-name myth and drew होरा's arrow the other
way, from Greek into Sanskrit. Chapter 4 named the verb pattern you already owned.
Chapter 5 explained मह्यम् रोचते. Chapter 6 gave two famous stories and three sayings
checkable sources. The 1.x course printed much of this material. The 2.0 course kept it
and corrected it in the open, and today's exam tests only what the volume delivered.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** "The Thirsty Crow" is an Aesopic fable ("The Crow and the
> Pitcher", Perry 390). Its earliest known versions are Greek and Latin, and no version
> is known in the Pañcatantra, the Hitopadeśa or the Jātakas; the "Pañcatantra story"
> label it often carries today is modern. "The Lion and the Mouse" is also Aesopic
> (Perry 150), and the same tale is told inside an Egyptian narrative written in Demotic
> in the 2nd century CE. India's fable books have cousins of it: in Pūrṇabhadra's
> recension of the Pañcatantra (Book 2), mice gnaw through the ropes that tie a herd of
> elephants. Stories like these travelled widely; the course makes no claim about which
> came first. [REF:perry1952] (nos. 390, 150); [REF:lichtheim1980]; [REF:ryder1925]

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** अहिंसा परमो धर्मः "non-harming is the highest dharma" occurs
> five times in the critical edition of the Mahābhārata (1.11.12; 3.198.69; 13.116.1;
> 13.116.25; 13.117.37). The popular continuation "धर्महिंसा तथैव च" is not in the
> critical edition at any of these places; it is a documented modern misquotation.
> उद्यमेन हि सिध्यन्ति कार्याणि न मनोरथैः… is Hitopadeśa, introduction (prastāvikā),
> verse 36. [REF:mahabharata 1.11.12]; [REF:hitopadesha prastāvikā 36]

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** In the subhāṣita collections, परोपकारः पुण्याय पापाय
> परपीडनम् is traditionally quoted as the two-word essence of Vyāsa's teaching, usually
> after the line अष्टादशपुराणेषु व्यासस्य वचनद्वयम्. That first line is not traced to any
> Purāṇa; it is a later summary that attributes the thought to Vyāsa.
> [REF:apte1890 s.v. पर]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spaced practice produces far more durable learning than the same
> practice massed together. [REF:cepeda2006]

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why a volume exam ends on a story you tell, not one you recite.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Retrieval practice strengthens memory more than re-reading —
> the testing effect. [REF:roediger2006]

Each task retrieves a different layer of the volume. Task A asks you to understand
Sanskrit at speaking pace, and the asker at the front of each question tells you what
kind of answer to look for. Task C climbs the ladder the volume itself climbed, from a
two-word greeting to a whole story to a verse. Every rung retrieves the one below it at
speed, which is the fluency strand at work on known material [REF:nation2013]. Task D
checks boundary knowledge: what the volume claimed, how firmly, and what it refused to
claim.

Task E is the hardest and the most useful. Reciting a story tests memory for a text.
Retelling one makes you rebuild every sentence from owned parts: a verb in the right
person, a noun with its ending, the story's turn in the right place. Retrieval plus
recombination is the strongest rehearsal pair there is [REF:roediger2006]. That is why
the retelling scores owned sentences, not the original's order, and why the role-card
conversation, which you passed once in Chapter 5, comes back a chapter later: a skill
retrieved after a gap is a skill that lasts [REF:cepeda2006].

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the exam's frames, fully readable:

> **बुद्धिः एव बलम्।**
> (buddhiḥ eva balam.) "Intelligence itself is strength." — बुद्धि ही बल है।

> **न कः अपि लघुः। — न कोऽपि लघुः।**
> (na kaḥ api laghuḥ. — na ko'pi laghuḥ.) "No one is small." — कोई भी छोटा नहीं है।

> **अहिंसा परमः धर्मः। — अहिंसा परमो धर्मः।**
> (ahiṃsā paramaḥ dharmaḥ. — ahiṃsā paramo dharmaḥ.) "Non-harming is the highest
> dharma." — अहिंसा परम धर्म है।

> **अहम् काकः अस्मि। अहम् पिपासितः अस्मि। अहम् पाषाणम् क्षिपामि।**
> (aham kākaḥ asmi. aham pipāsitaḥ asmi. aham pāṣāṇam kṣipāmi.) "I am a crow. I am
> thirsty. I throw a stone." — मैं कौआ हूँ। मैं प्यासा हूँ। मैं पत्थर डालता हूँ।

The last frame is today's real lesson in Sanskrit. The story was told in the he-form;
you can tell it in the I-form, because the person lives inside the word: क्षिपति
becomes क्षिपामि by the Chapter 4 pattern, and nothing else has to change. The exam's
grammar fits in one line: pause form, present singular, the person inside the verb, and
role-dependent endings (घटे, वने, सिंहस्य) read whole.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

आज की परीक्षा किसी एक अध्याय की नहीं, पूरे दूसरे खण्ड की है। तीन बातें ध्यान में रखिए।

सुनेंगे, फिर नौ प्रश्नवाचक शब्दों वाले बारह प्रश्नों के उत्तर देंगे। एक शब्द का उत्तर पूरा
उत्तर है; जहाँ उत्तर के लिए ऐसा व्याकरण चाहिए जो अभी नहीं पढ़ाया गया, वहाँ अंग्रेज़ी या
हिन्दी में उत्तर दीजिए — यह सुनने की परीक्षा है, बोलने की नहीं। कार्य B श्रुतलेख है। कार्य C
पठन-सोपान है: अभिवादन से शुरू होकर परिवार, तिथि, क्रियाएँ, फिर प्यासे कौए की पूरी कथा

मनोरथैः।

दूसरी — **कार्य E: कथा आपकी अपनी।** पिछले पाठ के गृहकार्य में आपने एक कथा चुनी थी। आज
उसे बिना पन्ने के, अपनी संस्कृत में, कम से कम छह वाक्यों में सुनाना है। कथा को रटकर दोहराना
नहीं है; उसे फिर से गढ़ना है। आप क्रम बदल सकते हैं, कोई छोटा ब्योरा छोड़ सकते हैं, और
चाहें तो कौए को ख़ुद बोलने दें: अहम् पिपासितः अस्मि। अहम् पाषाणम् क्षिपामि। — "मैं"
वाला रूप चौथे अध्याय के नियम से अपने-आप बनता है। शर्त बस इतनी है कि कथा वही रहे: शुरुआत,
समस्या, मोड़, अन्त और नीति-वाक्य। उसके बाद पाँचवें अध्याय के भूमिका-कार्डों से एक छोटा

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — exam-day checks; reference values live in the lesson tables. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c06l05-listening-test.mp3`,
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c06l05-dictation-set.mp3`.

**The story at natural speed.** Rung 5 is read at sentence pace, the middle speed of the
three-speed protocol (v2.c04.l04 §9, l01). Speed comes from fewer pauses between words,
never from shorter words: every final vowel full (पिबति, never "pibat"), every visarga
breathed (पिपासितः, पाषाणः), every final म् closed (जलम्, घटम्). पुनः पुनः is two words,
two visargas.

**The welds of Chapter 6.** क्ष in क्षिपति (one onset, no vowel between क and ष) · द्ध in
बुद्धिः · ह्ण in गृह्णाति · ञ्च in मुञ्च · न्त in कृन्तति · द्य in उद्यमेन · ध्य in
सिध्यन्ति. The anusvāra of अहिंसा and सिंहः nasalizes the vowel before the sibilant or ह.

**The verse.** Read it in pause form, one half-line at a time, with a hair of silence at
the daṇḍa. It has no joins between words; मनोरथैः is one word. Melody belongs to
recitation practice and is welcome at home, but today scores the plain reading.

**The seams you only read.** कोऽपि is two syllables with a long pure ओ; the ऽ is not
pronounced. परमो धर्मः keeps the same pure ओ, never English "go".

**Warm-up before recording:** the nine askers once at pace; बुद्धिः एव बलम् three times,
slow to natural; the verse's first half-line once.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — three worked models before you start.

**Model retelling (task E).** The learner chose the lion story. Nine sentences, all in
owned Sanskrit, with the original's words reused and its order changed:

1. एकदा एकः सिंहः वने स्वपिति। — "Once a lion is sleeping in the forest."
2. एकः मूषकः आगच्छति। — "A mouse comes."
3. सिंहः क्रुद्धः भवति। सः मूषकम् गृह्णाति। — "The lion gets angry. He seizes the mouse."
4. मूषकः वदति — माम् मुञ्च! — "The mouse says: 'Let me go!'"
5. सिंहः हसति। सः मूषकम् मुञ्चति। — "The lion laughs. He lets the mouse go."
6. एकदा सिंहः जाले बद्धः भवति। — "One day the lion is caught in a net."
7. मूषकः जालम् कृन्तति। — "The mouse cuts the net."
8. सिंहः मुक्तः भवति। सः सुखी भवति। — "The lion is free. He is happy."
9. न कः अपि लघुः। — "No one is small."

In Hindi: एक बार एक शेर जंगल में सोता है। एक चूहा आता है। शेर क्रोधित होकर चूहे को पकड़
लेता है। चूहा कहता है — मुझे छोड़ दो! शेर हँसता है और चूहे को छोड़ देता है। एक दिन शेर
जाल में बँध जाता है। चूहा जाल काट देता है। शेर मुक्त और सुखी हो जाता है। कोई भी छोटा
नहीं है।

Why it scores in full: एकदा moved to the opening; the "friend" line was cut and the
story still turns; सुखी was borrowed from the crow story; every verb is a he-form the
learner owns; वने, जाले and माम् are the story's own whole-word forms. Beginning, problem,
turn, ending and moral are all there.

**Model honesty cards (task D).** Card: "The thirsty-crow story is a Pañcatantra tale."
It sounds right, and many school readers print it that way. But it names a source, so
it must survive a source-check, and it does not: no version is known in the Pañcatantra,
the Hitopadeśa or the Jātakas. **Reject**, and replace rather than just delete: "an
Aesopic fable (Perry 390); the Pañcatantra label is modern" is the full-credit line.
Now a second card: "Yoga is a loan from Sanskrit योग." English took the word itself, so
the arrow is real: **FACT**. One story, one word, two verdicts. The skill is the same.

**Model role-card conversation (task E, part 2).** The learner draws the card for
**अर्जुनः** (family line एषः मम भ्राता। · wants जलम् · likes पुस्तकम्):

1. **Greet.** — नमस्ते! अपि कुशलम्? — कुशलम्! सर्वम् कुशलम्।
2. **Introduce.** — मम नाम अर्जुनः। एषः मम भ्राता। (pointing at a sketch)
3. **Want and like.** — किम् इच्छसि? — अहम् जलम् इच्छामि। पुस्तकम् मह्यम् रोचते।
4. **When or why.** (rushed) — किमर्थम् त्वम् पठसि? — अहम् न अवगच्छामि। भवान् पुनः वदतु।
   — अस्तु! किमर्थम् … त्वम् … पठसि? — यतः अहम् छात्रः अस्मि।
5. **Farewell.** — धन्यवादः! स्वस्ति! — स्वस्ति! पुनः मिलामः!

Five moves, one rescue, no names called out (the card's name goes in मम नाम …, never in
an address).

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — the volume's rules on one page, the honesty program in one table, and the
deferral tracker handed on to Volume 3.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** **The person pattern:** a Sanskrit finite verb marks its person
> inside the word — present singular -मि (I) · -सि (you) · -ति (he/she/it), with the
> stretch to -आमि (गच्छामि, क्षिपामि); शृणोति, जानाति, करोति, गृह्णाति and स्वपिति build
> their stems differently and are learned whole. **The askers:** कः का किम् कुतः कथम्
> कुत्र कति कदा किमर्थम्, each asking for its own kind of answer; a bare answer is
> complete. **The liking build:** फलम् मह्यम् रोचते, literally "the fruit pleases me".
> **The request chunks:** वदतु, ददातु, अस्तु, मुञ्च are imperatives learned whole.
> **Pause form:** every word complete at every seam. **The seams seen, not yet taught:**
> a final -अः becomes -ओ before a voiced consonant (परमः धर्मः → परमो धर्मः), and before
> अ, where the अ also drops, marked ऽ (कः अपि → कोऽपि). [REF:whitney1889]; [REF:apte1890]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** **The direction toolkit:** a **cognate** pair shares descent from
> one ancestor, and nobody borrowed (माता ~ Latin *māter* ~ *mother*). A **loan** is a word
> taken across, and the arrow can run either way: *yoga* and *karma* went from Sanskrit
> into English, and होरा came into Sanskrit from Greek. A **Latin route** word reached
> English from Latin, whose root is cognate with a Sanskrit one (*video*, *serpent*,
> *dental*, *October*). A **false** pair only looks related (*juggle* and युग).
> [REF:mallory2006]; [REF:oed]; [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. होरा]

is thirsty…" — मैं पढ़ रहा हूँ। एक कौआ है। वह प्यासा है…

शेर पसंद है! कोई भी छोटा नहीं!

through the turn, सिंहः मह्यम् रोचते, the lion pleases me. Both morals are quoted whole,

## 17. Story

कथा — the volume's last story, and the first one told by you: **the crow, in his own
words.** Volume 1's last story was six lines read by you. Volume 2's is told by you, in
the I-form, from a story you first met in the he-form. Each line: Devanagari, IAST,
English, Hindi.

1. अहम् काकः अस्मि। अहम् पिपासितः अस्मि।
   (aham kākaḥ asmi. aham pipāsitaḥ asmi.) "I am a crow. I am thirsty." — मैं कौआ हूँ।
   मैं प्यासा हूँ।
2. अहम् जलम् इच्छामि।
   (aham jalam icchāmi.) "I want water." — मैं पानी चाहता हूँ।
3. अहम् एकम् घटम् पश्यामि। घटे अल्पम् जलम् अस्ति।
   (aham ekam ghaṭam paśyāmi. ghaṭe alpam jalam asti.) "I see a pot. There is a little
   water in the pot." — मैं एक घड़ा देखता हूँ। घड़े में थोड़ा पानी है।
4. अहम् पाषाणम् क्षिपामि। पुनः पुनः क्षिपामि।
   (aham pāṣāṇam kṣipāmi. punaḥ punaḥ kṣipāmi.) "I throw a stone. I throw again and
   again." — मैं पत्थर डालता हूँ। बार-बार डालता हूँ।
5. जलम् उपरि आगच्छति! अहम् जलम् पिबामि।
   (jalam upari āgacchati! aham jalam pibāmi.) "The water comes up! I drink the water."
   — पानी ऊपर आता है! मैं पानी पीता हूँ।
6. अहम् सुखी अस्मि। बुद्धिः एव बलम्!
   (aham sukhī asmi. buddhiḥ eva balam!) "I am happy. Intelligence itself is strength!"
   — मैं सुखी हूँ। बुद्धि ही बल है!

Three honest footnotes. Every verb is built by the Chapter 4 pattern: क्षिपामि from
क्षिपति, पश्यामि, पिबामि, इच्छामि, each with the stretch to -आमि. Line 5 keeps one
he-form, because the water is still a "he/she/it". And घटे and अल्पम् जलम् are the
story's own phrases, kept whole; the case and the agreement behind them are Volume 3's.

**Pass** = every task at partial-or-better and quiz 7+/10 → **Volume 2 complete**, and
the gate to Volume 3 opens. A task below partial re-runs tomorrow, after its remediation;
frame it as scheduling, not failure. A volume gate is worth a clean close.

**Score the retelling for ownership, not resemblance.** A learner who tells the crow in
the I-form, cuts a sentence, or borrows सुखी for the lion has done the task better than
one who recites l01 word for word. Count a slip only where the learner's own sentence
breaks: a wrong person ending, a clipped final, a lost visarga. The original's own
whole-word forms (घटे, वने, माम्) are never slips. Prompt a beat only after a long stall;
a prompted beat scores partial, not zero.

**The honesty cards are the volume's graduation.** Six cards reject something: a myth
(3, 7), a misquotation (6), a modern label passed off as classical (1, 5), and a
translation mistaken for a construction (9). Card 5 has a trap on both sides. Some
learners will overcorrect to "it does not come from India", and that answer gets no
credit: the evidence supports absence of attestation, nothing more. Ask "how would we
know?", and let the room find the hedge.

**What students find hard:** retelling without the page (rehearse the five beats as five
verbs: पश्यति, क्षिपति, आगच्छति, पिबति, भवति); the reading ladder's rung 5 clock (slow
pass first, then sentence pace); keeping cards 7 and 8 apart (one is a word taken, the
other a root shared); and trusting that a rescue in task E is a success. Redirect ambition
gently: plurals, cases and sandhi are Volume 3's, and say so.

**The completion moment:** quiet room, booklets closed. Say the counts aloud: 237 words
entering, 123 added, 360 leaving; two stories, three sayings, ten honest cards. Then the
class says both morals together, and Volume 2 is closed.
