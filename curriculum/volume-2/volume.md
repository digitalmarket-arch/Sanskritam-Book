# Volume 2 — शब्दाः वाक्यानि च · Words & Sentences

Volume 1 handed over the writing system: 31 lessons, 237 readable words, every sign of
the script named and owned. Volume 2 is what the system is *for*: vocabulary at scale,
real sentence patterns, and the first floor of the verb system — carried by the same
27-section lessons, the same four-register honesty, and the same pause-form reading
convention (sandhi remains Volume 3's subject, previewed at seams, never smuggled).

## What changes from Volume 1

- **The load shifts from script to words.** No new letters or signs exist to teach;
  §9–§11 pivot from articulation labs to prosody, fluency maintenance, and
  listening-speed work. Word loads run 10–16 per lesson [REF:nation2013].
- **Frames grow into patterns.** Volume 1 ended with formulaic chunks (मम नाम …, अहम् …
  अस्मि). Volume 2 keeps teaching chunks first — and from Chapter 4 begins to open them:
  the 10-verb bank and the laṭ present, third person first, as the gentle start of the
  verb ramp. Nouns stay whole-word glossed until Volume 3's case system.
- **A new honesty axis: classical vs modern spoken usage.** The courtesy formulas of
  living spoken Sanskrit (धन्यवादः as everyday "thank you", सुप्रभातम् as "good
  morning") are real, standard, and worth teaching — and some are modern conventions
  rather than classical idiom. The course labels them exactly as it labeled विद्यालयः:
  attested word, modern conversational convention, said so in one honest line. Classical
  courtesy (स्वस्ति, भद्रम् ते) is taught beside them with its own label.
- **The 1.x salvage lands here.** The audited-correct material of the old curriculum —
  thematic vocabulary sets, the 10-verb bank, the graded readers (sandhi-resolved), the
  first subhāṣitas, the Indo-European cognate sets reframed as shared descent — arrives
  across these six chapters per the preservation map in ROADMAP.md.

## Entry and exit

**Entry:** Volume 1 complete — the Volume Exam passed (v1.c06.l04), the three artifacts
in hand. **Exit:** ~450 readable words in active use, the laṭ present (3rd person)
productive, connected dialogues and graded-reader stories read with comprehension, and
the first subhāṣitas recited with sources — the A2 exit that Volume 3's grammar assumes.
