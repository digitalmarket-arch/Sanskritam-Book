## 1. Hook

Say *mother*. Now say माता. You have just said the same word twice — not a translation, not
a borrowing, the *same inherited word*, worn down two different ways by five thousand-odd
years of separate travel. Your course met माता in its fourth lesson, when आ was brand new;
today she finally gets her household: a father, a brother, two words for sister, two words
for daughter, a home to stand in, and one word to hold them all — परिवारः. Chapter 2 opens
at your own front door.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — the oldest words in the book, and two honest divisions of labor.

Kinship words live in the deepest drawer of a language's vocabulary — used daily, learned
in infancy, replaced almost never. That is why today's list reaches further back than
anything else this course has taught, and why it will carry next lesson's showcase.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The core kin terms are ṛ-stems: मातृ (mātṛ), पितृ (pitṛ), भ्रातृ
> (bhrātṛ), दुहितृ (duhitṛ), स्वसृ (svasṛ) — the dictionary's citation shapes, exactly as
> पितृ was noted beside पिता in v1.c03.l05. What you *speak* are the nominative forms this
> lesson teaches: माता, पिता, भ्राता, दुहिता, स्वसा. All are attested across the classical
> language. [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.vv.] [REF:burrow2001]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Sanskrit keeps two working "sister" words with different careers:
> स्वसा, the older word, at home in the Vedic corpus; and भगिनी, the everyday word of
> classical and later Sanskrit. भगिनी is also the ancestor of Hindi बहन, as भ्राता stands
> behind Hindi भाई — regular descent down the Indo-Aryan line, the गृह → घर story again.
> [REF:apte1890 s.v. भगिनी] [REF:masica1991]

The daughter pair divides the same way: पुत्री is the everyday word beside पुत्रः, and
दुहिता is the ancient one — §13 tells you what that one is worth. One line of course
history: the 1.x curriculum's Unit 13 (PDF pp.31–32) taught this family set correctly *as
words* — the audit kept them. What it lacked was the labels and the honest cognate story;
this chapter is that salvage, delivered with the labels on.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why family words, why in frames, and why they carry the evidence.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** New vocabulary sticks best when it arrives inside usable patterns and
> gets retrieved from them, not memorized as bare lists — the frame मम ___ अत्र अस्ति turns
> every word on today's list into a sentence you can actually say the hour you learn it.
> [REF:nation2013]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spaced retrieval beats massed re-study; today's Station 0 is
> v2.c01.l05's +1-week interval landing exactly on schedule, and this lesson's own +1-day
> and +1-week re-runs are set before it ends (§20). [REF:cepeda2006] [REF:roediger2006]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Kin terms are among the most stable items of basic vocabulary —
> rarely borrowed, slowly replaced. That stability is why they preserve the systematic
> sound-correspondences historical linguists use as evidence of shared descent, and why
> the kinship set is the classic showcase of the Indo-European family's unity.
> [REF:mallory2006]

Hold that third fact lightly today: it is the reason this chapter can promise you the
cognate shelf next lesson and mean it, with no magic and no "mother of all languages."

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the family, told in sentences you fully own:

> **मम परिवारः अत्र अस्ति।**
> (mama parivāraḥ atra asti.) "My family is here." — मेरा परिवार यहाँ है।

> **मम पिता अत्र अस्ति। मम भगिनी तत्र अस्ति।**
> (mama pitā atra asti. mama bhaginī tatra asti.) "My father is here. My sister is
> there." — मेरे पिता यहाँ हैं। मेरी बहन वहाँ है।

> **मम भ्राता अर्जुनः।**
> (mama bhrātā arjunaḥ.) "My brother is Arjuna." — मेरा भाई अर्जुन है।

Two frames, both old friends: the location sentence carries अस्ति, exactly as it has
since Volume 1; the naming sentence needs no verb at all — मम भ्राता अर्जुनः is complete,
on the model of अहम् रामः and मम नाम माया (v1.c06.l02). मम in front of a family word is
the whole possessive art for now.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

आज की शब्दावली आधी आपके घर में पहले से रहती है। पिता, माता, पुत्र, पुत्री, परिवार — ये
शब्द हिन्दी ने संस्कृत से सहेजे हैं; और भाई-बहन भी असल में भ्राता-भगिनी की ही सन्तानें हैं,
सदियों की ध्वनि-यात्रा के बाद — वैसे ही जैसे गृह चलते-चलते घर बना। इसलिए पढ़ने में आज कुछ
नया नहीं रोकेगा; काम वही पुराने अनुशासन करेंगे।

पहला — अन्त सँभालिए। परिवारः के अन्त की हल्की साँस (विसर्ग) हिन्दी की आदत में छूट जाती
है — "परिवार" नहीं, परिवारः। गृहम् का म् होंठ बन्द करके पूरा कीजिए, और गृ में ऋ की मात्रा
को "गिर" या "गुर" मत बनने दीजिए। भगिनी और पुत्री की अन्तिम ई पूरी दो मात्राएँ माँगती है —
"भगिनि" नहीं।

दूसरा — एक रिश्ते के दो-दो शब्द देखकर घबराइए मत; यह घाटा नहीं, सम्पदा है। बहन के लिए रोज़
का शब्द भगिनी है; स्वसा उसी अर्थ का पुराना, वैदिक रंग का शब्द है। बेटी के लिए रोज़ का शब्द
पुत्री है — पुत्रः की जोड़ीदार; दुहिता वह पुरानी धरोहर है जिसकी सगी बहन अंग्रेज़ी की
*daughter* है। और दिशा का सच याद रखिए: अंग्रेज़ी *mother* संस्कृत से नहीं आई, न संस्कृत
ने किसी से ली — माता, māter और mother एक ही पुरखे की तीन सन्तानें हैं। उधार कहीं नहीं
हुआ; यही आज का सबसे बड़ा तथ्य है, और पूरी ताालिका अगले पाठ में खुलेगी।

तीसरा — साँचे वही पुराने हैं। मम और तव अब परिवार के काम पर हैं: मम पिता अत्र अस्ति। तव
भ्राता कः? नाम बताना हो तो क्रिया भी नहीं चाहिए: मम भगिनी कमला। एक बात अभी *नहीं* आती —
उँगली से इशारा करके "ये मेरे पिता हैं" कहना; उसके लिए एषः और एषा अगले पाठ में आ रहे हैं।
आज केवल शब्द, साँचे और सच।

## 8. English Explanation

No new letter, no new sign — Volume 2's standing luxury. The load is eight headwords, two
old friends back on duty, two honest word-pairs, and one upgrade.

### The set — ten words on duty

**परिवारः** (parivāraḥ) "family" is the container: classically "the circle around one" —
परि "around", as in the dictionaries' "retinue, surroundings, family"
[REF:apte1890 s.v. परिवार]. Inside it: **माता** (mātā) and **पिता** (pitā) — mother back
from v1.c01.l04, father first met with the story cast in v1.c03.l05 and starting full
family duty today. **भ्राता** (bhrātā) "brother" — the ्र stroke of v1.c05.l04 opening a
breathy भ्. **भगिनी** (bhaginī) "sister" — the everyday word (§4). **पुत्रः** (putraḥ)
"son" back from v1.c05.l03, with **पुत्री** (putrī) "daughter" as his everyday partner.
Then the two heirlooms, **दुहिता** (duhitā) and **स्वसा** (svasā) — §13's business. And
**गृहम्** (gṛham) "home": गृह has been readable since v1.c04.l03 (Hindi घर is its
descendant); today it puts on its sentence dress म् and enters active use — an upgrade,
not a new word, exactly like तव in v1.c06.l02.

### Two of everything — the honest pairs

Sanskrit keeps *two* working words each for sister and daughter, and the honest lesson is
which does which job. **भगिनी** is the sister-word of everyday classical Sanskrit — the
one your conversations run on; **स्वसा** is its Vedic-flavored elder, the word you will
meet in old texts and in next lesson's cognate shelf. **पुत्री** is the daughter-word of
everyday use, transparently beside पुत्रः; **दुहिता** is the ancient word — the one
English *daughter* is sibling to (§13). Nothing here is a synonym-trap: use भगिनी and
पुत्री when you speak; know स्वसा and दुहिता for what they carry.

### The frames

मम and तव go in front of the family word and the old frames do the rest: **मम परिवारः
अत्र अस्ति।** — **तव भ्राता कः?** — **मम भ्राता अर्जुनः।** Location takes अस्ति; naming
takes nothing (§6). That is the entire grammar of today, and §16 runs a whole house
visit on it. Two things are deliberately *not* here yet: pointing at a person ("THIS is
my father") waits for एषः/एषा next lesson, and asking *where* someone is waits for कुत्र
in lesson 4. The frames will be ready when they arrive.

### The dress parade — observed, not legislated

Line today's words up by their endings and *look*: परिवारः and पुत्रः wear ः; गृहम् wears
म्; माता, पिता, भ्राता, दुहिता, स्वसा end in आ; भगिनी and पुत्री end in ई. Now the honest
part: the आ-rail holds mothers *and* fathers — shape alone decides nothing — and Chapter
1 already showed you रात्रिः, a feminine word in ः-dress. So today you observe the rails
(§15), you do not extract a law. The system that explains them is later Volume 2
business, and it will be built on exactly these observations.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — no new sounds; the work is breathy onsets, one syllabic vowel, and full
finals. Audio: 🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c02l01-drill.mp3` (drills 1–3),
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c02l01-word-list.mp3` (three speeds + dictation track).

**Drill 1 — the breathy openers.** Palm on chest: भा — भ्रा — भ्राता; भ — भगि — भगिनी.
The भ् must hum *and* push air — /bʱ/, voice plus breath, never plain b. Then दुहिता in
slow motion: the middle ह is a voiced breath *between* vowels — du-hi-tā, the airflow
never stops, the voice never cuts.

**Drill 2 — the family roll-call.** With the audio, fixed order, three passes (slow ·
natural · timed): माता — पिता — भ्राता — भगिनी — पुत्रः — पुत्री, then the heirlooms
दुहिता — स्वसा, then the container परिवारः and the house गृहम्. The order is the memory
palace; your mouth learns it so §18's presentations come out unprompted.

**Drill 3 — the frame ladder.** The audio calls a family word; you answer inside the
frame before the beat runs out: मम ___ अत्र अस्ति। Second round: the audio asks तव
___ कः? (or का?) and you answer with any honest name — मम भ्राता अर्जुनः। Timed retrieval
of chunks: the fluency strand doing its job [REF:nation2013].

**Drill 4 — three speeds.** The §14 list, block by block, natural, target pace; log the
third time (§18 exercise 5). The standing law at every speed: *parivāraḥ* breathed to the
end, *bhaginī* and *putrī* with two full beats of ī, *gṛham* closed on the lips.

every ऋ word since v1.c02.l03. The regional habit [ɾɪ] ("grih") is noted as a habit, not
the target. Then /ɦ/ into a full final /ɐm/.

**Narrow note — the two long ī words:** भगिनी /bʱɐgin̪iː/ and पुत्री /put̪ɾiː/ end in
/iː/ — dīrgha, twice the length of the इ inside भगिनी. Final-vowel length is meaning-
bearing in Sanskrit; give it its two beats.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — four checks; the articulators know all of this already. The word-anatomy
strip on the family-tree card (`planned:assets/images/volume-2/v2c02l01-family-tree.svg`)
marks the भ्रा and गृ joins.

1. **The breathy check.** Palm on your chest for भ्राता and भगिनी: hum plus push of air
   together /bʱ/. A plain "b" is missing the breath; a "ph" is missing the voice.
2. **The गृ check.** Say गृहम् in slow motion and freeze after ग: the tongue tip curls
   and *holds a beat with no vowel before it* — syllabic /r̩/. If you hear "gi-r" or
   "gu-r", a vowel leaked; re-run the v1.c02.l03 ऋ drill.
3. **The length check.** Mirror on भगिनी and पुत्री: lips stay spread through two full
   beats of the final ई — no early release into silence.
4. **The lips-close check.** गृहम् ends with your lips visibly together — [m]. Open lips
   and a nasal vowel mean Hindi-style अं has intruded; same mirror test as सायम्
   (v2.c01.l03).

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; these feed the quiz distractors.

1. **परिवारः clipped to Hindi परिवार.** The final visarga is a light voiceless breath,
   and it is there. *Fix:* the §11 breath check — the exam discipline of v2.c01.l05
   never retires.
2. **Short final i — "bhagini", "putri".** Hindi habit shortens the unstressed final.
   *Fix:* dīrgha ई = two beats (§10); log yourself in drill 4 until it holds at speed.
3. **गृहम् read "grih" or "gruh".** The Hindi habit re-vowels the ऋ mātrā. *Fix:*
   syllabic /r̩/, no vowel before the r — §11 check 2.
4. **The direction flip:** "English *daughter* comes from Sanskrit दुहिता", "*mother*
   comes from माता". *Fix:* siblings, not parent and child — all descend from one
   reconstructed ancestor; nobody borrowed anything (§13). The chapter exam's direction
   cards (v2.c02.l05) are built from this exact line.
5. **The pair collapsed:** treating स्वसा as "the correct, purer sister" and भगिनी as
   lesser — or reaching for दुहिता in conversation. *Fix:* भगिनी and पुत्री are the
   working words; स्वसा and दुहिता are heirloom carriers, honored for what they carry
   (§4, §8, §13).
6. **Shape overreach:** "आ-enders are feminine" (पिता? भ्राता?) or "ः marks masculine"
   (रात्रिः!). *Fix:* §15 — dresses are observed today, legislated later; the rule of
   thumb from v1.c06.l02 already has its first honest exceptions.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — three stories inside today's list.

### माता and her sisters — the first cognate line

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Sanskrit मातृ/माता, Latin *māter*, Greek *mētēr*, English *mother*:
> parallel descendants of one Proto-Indo-European word — siblings by shared descent.
> No borrowing happened in any direction: English did not take *mother* from Sanskrit,
> nor Sanskrit from anyone. [REF:mallory2006]

One piece of course history, stated as the fix it is: the 1.x book (PDF p.14) captioned
this with "म is the universal sound of nurture" — a lovely line and not a fact. The
honest version is *better*: not a mystical syllable but a traceable family, evidence you
can check word by word. This is the volume's flagship correction of "Sanskrit is the
mother of all languages": Sanskrit is a *sibling* in this family — the eldest-attested in
its branch, and the ancestor of Hindi and its Indo-Aryan sisters, not of Latin or
English. Today you carry one line; the full shelf — पिता, भ्राता and the rest beside
*pater* and *frāter* — is next lesson's centerpiece. We will not spill it early.

### Two words for daughter

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** पुत्री is the everyday classical word for "daughter", the feminine
> partner of पुत्रः. दुहिता (stem दुहितृ) is the ancient word for the same relation — the
> word English *daughter* and Greek *thygátēr* are siblings to, one of the textbook
> regular cognate sets. Both are attested Sanskrit; they differ in career, not in
> meaning. [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.vv.] [REF:mallory2006]

So the honest one-liner: *speak* पुत्री, *know* दुहिता — the second is the one carrying
five millennia of receipts.

### Two words for sister — and a poetic pair on display

भगिनी is the working word, and Hindi बहन is its direct descendant (§4) — a true
parent-to-child line *inside* Indo-Aryan, exactly the kind of claim that is honest
downward and false sideways. स्वसा is the Vedic-flavored elder; it is also the carrier of
the *sister/soror* cognate line, which will take its place on next lesson's shelf — one
promise, kept there. And for display only: Sanskrit also owns the poetic pair **जनकः**
(janakaḥ) "father, the begetter" and **जननी** (jananī) "mother, the one who bears" —
beautiful, real, off today's ledger.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — the possessive frames at work, and a parade you watch without legislating.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** (1) मम "my" and तव "your" stand before the word they own — मम
> पिता, तव भगिनी — pause form, both words whole; they are fixed chunks today, their
> machinery Volume 3's. (2) The naming sentence is verbless (मम भ्राता अर्जुनः); the
> location sentence carries अस्ति (मम पिता अत्र अस्ति). (3) Nouns remain whole-word
> glossed: no endings are analyzed in Volume 2. [REF:whitney1889]

### The dress parade — look, don't legislate

| Dress | Today's wearers | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| ः | परिवारः · पुत्रः | the ः-crew has answered to कः since v1.c06.l02 — a rule of thumb, and रात्रिः (v2.c01.l03) already bent it |

## 22. Teacher Notes

अध्यापकटिप्पणी

**Open with the riddle, not the list.** Write *mother* and माता on the board and ask
which came from which. Collect the votes; then deliver the third answer — neither, one
grandparent, no borrowing — and promise the full shelf next lesson. The lesson's honesty
axis lands better as a resolved riddle than as a warning.

**Timing plan (45 min):** Station 0 + prompts, scores logged (7) → the mother-riddle on
the board (5) → set walk-through on the family-tree card; the two heirloom tags placed
(8) → pair-honesty chips: "working word" vs "heirloom" on भगिनी/स्वसा, पुत्री/दुहिता
(5) → frame ladder in pairs: मम/तव + roll-call, question game (8) → dress parade sort on
the word-shapes card; the आ-rail discussion (5) → word list at three speeds; dictation
launch (4) → exercises, homework, the photo task (3).

**Board work:** one house outline (गृहम्), family words pinned inside as they arrive,
परिवारः written around the outline — the word's own "circle around" image doing the
teaching. Keep a two-column chip tray: रोज़ का शब्द / धरोहर-शब्द (working / heirloom).

**Run the direction talk carefully.** Someone will say "so बहन comes from भगिनी — and
*mother* comes from माता, same thing, no?" That is the teachable moment of the chapter:
draw ONE tree — Sanskrit and English as branches, Hindi growing *out of* the Sanskrit
branch. Downward claims true, sideways claims false. Exercise 4's card pair (c)/(d) is
built to force exactly this conversation.

**Differentiation:** Hindi speakers own half the list — their work is the finals
(परिवारः breathed, ई held long, गृ not "grih") and the direction honesty, where instinct
("these are just Hindi words") is precisely what exercise 4 examines. Script-side
learners drill भ्रा from components (भ् + र, v1.c05.l04) before speed work.

**What students find hard:** two words for one relative feels like a burden until you
offer English *dad/father* as the everyday/formal analogy — then it feels normal;
resisting the urge to point ("this is my father") — promise एषः/एषा tomorrow and park
it; keeping स्वसा out of conversation practice without disrespecting it (it is an
heirloom, not an error); and the आ-rail surprise (पिता masculine!) — let the parade table
be read aloud and leave the system honestly unbuilt.
