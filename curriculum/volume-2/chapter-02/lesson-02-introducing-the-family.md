## 1. Hook

Point at your father and say एषः मम पिता — you have just introduced him in a sentence
Pāṇini would parse without blinking. Now listen to three languages say one word: माता.
Latin *māter*. English *mother*. Nobody copied anybody — and that is today's biggest
fact. The words for the people you love are the oldest shared inheritance your language
family owns, and by the end of this lesson you will introduce your whole family in
Sanskrit *and* tell the true story of why those introductions sound half-familiar.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिक सन्दर्भः — the oldest words in the house.

The kinship words you will speak today are not later coinages — they are already there,
fully formed, in the earliest layer of the tradition.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** The kinship terms मातृ, पितृ, भ्रातृ, दुहितृ and स्वसृ are
> attested from the Ṛgveda onward — composed c. 1500–1200 BCE on the scholarly
> consensus dating — and belong to the language's oldest inherited word stock.
> [REF:jamison2014 p.5]; [REF:burrow2001]

Today's third word widens the circle. **कुटुम्बम्** (kuṭumbam) "household" is the
classical word for the family as one living unit — परिवारः names the people around
you; कुटुम्बम् names the whole household they make. The word carries one of the
tradition's most quoted lines:

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** The Hitopadeśa (Mitralābha, verse 71 in common editions;
> the line also appears in the Mahopaniṣad) says of the generous-hearted: वसुधैव
> कुटुम्बकम् (vasudhaiva kuṭumbakam) — "the earth itself is one household." A statement
> of the tradition's ideal, quoted for the word inside it. [REF:hitopadesha
> mitralābha 71]

Hold that line until §16 — the आचार्यः has plans for it.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिक व्याख्या — two sciences share today: how you learn introductions, and how we
know the family words are family.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Introduction formulas are classic chunk material: fixed frames
> with one open slot (एषः मम …), usable whole from day one, produced under social time
> pressure exactly when sentence-building would be slowest — and every run of the
> family round is retrieval practice. [REF:nation2013]; [REF:roediger2006]

The second science began with a famous observation.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** In 1786 William Jones told the Asiatic Society in Calcutta
> that Sanskrit, Greek and Latin bore a resemblance too strong to be accidental — that
> all three had "sprung from some common source, which, perhaps, no longer exists."
> That common source is what linguists now reconstruct as Proto-Indo-European (PIE).
> [REF:mallory2006]

What makes this science rather than pattern-spotting is *regularity*: where English
says *f*, Latin and Sanskrit answer with *p* — *father*, *pater*, पितृ — and the same
correspondence returns in word after word of the same class. Loans do not behave that
way; inherited words do [REF:mallory2006]; [REF:burrow2001]. §13 puts the evidence
shelf in your hands.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतव्याख्या — the lesson's idea in the simplest possible Sanskrit.

एषः मम पिता। एषा मम माता।
(eṣaḥ mama pitā. eṣā mama mātā.) "This is my father. This is my mother." — यह मेरे
पिता हैं। यह मेरी माता हैं।

एषा मम भगिनी। एतत् मम गृहम्।
(eṣā mama bhaginī. etat mama gṛham.) "This is my sister. This is my house." — यह मेरी
बहन है। यह मेरा घर है।

तव भ्राता कः? — एषः मम भ्राता!
(tava bhrātā kaḥ? — eṣaḥ mama bhrātā!) "Who is your brother? — This is my brother!" —
तुम्हारा भाई कौन? — यह मेरा भाई!

The round is four chunks and one decision: who are you pointing at? A man or boy —
एषः. A woman or girl — एषा. A thing — एतत्, your pointer since v1.c06.l02. All three
are one word wearing three shapes; the system behind the shapes is Volume 3's business,
and today the chunks are simply yours.

## 8. English Explanation

Three new words, one decision rule, a round of introductions — then §13 opens the
volume's flagship cabinet.

### The pointing trio, completed

Since v1.c06.l02 you have pointed at things: एतत् किम्? एतत् जलम्। Today the trio
completes so you can point at *people*:

**एषः** (eṣaḥ) "this (man, boy)" — एषः मम पिता। एषः रामः।
**एषा** (eṣā) "this (woman, girl)" — एषा मम माता। एषा सीता।
**एतत्** (etat) "this (thing)" — एतत् मम गृहम्। एतत् जलम्।

The rule is one line long: **the person decides the pointer.** Not the word's ending —
the person. पिता and भ्राता end in -आ exactly like सीता, and they still take एषः,
because your father is a he. The old मित्रम् exception survives untouched: when you
route a person through a thing-word, the word wins — एतत् मित्रम् remains correct and
remains funny. And एषः itself is an old friend finally hired: it has asked एषः कः
वर्णः? at every letter since Chapter 1's classroom, a glue word 🔊 all this time.

### The introduction round

**एषः मम पिता। एषा मम माता। एषा मम भगिनी। एतत् मम गृहम्।** — a complete family
introduction: four verbless sentences, every word yours. Lesson 1 gave the people;
today gives the pointing. Add v1.c06.l02's question kit and the round becomes a
conversation: **तव भ्राता कः?** "who is your brother?" — एषः मम भ्राता! **एषा का?**
"who is she?" — एषा मम भगिनी!

### कुटुम्बम् — the household

**कुटुम्बम्** (kuṭumbam) "household" — the classical word for the family as one unit,
§4's वसुधैव कुटुम्बकम् word. Use परिवारः for "my family" as people, कुटुम्बम् when you
mean the whole house as one body. [REF:apte1890 s.v. कुटुम्ब]

### One honest display line

You own तस्य "his" from v1.c06.l03 — तस्य नाम अर्जुनः। For a woman, Sanskrit says
**तस्याः** (tasyāḥ) "her": तस्याः नाम माया। Shown because the scenes want it — no
paradigm today, nothing to memorize beyond the pair.

### The ledger

Lesson 1 left the readable ledger at 267. Today's three — एषः, एषा, कुटुम्बम् — take it
to **270**. Everything else here is owned material doing family work.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — the work is the ष curl and the round at speed. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c02l02-dialogue.mp3` (both scenes: slow, natural, and
roleplay-gap tracks), 🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c02l02-word-list.mp3` (three
words, the round, and the dictation track).

**Drill 1 — the curl ladder.** Chapter 4's three hisses: स (teeth) → ष (tip curled to
the dome) → श (blade at the palate). Then the pair that matters, ten times, pointing
as you speak: एषः — एषा — एषः — एषा. Only the tail differs; the curl stays.

**Drill 2 — the round chant.** With a family photo (or the worksheet's picture card):
एषः मम पिता। एषा मम माता। एषा मम भगिनी। एतत् मम गृहम्। Pause form, one beat between
sentences, finger landing on each person. Three rounds, faster each time — the
pointing hand keeps the pointer honest.

**Drill 3 — the question volley.** Call and answer with the audio: तव पिता कः? — एषः
मम पिता! · तव माता का? — एषा मम माता! · एषा का? — एषा मम भगिनी! Reply within a beat;
the pointer must come out matched without thinking time.

**Drill 4 — the shelf, read aloud.** §13's citation forms, slowly: माता — पिता —
भ्राता — दुहिता — स्वसा. The Latin and English cousins on the chart are read as Latin
and English — they are evidence, not pronunciation targets.

## 10. IPA

No new values. **Word transcriptions:** एषः /eːʂɐh/ · एषा /eːʂɑː/ · कुटुम्बम्
/kuʈumbɐm/ · तस्याः /t̪ɐsjɑːh/ · मातृ /mɑːt̪r̩/ · पितृ /pit̪r̩/ · भ्रातृ /bʱɾɑːt̪r̩/ ·
दुहिता /d̪uɦit̪ɑː/ · स्वसा /sʋɐsɑː/.

**The frames:** एषः मम पिता /eːʂɐh mɐmɐ pit̪ɑː/ · एषा मम माता /eːʂɑː mɐmɐ mɑːt̪ɑː/ ·
एतत् मम गृहम् /eːt̪ɐt̪ mɐmɐ gr̩ɦɐm/.

**Narrow notes:** एषः and एषा open on the long steady [eː] — never English "ay" [eɪ] —
and carry the retroflex [ʂ]: tip curled, hiss from the dome, not the teeth. एषः ends in
a clean visarga breath [h]; एषा in full [ɑː] — the pair differs only in its tail, so
the tail must be audible. कुटुम्बम् is [kuʈumbɐm]: retroflex [ʈ] mid-word, the म्ब seam
one lip closure released into [b], final bare [m] held closed. The stems on §13's shelf
end in syllabic [r̩] — मातृ [mɑːt̪r̩], the ऋ of संस्कृतम्, never "ri".

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थानम् — three checks, mirror at hand.

1. **The curl.** Say एषा slowly and freeze on the middle: tongue tip curled up and
   back, hissing at the dome. At the teeth you said स; at the front palate, श.
2. **The frozen ए.** Watch your jaw through the first beat of एषः, एषा, एतत् — all
   three pointers open on the same long [eː], lips and jaw motionless. A glide means
   English got in.
3. **The lip work of कुटुम्बम्.** Two rounded [u]s, then one closure for म्ब — closed
   on [m], released into [b] — then closed again for the bare final म्. Three lip
   events, no extra vowel after the last.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्य-भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix. These feed the quiz distractors.

1. **The pointer mismatched to the person.** एषा रामः, एषः सीता. *Fix:* the person
   decides — एषः रामः। एषा सीता। Run §9 drill 3 until the match is reflex.
2. **एतत् kept for people.** एतत् मम पिता — the pointing-game habit stretched too far.
   *Fix:* people get एषः/एषा; things keep एतत्. (The exception stays: एतत् मित्रम् is
   right because मित्रम् the *word* is a thing-word.)
3. **The आ trap.** एषा मम पिता "because पिता ends like सीता". *Fix:* shapes hint,
   persons decide — पिता and भ्राता are he-words in आ-clothing: एषः मम पिता।
4. **THE direction error: "English *mother* comes from Sanskrit मातृ."** The single
   most common mistake this course exists to fix. *Fix:* माता, māter and mother are
   siblings from one reconstructed ancestor — no borrowing in any direction, ever
   (§13). The exam's register cards test exactly this.
5. **Cognate and loan blurred.** "So mother is a Sanskrit word like karma?" — no:
   *karma* and *dharma* really were **loans**, carried into English along documented
   modern routes; *mother* is a **cognate**, inherited in parallel. Two relationships,
   both real, both in hand since v1.c01.l01 (§13).
6. **कुटुम्बम् clipped to Hindi shape.** "कुटुम्ब" with the ending swallowed. *Fix:*
   the Sanskrit word wears म् and every vowel full — कु-टु-म्ब-म्, retroflex ट, final
   म् closed and complete.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — the volume's flagship: the kinship cognate shelf.

Lesson 1 opened one line of it: माता beside *mother*. Here is the whole shelf — five
words your family carries, set beside their cousins:

| Sanskrit stem | in your sentences | a classical cousin | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| मातृ (mātṛ) | माता | Latin māter | mother |
| पितृ (pitṛ) | पिता | Latin pater | father |
| भ्रातृ (bhrātṛ) | भ्राता | Latin frāter | brother |
| दुहितृ (duhitṛ) | दुहिता | Greek thygátēr | daughter |
| स्वसृ (svasṛ) | स्वसा | Latin soror | sister |

The dictionaries list these words by stem (मातृ, पितृ…); in your sentences they stand
as माता, पिता, भ्राता, दुहिता, स्वसा. Two of the five are quiet guests: दुहिता is
lesson 1's cognate carrier beside everyday पुत्री, and **स्वसा** (svasā) "sister" — the
older, Vedic-flavored sibling of your working word भगिनी — appears here for the shelf
and stays off the ledger: display only, read it and let it point.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** मातृ, Latin māter and English *mother* descend in parallel from
> one reconstructed Proto-Indo-European word — written in the field's notation as
> \*méh₂tēr, the asterisk meaning "reconstructed, not recorded". The same holds across
> the shelf: पितृ ~ pater ~ father, भ्रातृ ~ frāter ~ brother, दुहितृ ~ thygátēr ~
> daughter, स्वसृ ~ soror ~ sister. Siblings from a shared ancestor: no borrowing
> happened in any direction. [REF:mallory2006]; [REF:burrow2001]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Cognate and loan are different relationships, and both are real.
> English *mother* was never borrowed from Sanskrit — the two lines simply never lost
> the word they jointly inherited. English *dharma*, *karma* and *yoga* WERE borrowed
> from Sanskrit, along documented modern routes. One family resemblance, one shipping
> receipt — you have owned both tools since v1.c01.l01. [REF:mallory2006];
> [REF:burrow2001]

One line of housekeeping with the past: the 1.x edition of this course called Sanskrit
"the mother of all languages." The truth this shelf shows is different and — we think —
better: Sanskrit is the *eldest sister we can still hear at full voice*, standing
beside Latin and Greek, all daughters of one reconstructed ancestor. And the shelf is
not done: next lesson अग्निः meets Latin *ignis* — same shelf, new row. The wall chart
🖼 (`planned:assets/images/volume-2/v2c02l02-kinship-cognate-wall-chart.svg`) shows
three columns under one ancestor node — and, deliberately, **no arrows between the
daughter columns**. Sisters do not descend from each other.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — one decision rule, one chunk family, one honest observation.

### The pointing trio

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** एषः (masculine), एषा (feminine) and एतत् (neuter) are three
> shapes of one demonstrative stem, एतद् — "this (near me)". In this course they are
> fixed chunks matched to the person or thing pointed at; the demonstrative system
> proper (distant partners, case forms) is Volume 3's business. [REF:apte1890 s.v.
> एतद्]

| Pointing at | Word | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a man or boy | एषः | एषः मम पिता। एषः रामः। |
| a woman or girl | एषा | एषा मम माता। एषा सीता। |
| a thing | एतत् | एतत् मम गृहम्। एतत् जलम्। |

Boundary notes, honestly stated: मित्रम् routes people through a thing-word, so एतत्
मित्रम् stays correct — the word decides there. And in connected texts you will meet
एष रामः with no visarga — the seam changes shape exactly as पुनः did in पुनर्मिलामः;
noticed, previewed, deferred (Volume 3). Your pause form today is एषः रामः।

### The possessive chunk family

मम "my" — तव "your" — तस्य "his" (v1.c06.l03) — and today's display: **तस्याः** "her".
तस्य नाम अर्जुनः। तस्याः नाम माया। One line, no paradigm: the pair exists, you can
read both, and the machinery behind the endings stays in Volume 3 where it belongs.

### Shapes observed, not yet taught

Keep noticing what you have noticed since Chapter 3: ः-words mostly he (रामः, पुत्रः),
आ/ई-words mostly she (माता, भगिनी, पुत्री), म्-words things (गृहम्, जलम्) — and today
two honest exceptions on display: पिता and भ्राता, he-words in आ-clothing. The shapes
hint; the person decides; the system is later business.

## 16. Conversation

सम्भाषणम् — two scenes; every word self-read. 🔊 audio:
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c02l02-dialogue.mp3` (slow, natural, and roleplay-gap
tracks).

### Scene 1 — सीता visits

At माया's house. The door opens.

— माया: स्वागतम्! एतत् मम गृहम्।
(svāgatam! etat mama gṛham.) "Welcome! This is my house." — स्वागत है! यह मेरा घर है।

— माया: एषः मम पिता। एषा मम माता।
(eṣaḥ mama pitā. eṣā mama mātā.) "This is my father. This is my mother." — यह मेरे
पिता हैं। यह मेरी माता हैं।

— सीता *(a boy is reading in the corner)*: नमस्ते! … एषः कः?
(namaste! … eṣaḥ kaḥ?) "Greetings! … And who is this?" — नमस्ते! …और यह कौन है?

— माया: एषः मम भ्राता। तस्य नाम अर्जुनः।
(eṣaḥ mama bhrātā. tasya nāma arjunaḥ.) "This is my brother. His name is Arjuna." —
यह मेरा भाई है। इसका नाम अर्जुन है।

— सीता: अर्जुनः! सः अपि छात्रः!
(arjunaḥ! saḥ api chātraḥ!) "Arjuna! He's a student too!" — अर्जुन! वह भी छात्र है!

— माया: आम्! एषा मम भगिनी — तस्याः नाम लीला।
(ām! eṣā mama bhaginī — tasyāḥ nāma līlā.) "Yes! And this is my sister — her name is
Līlā." — हाँ! और यह मेरी बहन है — इसका नाम लीला है।

### Scene 2 — the आचार्यः asks around

Family-picture day at school: every student holds a drawing.

— आचार्यः: भोः! तव पिता कः?
(bhoḥ! tava pitā kaḥ?) "Well then — who is your father?" — अरे! तुम्हारे पिता कौन हैं?

— रामः *(holding his drawing high)*: एषः मम पिता!
(eṣaḥ mama pitā!) "THIS is my father!" — यह मेरे पिता हैं!

— आचार्यः *(pointing at the girl in माया's drawing)*: एषा का?
(eṣā kā?) "Who is she?" — यह कौन है?

— माया: एषा मम भगिनी।
(eṣā mama bhaginī.) "She is my sister." — यह मेरी बहन है।

— रामः *(on a roll, pointing at his drawing of a house)*: एषः मम गृहम्!
(eṣaḥ mama gṛham!) "This is my house!" — यह मेरा घर है!

— रामः *(the room turns; he grins)*: एतत्! एतत् मम गृहम्!
(etat! etat mama gṛham!) "एतत्! This is my house!" — एतत्! यह मेरा घर है!

— आचार्यः *(laughing, arms wide at the whole class)*: भद्रम्! एतत् अपि मम कुटुम्बम्!
(bhadram! etat api mama kuṭumbam!) "Well done! And this, too, is my household!" —
शाबाश! और यह भी मेरा कुटुम्ब है!

Housekeeping: लीला (līlā) is a name, new to the story, not to the grammar — names ride
free. रामः's slip and self-repair *is* §12's mistake 2, staged and fixed. And the
आचार्यः gets the last word by pointing एतत् at the class-as-household — वसुधैव
कुटुम्बकम् (§4) scaled to one room.

## 17. Story

कथा — परिवारः · The Family. Six lines; each line: Devanagari, IAST, English, Hindi.

1. सीता गृहम् चलति। माया अपि चलति।
   (sītā gṛham calati. māyā api calati.) "Sītā goes to the house. Māyā goes too." —
   सीता घर जाती है। माया भी जाती है।
2. माया वदति — स्वागतम्! एतत् मम गृहम्।
   (māyā vadati — svāgatam! etat mama gṛham.) "Māyā says: Welcome! This is my house."
   — माया कहती है — स्वागत! यह मेरा घर है।
3. एषः मम पिता। एषा मम माता।
   (eṣaḥ mama pitā. eṣā mama mātā.) "This is my father. This is my mother." — यह मेरे
   पिता हैं। यह मेरी माता हैं।
4. अर्जुनः तत्र अस्ति। सः पठति।
   (arjunaḥ tatra asti. saḥ paṭhati.) "Arjuna is there. He is reading." — अर्जुन वहाँ
   है। वह पढ़ रहा है।
5. सीता वदति — नमस्ते! त्वम् मम मित्रम्!
   (sītā vadati — namaste! tvam mama mitram!) "Sītā says: Greetings! You are my
   friend!" — सीता कहती है — नमस्ते! तुम मेरे मित्र हो!
6. माता हसति — सीता अपि मम पुत्री!
   (mātā hasati — sītā api mama putrī!) "The mother laughs: Sītā too is my daughter!"
   — माता हँसती हैं — सीता भी मेरी बेटी है!

Line 4 is v1.c06.l03's boy at home — the passage told you तस्य नाम अर्जुनः long before
you knew whose brother he was. Line 5 leans on the old मित्रम् rule one more time. And
line 6 is कुटुम्बम् in action without saying the word: the guest folded into the
household, which is what the word is *for*.

## 22. Teacher Notes

शिक्षक-टिप्पणी

**The flagship needs staging, not lecturing.** Build §13's shelf on the board row by
row and let students call out the English column before you write it — the gasp at
*daughter* under दुहितृ is the lesson landing. Then ask, honestly: "so which one
copied?" Someone will say English copied Sanskrit — §12's mistake 4 arriving on
schedule. The correction is one warm sentence: siblings from one ancestor — nobody
borrowed anybody's mother. If a student raises the old "mother of all languages" line,
never mock it: name it as the 1.x framing, show what the evidence supports, and let
the eldest-sister image do the emotional work. The direction fact must leave the room
feeling like a *gain*, not a demotion.

**Timing plan (45 min):** §3 retrieval, written (5) → the trio on the board, live
pointing — with consent and comedy (7) → the round chanted with photos or drawings
(6) → the question volley in pairs (6) → §16 scenes performed, रामः's self-repair
acted twice (8) → THE SHELF, staged as above (8) → कुटुम्बम् + the class-as-household
closer, वसुधैव कुटुम्बकम् on the board (3) → homework briefing (2).

**Drill the आ trap explicitly.** Write सीता and पिता side by side, ask which takes
एषा — half the room falls for it once, which is exactly the inoculation. Same move
with मित्रम् for the other boundary.

**Heritage learners:** hand them the बहन ← भगिनी descent line early — it delights, and
it sets up the day's sharpest contrast: Hindi really is Sanskrit's daughter; English
is Sanskrit's cousin; one word — *mother* — keeps both relationships straight forever.
Their pronunciation work is the ष curl and कुटुम्बम्'s full ending against Hindi
कुटुम्ब. English-habit learners: the steady [eː] in all three pointers, and the
pointer drifting to एतत्-for-everything under pressure.

**What students find hard:** producing एषः/एषा matched at speed (the volley cures
it); believing the direction fact gladly rather than grudgingly (the eldest-sister
frame and the karma-was-a-loan contrast both help); and remembering that स्वसा and
तस्याः are display, not drill — keep them off any test you write.
