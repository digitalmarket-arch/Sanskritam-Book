## 1. Hook

Today's haul is two words, and one of them is a single letter. With the first you can ask where
anything in your 280-word world is. With the second you can join any two things you own — mother
and father, river and tree, moon and star — using the same little connector Rome carved into its
monuments, in the same odd position: *behind* the words it joins. Along the way this lesson
repairs one old error kindly, keeps a promise made back in Volume 1, and finally lets you read
this chapter's own title out loud. Two words have rarely done so much work.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिक सन्दर्भः — the oldest "and" you will ever meet.

When Rome signed its state *Senatus Populusque Romanus* — "the Senate and People of Rome" — the
bit doing "and" duty was *-que*, and it did not stand between the two nouns. It attached
*behind* the second one: *populus-que*, "the-people-and". If that placement looks strange, hold
it next to today's Sanskrit: रामः सीता च "Rāma and Sītā" — the च walking behind, exactly where
Latin put its *-que*. No coincidence, and no borrowing.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Sanskrit च, Latin *-que* and Greek *te* are parallel descendants of one
> Proto-Indo-European connector (reconstructed \*kʷe) — cognates, siblings from a shared
> ancestor, with no borrowing in any direction. The behind-placement is part of the
> inheritance: in both Sanskrit and Latin the little word leans on what it joins.
> [REF:burrow2001]; [REF:mallory2006]

And one kind correction from the audit of the old course: the 1.x book glossed और as the
Sanskrit for "and". और is Hindi — a fine word, doing honest work in its own language.
Sanskrit's own connector is च, and it stands after the words it joins, not between them. The
gloss is repaired today; no scorn for the old page, and none for Hindi — just each word back in
its own language.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिक व्याख्या — words that lean, and words that come in tables.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** च belongs to a class linguists call **enclitics** — from a Greek verb
> meaning "to lean". An enclitic cannot begin a sentence; it has no accent of its own and
> leans on the word before it. Sanskrit grammar treats च exactly so, and अपि in its "too" job
> (X अपि) keeps the same manners. [REF:whitney1889]; [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. च]

The second science of the day is about tables. कुत्र is not an isolated word; it is a cell in
a grid — the place-suffix -त्र on four stems: अ- "near me", त- "over there", य- "which
(relative)", कु- "what?". Fill the grid and you get अत्र, तत्र, यत्र, कुत्र. Hindi speakers own
the same grid natively — यहाँ, वहाँ, जहाँ, कहाँ — the same four-way system living on in the
daughter language [REF:masica1991]. Learning a cell in a table you already half-own is the
cheapest vocabulary there is: function words learned inside frames stick as chunks
[REF:nation2013], and every round of today's asking-and-pointing game is retrieval practice,
the strongest rehearsal known [REF:roediger2006].

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतव्याख्या — the lesson's idea in the simplest possible Sanskrit.

माता कुत्र अस्ति?
(mātā kutra asti?) "Where is mother?" — माता कहाँ है?

माता अत्र अस्ति।
(mātā atra asti.) "Mother is here." — माता यहाँ है।

माता पिता च अत्र।
(mātā pitā ca atra.) "Mother and father are here." — माता और पिता यहाँ हैं।

Three lines, the whole lesson: a question, a full answer, a joined pair. Watch the च in the
third line — it does the work of "and" from *behind* पिता, and the sentence carries two
subjects with no verb at all, which is deliberate and fully classical (§8, §15). व्याकरण की एक
झलक: च, अपि, अत्र, तत्र, कुत्र — ये सब **अव्यय** (avyaya) हैं, यानी कभी रूप न बदलने वाले शब्द —
one honest name, more in §15.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी व्याख्या

आज का पाठ आपकी हिन्दी के कंधे पर चढ़कर चलता है — दो नये शब्द, और दोनों आधे-पहचाने।

पहला: **कुत्र** — "कहाँ?"। पाँचवें अध्याय (खण्ड 1) से आपके पास अत्र-तत्र-यत्र की तिकड़ी है; आज
उसका पूछने वाला चौथा भाई आ पहुँचा: अत्र "यहाँ", तत्र "वहाँ", यत्र "जहाँ", कुत्र "कहाँ?"। यह
चौकड़ी आपकी अपनी यहाँ-वहाँ-जहाँ-कहाँ वाली चौकड़ी ही है, बस संस्कृत की पोशाक में। और प्रश्न-किट
भी आज पूरी हुई: कः, का, किम्, कुतः, कथम् — और अब कुत्र। सावधानी एक: कुत्र "कहाँ" पूछता है,
कुतः "कहाँ से" — दोनों अलग प्रश्न हैं।

दूसरा: **च** — "और"। यहाँ एक ईमानदार सुधार भी है। पुरानी पाठ्यपुस्तक ने "और" को ही संस्कृत का
शब्द बता दिया था। सच यह है: और हिन्दी का अपना शब्द है — अपनी भाषा में बिलकुल सही; संस्कृत का
अपना जोड़-शब्द है च। और च की चाल हिन्दी से उलटी है — वह जोड़े हुए शब्दों के **बीच** नहीं,
**पीछे** चलता है: रामः सीता च — "राम और सीता"। याद कीजिए "भी" वाला अपि — वह भी अपने शब्द के
पीछे बैठता है। संस्कृत के ये छोटे शब्द पीछे से काम करते हैं; वाक्य का पहला स्थान इन्हें कभी
नहीं मिलता।

तीसरी बात — अध्याय के नाम का ताला आज खुला: **परिवारः जगच्च**। यह जगत् + च है — "परिवार और
जगत्"। बोलने में जगत् का त् अगले च से मिलकर जगच्च बन जाता है — वैसे ही जैसे न + अस्ति जुड़कर
नास्ति बना था। जुड़ने के ये नियम (सन्धि) तीसरे खण्ड का विषय हैं; आज विराम-रूप जगत् च पढ़िए, और
जगच्च को देखकर पहचानिए भर।

और एक सावधानी: "माता और पिता घर में हैं" कहने का मन करेगा — पर "घर **में**" के लिए जो रूप
चाहिए (विभक्ति), वह अभी नहीं आया। आज का शुद्ध रास्ता: माता पिता च अत्र। — बिना क्रिया के, और
फिर भी पूरी, सच्ची संस्कृत।

## 8. English Explanation

Two words, four moves — ask where, point the answer, join with च, and read the title.

### कुत्र — one word, two kits complete

**कुत्र** (kutra) "where?" has been *readable* since v1.c05.l03 gave you the त्र ligature;
today it is *yours*. It completes two collections at one stroke. The location kit: **अत्र**
"here" · **तत्र** "there" · **यत्र** "where (relative — points, never asks)" · **कुत्र**
"where? (asks)". And the question kit built since Volume 1: **कः · का · किम् · कुतः · कथम् ·
कुत्र** — every one beginning with the ancient asking k- (the cognate of English wh-, as
v2.c01.l02 showed). Keep the near-twins apart: **कुत्र** asks *where at*; **कुतः** asks *where
from*.

### The Q&A frame — ask, point, answer

| Ask | Answer (full) | Answer (short) |
|---|---|---|
| माता कुत्र अस्ति? | माता अत्र अस्ति। | माता अत्र। |
| मेघः कुत्र अस्ति? | मेघः तत्र अस्ति। | मेघः तत्र। |

The कुत्र-question keeps the level tune — the question word does the asking, exactly as कथम्
and किम् always have (v2.c01.l02's rule). The short verbless answer — माता अत्र। — is
complete, correct classical Sanskrit, the same verbless frame you have trusted since इह रामः
in Volume 1.

### च — the connector that walks behind

**च** (ca) "and" is the smallest headword in the course — one akṣara, tying with न — and the
busiest. Its single rule: **च stands behind what it joins, never between.**

रामः सीता च
(rāmaḥ sītā ca) "Rāma and Sītā" — राम और सीता

Not "रामः च सीता" on the English pattern — the च tucks in after the *last* joined word. One
honest extra line: doubled, **X च Y च** means "both X and Y" — रामः च सीता च "both Rāma and
Sītā" — a warmer, emphatic join you will meet often in classical texts. Both patterns are
yours today; the single trailing च is the everyday one.

And said once more, plainly, because the old course said otherwise: **और is Hindi; Sanskrit's
own "and" is च** — after, not between. When your Hindi reflex reaches for और mid-sentence, let
it remind you: *behind, with* च. Your अपि habit is the bridge — you have parked "too" behind
its word since Volume 1; "and" lives in the same parking spot.

### Joined subjects — the verbless frame

माता पिता च अत्र।
(mātā pitā ca atra.) "Mother and father are here." — माता और पिता यहाँ हैं।

Where did अस्ति go? Held back, honestly: joined subjects change the verb's shape — Sanskrit
keeps special verb forms for two doers and for many (you met plural वदन्ति in stories, glossed
whole) — and that machinery is later business, taught properly when it arrives. Today the
verbless frame carries every joined pair, and it is no workaround: it is fully classical
Sanskrit doing what it has always done.

### The title unlocked — जगच्च

This chapter has worn its name since lesson 1: **परिवारः जगच्च** (parivāraḥ jagacca) "Family
and the World". You can now read its skeleton: परिवारः + जगत् + च — the च walking behind
जगत्. Spoken together, जगत् + च fuses into **जगच्च** — the final त् leaning into the च —
exactly the kind of seam you met in नास्ति and पुनर्मिलामः. Pause form today: जगत् च. The
joining system is Volume 3's subject; the full unpacking is in §13.

### The ledger

The chapter entered at 260 readable words; three lessons of family and world have brought the
ledger to 280. Today adds exactly two — कुत्र and च — for **282**. Smallest haul of the
volume, largest reach: with these two, every word you own can now be asked about, found, and
joined to its neighbor.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — the work is rhythm and tune, not new sounds. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c02l04-dialogue.mp3` (both scenes + the where-game gap
track), 🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c02l04-word-list.mp3` (words, kit recitals,

**Drill 1 — the level ask, the settling point.** कुत्र-questions carry a question word, so the
tune stays level: माता कुत्र अस्ति? Then the answer settles: माता अत्र अस्ति। Ask-point-answer,
three rounds over the world set, pause form between turns.

**Drill 2 — the lean.** च takes no beat of its own. Say रामः सीता च as one breath group: the
stress lives on सी, and च tucks in behind like a passenger. If you can whisper the च and still
be understood, the lean is right.

**Drill 3 — कुत्र vs कुतः.** Alternate the pair aloud, five times: कुत्र (tap and open vowel) ·
कुतः (vowel and a clean visarga breath). Your ear must file them as different questions before
§12 tries to merge them.

**Drill 4 — the seam.** Read the chapter title in pause form — परिवारः जगत् च — then hear the
audio speak the joined जगच्च once, labeled. Feel the doubled च्च hold for an extra beat.
Recognize; do not yet produce — the pause form is today's reading.

## 10. IPA

No new values. **Word transcriptions:** कुत्र /kut̪ɾɐ/ · च /t͡ɕɐ/ · कुतः /kut̪ɐh/ · जगत्
/d͡ʑɐgɐt̪/ · जगच्च /d͡ʑɐgɐt͡ɕt͡ɕɐ/ · display: स्तः /st̪ɐh/.

**The frames in pause form:** माता कुत्र अस्ति /mɑːt̪ɑː kut̪ɾɐ ɐst̪i/ · रामः सीता च
/ɾɑːmɐh siːt̪ɑː t͡ɕɐ/ · माता पिता च अत्र /mɑːt̪ɑː pit̪ɑː t͡ɕɐ ɐt̪ɾɐ/ · नदी वृक्षः च तत्र
/n̪ɐd̪iː ʋr̩kʂɐh t͡ɕɐ t̪ɐt̪ɾɐ/.

**Narrow notes:** च is the palatal affricate [t͡ɕ] with the short open [ɐ] — one crisp
syllable, never stretched to "chaa". As an enclitic it is unstressed: the phrase रामः सीता च
carries its prominence on सीता, the च riding the tail. In जगच्च the doubled च्च is a *held*
affricate — one contact, one extra beat of closure, one release — not two cha-cha strikes. And
कुत्र ends in tap-plus-vowel [t̪ɾɐ] while कुतः ends in vowel-plus-breath [t̪ɐh]: the whole
where/whence contrast hangs on that final beat.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थानम् — nothing new to place; three checks to run.

1. **The च station.** Blade of the tongue to the hard palate — the तालव्य station owned since
   the च-वर्ग lesson — brief full contact, then release into the hiss. Say च, चन्द्रः, जगच्च;
   if the tongue slides forward to the teeth you will hear "tsa", not च.
2. **The त्र weld of कुत्र.** Dental त् released straight into the tap र — one gesture, no
   vowel leaking between, exactly as in अत्र and तत्र since v1.c05.l03. कु-त्र: two beats.
3. **The held च्च.** Say जगच्च slowly at a mirror: the tongue touches the palate once and
   *waits* one full extra beat before releasing. Two separate jaw movements = two releases —
   rejoin them.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्य-भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix. These feed the quiz distractors.

1. **च parked in the middle.** "रामः च सीता" as a rendering of "Rāma and Sītā" — the
   English/Hindi word order with a Sanskrit word dropped into the "and" slot. *Fix:* च walks
   behind: रामः सीता च. (The doubled रामः च सीता च "both Rāma and Sītā" is real — but then
   *each* joined word carries its च; the single mid-parked च is the error.)
2. **और inside a Sanskrit sentence.** "माता और पिता अत्र" — the 1.x gloss living on in the
   mouth. *Fix:* the correction, kindly: और is Hindi; Sanskrit's connector is च, and it
   follows — माता पिता च अत्र। (§4, §8).
3. **कुत्र and कुतः merged.** Asking कुतः when you mean "where at". *Fix:* -त्र = place *at*,
   -तः = place *from* (ततः "from there" has been yours since Volume 1); §9 drill 3 until the
   final beat sorts them.
4. **The dual temptation.** Reaching for a verb with joined subjects — सीता रामः च अत्र स्तः —
   because English says "are". स्तः "the two are" is real Sanskrit, but it is later machinery,
   and half-taught machinery breaks. *Fix:* today the verbless frame carries joined subjects:
   सीता रामः च अत्र। — complete and classical (§8, §15).
5. **च standing first.** "च नदी तत्र" on the English pattern "And the river…". *Fix:* an
   enclitic can never open a sentence — it has nothing to lean on. Re-park it: नदी च तत्र।
6. **यत्र asked as a question.** "यत्र माता?" for "where is mother?" *Fix:* यत्र points, कुत्र
   asks (v1.c05.l03's rule, now with the asking cousin on stage): माता कुत्र?

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — three items with something inside them.

### कुत्र — a cell in a table you already own

**कुत्र** (kutra) "where?" — कहाँ · /kut̪ɾɐ/

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The -त्र of कुत्र is a place-suffix: on the four stems अ-, त-, य-,
> कु-/क- it builds अत्र "here", तत्र "there", यत्र "where (relative)", कुत्र "where?". Hindi
> carries the same four-way grid — यहाँ, वहाँ, जहाँ, कहाँ — descended within Indo-Aryan from
> the same system. [REF:whitney1889]; [REF:masica1991]

One suffix, four addresses — and your Hindi has been rehearsing the grid for you all along.
Keep the from-family in view beside it: ततः "from there", कुतः "from where?" — the -तः row of
the same table, met in Volume 1.

### च — the one-letter "and"

**च** (ca) "and" — और · /t͡ɕɐ/

नदी वृक्षः च तत्र।
(nadī vṛkṣaḥ ca tatra.) "The river and the tree are there." — नदी और पेड़ वहाँ हैं।

चन्द्रः च तारा च तत्र।
(candraḥ ca tārā ca tatra.) "Both the moon and a star are there." — चाँद और तारा दोनों वहाँ हैं।

The trailing च joins; the doubled च…च insists. Rome's *-que* did the same jobs in the same
position — *populusque* — because Latin and Sanskrit inherited the little word, manners and
all, from one ancestor (§4).

### जगच्च — the title seam, opened

**जगत् च** (jagat ca) "and the world" — और जगत् · pause form of जगच्च

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** In connected speech and writing, जगत् + च regularly joins as जगच्च: the
> final dental त् assimilates to the following palatal च, giving the doubled च्च. The joining
> rules (sandhi) are Volume 3's subject; the pause form जगत् च is today's reading, exactly as
> न अस्ति stands beside नास्ति. [REF:whitney1889]

So the chapter title परिवारः जगच्च (parivāraḥ jagacca) reads: "the family, and the world" —
two nouns and one enclitic, wearing one seam. Three seams shown so far — नास्ति, पुनर्मिलामः,
जगच्च — and you owe production of none of them yet. Noticing is today's whole duty.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — one placement rule, two finished kits, one honest deferral.

### The enclitic family — words that walk behind

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** च "and" stands after the word or pair it joins and never opens a
> sentence: रामः सीता च "Rāma and Sītā"; doubled, रामः च सीता च "both Rāma and Sītā". अपि in
> its "too" job keeps the same manners (सीता अपि "Sītā too"). These little after-words are
> classical Sanskrit's standing habit, shared with its Latin cousin *-que*.
> [REF:apte1890 s.v. च]; [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. च]

### The question kit — complete

कः (kaḥ) "who? (m)" · का (kā) "who? (f)" · किम् (kim) "what?" · कुतः (kutaḥ) "from where?" ·
कथम् (katham) "how?" · कुत्र (kutra) "where?" — six askers, one ancient k- at the head of
each. Recite the kit as a chant; it is lesson 5's warm-up and Volume 3's scaffolding.

### The location kit — complete

अत्र (atra) "here" · तत्र (tatra) "there" · यत्र (yatra) "where — relative, points" · कुत्र
(kutra) "where? — asks". Four cells, one suffix, and the whole of today's conversation
machinery.

### Joined subjects — why the verb waits

माता पिता च अत्र। has no verb, deliberately: joined subjects change the verb's shape —
Sanskrit keeps distinct forms for two doers and for many (स्तः "the two are" is shown here as
display only). That machinery is later business, taught whole when its chapter comes. Until
then the verbless frame — fully classical — carries every joined subject. One name rides
along, honestly: words like च, अपि, न, अत्र, तत्र, यत्र, कुत्र never change shape at all; the
tradition calls them **अव्यय** (avyaya) "unchanging". Every word taught today is one — which
is why each was learnable in a single line.

### The जगच्च seam

Pause form जगत् च in all reading and speech; the joined जगच्च recognized on sight in the
chapter title. Same policy as नास्ति and पुनर्मिलामः: noticed, previewed, deferred to
Volume 3.

## 16. Conversation

सम्भाषणम् — two scenes; every word self-read. 🔊 audio:
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c02l04-dialogue.mp3` (slow, natural, and where-game gap
tracks).

### Scene 1 — hide-and-seek by the river

— भगिनी कुत्र? भगिनी कुत्र अस्ति?
(bhaginī kutra? bhaginī kutra asti?) "Where's sister? Where IS sister?" — बहन कहाँ? बहन कहाँ
है?

— पश्य! वृक्षः तत्र — भगिनी वृक्षः च तत्र!
(paśya! vṛkṣaḥ tatra — bhaginī vṛkṣaḥ ca tatra!) "Look! The tree is there — sister and tree,
both there!" — देखो! पेड़ वहाँ — बहन और पेड़ दोनों वहाँ!

— आम्! अहम् अत्र!
(ām! aham atra!) "Yes — I'm here!" — हाँ! मैं यहाँ!

— मेघः कुत्र अस्ति?
(meghaḥ kutra asti?) "Where is the cloud?" — बादल कहाँ है?

— मेघः आकाशः च तत्र! पश्य!
(meghaḥ ākāśaḥ ca tatra! paśya!) "Cloud and sky are there — look!" — बादल और आकाश वहाँ! देखो!

### Scene 2 — the evening round-up

— माता पिता च कुत्र?
(mātā pitā ca kutra?) "Where are mother and father?" — माता और पिता कहाँ?

— माता पिता च अत्र! स्वागतम्!
(mātā pitā ca atra! svāgatam!) "Mother and father are here — welcome!" — माता और पिता यहाँ!
स्वागत!

— चन्द्रः कुत्र? तारा कुत्र?
(candraḥ kutra? tārā kutra?) "Where's the moon? Where's a star?" — चाँद कहाँ? तारा कहाँ?

— पश्य! चन्द्रः तारा च तत्र!
(paśya! candraḥ tārā ca tatra!) "Look — moon and star, both there!" — देखो! चाँद और तारा वहाँ!

— नदी वृक्षः च तत्र। परिवारः अत्र। सर्वम् कुशलम्!
(nadī vṛkṣaḥ ca tatra. parivāraḥ atra. sarvam kuśalam!) "River and tree are there; the family
is here. All is well!" — नदी और पेड़ वहाँ। परिवार यहाँ। सब ठीक!

Housekeeping: every "where" was answered with अत्र or तत्र — never "in the sky" or "in the
house". Saying *in* something (आकाशे, गृहे — you may have felt the itch) takes case machinery
that arrives in Volume 3; until then तत्र does all the pointing, in perfect classical order.
And count the verbs: joined subjects rode the verbless frame every time, exactly as §15
promised. The scenes are a game — play them as one.

## 17. Story

कथा — लुकाछिपी · Hide-and-Seek. Six lines; each line: Devanagari, IAST, English, Hindi.

1. सायम् अस्ति। चन्द्रः तारा च तत्र।
   (sāyam asti. candraḥ tārā ca tatra.) "It is evening. The moon and a star are out." — शाम
   है। चाँद और तारा वहाँ।
2. माया वदति — भ्राता कुत्र? भ्राता कुत्र अस्ति?
   (māyā vadati — bhrātā kutra? bhrātā kutra asti?) "Māyā says: Where's brother? Where is
   he?" — माया कहती है — भाई कहाँ? भाई कहाँ है?
3. सीता हसति — पश्य! वृक्षः तत्र। भ्राता वृक्षः च तत्र!
   (sītā hasati — paśya! vṛkṣaḥ tatra. bhrātā vṛkṣaḥ ca tatra!) "Sītā laughs: Look — the tree
   is there, and brother and tree are both there!" — सीता हँसती है — देखो! पेड़ वहाँ। भाई और
   पेड़ दोनों वहाँ!
4. बालकः वदति — आम्, अहम् अत्र! क्षमस्व!
   (bālakaḥ vadati — ām, aham atra! kṣamasva!) "The boy says: Yes, I'm here — sorry!" — बालक
   कहता है — हाँ, मैं यहाँ! क्षमा करो!
5. माता वदति — स्वागतम्! माता पिता च अत्र।
   (mātā vadati — svāgatam! mātā pitā ca atra.) "Mother says: Welcome! Mother and father are
   here." — माता कहती है — स्वागत! माता और पिता यहाँ।
6. सर्वे वदन्ति — परिवारः जगत् च — सर्वम् कुशलम्!
   (sarve vadanti — parivāraḥ jagat ca — sarvam kuśalam!) "All say: The family and the world —
   all is well!" — सब कहते हैं — परिवार और जगत् — सब ठीक!

Two honest notes: वदन्ति "they say" is a plural verb glossed whole, as ever — its machinery is
this volume's later business (सर्वे you have known since the maṅgala śloka). And line 6 is the
chapter title spoken in pause form by its own cast — जगत् च today, जगच्च on the cover, one
seam apart.

## 18. Exercises

अभ्यासाः — six exercises; 1, 3, 4 and 5 are production tasks. Do them in order.

**अभ्यासः 1 · कुत्र-क्रीडा (the where-game — production).** Lay the world-set and family
cards face down ✍ (`planned:assets/worksheets/volume-2/v2c02l04-where-game.pdf`). Partner
draws a card and asks: X कुत्र अस्ति? You place or point it and answer: X तत्र अस्ति। or
X अत्र। Ten rounds, swap roles. Solo: the gap track asks; you point at real things and answer
aloud.

**अभ्यासः 2 · च-शल्यक्रिया (placement surgery — written).** Repair each line so the join is
Sanskrit's, not English's: (a) रामः च सीता (meaning "Rāma and Sītā") (b) माता और पिता अत्र
(c) च नदी तत्र (meaning "And the river is there") (d) "both the moon and a star are there" —
build it with the doubled pattern (e) सीता और माया अत्र. Check every repair against one
question: is the च *behind*?

**अभ्यासः 3 · अभिनयः (the scenes — production).** Perform §16's two scenes with a partner
from the role cards ✍, swap, repeat — then replay Scene 1 with new hiders: पुत्री, बालकः,
तारा. Pause form throughout; point at everything you place with तत्र.

**अभ्यासः 4 · अनुवादः (translation — production).** Into Sanskrit, aloud and then in writing:
"Where is the river?" · "The river is there." · "Mother and father are here." · "Both the
moon and a star are there." · "Where is the family? — The family is here!" Check against §8's
frames: level tune on the ask, च behind on every join, no verb on joined subjects.

**अभ्यासः 5 · श्रुतलेखनम् (dictation — production).** The dictation track 🔊: कुत्र, च, and
eight review words from the family and world sets, twice each; then one full line — माता पिता
च अत्र। Write in Devanagari; every visarga present, the त्र welded. 8/10 plus the line intact
is a pass; re-take tomorrow (§20).

**अभ्यासः 6 · किट-पाठः (the kit recital + teach-back).** Recite both completed kits from
memory — अत्र तत्र यत्र कुत्र, then कः का किम् कुतः कथम् कुत्र — and teach someone the chapter
title in two lines: what परिवारः जगच्च says, and what जगत् च is. The teach-back is lesson 5's
tasks D and E in rehearsal.

## 22. Teacher Notes

शिक्षक-टिप्पणी

**Deliver the correction with warmth.** The और fix is this lesson's headline honesty — and the
easiest thing to deliver badly. Never mock the 1.x book, and never let और sound like an error
*in Hindi* — it is Hindi's own excellent word. The formula that lands: "each word back in its
own language." Heritage learners feel ownership of और; give them the SPQR story immediately
after the correction so the room's energy goes to the marvel, not the mistake.

**Timing plan (45 min):** §3 retrieval, written (5) → the two kits assembled live on the
board, chanted (8) → च with the SPQR story and the correction (7) → placement-surgery rounds,
board-and-shout (7) → §16 scenes performed, then the where-game with real classroom objects
(10) → the जगच्च seam on the chapter cover (4) → homework briefing incl. the और hunt (4).

**Play the game for real.** Hide a card, ask कुत्र, demand pointing with every तत्र — the
physical point is the memory hook, and it keeps answers verbless and legal. Ban "in the…"
answers cheerfully: "तत्र is all the geography we own until Volume 3."

**Watch for:** the mid-parked च under speed (§12 mistake 1 — the last habit to die);
कुत्र/कुतः merging in dictation; a fast student producing स्तः from outside reading — praise
the find, label it later machinery, return to the verbless frame. The अपि precedent ("you have
parked भी behind words all your life") unlocks the enclitic idea for Hindi speakers in one
sentence. Early finishers build both-form chains (चन्द्रः च तारा च मेघः च…); strugglers run
the where-game with two cards only, अत्र vs तत्र, until the frame is reflex.
