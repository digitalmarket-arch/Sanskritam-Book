## 1. Hook

Your third lesson in this course — before you could read a single letter — put four words in
your ear: अमृत, अग्नि, अर्थ, अहम्. Three of them long ago earned their own rows in your word
ledger. One never did. अग्नि has been readable since Chapter 5 handed you the weld mechanism,
and it has waited in the wings ever since — today the fire finally takes its seat. And it does
not arrive alone: it brings the whole world with it — earth and sky, river and ocean, tree,
flower, star, cloud — and one city that has been hiding inside the script's own name since the
day you first said देवनागरी.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — the oldest word on today's list opens the oldest book.

The world set is plain classical vocabulary — rivers, trees and stars have never needed
replacing. But one of today's words carries a distinction no other word in your ledger can
match:

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** The first word of the Ṛgveda — the oldest Sanskrit text, composed
> c. 1500–1200 BCE on the scholarly consensus dating — is अग्निम् (agnim), "Agni, fire": the
> opening hymn is addressed to the fire of the sacrifice. The word you add to your ledger today
> stands at the head of the tradition's first sentence. [REF:rigveda 1.1.1] [REF:jamison2014 p.5]

Two smaller histories ride along. **नगरम्** (nagaram) "city" is the word inside the script's own
name: v1.c06.l01 §4 told you that नागरी (nāgarī) means "the urban script", from नगर — today the
city itself joins your vocabulary, and the script's name becomes self-explaining. And **पृथिवी**
(pṛthivī) "earth" circulates in two standard spellings:

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** पृथिवी and पृथ्वी (pṛthvī) are both attested, standard forms of the word
> for "earth" — the dictionaries list them side by side. This course teaches पृथिवी and reads
> both without blinking, exactly as it reads गङ्गा and गंगा. [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. pṛthivī]

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why a scene beats a list, and why drills keep asking you to retrieve.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Concrete, imageable nouns — river, tree, star — are among the easiest
> vocabulary to learn, and they are learned best bound to scenes rather than floating in lists:
> a thematic set anchored to one picture gives every word a place, and the picture cues recall
> later. Today's lesson is built as one landscape, not ten flashcards. [REF:nation2013]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Retrieval practice — producing a word from memory against a cue — beats
> re-reading it, and the effect grows when retrieval is a little effortful. The scene drills of
> §9 and §18 are retrieval engines: the card shows a river, and *you* must find नदी.
> [REF:roediger2006]

One more distinction does quiet work today. A word's *meaning* is a lexical fact — आकाशः means
"sky, space" because speakers use it so and dictionaries record it. A *classification* of things
in the world — "these five are the elements everything is made of" — is a theory, with authors
and a history. Both can share the same five words. Keeping the two claims separate is the
register skill this chapter trains, and §8 delivers its showcase.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the world, told in sentences you fully own:

> **एतत् जगत्।**
> (etat jagat.) "This is the world." — यह जगत् है।

> **नदी वहति। सूर्यः तत्र अस्ति।**
> (nadī vahati. sūryaḥ tatra asti.) "The river flows. The sun is there." — नदी बहती है। सूर्य वहाँ है।

> **वृक्षः अत्र अस्ति। पुष्पम् अपि अत्र अस्ति।**
> (vṛkṣaḥ atra asti. puṣpam api atra asti.) "The tree is here. The flower is here too." —
> पेड़ यहाँ है। फूल भी यहाँ है।

> **रात्रिः अस्ति। एषा तारा।**
> (rātriḥ asti. eṣā tārā.) "It is night. This is a star." — रात है। यह तारा है।

Not one new frame anywhere. वहति has flowed since v1.c04's वायुः वहति, अत्र and तत्र since
Chapter 5, एषा since last lesson. Ten new words drop into machinery you already run — that is
what a vocabulary lesson feels like from now on.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

आज की सूची पर आपका पुराना अधिकार है। नदी, तारा, मेघ, समुद्र, वृक्ष, पुष्प, आकाश, नगर — आठों
हिन्दी में जीवित हैं; अर्थ कोई नया नहीं सीखना। मेहनत तीन जगह है।

पहली — वही पुरानी कसौटी, शब्द का पूरा पहनावा। हिन्दी का नगर संस्कृत में नगरम् है — अन्त का म्
होंठ बन्द करके; समुद्र यहाँ समुद्रः है — विसर्ग की हल्की साँस समेत। और ऋ की सधी ध्वनि: पृथिवी
और वृक्षः में ऋ को "रि" मत बनाइए — व्यंजन-सा ठहरा हुआ /r̩/, जैसा v1.c02.l03 ने सिखाया था।
"पृथ्वी" को "प्रिथ्वी" कहना हिन्दी की क्षेत्रीय आदत है; आदत जानिए, लक्ष्य मत बनाइए।

दूसरी — वर्तनी का धीरज। पृथिवी और पृथ्वी दोनों शुद्ध हैं, दोनों शब्दकोशों में हैं। यह पाठ्यक्रम
पृथिवी लिखता है और दोनों पढ़ता है — ठीक वैसे ही जैसे आपकी आँख गंगा और गङ्गा दोनों को एक भाव से
पढ़ती आई है। किसी को "ग़लत" कहने की ज़रूरत नहीं।

तीसरी — आज का असली पाठ, फिर वही लेबल की कला। 1.x पाठ्यक्रम ने आकाश-वायु-अग्नि-जल-पृथिवी को
"पञ्चतत्त्व" कहकर शब्दावली की तरह पढ़ाया था। सच दो परतों में है: पाँचों *शब्द* साधारण शब्दावली
हैं — आकाश का अर्थ आकाश, जल का अर्थ जल; यह तथ्य है। पर इन पाँचों का "तत्त्व" होना — यानी संसार
इन्हीं पाँच से बना है — यह भारतीय दर्शन-परम्पराओं (सांख्य, वैशेषिक आदि) का सिद्धान्त है: गहरा,
सम्मान-योग्य, और अपने लेबल के साथ। दोनों बातें अपनी-अपनी जगह सच हैं — जैसे सुप्रभातम् के दो लेबल
सच थे। शब्द सीखिए बेझिझक; सिद्धान्त जानिए ससम्मान; दोनों को मिलाइए मत।

और हाँ — अग्निः की घर-वापसी। तीसरे ही पाठ से यह शब्द आपके कान में है, पाँचवें अध्याय से आँख भी
इसे पढ़ सकती है (ग् + न की वही जानी-पहचानी जुड़ाई), पर शब्द-सूची में अपनी पंक्ति आज पहली बार
मिली। देर आयद, दुरुस्त आयद।

## 8. English Explanation

No new letters, no new signs, no new grammar. The load is ten words, one honest split, and a
landscape to talk about.

### The scene, top to bottom

Look up: **आकाशः** (ākāśaḥ) "sky, space" — the everyday partner of v1.c03.l04's poetic गगन. In
it: **मेघः** (meghaḥ) "cloud" — readable since v1.c03.l01, today in sentence-dress and
scene-active — and, after dark, **तारा** (tārā) "star"; सूर्यः and चन्द्रः keep their posts from
the day-cycle lesson. Look down: **पृथिवी** (pṛthivī) "earth" holds everything; **नदी** (nadī)
"river" runs through it — गङ्गा is one — and **समुद्रः** (samudraḥ) "ocean" receives it. On the
bank, **वृक्षः** (vṛkṣaḥ) "tree" and **पुष्पम्** (puṣpam) "flower"; behind them जलम्, वनम्,
पर्वतः and हिमालयः do review duty. And one word is built, not grown: **नगरम्** (nagaram) "city" —
the word you have carried inside देवनागरी since Volume 1, नागरी being "the urban script", from
नगर (v1.c06.l01 §4). Today the city steps out of the script's name and into your ledger.

### अग्निः comes home

**अग्निः** (agniḥ) "fire" has the longest biography in today's list. It entered your ear in
v1.c01.l03 as one of the four original ear-words — अमृत, अग्नि, अर्थ, अहम् — glossed, audio-only,
waiting for letters. v1.c05.l02's weld mechanism made ग्न an ordinary stem-loss join — ग् + न,
silent one shrinks, speaker stands — and अग्नि has been mechanically readable ever since. But it
never got a vocabulary row: अमृत took one in v1.c02.l03, अर्थ came home in v1.c05.l04, अहम् in
Chapter 5 — the fire waited. Today it enters the ledger as a sentence-dressed headword, counted
ledger-new. Fifty-some lessons from ear to row: the longest promise this course has kept.

### Two spellings, one earth

**पृथिवी** and **पृथ्वी** are both standard, both in the dictionaries, one word (§4). The course
writes पृथिवी; your eye accepts both, exactly as it accepts गङ्गा and गंगा — one reading note,
filed forever.

### Five words, one doctrine — the honest split

Now the register showcase. The 1.x curriculum presented आकाश, वायु, अग्नि, जल, पृथिवी as "the
5 elements (पञ्चतत्त्व)" — one flashcard, doctrine taught as vocabulary. This course hands you
the same five words and splits the claim honestly:

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** आकाशः "sky, space" · वायुः "wind" · अग्निः "fire" · जलम् "water" ·
> पृथिवी "earth" — five ordinary vocabulary items, used for sky, wind, fire, water and earth in
> every register of the language, glossed so in the dictionaries. Learning them commits you to
> no philosophy. [REF:apte1890 s.vv.]

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** In the classical schools of Indian philosophy — the Sāṅkhya and
> Vaiśeṣika lineages foremost — these five name the पञ्चतत्त्व (pañcatattva), the five elements
> of which the manifest world is constituted. A doctrine elaborated over centuries, foundational
> to traditional cosmology and medicine, and named here as what it is: the tradition's account,
> not a claim of modern physics — and never quizzed as one.

Both layers are true, each in its register — the सुप्रभातम् move again (v2.c01.l03), now on
five words at once. The words stay fully yours; the doctrine stays fully the tradition's; each
wears its own label.

### Building scenes

The frames do the rest. Full frame: **वृक्षः अत्र अस्ति। नगरम् तत्र अस्ति।** Pointing
exclamation, verbless — legal since गङ्गा तत्र! in v1.c06.l01: **मेघः तत्र!** The l02 pair
points too: **एषा तारा।** And नदी takes the one verb every scene wants: **नदी वहति।** One
warning while the night sky is up: ten thousand stars will tempt you toward a plural. Hold —
एषा तारा, one at a time; the plural machinery is real, and it is later business.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — no new sounds; the work is welds, the syllabic ऋ, and speed. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c02l03-drill.mp3` (drills 1–3),
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c02l03-word-list.mp3` (three speeds + dictation and scene-call
tracks).

**Drill 1 — weld warm-up.** Components first, then the block, then the word: ग् + न → ग्न →
अ-ग्निः → अग्निः; क् + ष → क्ष → वृ-क्षः → वृक्षः; ष् + प → ष्प → पु-ष्पम् → पुष्पम्; द् + र →
द्र → स-मु-द्रः → समुद्रः. No vowel inside any join — "agani" means a vowel leaked (§12).

**Drill 2 — the ऋ ladder.** Hold the syllabic r alone — /r̩/, tongue curled, no vowel riding it —
then climb: ऋ → पृ → पृथिवी; ऋ → वृ → वृक्षः. Then the contrast pass: say "pri – पृ, vri – वृ"
and *feel* the difference — the first has a vowel after the r, the second is the r being its own
vowel (v1.c02.l03's lesson, back on shift).

**Drill 3 — the set at three speeds.** The §14 list three times: block by block, natural pace,
target pace. Log the third time (§18 exercise 2). The standing law holds at every speed:
*nagaram* full to the म्, every visarga breathed, no ऋ collapsing to "ri".

**Drill 4 — scene calls.** The audio names a scene element in English or Hindi ("river!",
"बादल!"); you answer with the Sanskrit word inside two seconds — then, second round, with a
whole frame sentence: नदी! → नदी वहति। Retrieval under a clock is the fluency strand doing its
job [REF:roediger2006].

**Narrow note — the syllabic ऋ:** in /pr̩t̪ʰiʋiː/ and /ʋr̩kʂɐh/ the /r̩/ carries the syllable
itself — no /i/ or /ɐ/ beside it. The modern habit [ɾɪ] ("pri-", "vri-") is a regional
pronunciation, noted as a habit and not the target (STYLE_GUIDE's standing rule since
v1.c02.l03).

**Narrow note — the ग्न join:** in /ɐgn̪ih/ the /g/ releases straight into the dental nasal
/n̪/ — one gesture, no vowel between. The breathy मेघः keeps its /gʱ/: voice and breath
together, never a plain [g].

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** Recitation practice echoes the vowel after a visarga — अग्निः
> chanted as [ɐgn̪ihi], as रामः is [rɑːmɐhɐ]. A tradition-register habit of recited text, noted
> as such; plain speech keeps the light /h/.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — three checks; the articulators already know everything here. The word-anatomy
strip on the scene card (`planned:assets/images/volume-2/v2c02l03-world-scene.svg`) marks the
welds and the ऋ words.

1. **The ऋ check.** Say पृ in slow motion with a mirror: the tongue tip curls up and holds; the
   jaw does not drop for a vowel until थि arrives. If you see the jaw bounce right after the प,
   a "ri" slipped in — re-run drill 2.
2. **The ष्प seam.** In पुष्पम् the retroflex hiss ष ends with the tongue curled behind the
   ridge, and the very next gesture is the lips closing for प — tongue back, lips front, no
   vowel bridging them. Feel the handoff happen in one beat.
3. **The breathy check.** Palm on your chest for मेघः: the घ should hum *and* push air — voice
   plus breath /gʱ/ (v1.c03.l01's own drill). And end नगरम् and पुष्पम् with lips visibly
   closed — every final म् is a closure you can see.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; these feed the quiz distractors.

1. **ऋ flattened to "ri".** पृथिवी read "prithivī", वृक्षः read "vrikshah". *Fix:* the syllabic
   /r̩/ — tongue curled, no vowel riding it (§9 drill 2). The [ɾɪ] habit is regional and real;
   the course notes it and targets /r̩/.
2. **The dress dropped — or over-applied.** नगरम् clipped to "nagar", समुद्रः losing its
   visarga — the Hindi habit imported; or, opposite error, a visarga pinned on everything (नदी
   given a ः it never wears). *Fix:* each word's dress is a fact about that word — learn the
   word *with* its ending, §14's whole design.
3. **A vowel leaked into a weld.** अग्निः read "aganiḥ", पुष्पम् read "puṣapam". *Fix:* no vowel
   inside a conjunct — build from components, ग् + न, ष् + प, one block each (§9 drill 1).
4. **One spelling declared wrong.** "पृथ्वी is a typo" — or "पृथिवी is archaic". *Fix:* both are
   standard and attested; the course writes पृथिवी and reads both — the गङ्गा/गंगा policy (§4, §8).
5. **Doctrine collapsed into dictionary.** "आकाशः *means* the ether-element" — the 1.x
   flashcard's error. *Fix:* the word means "sky, space" (FACT); its role as a तत्त्व is
   Sāṅkhya/Vaiśeṣika doctrine (TRADITION, attributed) — two labels, both true, never merged (§8).
6. **The cognate direction flipped.** "Latin *ignis* comes from Sanskrit अग्नि." *Fix:* siblings,
   not parent and child — both descend from one Proto-Indo-European ancestor; nobody borrowed
   anything (§13). The chapter's direction-honesty rule has no exceptions.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — three stories inside today's list.

### अग्निः and its Latin sibling

**अग्निः** (agniḥ) "fire" — the ear-word of v1.c01.l03, the Ṛgveda's opening word (§4), and
today a headword at last. Its family is the point:

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Sanskrit अग्नि and Latin *ignis* "fire" are cognates — parallel
> descendants of a shared Proto-Indo-European ancestor, like मातृ and *māter* on last lesson's
> shelf. Neither language borrowed from the other; the resemblance is inheritance, not exchange.
> English *ignite* and *igneous* carry the Latin branch into your daily vocabulary.
> [REF:mallory2006]

And one honest silence: **नदी** gets no cognate row. It has no solid, everyday English or Latin
sibling to show, so the shelf says nothing — this course does not invent relatives to make a
word feel important. A word with no shelf entry is still a perfectly good river.

### The city inside the script

**नगरम्** (nagaram) "city" — नगर; शहर · /n̪ɐgɐɾɐm/

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** नागरी — the script name inside देवनागरी — is "the urban (script)", a
> derivative of नगर "city" (v1.c06.l01 §4). Every time you have named the script since Volume 1,
> you have said the word "city" without knowing you owned it. Today the loan is called in.
> [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. nāgarī]

### One earth, two spellings

**पृथिवी** (pṛthivī) "earth" — and its equally standard twin पृथ्वी (pṛthvī). The dictionaries
carry both; classical texts use both; the 1.x curriculum taught the shorter form. The course
writes पृथिवी, your eye reads either, and the precedent is already in your reflexes: गङ्गा and
गंगा have been one river to you since v1.c06.l01. Two circulating forms, one word, zero alarm.

## 15. Grammar

### Shapes observed, not taught

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** In today's set the ः-dressed words — आकाशः, वृक्षः, समुद्रः, मेघः,

### Scene syntax, inventoried

## 22. Teacher Notes

अध्यापकटिप्पणी

**Open with the riddle, then the fire.** Write देवनागरी on the board and ask what's hiding
inside it; circle नगर when someone finds it — "you have been saying 'city' since Volume 1."
Then the second reveal: play the four ear-words from v1.c01.l03 and ask which one never got a
vocabulary row. The homecoming framing makes अग्निः the lesson's emotional anchor, and both
reveals cost zero new machinery.

**Timing plan (45 min):** §3 retrieval, written (5) → the scene build: draw a horizon line on
the board and add each element as its word lands — sky, cloud, star above; earth, river, ocean,
tree, flower below; city on the horizon; fire beside it (10) → the अग्निः homecoming + अग्नि ~
*ignis* shelf moment (5) → the पञ्चतत्त्व split: two chips, FACT on the word row, TRADITION on
the doctrine card — build it live, name the lineages, promise it is never quizzed as physics
(7) → ऋ and weld lab: पृ-वृ contrast, ग्न-क्ष-ष्प-द्र from components (6) → scene relay: teams
describe a card, one sentence per learner, frames only; a plural or a locative attempt sits
down smiling (7) → word list at three speeds; dictation launch; homework (5).

**Run the relay strictly but warmly.** The scene relay's whole point is that the old frames are
*enough* — when a learner reaches for "stars are in the sky", coach it back to एषा तारा। तारा
तत्र। and say out loud that the plural and the locative are coming; deferral stated is honesty,
deferral hidden is confusion.

**The register moment needs the सुप्रभातम् warmth.** Some students will hear the पञ्चतत्त्व
split as the course calling the tradition wrong; some will hear the doctrine card as the course
teaching physics. Read §8's two callouts aloud, in order, and land the same line that worked in
v2.c01.l03: two things are true, each in its register — respect flows *from* the labels, not
despite them.

**Differentiation:** Hindi speakers own most of these words — their work is the dress (नगरम्
full to the म्, विसर्ग breathed), the ऋ target, and the register sort, where familiarity
carries no information; deputize them for the teach-back. Script-side learners find पृथिवी and
वृक्षः dense — the count-first protocol (पृ-थि-वी, वृ-क्षः) shrinks both.

**What students find hard:** the syllabic ऋ against a lifetime of "ri" (mirror drill, little
and often); believing both पृथिवी and पृथ्वी are right (the गङ्गा precedent settles it fast);
the plural urge under a starry sky (hold the singular frames, promise the machinery); and the
doctrine/vocabulary split (see above — warmth plus labels, never labels alone).
