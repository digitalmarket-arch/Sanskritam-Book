## 1. Hook

This chapter handed you two of the oldest word-hoards a human being owns — the people at
home and the world outside the door — and, hidden inside them, the best story in
historical linguistics: your English *mother* and your Sanskrit माता are cousins, not
copies. Today you prove all of it: present a family, describe a world, join things with
च, find things with कुत्र — and sort the claims one more time, because the direction of
a fact is part of the fact. Twenty-two ledger-new words this chapter. Time to show they
work.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — one paragraph, as in every review lesson.

Every exam in this course has tested sounds, signs or words. Today's tests something
rarer: **direction**. Three kinds of word-relations crossed this chapter — cognates
(माता ~ mother: one ancestor, two heirs), loans outbound (dharma → English), and, from
the last chapter's होरा-preview forward, loans inbound. The 1.x curriculum blurred all
three into "English comes from Sanskrit"; the 2.0 exam hands you claims and asks for
the arrow.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spaced practice produces far more durable learning than the same
> practice massed together. [REF:cepeda2006]

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why the presentation and the scene are the real exam.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Retrieval practice strengthens memory more than re-reading — the
> testing effect. [REF:roediger2006]

Recognition tests would let this chapter's words hide in a list. Production is the
honest measure: a family presented aloud forces एषः/एषा to be *chosen*, not recognized;
a scene described forces the world set to be *retrieved*, not matched. Tasks C and E
are therefore recordings, and the rubric (§22) scores choices, not just words
[REF:nation2013].

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the exam's two frames, fully readable:

> **एषः मम पिता। एषा मम माता। एतत् मम गृहम्।**
> (eṣaḥ mama pitā. eṣā mama mātā. etat mama gṛham.) — "This is my father. This is my
> mother. This is my home." — यह मेरे पिता हैं। यह मेरी माता हैं। यह मेरा घर है।

> **वृक्षः अत्र अस्ति। नदी तत्र। माता पिता च गृहे?? न — माता पिता च अत्र।**
> Corrected on the page as you read: no locatives yet — **माता पिता च अत्र।** "Mother
> and father are here." — माता और पिता यहाँ हैं।

The self-correcting second line is deliberate: the exam rewards staying inside what the
course has honestly taught — verbless frames carry joined subjects until the verb
machinery arrives (l04 §15).

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — exam-day checks; the reference values live in the lesson tables. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c02l05-listening-test.mp3`,
`planned:assets/audio/volume-2/v2c02l05-dictation-set.mp3`.

**The kinship finals.** पिता, भ्राता, माता end on long आ held two beats; भगिनी and
पुत्री on long ई. The presentation's most common slip is clipping these under speech
pressure — the recording catches it.

**The cluster row.** वृक्षः (the क्ष at work), पुष्पम् (ष्प), समुद्रः (द्र), कुटुम्बम्
(म्ब) — one onset each, no seam vowels, exam pace.

**The च rhythm.** माता पिता च — the enclitic leans on पिता: say it as one breath-group,
mā-tā pi-tā-ca, a hair of pause before, none after पिता.

**Warm-up before recording:** the pointing trio aloud (एषः एषा एतत्), one family line,
one scene line with च, one कुत्र question with the rise.

## 10. IPA

पृथ्वी reads /pr̩t̪ʰʋiː/ (one honest note: two standard spellings, l03 §8). च is /t͡ɕɐ/
— full vowel, never reduced. दुहिता /d̪uɦit̪ɑː/ keeps its medial /ɦ/ voiced — the
बहुत-habit check from Volume 1 rides again.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the exam circuit, one check per task.

- **Task A (ears):** who vs where — the questions differ in one word (कः vs कुत्र);
  listen for the question word, not the melody.
- **Task B (pen):** every nasal-before-stop asks the stop (कुटुम्बम् — म्ब, the labial
  row's own nasal); every visarga is a real breath.
- **Task C (mouth):** the एषः/एषा choice happens *before* the mouth opens — pick the
  form, then speak; a corrected form mid-word counts as a slip.
- **Task E (eyes then mouth):** scan the scene card fully first; plan the च join in
  advance — the enclitic fails when improvised last.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — two worked model answers before you start.

**Model family line (task C).** Role card: an older sister named लता. Choose the
pointer: a woman → एषा. Choose the word: everyday sister → भगिनी. Frame it: **एषा मम
भगिनी। तस्याः नाम लता।** — and note what the second sentence did: तस्याः "her" (the
display form from l02) beside नाम, the v1.c06 name-frame reused with a new possessor.
If तस्याः feels beyond reach on exam day, the honest fallback is full credit too:
**एषा मम भगिनी। भगिन्याः?? न —** stay inside the taught: **एषा मम भगिनी। मम भगिनी
लता।** Simplest correct beats ambitious wrong — that rule *is* the course.

**Model direction sort (task D).** Card: "दुहिता and *daughter* are cognates." Check
the shelf (l02 §13): दुहितृ ~ Gk. thygátēr ~ Eng. daughter — the textbook set. Verdict:
**FACT**, with the reasoning line "shared PIE ancestor; no borrowing in either
direction." Compare the trap card: "English *mother* comes from Sanskrit मातृ" — same
family, wrong arrow: **reject**, and the correction is the full answer: "cognates —
siblings from one ancestor; nobody borrowed anybody's mother."

## 16. Conversation

सम्भाषणम् — the exam-morning scene; every word self-read. 🔊 in the listening-test
audio.

— सुप्रभातम्! अपि सर्वम् कुशलम्?
(suprabhātam! api sarvam kuśalam?) "Good morning! All well?" — सुप्रभात! सब ठीक?

— आम्, सर्वम् कुशलम्! एषः मम भ्राता।
(ām, sarvam kuśalam! eṣaḥ mama bhrātā.) "Yes, all well! This is my brother." — हाँ, सब
ठीक! यह मेरा भाई।

— नमस्ते! तव नाम किम्?
(namaste! tava nāma kim?) "Greetings! What is your name?" — नमस्ते! तुम्हारा नाम क्या
है?

— मम नाम अर्जुनः। भगिनी अहम् च अत्र!
Careful — the enclitic leans on the LAST joined word: **भगिनी अहम् च अत्र!**
(mama nāma arjunaḥ. bhaginī aham ca atra!) "My name is Arjuna. My sister and I are
here!" — मेरा नाम अर्जुन। बहन और मैं यहाँ हैं!

— स्वागतम्! परीक्षा अत्र अस्ति। स्वस्ति!
(svāgatam! parīkṣā atra asti. svasti!) "Welcome! The exam is here. Good luck!" —
स्वागत है! परीक्षा यहाँ है। कल्याण हो!

## 17. Story

कथा — production, not consumption: **the presentation rehearsal.** From the role-card
sheet, draw a family card (six members, names, one home) and rehearse the full
presentation aloud twice — first slow, then at pace:

एषः मम पिता। तस्य नाम …। एषा मम माता। … एषः मम भ्राता। एषा मम भगिनी। एतत् मम गृहम्।
मम परिवारः अत्र अस्ति!

Worked closing line, for comparison after your attempt: **मम माता मम पिता च अत्र। अहम्
अपि अत्र। एतत् मम कुटुम्बम्!** — the च and अपि enclitics both walking correctly behind
their words, the neuter एतत् landing on कुटुम्बम्. (Retrieval + recombination — the
strongest rehearsal pair there is [REF:roediger2006].)
