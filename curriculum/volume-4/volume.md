# Volume 4 — पाणिनीयम् · The Pāṇinian System

Volume 3 handed over 508 readable words, the cases in their tables, the present-system verb
in every person, the joins between words, the pronouns and the compound, and 28 sūtras, each
quoted where it stated in one line what the learner had just observed. Volume 4 takes the
forms the learner has used since Volumes 1–3 — रामः, रामेण, भवति, नमस्ते, अगच्छत्, शृणोति,
उवाच, कृतम्, आगत्य, पठ्यते — and asks what made each one: which affix, which marker, which
rule. Every teaching lesson is built round the derivation of a form the learner already owns,
and a rule, a term or a marker enters the course at the moment that derivation needs it and
not before. The volume is a growing toolbox. Its last task is the learner deriving भू → भवति
alone (v4.c06.l04, task C).

The binding plan for the volume — every lesson's grammar point, forms, sūtras with their
tiers, derivations step by step, headwords and ledger figures, the texts with a status for
every word-form, and the decisions behind them — is `research/notes/vol4-plan.md`. The
scripts it cites are kept in `research/evidence/vol4-plan/`.

## What changes from Volume 3

- **Derivation first.** Volume 3 opened chunks into systems; Volume 4 opens the system into
  the rules that made each cell. Every teaching lesson follows one recipe: an owned form; the
  question it raises ("why शृणोति but शृण्वन्ति?"); the one or two new tools that answer it;
  the derivation, done once with the teacher, each step with its sūtra; the same tools run by
  the learner on new forms; production. A table is never the first thing on the page; neither
  is a sūtra. Each tool explains forms and also makes new ones: the reduplication of 6.1.8,
  which explains उवाच, also makes जगाम, चकार and ददौ.
- **A term or rule appears only where a derivation uses it** (IR-041). There is no list of
  all the pratyāhāras or the meta-rules; each marker arrives with the form it explains (c03.l03
  collects them on one card); and a count prints only with its method.
- **Five tiers of sūtra, and a budget.** Q: quoted with a word-by-word gloss and its
  padaccheda, recognised by gist, at most three new in a lesson. B: the 26 sūtras of the भवति
  derivation, recognised by number and gist. D: the authority for one derivation step, never
  recalled. K: on a reference card. R: read with the Kāśikā or the Laghusiddhāntakaumudī in
  Chapter 6. The plan counts 238 distinct Aṣṭādhyāyī sūtras in learner text — B 26 · Q 25 ·
  R 11 · D 121 · K 51, and 4 owned Volume 3 sūtras re-read; the fourteen Māheśvara sūtras are
  counted apart. The memory load at exit is 25 new Q and 25 new B by gist.
- **Five memorised tables, the rest derived.** T9–T13 (the fourteen sūtras, the perfect
  endings, the -अत् participle, the future, the passive) join Volume 3's eight. Everything
  else is a Δ card or a rule-made form; the aorist, the conditional, the periphrastic future
  and the benedictive stay on one recognition card, with the chunk मा भैषीः as the one
  productive form.
- **What the language does and what Pāṇini says about it are kept apart.** Usage is FACT,
  cited to Whitney or a text; Pāṇini's analysis is TRADITION, and every derivation item is
  headed "Derive, according to Pāṇini, …". Where the two disagree, both are printed (four
  times: आह, class 10, guṇa and the perfect). Dates are HISTORY, with a key and a hedge; the
  Śiva-drum origin of the fourteen sūtras is TRADITION, labelled legend, and never assessed.
- **The derivation is a production task.** From c01.l03 on, every lesson has a "derive it
  yourself" item beside sentences to write or say. The toolbox card grows lesson by lesson;
  the volume exam's card holds 60 sūtras — the 26 of the भवति derivation and 34 distractors, in number
  order — so that the task tests choosing and ordering, not recall of numbers.
- **The ledger counts what the learner owns, not what the tools build** (STYLE_GUIDE §8,
  rules 16–23). Roots, the kṛt products of owned verbs, transparent taddhitas, the ordinals
  and labels such as pratyāhāras and affix names add 0. Each lesson's §14 prints "tools
  added: n" beside "cells drilled: n".
- **Joined text throughout, and the serial continued.** Every course text is printed joined,
  with its pause form beneath; a derivation shows its seams (भू + अ + ति) and ends on the
  joined word. The §17 serial retells the monkey and the crocodile after Pañcatantra 4,
  labelled a retelling, and stops before the monkey's trick, which the Chapter 5 exam reads
  in the original, unseen.

## Entry and exit

**Entry:** Volume 3 complete — the Volume 3 exam passed (v3.c06.l05); 508 readable words;
the 28 sūtras of Volume 3 by number and gist; the tradition's names for the cases, the
persons, the voices, the present-system lakāras, the sandhi kinds and the compound kinds;
an unseen Hitopadeśa or Pañcatantra prose passage read joined, with at most one gloss in
five words.

**Exit:** 627 readable words (508 plus 119 ledger-new; 3–8 in a teaching lesson, 0 in an
exam; chapter exits 521 · 537 · 565 · 591 · 617 · 627). The learner reads an unseen passage
of classical narrative prose of about 60–150 words, printed joined, with marginal glosses for
no more than one word in six, names excluded, and parses every finite form and participle in
it; narrates in the past with the perfect, the -त participle and absolutive chains; speaks of
the future and of purpose, of what must be done, and of what one has someone else do; turns
active sentences into passive and back, and sets a scene with the locative absolute; declines
the listed consonant stems and the numerals; reads a short sūtra by its case code, supplies
the word carried down when told which word is carried, and checks it against a line of the
Kāśikā; follows the Laghusiddhāntakaumudī's derivation of बभूव; recites the fourteen sūtras; names the three
meta-rules and what each decides; says, according to Pāṇini, what a kāraka is and how case
follows from it; sorts statements about the grammar into FACT, TRADITION, HISTORY, legend and
banned claim; and derives, according to Pāṇini, भू → भवति by themselves, every step in order
with its sūtra. Passing v4.c06.l04 gates Volume 5. The labels are CEFR-informed descriptions
of function; Sanskrit has no official CEFR mapping [REF:cefr2020].
