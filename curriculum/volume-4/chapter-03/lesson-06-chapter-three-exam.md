## 1. Hook

In Volume 3 भवति, अभवत्, भवतु and भवेत् were four rows of one table, said and written whole. Chapter
3 took that table apart. Lesson 1 found the ल् under every ending; lesson 2, the sign that stands
between the root and the ending; lesson 3, the प् that decides whether the stem before an ending is
strong or weak; lesson 4, the doubled syllable of बभूव; lesson 5, the स्य of भविष्यति. Today the
table goes back together, and it is longer than it was: the classical table of √भू has ten rows. You
fill six of them from memory and name the other four; you read, cold, one rule about a lakāra; you
sort twelve verbs into their classes; you fill eight strong and weak cells and say why; you read a
scene of the Hitopadeśa you have never seen; and you tell the serial in the perfect and then in the
future. The title, **तृतीयपरीक्षा** (tṛtīyaparīkṣā) "third exam", is one word, read whole. 565 words,
89 tools. Pass, and Chapter 3 is complete.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — what the language does in the forms of today's tasks, in Whitney's words; and
why the exam asks for the same knowledge by five routes.

No new fact about Sanskrit enters today. What the language does is FACT, and Whitney states it
without any rule-numbers. These are the lines the chapter set beside its rules, one sentence each, in
four groups, each as he wrote it, final -s and all; the course's own cells print the visarga where he
writes -s (बभूवतुः for his *babhūvátus*).

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The present system, in Whitney's words:
> - "These classes have in common, as their most fundamental characteristic, a shift of accent: the tone being now upon the ending, and now upon the root or the class-sign." [REF:whitney1889 §604]
> - "The Hindu grammarians reckon a tenth class or *cur*-class, having a class-sign *áya* added to a strengthened root (thus, *coráya* from √*cur*), and an inflection like that of the other *a*-stems." [REF:whitney1889 §607]
> - "The roots *rud* weep, *svap* sleep, *an* breathe, and *śvas* blow insert a union-vowel *i* before all the endings beginning with a consonant, except the *s* and *t* of 2d and 3d sing. impf., …" [REF:whitney1889 §631]
> - "The root *brū* speak, say … takes the union-vowel *ī* after the root when strengthened, before the initial consonant of an ending". [REF:whitney1889 §632]
> - "The root अस् *as* be loses its vowel in weak forms (except where protected by combination with the augment)." [REF:whitney1889 §636]
> - "The root *han* smite, slay … in weak forms, it loses its *n* before an initial consonant (except *m* and *v*) of a personal ending (not in the optative), and its *a* before an initial vowel — and in the latter case its *h*, in contact with the *n*, is changed to *gh*". [REF:whitney1889 §637]
> - Of कृ: "It has the irregularity that in the strong form of stem it (as well as the class-sign) has the *guṇa*-strengthening, and that in the weak form it is changed to *kur*, so that the two forms of stem are करो *karó* and कुरु *kuru*." [REF:whitney1889 §714]
> - "The present-stem of this class is made by adding अ *a* to the root, which has the accent, and, when that is possible …, is strengthened to *guṇa*." [REF:whitney1889 §734]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The perfect:
> - The endings, active: "1 *a* *vá* *má* · 2 *tha* *áthus* *á* · 3 *a* *átus* *ús*" — the nine of T10. [REF:whitney1889 §553]
> - "But the *ū* of √*bhū* remains unchanged, and adds *v* before a vowel-ending: thus, *babhū́va* etc." [REF:whitney1889 §793]
> - The 3rd persons of √*kṛ*: *cakā́ra*, *cakrátus*, *cakrús*. [REF:whitney1889 §800]
> - "The root *ah* speak occurs only in the perfect indicative, and only in the 3d persons of all numbers and in the 2d sing. and du., in active …" [REF:whitney1889 §801]
> - "In the later language, it is simply a preterit or past tense, equivalent with the imperfect, and freely interchangeable or coördinated with it." [REF:whitney1889 §821]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The aorist: "The aorist-system is a formation of infrequent occurrence in much of
> the classical Sanskrit (its forms are found, for example, only twenty-one times in the Nala, eight in
> the Hitopadeśa, seven in Manu …)". [REF:whitney1889 §826]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The futures:
> - "The tense-sign of this future is the syllable स्य *syá*, added to the root either directly or by an auxiliary vowel इ *i* (in the latter case becoming इष्य *iṣyá*)." [REF:whitney1889 §932]
> - Of the roots that end in a vowel, those in ā add the tense-sign with no इ; नी and लभ् have both forms, with इ and without. [REF:whitney1889 §935]
> - "From the future-stem is made an augment-preterit, by prefixing the augment and adding the secondary endings, in precisely the same manner as an imperfect from a present-stem in अ *a*. This preterit is called the conditional." [REF:whitney1889 §940]
> - The 3rd persons of the periphrastic future of √भू: *bhavitā́*, *bhavitā́rāu*, *bhavitā́ras*. [REF:whitney1889 §944]
> - "It expresses in general what is going to take place at some time to come — but often, as in other languages, adding on the one hand an implication of will or intention, or on the other hand that of promise or threatening." [REF:whitney1889 §948]
> - "The periphrastic future is defined by the grammarians as expressing something to be done at a definite time to come. And this, though but faintly traceable in later use, is a distinct characteristic of the formation in the language where it first makes its appearance." [REF:whitney1889 §949]

How Pāṇini's rules arrive at those shapes is TRADITION — a derivation, and the reason a stem is
strong or weak, are his analysis — and so every such item is headed "according to Pāṇini" and is
marked only in that frame. The two accounts agree on every form of today's tasks. Where they speak
differently, the chapter said so: Whitney's shift of accent is Pāṇini's प् [l03 §5]; Whitney's "root
*ah*", found only in the perfect, is Pāṇini's substitute in the present of ब्रू [l04 §5]. Neither
difference is asked today.

Each task retrieves the chapter by a different route — the retrieval of §3, run five times: A from a
lakāra to its cells, and from a rule never met to its meaning; B from a verb to its class; C from an
ending to the grade of the stem before it; D from a page of Sanskrit to what it says; E from a story
to the forms that tell it.

The course principle behind the chapter, with its one source: formulaic chunks are taught whole and
analysed later [REF:nation2013]. उवाच and आह were whole items in Volume 3; this chapter is the "later"
for both, and for the rows of the √भू table you said in Volume 3 without their rules.

Spaced practice helps retention [REF:cepeda2006]: that is why the exam opens with Station 0, and why
§20 books two items for Chapter 4.

is a root as the Dhātupāṭha cites it, with its final अ; तिङन्तम् is a label. नलः is a name.

The seams are Volume 3's. अ + इ → ए: पठेति. इ + इ → one long ई: पठतीति. अ + अ → आ: तवाभिप्रायः.
अ + उ → ओ, inside a compound: सूर्योदये. -अः before a voiced consonant → ओ: ऋषयो देवान्, वीरो नदीम्,
व्याधो मृगान्, नलो नाम. -अः before अ → -ओ, and the अ elided, marked ऽ: श्वोऽहम्. -आः before a vowel
→ -आ and a gap: मृगा उत्तिष्ठन्ति. सः before a vowel → स and a gap: स आगमिष्यति. म् → ं before a
consonant: तिङन्तं पदम्, नदीं तरति, ग्रामं यन्ति, ग्रामं गमिष्यामि, नूनं सः. A consonant-final word is
written together with the next: देवान्यजन्ति, मृगान्हन्ति, शिष्यानुवाच. A comma, a daṇḍa and a "?" stop
every seam: धातुः and उत्तिष्ठन्ति stand in their pause form before the comma. A compound is one word,
its members hyphenated in the pause form: सूर्य-उदये.

### (4) Strong and weak — the rule pair (l03)

> **According to Pāṇini, the p decides.** A sārvadhātuka affix without the marker p counts as
> ṅ-marked (1.2.4), and before a k- or ṅ-marked affix guṇa and vṛddhi do not apply (1.1.5). So the
> singular parasmaipada endings तिप्, सिप्, मिप्, which carry p, leave a **strong** stem before them
> (शृणो-, करो-, अस्-, ब्रवी-, ए-, युनज्-); every other ending leaves a **weak** one (शृणु-, कुरु-,
> स्-, ब्रू-, इ-, युञ्ज्-). Among the signs, शप् has p, and the root before it takes guṇa (नयति); श,
> श्नु and श्ना have none, and the root before them takes none (लिखति, शृणोति's शृ, गृह्णाति). One rule
> per verb finishes the weak stem: 6.4.110 कुरु-; 6.4.111 स्-, युञ्ज्-; 6.1.16 पृच्छ्-, गृह्-; and on the
> card, 6.4.113 गृह्णी-. The -अ stems have no split.

The full present of the twelve athematic verbs, seven cells each, and the laṅ and loṭ split are l03
§15's tables; the anubandha card there is K.
