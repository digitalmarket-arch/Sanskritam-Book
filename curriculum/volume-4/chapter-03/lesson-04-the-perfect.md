## 1. Hook

Volume 3 handed you उवाच and आह as whole words (v3.c03.l03), "(he) said" and "(he) says", and the
Pañcatantra's ददौ "he gave" with a gloss in the margin (v3.c06.l03). Look at them again. उवाच
comes from वच्, yet it begins with an उ- that वच् does not have. ददौ comes from दा, as ददाति does,
yet it ends in -औ.

And the गच्छति you have said since Volume 2 has a perfect too: जगाम, "he went". Why do जगाम and
ददौ begin with a second copy of their root's first sound? Today one rule doubles the root, and a
handful of rules trim the copy.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — where today's rules stand in the grammar, and two accounts, side by side, of
what the perfect is for.

Today's Q rule, 3.2.115 परोक्षे लिट्, stands in Book 3, among the rules that give each l-affix
its time; it sits under a heading of that stretch of the grammar, भूते "in the past" (3.2.84), and
lesson 1's 3.2.123 वर्तमाने लट् comes a little after it. The endings of the perfect (3.4.82) stand
beside lesson 1's eighteen (3.4.78), in the last quarter of the same book. The doubling of the
root opens Book 6 (6.1.8), and the rules that trim the doubled syllable stand together in Book 7
(7.4.59, 7.4.60, 7.4.62), under the heading अङ्गस्य, 6.4.1, that you met in c02.l03. A derivation
brings them together.

What is the perfect for? The tradition gives one answer, and the language, as it is written,
gives another. Both are printed, and both are asked: the first only in its attributed form
(§19 Q1), the second as Whitney's statement (§19 Q8).

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** According to Pāṇini, liṭ is the past "out of sight" (3.2.115); the
> Kāśikā: "the past, not of today, and out of sight". [REF:kashika 3.2.115]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Whitney: "there is no evidence of its being either exclusively or
> distinctively so employed at any period"; in the later language "it is simply a preterit or
> past tense, equivalent with the imperfect … on the whole less common than the imperfect,
> although the preferences of different authors are diverse, and it sometimes exceeds the
> imperfect in frequency". [REF:whitney1889 §821]

The Kāśikā's words are given in English; its own line is in the teacher's notes. Whitney's sentence
begins with the tradition's account — "According to the Hindu grammarians, the perfect is used in
the narration of facts not witnessed by the narrator" — and then says what the texts show
[REF:whitney1889 §821].

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** The Kāśikā, the commentary whose glosses the English lines of this
> volume follow, is placed c. 7th century CE [REF:kashika]. The Hitopadeśa, the book of today's
> reading, is placed c. 9th–12th century CE on the scholarly estimate [REF:hitopadesha].

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — what the language does: the perfect's four marks, what a corpus count shows
of its use, and three statements on आह.

Whitney lists the marks of the perfect before he gives a single form.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "The characteristics of the formation are these: 1. a stem made by
> reduplication of the root; 2. a distinction between stronger and weaker forms of stem, the
> former being used (as in presents of the First or non-*a*-conjugation) in the singular active,
> the latter in all other persons; 3. endings in some respects peculiar, unlike those of the
> present; 4. the frequent use, especially in the later language, of a union-vowel इ *i* between
> stem and endings." [REF:whitney1889 §781]

You can see three of the four in the first forms of today. जगाम has a stem made by doubling the
root. जगाम beside जग्मुः has a strong form in the singular and a weak one elsewhere — the split
lesson 3 found in the athematic present, the "First or non-*a*-conjugation" of Whitney's
parenthesis. Its endings, -अ, -अतुः, -उः, are not the present's -ति, -तः, -अन्ति. The fourth mark,
the union vowel इ, stands in none of the 3rd-person cells of §15's production table.

How much the perfect is used is a question for a count, and the count prints with its method.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** In the Digital Corpus of Sanskrit's tagging of the Hitopadeśa, 271 finite
> verb tokens carry the tag 'past': 258 perfects (7 of them periphrastic), 9 aorists, 3 benedictives
> and one adverb, tatra, tagged as a verb; the forms counted more than ten times are āha (112), uvāca
> (63) and ūcuḥ (13, and once ūcur); in the Viṣṇupurāṇa the perfect is 17.4% of all verb tokens, the
> imperfect 4.5%. (Counted by the course from the DCS's morphological tags, 2026-11; the aorists and
> benedictives, which carry the same tag, told apart by the corpus's mood and formation tags, one
> aorist and the adverb by hand; the tag 'past' marks a form, not its time.) [REF:dcs]

The forms counted more than ten times are all 3rd persons: the narrator's. That is the part of the
perfect this volume makes yours. The first of them, āha, needs three statements, and the course
prints all three.

| Who | Register | What is said of आह |
|---|---|---|
| Pāṇini, 3.4.84 [K, on §15's card] | TRADITION | According to Pāṇini, after ब्रू the first five P endings of laṭ are optionally replaced by the first five of liṭ, and ब्रू by आह: आह, आहतुः, आहुः, आत्थ, आहथुः. In his account they are present forms. [REF:ashtadhyayi 3.4.84]; [REF:kashika 3.4.84] |
| Whitney | FACT | "The root *ah* *speak* occurs only in the perfect indicative, and only in the 3d persons of all numbers and in the 2d sing. and du., in active …" — and: "The perfects *veda* and *āha* are everywhere used with present value." [REF:whitney1889 §801]; [REF:whitney1889 §821] |
| the corpus | FACT (a tag) | The DCS tags the Hitopadeśa's 112 tokens Tense=Past: a tag of the form, not of its time. [REF:dcs] |

Where Pāṇini and Whitney disagree, both are printed. Pāṇini says how his grammar makes the form,
and files it with the present of ब्रू; Whitney files it under a root of its own, as a perfect used
with present value; the corpus tags the form's shape. One more observation, stated and not quoted:
in the Hitopadeśa's story of Vīravara (Book 3), the narration frames the speakers' words now with
आह, now with the present ब्रूते — the ब्रूते you read in today's passage (§13). No statement here is
"the Hitopadeśa uses आह as a past": that is §12's mistake 6.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the day's idea in three lines, each printed joined, its pause form beneath,
then the gloss.

> पुरा नलो नाम राजा बभूव। (purā nalo nāma rājā babhūva.)
> पुरा नलः नाम राजा बभूव। (purā nalaḥ nāma rājā babhūva.)
> "Long ago there was a king named Nala." — बहुत पहले नल नाम के एक राजा थे।

> स राजा नगराद्बहिर्जगाम। (sa rājā nagarād bahir jagāma.)
> सः राजा नगरात् बहिः जगाम। (saḥ rājā nagarāt bahiḥ jagāma.)
> "That king went out of the city." — वह राजा नगर से बाहर गया।

> जगामेति लिट्, गमिति धातुः। (jagāmeti liṭ, gam iti dhātuḥ.)
> जगाम इति लिट्, गम् इति धातुः। (jagāma iti liṭ, gam iti dhātuḥ.)
> "jagāma is liṭ; the root is gam." — जगाम लिट् है, और धातु गम्।

These are model sentences 1 and 2 and a third line; model sentences 3–6 are in §13. बभूव and
जगाम are today's cells: the perfects of भवति and गच्छति, 3rd singular. The other words are yours:
पुरा "long ago" [v3.c03.l03], नाम, राजा [c02.l01], सः, नगरम् [नगरात्], बहिः [v3.c04.l02]; नलः is a
king's name, and नल has been a word of yours since v1.c03.l04. लिट् is a label, and so is the root
गम्; धातुः is lesson 1's headword. The narrator's perfect and the old story belong together:
model sentence 1 is how a tale can open.

The joins are Volume 3's: नलः नाम → नलो नाम — -अः before a voiced consonant → ओ; सः राजा → स राजा —
सः before a consonant → स; नगरात् बहिः → नगराद्बहिः — a final stop voiced before a voiced sound, and
written together; बहिः जगाम → बहिर्जगाम — -इः before a voiced sound → र्; जगाम इति → जगामेति — अ
+ इ → ए; गम् इति → गमिति — a consonant-final word written together with the vowel after it. The
comma stops the seam after लिट्, which stands in its pause form. The quoted form जगाम is joined to
the इति after it, as every quoted form is.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

उवाच "(उसने) कहा", आह "(वह) कहता है", ददौ "(उसने) दिया" — ये शब्द आप तीसरे खण्ड से पढ़ते आ रहे
हैं, पर पूरे-के-पूरे, बिना खोले। आज वे खुलते हैं। उवाच और ददौ एक ही लकार के रूप हैं — **लिट्**।
गच्छति का लिट् है जगाम, "(वह) गया"। आह की बात कुछ अलग है; वह नीचे आएगी।

**द्वित्व और अभ्यास।** लिट् की पहली पहचान यह है कि धातु दो बार आता है। इसे **द्वित्व** कहते हैं, और
पाणिनि का सूत्र 6.1.8 यही कहता है: लिट् से पहले धातु दोहराया जाता है। पहली प्रति का नाम है
**अभ्यास**। यह शब्द आपका पहले खण्ड से है, बार-बार करने के अर्थ में; व्याकरण में अभ्यास धातु की
दोहराई हुई पहली प्रति है। पहले गम् से गम्गम् बनता है। फिर अभ्यास छाँटा जाता है। उसके
व्यञ्जनों में से केवल पहला बचता है, बाक़ी व्यञ्जन हट जाते हैं, स्वर बना रहता है — सूत्र
7.4.60 — तो ग + गम्। अभ्यास में कवर्ग का वर्ण या
ह, चवर्ग का वर्ण बन जाता है — सूत्र 7.4.62 — तो ग की जगह ज: ज + गम्। और अभ्यास का स्वर ह्रस्व
होता है — सूत्र 7.4.59।

**लिट् के अपने प्रत्यय।** लट् के ति, तः, अन्ति की जगह लिट् में परस्मैपद के नौ अपने प्रत्यय आते
हैं — सूत्र 3.4.82। प्रथम पुरुष में ये हैं णल्, अतुस्, उस्, जो सुनाई देते हैं -अ, -अतुः, -उः। णल् के
ण् और ल् इत् हैं; बचता है केवल अ। पर ण् बेकार नहीं: णल् **णित्** प्रत्यय है, और णित् प्रत्यय से
पहले **उपधा** का अ — यानी अङ्ग के अन्तिम वर्ण से ठीक पहले का अ — आ हो जाता है। यह वृद्धि है, सूत्र
7.2.116। इसलिए ज + गम् + अ से **जगाम**।

**सबल और निर्बल, फिर से।** पिछले पाठ में आपने देखा कि पित् प्रत्यय से पहले अङ्ग सबल होता है और
बाक़ी प्रत्ययों से पहले निर्बल। लिट् में भी यही बँटवारा है। एकवचन के रूप सबल हैं — जगाम, चकार;
बाक़ी निर्बल — जग्मतुः, जग्मुः; चक्रतुः, चक्रुः। णल् में प् दिखाई नहीं देता, फिर भी वह पित् गिना जाता
है, क्योंकि वह तिप् के स्थान पर आया है, और आदेश अपने स्थानी जैसा, यानी जिसकी जगह आया उस जैसा,
व्यवहार करता है — यह नियम छठे अध्याय में पढ़ेंगे। बाक़ी प्रत्यय कित् माने जाते हैं, और कित्
प्रत्यय से पहले न गुण होता है, न वृद्धि।

**ददौ और बभूव।** दा का लिट् एक और सूत्र दिखाता है: आ पर समाप्त होने वाले अङ्ग के बाद णल् के स्थान
पर औ आता है — सूत्र 7.1.34। काशिका कार्यों का क्रम भी बताती है: पहले औ, फिर आ और औ का एक औ,
फिर द्वित्व; उसके बाद, हर द्वित्व की तरह, अभ्यास का स्वर ह्रस्व होता है। दा + औ, दौ, दा + दौ,
द + दौ: **ददौ**। भू का लिट्
बभूव है, और उसमें वृद्धि नहीं: भू के बाद लिट् के सभी प्रत्यय कित् गिने जाते हैं, एकवचन के भी।

**केवल प्रथम पुरुष।** इस खण्ड में उत्पादन-तालिका की आठ क्रियाओं के लिट् प्रथम पुरुष के तीनों वचन
आपके अपने हैं — एकवचन, द्विवचन, बहुवचन — और शब्द-कार्ड पर ✓ के साथ छपी बारह क्रियाओं का लिट्
प्रथम पुरुष एकवचन; उन बारह के द्विवचन और बहुवचन नहीं। ये कथा कहने वाले के रूप हैं: राजा जगाम,
ऋषिः उवाच। मध्यम और उत्तम पुरुष के रूप आप पढ़कर पहचानेंगे, बनाएँगे नहीं। "मैं गया" के लिए
अगच्छम् कहिए — लङ् का रूप, जो आपका है।

**परोक्ष।** पाणिनि के अनुसार लिट् उस बीते काल के लिए है जो **परोक्ष** है — आँखों से ओझल, जो बोलने
वाले के सामने नहीं हुआ — सूत्र 3.2.115। काशिका इसका क्षेत्र बताती है: बीता हुआ, आज का नहीं, और
परोक्ष। 1.x की पुरानी पुस्तक ने अपने कार्ड पर लिट् को देखी हुई घटना का भूतकाल लिखा था, और उसी
पृष्ठ के अपने प्रश्न में अनदेखी घटनाओं का उत्तर लिट् को ही बताया था; यहाँ वह भूल सुधारी गई है।
भाषा वास्तव में क्या करती है, यह ह्विटनी बताते हैं: बाद की संस्कृत में लिट् बस एक भूतकाल है, लङ्
के बराबर, और दोनों एक-दूसरे की जगह आ सकते हैं। दोनों बातें छपी हैं। अभ्यास और परीक्षा में पाणिनि
की बात "पाणिनि के अनुसार" कहकर ही पूछी जाती है।

**आह।** आह के बारे में तीन कथन साथ-साथ छपे हैं। पाणिनि के अनुसार आह ब्रू के लट् का एक वैकल्पिक
आदेश है — यानी उनके हिसाब से वर्तमान का रूप — सूत्र 3.4.84। ह्विटनी के अनुसार आह √अह् का लिट्
है, जो हर जगह वर्तमान के अर्थ में आता है। और डिजिटल कॉर्पस हितोपदेश के आह को भूत का चिह्न देता
है — यह रूप का चिह्न है, समय का नहीं। इनमें से कोई कथन पूछा नहीं
जाता: पाणिनि का सूत्र 3.4.84 कार्ड पर है, केवल देखने के लिए।

**आज के शब्द।** **पतिः** — स्वामी, पति; **द्वयम्** — जोड़ा, प्रायः समास के अन्त में, जैसे श्लोकद्वयम्,
दो श्लोकों का जोड़ा; **श्लोकः** — श्लोक, पद्य; **द्वारम्** — द्वार, दरवाज़ा; **खड्गः** — तलवार;
**प्राप्नोति** — पाता है, पहुँचता है; इसका लिट् है प्राप।

## 8. English Explanation

The recipe of this volume, once more: forms you own, the question they raise, the tools that
answer it, the derivation with the teacher, the tools run by you, then production. Today's one
grammar point: the perfect (लिट्) is made by doubling the root (6.1.8) and adding its own endings
(3.4.82); its singular is strong, the rest weak; and according to Pāṇini it is the past "out of
sight" (3.2.115), with what the language does printed beside it.

### Step 1 — the forms you own

| owned form | where owned | its root | what it raises |
|---|---|---|---|
| उवाच | v3.c03.l03, a chunk: "(he) said" | वच् | an उ- that वच् does not have |
| आह | v3.c03.l03, a chunk: "(he) says", in stories "said" | — | which root, which tense? |
| ददौ | v3.c06.l03, glossed: "he gave" | दा | a second द-, and an -औ at the end |
| गच्छति | v2.c04.l01 | गम् | its perfect, जगाम, begins with ज- |

Volume 3 called उवाच "the perfect 3sg of √वच्" and ददौ "the perfect (लिट्), Volume 4", each with a
pointer to this chapter, v4.c03. Today is that lesson.

### Step 2 — the question

**What kind of form is उवाच, and why does जगाम begin with ज-?** Lesson 1 gave every finite verb one
frame: a root, an l-affix, its ल् replaced by one of eighteen endings. उवाच and जगाम fit no present
you own: something stands before the root, and the endings are not -ति, -तः, -अन्ति. Today's tools
answer: the l-affix liṭ; a rule that doubles the root; three rules that trim the first copy; endings
of its own; and the marker of one of them.

### Step 3 — the tools

Each is printed once, here, with its tier and its bracket line; the Q tool also with its
padaccheda. **Q** is quoted, read word by word and recognised by its gist; **D** is the authority
for one derivation step, never recalled by number; **K**, in §15, is on a card, looked up and
never assessed.

**1. The perfect — Q, 3.2.115 परोक्षे लिट्** (parokṣe liṭ).

- Padaccheda: परोक्षे [सप्तमी: "in the sense 'out of sight'" — a locative of meaning] · लिट् [प्रथमा:
  the affix] · [अनद्यतने, carried from 3.2.111: "not of today"; भूते, the heading 3.2.84, by name: "in
  the past"].
- Bracket line: [carried (data file): कृत्, 3.1.93 (*preview*, c04.l01); अनद्यतने, 3.2.111] [headings (data file): प्रत्ययः, 3.1.1; परश्च, 3.1.2; धातोः, 3.1.91; भूते, 3.2.84 (not a tool; printed by name, as the heading the reading needs)] (further headings: Teacher Notes)
- English: "[After a root, for the past not of today,] liṭ, when it is out of sight."
  [REF:ashtadhyayi 3.2.115]; [REF:kashika 3.2.115]

परोक्षे is a locative, but not the case code's "before": no affix is called "out of sight". It is a
locative of meaning, as कर्तरि in 3.1.68 was (lesson 2). अनद्यतने "not of today" is carried from a
rule a little before this one; भूते "in the past" is the heading over this stretch of the grammar,
printed by name because the reading needs it, though it is not one of your tools; कृत् is Chapter
4's; प्रत्ययः, परश्च and धातोः are lesson 1's. So: after a root, for an action past, not of today and
out of sight — liṭ.

**IR-019, corrected in the open.** The 1.x book's recital card labelled लिट् 'perfect (remote,
witnessed)' (p. 75), and its own question on the same page — 'Which is for events the speaker
did NOT witness?' — gave लिट् as the answer. The sūtra says परोक्षे, 'out of sight', and the Kāśikā
glosses its domain as the past, not of today, and out of sight. What the language does is another
matter (below).

**2. The endings — D, 3.4.82 परस्मैपदानां णलतुसुस्थलथुसणल्वमाः** (parasmaipadānāṃ
ṇalatususthalathusaṇalvamāḥ).

- Bracket line: [carried (data file): लिटः, 3.4.81] [headings (data file): प्रत्ययः, 3.1.1; परश्च, 3.1.2; धातोः, 3.1.91; लस्य, 3.4.77] (further headings: Teacher Notes)
- English: "In liṭ, the nine P endings are replaced by णल्, अतुस्, उस्, थल्, अथुस्, अ, णल्, व, म."
  [REF:ashtadhyayi 3.4.82]; [REF:kashika 3.4.82]

When the l-affix is liṭ — the carried लिटः, "of liṭ" — this rule replaces lesson 1's first nine
endings again, in order: तिप् → णल्, तस् → अतुस्, झि → उस्, and so on. The ल् of णल् is a marker by
1.3.3, its ण् by a rule in Teacher Notes: णल् is heard as अ. The Kāśikā says the ण् is for vṛddhi
(tool 6). The nine are T10, in §15.

**3. The doubling — D, 6.1.8 लिटि धातोरनभ्यासस्य** (liṭi dhātor anabhyāsasya).

- Bracket line: [carried (data file): अजादेः, 6.1.2; द्वितीयस्य, 6.1.2 (the file carries them; they matter only for a root that begins with a vowel)] (further headings: Teacher Notes) [carried (course): द्वे "two [copies]", from the heading of 6.1.1]
- English: "Before liṭ, [the first part, with its one vowel,] of a root that is not already doubled
  is doubled." [REF:ashtadhyayi 6.1.8]; [REF:kashika 6.1.8]

The first copy is the **doubled syllable**, अभ्यासः (abhyāsaḥ) — owned since Volume 1 as
"practice"; in grammar it names the first copy, by a rule in Teacher Notes. For a root of one
syllable, such as गम्, the whole root is doubled: गम्गम्. The words carried from 6.1.2 concern roots
that begin with a vowel; the course line द्वे is the word the rule needs from its heading.

**4. The first consonant stays — D, 7.4.60 हलादिः शेषः** (halādiḥ śeṣaḥ).

- Bracket line: [carried (data file): अभ्यासस्य, 7.4.58] [headings (data file): अङ्गस्य, 6.4.1]
- English: "In the doubled syllable, the first consonant remains; the others go."
  [REF:ashtadhyayi 7.4.60]; [REF:kashika 7.4.60]

गम्गम् → ग + गम्: ग stays, म् goes. The carried अभ्यासस्य, "of the doubled syllable", limits the
rule to the first copy.

**5. k-row to c-row — D, 7.4.62 कुहोश्चुः** (kuhoś cuḥ).

- Bracket line: [carried (data file): अभ्यासस्य, 7.4.58] [headings (data file): अङ्गस्य, 6.4.1] (further headings: Teacher Notes)
- English: "In the doubled syllable, a k-row sound or ह becomes a c-row sound." [REF:ashtadhyayi
  7.4.62]; [REF:kashika 7.4.62]

कुहोः is a genitive, "in place of a k-row sound or ह"; चुः, the result. Which c-row sound? The
nearest, by 1.1.50: ग, voiced and unaspirated, becomes ज.

**6. vṛddhi of a penultimate अ — D, 7.2.116 अत उपधायाः** (ata upadhāyāḥ).

- Bracket line: [carried (data file): वृद्धिः, 7.2.114; अचः, 7.2.115 (the file carries it; this rule does not use it: the vowel here is named by अतः); ञ्णिति, 7.2.115] [headings (data file): अङ्गस्य, 6.4.1]
- English: "Before a ñ- or ṇ-marked affix, a penultimate अ is replaced by vṛddhi (आ)."
  [REF:ashtadhyayi 7.2.116]; [REF:kashika 7.2.116]

अतः and उपधायाः are genitives: in place of a short अ that is penultimate, the sound just before
the aṅga's last sound (lesson 2). The carried वृद्धिः is the result, the carried ञ्णिति
"before a ñ- or ṇ-marked [affix]" the condition. णल् is ṇ-marked: ज + गम् + अ → ज + गाम् + अ.

**7. The short vowel — D, 7.4.59 ह्रस्वः** (hrasvaḥ).

- Bracket line: [carried (data file): अभ्यासस्य, 7.4.58] [headings (data file): अङ्गस्य, 6.4.1] (further headings: Teacher Notes)
- English: "The vowel of the doubled syllable is shortened." [REF:ashtadhyayi 7.4.59];
  [REF:kashika 7.4.59]

One word, the result: ह्रस्वः "a short [vowel]"; the carried अभ्यासस्य says where. दा + दौ → द + दौ.

**8. औ for णल् — D, 7.1.34 आत औ णलः** (āta au ṇalaḥ).

- Bracket line: [carried (data file): —] [headings (data file): अङ्गस्य, 6.4.1]
- English: "After an आ-final aṅga, णल् is replaced by औ." [REF:ashtadhyayi 7.1.34]; [REF:kashika
  7.1.34]

आतः is an ablative here, "after आ", as in Chapter 1's अतः pair; णलः the genitive, "in place of
णल्"; औ the result. दा + णल् → दा + औ.

**Re-read [owned]:** 3.4.78 [B], the eighteen endings (lesson 1); 1.3.3 हलन्त्यम् and 1.3.9 तस्य
लोपः [B], the markers; 1.1.50 [D], the nearest substitute; 6.1.88 वृद्धिरेचि [owned], आ + औ → औ in

### Step 4 — जगाम, with the teacher

The table is in §13, derivation (a). Work down it with the teacher, saying each line's tool. Step 0
runs lesson 1's frame in one line: liṭ for the past out of sight (3.2.115), its ल् → तिप् [3.4.78],
तिप् → णल् [3.4.82]. Step 1 drops the markers of णल्. Then the root doubled (6.1.8), the first copy
trimmed of every consonant but its first (7.4.60), its ग turned to ज [7.4.62]. Last, the ṇ of
णल् asks for vṛddhi: the penultimate अ of गम् becomes आ [7.2.116]. **जगाम** — laid out as lesson
2's नयति table, which your Station 0 item 2 has just rebuilt: the frame, the affix, then the root
before it.

### Step 5 — the endings, and the strong singular

The nine endings of 3.4.82 are T10 (§15), the one table of this lesson you learn by heart; in the
3rd person, -अ, -अतुः, -उः.

Lesson 3's split comes back. There, an ending with the marker p kept the stem strong, and every
other one, ṅ-marked by 1.2.4, kept it weak (1.1.5). In the perfect the singular active is strong —
vṛddhi in the 3rd singular: जगाम, चकार, शुश्राव — and the rest weak: जग्मुः, चक्रुः, शुश्रुवुः. But
णल् has no प् of its own. It counts as pit because it stands in place of तिप्, and a substitute
behaves like what it replaced [1.1.56, preview: c06.l03]. The other endings, after a root that does
not end in a conjunct, count as k-marked by a card rule, 1.2.5 असंयोगाल्लिट् कित् [K]; 1.1.5 blocks
guṇa and vṛddhi before them, and they take the weak root.

The **production table** of §15 gives eight verbs you own in their three 3rd-person cells, each
with its rules: D rules you now have, K rules on the card beside it, and rules in Teacher Notes. Two
rows need a word. **बभूव, not \*बभाव:** after भू, liṭ counts as k-marked even before णल् — a second
card rule, 1.2.6 इन्धिभवतिभ्यां च [K] — so there is no vṛddhi; how its ब- and its व arise is read in the
Laghusiddhāntakaumudī [preview: c06.l02]. **उवाच and ऊचुः** are read side by side in §13, not
derived: उ- in the doubled syllable (6.1.17 [K]), and उच्- for वच् before a k-marked ending (6.1.15
with 1.2.5 [K]).

### Step 6 — ददौ, and your runs

Now run the tools yourself (§18 exercise 3, at home). दा → ददौ has the order the Kāśikā gives
under 7.1.34: first औ for णल् [7.1.34]; then one vowel for आ + औ [6.1.88, owned]; then the doubling
(6.1.8), दौ doubled as if it were दा [a rule in Teacher Notes]. Last, as after every doubling, the
short vowel of the doubled syllable (7.4.59), a step the Kāśikā's list does not name. §12 mistake 4
shows what goes wrong otherwise. पठ् → पपाठ, पच् → पपाच and चर् →
चचार follow जगाम, except that a doubled प or च is not a k-row sound, so 7.4.62 does nothing; पा →
पपौ "drank" and या → ययौ "went" follow ददौ.

### Step 7 — what the perfect is for, and आह

According to Pāṇini, liṭ is the past out of sight (3.2.115); the FACT beside it is §4's: in the
later language a past tense, equivalent with the imperfect. आह gets the three statements of §5:
Pāṇini's (a substitute in the laṭ of ब्रू, a present in his account), Whitney's (a perfect of √अह्,
with present value), and the corpus tag (a "past" tag of the form). None of the three is asked:
3.4.84 is a card rule, looked up and never assessed.

**The main-risk line:** the perfect is the narrator's tense here: 3rd person. Your "I went" is the
imperfect अगच्छम्, which you own.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the step from ग to ज that 7.4.62 makes, the two b-sounds of बभूव, the श्र of शुश्राव
and the ड्ग of खड्गः. The perfect image (`planned:assets/images/volume-4/v4c03l04-the-perfect.svg`)
shows गम् doubled and its first copy turned to ज; say each stage as you point to it.

1. **ग and ज.** For ग /g/ the back of the tongue presses the soft palate. For ज /d͡ʑ/ the blade of the
   tongue presses the hard palate and releases with a short voiced hiss. The tongue moves forward
   from ग to ज: that is the k-row to the c-row of 7.4.62, felt in the mouth.
2. **ब and भ in बभूव.** Both close the lips. ब /b/ releases plainly; भ /bʱ/ releases with a breathy
   puff while the voice goes on. The doubled syllable has the plain one.
3. **श्र in शुश्राव.** The blade hisses at the palate for श /ɕ/, then the tongue tip makes one light
   tap for र /ɾ/, with no vowel between.
4. **ड्ग in खड्गः.** The tip curled back for ड /ɖ/, then the back of the tongue up for ग /g/: two
   closures, one after the other, and ख /kʰ/ at the start with a puff of breath.

Mirror check: say गम् — जगाम. The jaw opens for /ɐ/ and wider for /ɑː/; the tongue goes forward for
the ज and back for the ग.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; they feed the quiz distractors.

1. **Reaching for the perfect in the 1st person ("I went": जगाम).** *Fix:* the 1st-person perfect
   is ◐ here — you read it, you do not produce it; "I went" is अगच्छम्, which you own.
2. **\*बभाव for बभूव.** *Fix:* भू's liṭ is k-marked even before णल् [1.2.6, K]: no vṛddhi.
3. **\*गगाम.** *Fix:* the doubled ग becomes ज [7.4.62].
4. **\*दादौ for ददौ — the doubled syllable's vowel left long.** *Fix:* after the doubling, the
   short vowel (7.4.59); before it, the Kāśikā's order: औ first, then one vowel for आ + औ, then
   doubling.
5. **'लिट् = perfect (remote, witnessed)' — the 1.x recital card, p. 75.** *Fix:* परोक्षे: "out of
   sight" (3.2.115); in use, a past tense (Whitney §821).
6. **Saying "the Hitopadeśa uses आह as a past".** *Fix:* three statements: Pāṇini (a laṭ
   substitute, 3.4.84), Whitney (a perfect of √अह्, with present value), the DCS (a tag of the
   form).

### The perfect

> **According to Pāṇini, the perfect (liṭ) is the past "out of sight"** (3.2.115). Its ल् becomes
> one of the eighteen (3.4.78), and in liṭ the nine parasmaipada endings are replaced again (3.4.82).
> Before liṭ the root is doubled (6.1.8); the doubled syllable keeps its first consonant (7.4.60),
> has a short vowel (7.4.59), and turns a k-row sound or ह into a c-row one (7.4.62). The singular
> endings count as pit and give the strong stem: vṛddhi of a penultimate अ before the ṇ of णल्
> (7.2.116); after an आ-final root, णल् → औ [7.1.34]. The others count as k-marked (1.2.5, card) and
> give the weak stem. The tools' English lines and bracket lines are in §8 Step 3.
