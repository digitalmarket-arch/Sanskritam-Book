## 1. Hook

You have said शृणोति "hears" since v2.c04.l01. Volume 3 gave you four more of its cells, to be
read whole: शृण्वन्ति "they hear" (v3.c03.l01), अशृणोत् "he heard" and अशृण्वन् "they heard"
(v3.c03.l03), and शृणु "listen!" (v3.c03.l04). In v3.c03.l01 you also learned अस्ति · सन्ति and
करोति · कुर्वन्ति whole, because, the lesson said, "their reason is v4.c03's".

Put the pairs side by side. Why शृणो- in one cell and शृण्व्- in the next? Why अस्- in अस्ति, and
only स्- in सन्ति? And a question lesson 2 left open: नयति has guṇa — नी became नय- — so why does
लिखति keep its इ? One letter answers all three, and you never hear it: the प् of तिप्.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — where today's two rules stand, and the commentary that already gives today's
forms.

Today's two Q tools stand in Book 1 of the Aṣṭādhyāyī, among the rules that say how other rules
are to be read. 1.1.5 क्ङिति च stands two rules after 1.1.3 इको गुणवृद्धी, which you met in
c02.l02: 1.1.3 says what guṇa and vṛddhi replace; the rule between them, in Teacher Notes, and
1.1.5 say where they do not. 1.2.4 सार्वधातुकमपित् stands early in the second quarter of Book 1, in
a run of rules that say an affix counts as k- or ṅ-marked though it carries no such letter; the
first of them, the source of the word ङित् in today's bracket line, is in Teacher Notes. The rules
that shape the weak stems stand far from both: 6.4.110 and 6.4.111 in Book 6, under the heading
अङ्गस्य [6.4.1] of c02.l03; 7.1.3 in Book 7, under the same heading; 6.1.16 in the first quarter of
Book 6; and the class signs, 3.1.73 to 3.1.81, in Book 3, a few rules after lesson 2's शप्.

The commentators knew which forms the two rules are for, and they say so.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** The Kāśikā (c. 7th century CE) gives, under 1.2.4, the very forms of
> this lesson: कुरुतः, कुर्वन्ति beside करोति — 'why "not p-marked"? karoti …' (*apit iti kim?
> karoti …*) — that is, करोति keeps its guṇa because तिप् has a p. The commentary's examples are the
> course's derivations. [REF:kashika 1.2.4]

Read the commentary's examples against today's work: कुरुतः and कुर्वन्ति are items 2 and 3 of
§18 exercise 3, करोति its item 1. The Kāśikā asks why the rule says "not p-marked", and answers
with करोति, करोषि, करोमि: their endings तिप्, सिप्, मिप् are p-marked, so 1.2.4 does not touch them,
and the guṇa stands. The lines are in Teacher Notes; Chapter 6 reads them with you (c06.l02).

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — what the language does: in the classes whose stem does not end in अ, one verb
has two stems, and Whitney ties them to the accent.

Lesson 2 sorted your verbs by one thing you can hear: does the present stem end in अ? The six
classes whose stem does not — 2, 3, 5, 7, 8 and 9, Whitney's first conjugation (§603) — are the ones
whose cells do not follow the pattern of गच्छति. Whitney says what they share.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Of these classes Whitney writes that they share "a shift of accent: the tone
> being now upon the ending, and now upon the root or the class-sign"; with it goes a stem that has
> "a stronger or fuller form when the accent rests upon it, and a weaker or briefer form when the
> accent is on the ending". [REF:whitney1889 §604]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** On the root class: "The root takes the accent, and has *guṇa*, if capable of
> it, in the three persons sing. act." His first example is your एति: *émi, éṣi, éti* in the
> singular, *imás, ithá, yánti* in the plural. [REF:whitney1889 §612]

Read the two accounts side by side. Whitney's strong cells are the three singular persons of the
active — एति, एषि, एमि; करोति, करोषि, करोमि — and the rest are weak. Pāṇini's strong cells are those
whose ending is p-marked — तिप्, सिप्, मिप् — and the rest are weak because 1.2.4 makes their
endings count as ṅ-marked. The two accounts pick out the same cells and explain them differently:
Whitney by where the accent falls, Pāṇini by a marker letter. Both are printed; the quiz asks
Whitney's as FACT and Pāṇini's only in the form "According to Pāṇini…". Accent is Volume 5's: the
course names it and does not show it. Whitney's marks over *émi* and *yánti* show where the tone
fell.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the day's idea in three lines, each printed joined, its pause form beneath,
then the gloss.

> वयं गुरोर्वचनं शृणुमः। (vayaṃ guror vacanaṃ śṛṇumaḥ.)
> वयम् गुरोः वचनम् शृणुमः। (vayam guroḥ vacanam śṛṇumaḥ.)
> "We listen to the teacher's words." — हम गुरु की बात सुनते हैं।

> कुत्र स्तस्तौ? — गृहे स्तः। (kutra stas tau? — gṛhe staḥ.)
> कुत्र स्तः तौ? — गृहे स्तः। (kutra staḥ tau? — gṛhe staḥ.)
> "Where are those two? — At home." — वे दोनों कहाँ हैं? — घर पर हैं।

> तिपि पकारोऽस्ति, तसि नास्ति। (tipi pakāro 'sti, tasi nāsti.)
> तिपि पकारः अस्ति, तसि न अस्ति। (tipi pakāraḥ asti, tasi na asti.)
> "In tip there is a p; in tas there is none." — तिप् में पकार है, तस् में नहीं।

These are model sentences 1 and 3 and a third line; model sentences 2, 4, 5 and 6 are in §13.
Every verb in them is a weak cell but one: शृणुमः, before मस्, which has no p; स्तः, before तस्; and
अस्ति, before तिप्, the strong one. The third line names two affixes in the locative, तिपि "in tip"
and तसि "in tas": labels, display only, as शपि and झौ in §16. पकारः "the sound p" is a -कार name, a
label too.

The joins are Volume 3's: वयम् गुरोः → वयं गुरोः, वचनम् शृणुमः → वचनं शृणुमः — म् → ं before a
consonant; गुरोः वचनम् → गुरोर्वचनम् — -ओः before a voiced sound → -ओर्, written together; स्तः तौ →
स्तस्तौ — the visarga → स् before त; पकारः अस्ति → पकारोऽस्ति — -अः before अ → ओऽ; न अस्ति → नास्ति —
two like vowels, one long. A "?", a daṇḍa, a comma and the dash between the two speakers stop every
seam: तौ and पकारोऽस्ति's line stand in their pause form before them.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

शृणोति आप दूसरे खण्ड से बोलते आ रहे हैं, और तीसरे खण्ड में आपने उसी क्रिया का शृण्वन्ति भी पढ़ा था —
पर पूरे शब्द की तरह, बिना कारण जाने। एक ही क्रिया में एक जगह शृणो- है, दूसरी जगह शृण्व्-। अस्ति में
अस्- है, पर सन्ति में केवल स्-। करोति में करो-, पर कुरुतः में कुरु-। आज इन सबका कारण मिलता है, और
कारण है एक ऐसा अक्षर जो कभी सुनाई नहीं देता — तिप् का प्।

**पित् और अपित्।** जिस प्रत्यय में प् इत् हो, उसे **पित्** कहते हैं; जिसमें न हो, वह **अपित्** है।
पहले पाठ के अठारह प्रत्ययों में केवल तीन पित् हैं: तिप्, सिप्, मिप् — परस्मैपद के तीनों एकवचन। बाकी
पंद्रह अपित् हैं। विकरणों में भी यही भेद है: शप् पित् है; श, श्नु और श्ना अपित् हैं।

**सूत्र 1.2.4 सार्वधातुकमपित्।** जो सार्वधातुक प्रत्यय अपित् है, वह ङित् जैसा माना जाता है — मानो
उसमें ङ् इत् हो, भले ही वह लिखा न हो।

**सूत्र 1.1.5 क्ङिति च।** जिस प्रत्यय में क् या ङ् इत् हो, उससे पहले इक् के स्थान पर न गुण होता है,
न वृद्धि।

दोनों सूत्रों को साथ पढ़िए। तिप् पित् है, इसलिए 1.2.4 उसे छूता नहीं, और उसके पहले गुण होता है: नु का उ
ओ बन जाता है — शृणोति। झि अपित् है, इसलिए ङित् जैसा है, और उसके पहले गुण नहीं होता: उ बना रहता है,
और अगले स्वर से पहले व् बन जाता है — शृण्वन्ति। गुण वाले अङ्ग को **सबल अङ्ग** कहते हैं, जैसे शृणो-,
करो-; और बिना गुण वाले को **निर्बल अङ्ग**, जैसे शृणु-, कुरु-।

कुछ क्रियाओं के निर्बल अङ्ग में एक नियम और लगता है। करोति में, ङित् जैसे सार्वधातुक प्रत्यय से पहले,
कर्- का अ उ बन जाता है — सूत्र 6.4.110: कुरुतः, कुर्वन्ति। अस्ति में अस् का अ ही गिर जाता है — सूत्र
6.4.111: स्तः, सन्ति। हर क्रिया का अपना एक छोटा नियम है, पर सबके पीछे वही एक जोड़ी है: 1.2.4 और

## 8. English Explanation

The recipe of this volume, once more: forms you own, the question they raise, the tools that
answer it, the derivation with the teacher, the tools run by you, then production. Today's one
grammar point: according to Pāṇini, a sārvadhātuka affix without the marker p counts as ṅ-marked
(1.2.4), and before a k- or ṅ-marked affix guṇa and vṛddhi do not apply (1.1.5). So the singular
parasmaipada endings तिप्, सिप्, मिप्, which carry p, give a strong (guṇa) stem, and every other
ending gives a weak one; and the same marker explains why class 6, whose sign श has no p, has no
guṇa at all.

### Step 2 — the question

**Why two stems in one verb?** शृणो- before -ति, शृण्व्- before -अन्ति; अस्- before -ति, स्- before
-अन्ति; करो- before -ति, कुरु- before -तः. Volume 3 handed you every one of these whole. And why does
नयति have guṇa, where लिखति, with an इ that could take it, has none? Today's tools answer: one
marker, the p, present on some endings and signs and absent on the rest, and two rules that read
it.

### Step 3 — the tools

Bracket line: [carried (data file): ङित्, 1.2.1] [headings (data file): —]

English: "A sārvadhātuka affix that is not p-marked counts as ṅ-marked." [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.2.4];
[REF:kashika 1.2.4]

Read it with lesson 2's 3.4.113: every tiṅ ending, and every ś-marked sign, is sārvadhātuka. Of the
eighteen endings three are p-marked — तिप्, सिप्, मिप्, the singular of the first set — and the प् you

rule are nominatives; its result, ङित्, is carried from the first rule of this quarter of Book 1,

**2. And no guṇa or vṛddhi before it — Q, 1.1.5 क्ङिति च** (kṅiti ca).

Padaccheda: क्ङिति [सप्तमी: "before [an affix] with the marker क् or ङ्"] · च ["and"] · [इकः,
गुण-वृद्धी, carried from 1.1.3; न "not", carried from 1.1.4].

Bracket line: [carried (data file): इकः, 1.1.3; गुण-वृद्धी, 1.1.3; न "not", 1.1.4] [headings (data file): —]

English: "And before a k- or ṅ-marked affix, guṇa and vṛddhi [in place of an इक्] do not [apply]."
[REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.5]; [REF:kashika 1.1.5]

Three of its five words are carried. From 1.1.3 इको गुणवृद्धी [B, c02.l02] come इकः and

negative particle "not", not the sound न्. What 1.1.5 itself adds is the place, क्ङिति, "before an

locative as the occasion: *kṅin-nimitte ye guṇa-vṛddhī prāpnutaḥ, te na bhavataḥ* — "the guṇa and

ending guṇa does not happen. An ending with p is not touched by 1.2.4, so the guṇa of lesson 2's
7.3.84 goes ahead.

> **According to Pāṇini:** before तिप्, सिप्, मिप् — pit — the stem takes guṇa: the **strong** stem

its last consonant, as the उ of रुच् does in रोचते. But the sign of class 6 is श, and श has no p: by
1.2.4 it counts as ṅ-marked, and 1.1.5 blocks the guṇa: लिखति, never \*लेखति. नयति's sign is शप्, and
शप् has its p: नी → ने → नय्. One marker decides both.

अ is no इक्: nothing there can take guṇa or lose it. The root before शप् takes its guṇa every time,
because शप् is pit. So one stem, भव-, before every ending, and a weak \*भुवतः does not exist. Before
you split any verb, sort it: a stem in -अ, in classes 1, 4, 6 and 10, or not, in 2, 3, 5, 7, 8 and
9. §18 exercise 5 is that sort.

### Step 6 — laṅ and loṭ, the same split

Your past and your imperative split the same way: their endings are the eighteen again, in other
shapes — in c02.l02 the ल् of अगच्छत् became तिप्, and its इ was dropped. §15 prints the cells of
शृणोति and करोति. **laṅ** is strong in the three singulars only: अशृणोत्, अशृणोः, अशृणवम्, against
अशृणुताम्, अशृण्वन्, अशृणुत, अशृणुम. **loṭ** is strong in the 3rd singular and in every 1st person:
शृणोतु; शृणवानि, शृणवाम, against शृणुताम्, शृण्वन्तु, शृणु, शृणुत.

The rules behind this are in Teacher Notes: the singular endings of laṅ stand in place of तिप्, सिप्,
मिप् and count as p-marked; the 1st-person endings of loṭ take an augment and are themselves made
p-marked; and the 2nd singular ending of loṭ is made apit and is then dropped after the उ of नु —
शृणु, the R4 word of v3.c03.l04. करोति does the same: अकरोत्, अकुर्वन्; करोतु, कुर्वन्तु, कुरु,

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the curl of ण after ऋ, the व of शृण्वन्ति, the ङ् of युङ्क्तः and the cluster of
घ्नन्ति. The strong-and-weak image (`planned:assets/images/volume-4/v4c03l03-strong-and-weak.svg`)
shows one stem and two doors, शृणो- behind the first and शृणु-/शृण्व्- behind the second; say each
form as you point to its door.

1. **ण in शृणोति.** For the vowel ऋ /r̩/ the tongue tip is already raised and curled back; keep it
   there and close for the nasal /ɳ/, then release into the long ओ /oː/, lips rounded.
2. **व in शृण्वन्ति.** /ʋ/: the lower lip comes lightly to the upper teeth while the voice runs on —
   not English w, with rounded lips, and not a hard v with friction.
3. **ङ् in युङ्क्तः.** The back of the tongue rises to the soft palate for ङ /ŋ/ and stays there for
   क /k/; only then does the tip move to the teeth for त /t̪/.
4. **घ्न in घ्नन्ति.** /gʱ/: a voiced g with a breathy release, straight into the dental न /n̪/ with
   no vowel between.

Mirror check: say शृणोति — शृण्वन्ति. In the first the lips round for ओ; in the second they stay
neutral and the lower lip rises to the teeth for व.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; they feed the quiz distractors.

1. **Applying strong and weak to thematic verbs: a weak \*भुवतः for भवतः.** *Fix:* the thematic
   classes (1, 4, 6, 10) have no split: the sign अ takes the endings, and अ has no इक् to change.
   Sort first (§18 exercise 5).
2. **ङ् blocks only guṇa, क् blocks guṇa and vṛddhi** — the 1.x card's claim, its words quoted in §8
   Step 3. *Fix:* 1.1.5 blocks both, before both (§8 Step 3).
3. **\*शृणोन्ति, \*करोन्ति.** *Fix:* झि has no p: ṅit (1.2.4) → no guṇa (1.1.5): शृण्वन्ति, कुर्वन्ति.
4. **\*अस्न्ति or \*असन्ति.** *Fix:* 6.4.111 drops the अ of अस् before a kṅit ending: सन्ति, स्तः.
5. **\*प्रच्छति, with the root unchanged.** *Fix:* श has no p: ṅit (1.2.4); 6.1.16 gives samprasāraṇa
   before a ṅit affix: पृच्छ-. पृच्छ- has no guṇa because its ऋ is neither final nor penultimate.
   The class-6 ṅit that blocks guṇa shows in लिखति, not \*लेखति.
6. **Reading the weak cells as "irregular".** *Fix:* one rule pair (1.2.4 + 1.1.5) and one rule per
   verb (6.4.110, 6.4.111 …).

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — the rule pair in one box, the full present of the athematic verbs, the laṅ and loṭ
split, R4 closed, the anubandha card, the tools by tier, the toolbox card and the paradigm ledger.

### The rule pair

> **According to Pāṇini, the p decides.** A sārvadhātuka affix without the marker p counts as
> ṅ-marked (1.2.4), and before a k- or ṅ-marked affix guṇa and vṛddhi do not apply (1.1.5). So the
> singular parasmaipada endings तिप्, सिप्, मिप्, which carry p, leave a **strong** stem before them
> (शृणो-, करो-, अस्-, ब्रवी-, ए-, युनज्-); every other ending leaves a **weak** one (शृणु-, कुरु-,
> स्-, ब्रू-, इ-, युञ्ज्-). Among the signs, शप् has p, and the root before it takes guṇa (नयति); श,
> श्नु and श्ना have none, and the root before them takes none (लिखति, शृणोति's शृ, गृह्णाति). One rule
> per verb finishes the weak stem: 6.4.110 कुरु-; 6.4.111 स्-, युञ्ज्-; 6.1.16 पृच्छ्-, गृह्-; and on the
> card, 6.4.113 गृह्णी-. The -अ stems have no split.

The English line, padaccheda and bracket line of each tool are in §8 Steps 3–5. The strong/weak
table, with Whitney's sections, is in §13.

— छात्रा: गुरो, शृणोतीत्यत्र गुणः कस्मात्? (guro, śṛṇotīty atra guṇaḥ kasmāt?)
  गुरो, शृणोति इति अत्र गुणः कस्मात्? (guro, śṛṇoti iti atra guṇaḥ kasmāt?)
  "Teacher, why guṇa in śṛṇoti?" — गुरुजी, शृणोति में गुण क्यों?

— गुरुः: तिपः पकारात्। (tipaḥ pakārāt.)
  तिपः पकारात्। (tipaḥ pakārāt.)
  "Because of the p of tip." — तिप् के पकार के कारण।

— छात्रा: शृण्वन्तीत्यत्र किम्? (śṛṇvantīty atra kim?)
  शृण्वन्ति इति अत्र किम्? (śṛṇvanti iti atra kim?)
  "And in śṛṇvanti?" — और शृण्वन्ति में?

— गुरुः: झौ पकारो नास्ति, अतो गुणो न भवति। (jhau pakāro nāsti, ato guṇo na bhavati.)
  झौ पकारः न अस्ति, अतः गुणः न भवति। (jhau pakāraḥ na asti, ataḥ guṇaḥ na bhavati.)
  "In jhi there is no p, so there is no guṇa." — झि में पकार नहीं है, इसलिए गुण नहीं होता।

## 20. Revision

पुनरावृत्तिः — one screen to remember.

> **Strong and weak.** According to Pāṇini, a sārvadhātuka affix without the marker p counts as
> ṅ-marked (1.2.4), and before a k- or ṅ-marked affix guṇa and vṛddhi do not apply (1.1.5). **Pit:**
> तिप्, सिप्, मिप्, and the sign शप् — guṇa before them, the **strong** stem: शृणोति, करोति, अस्ति, एति.
> **Apit:** every other ending, and the signs श, श्नु, श्ना — no guṇa, the **weak** stem: शृणुतः, कुरुतः,
> स्तः, इतः. Before a vowel the उ of नु → व्: **शृण्वन्ति**. **One rule per verb:** 6.4.110, कर्- →
> कुर्-; 6.4.111, the अ of अस् and of श्न dropped: सन्ति, युङ्क्तः; 6.1.16, प्रच्छ् → पृच्छ्, ग्रह् → गृह्;
> 6.4.113 on the card, the आ of श्ना → ई before a consonant. **laṅ** strong in the singular only;
> **loṭ** strong in the 3rd singular and the 1st persons. **Class 6** has no guṇa — श has no p:
> लिखति. **The -अ stems** have no split: भवति, भवतः, भवन्ति. **The 1.x card:** ङ् and क् block guṇa
> and vṛddhi alike. **The ledger:** four new — 552; cells drilled: 84; tools added: 7; toolbox: 75.

so नृत्यति keeps its ऋ — never \*नर्त्यति — and the Chapter 3 exam's spiral reads सिध्यन्ति the same way.
Confirm it; do not teach it as a new point.

**What students find hard.** First, the main risk: splitting a thematic verb (\*भुवतः). Do exercise
5 before exercise 2, and when a learner hesitates, ask "does the ending touch the root, or an अ?".
Second, करु-: learners write it as a stem. It is only a string between guṇa and 6.4.110; the weak
stem is कुरु-. Third, 6.4.111: it drops the अ of श्न, the class-7 sign, and of अस् — never the vowel
of the class-9 श्ना; गृह्णन्ति and गृह्णीतः are other rules. Fourth, the order of derivation (a): the ण comes
last, after the yaṇ, never in step 1.

**Differentiation.** Fast learners: why does शृ take no guṇa before नु in step 1 (श्नु has no p)? Why
does करोति's root take guṇa before उ when शृणोति's does not (उ has no श्, so it is not sārvadhātuka and
1.2.4 cannot touch it)? Learners who struggle: give them the rule pair on a card and the strong/weak
table with the strong column filled; they write only the weak columns.
