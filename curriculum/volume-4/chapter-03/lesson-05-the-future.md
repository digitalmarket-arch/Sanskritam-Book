## 1. Hook

Volume 3 left a future in a story. In its telling of the lion and the mouse, the mouse promised
"…अहमपि कदाचित्तव साहाय्यं करिष्यामि", and करिष्यामि was glossed "I will do" and left for this
volume (v3.c06.l02). Volume 3 also named गमिष्यति "will go" as the real Sanskrit behind the 1.x
book's future of गच्छ, and left it for Volume 4 (v3.c03.l03).

Look at करिष्यामि. The root is कृ; the ending is the -मि you have said since Volume 1. What is
the -इष्य- between them — and why is its ष the ष of रामेषु?

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — what the language does: two futures, the sign of the one you learn, what it is
used for, what a corpus count shows, and the four on the card.

Whitney opens his chapter on the future with one sentence.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "THE verb has two futures, of very different age and character …"
> [REF:whitney1889 §931]

He calls them the *s*-future and the periphrastic future. The first is today's lṛṭ; the second is
the first row of today's card, luṭ.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "The tense-sign of this future is the syllable स्य *syá*, added to the root
> either directly or by an auxiliary vowel इ *i* (in the latter case becoming इष्य *iṣyá*). The root
> has the *guṇa*-strengthening. Thus, from √दा *dā* *give* is formed the future tense-stem दास्य
> *dāsyá* … from √भू *bhū* *be*, the stem भविष्य *bhaviṣyá*" [REF:whitney1889 §932]

Three marks, then: a sign स्य; an इ before it after some roots and not after others; guṇa. दास्य- has
no इ; भविष्य- has one, and its ओ from guṇa has become अव् before it. Pāṇini's rules of §8 make the
same three marks, step by step.

What is the future for? Whitney answers for the language as it is written.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "It expresses in general what is going to take place at some time to come —
> but often, as in other languages, adding on the one hand an implication of will or intention, or on
> the other hand that of promise or threatening." And: "As in other languages, the tense is also
> sometimes used for the expression of a conjecture or presumption". [REF:whitney1889 §948]

The second sentence is today's reading. The crocodile's wife does not foretell; she supposes: तन्नूनं
सा वानरी भविष्यति, "then surely she must be a she-monkey" (§13).

How much the future and the aorist are used is a question for a count, and the count prints with its
method. Lesson 4 counted the perfects of the Digital Corpus of Sanskrit's tagging of the Hitopadeśa
(its §5).

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The same tagging counts 73 futures in the Hitopadeśa and 532 in the
> Viṣṇupurāṇa; it counts 9 aorist tokens in the Hitopadeśa (5 indicative, 4 prohibitive: bhaiṣīḥ
> three times, kṛthāḥ once), where Whitney (§826) counted eight; his count and the tagging need not
> cover the same tokens. (Counted by the course from the DCS's morphological tags, 2026-11.)
> [REF:dcs]

The three bhaiṣīḥ are today's chunk, मा भैषीः. Whitney's own count, the one the corpus line names:

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "The aorist-system is a formation of infrequent occurrence in much of the
> classical Sanskrit (its forms are found, for example, only twenty-one times in the Nala, eight in
> the Hitopadeśa, seven in Manu …)". [REF:whitney1889 §826]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "The imperative negative, or prohibitive, is from the earliest period of the
> language regularly and usually expressed by the particle *mā́* with an augmentless past form,
> prevailingly aorist." Among his examples: मा भैषीः, "do not be afraid". [REF:whitney1889 §579]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "The periphrastic future is defined by the grammarians as expressing something
> to be done at a definite time to come. And this, though but faintly traceable in later use, is a
> distinct characteristic of the formation in the language where it first makes its appearance. It is
> especially often used along with *śvás* *tomorrow*." [REF:whitney1889 §949]

The grammarians' rule is on today's card: according to Pāṇini, luṭ is the future "not of today"
(3.3.15); Whitney says how much of that sense later texts keep. Both are printed; only "According to
Pāṇini …" is asked, and no form of the card is ever asked of you as production.

One word you already use in Hindi is the stem of today's lesson.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Hindi भविष्य 'the future' is the Sanskrit word भविष्य, whose letters are those
> of the future stem भविष्य- [Monier-Williams: 'future, imminent, impending']; Hindi took it whole,
> with the ष that 8.3.59 made in Sanskrit after the augment इ. [REF:kachru2006];
> [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. bhaviṣya]

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the day's idea in three lines, each printed joined, its pause form beneath, then
the gloss.

> श्वोऽहं ग्रामं गमिष्यामि। (śvo 'haṃ grāmaṃ gamiṣyāmi.)
> श्वः अहम् ग्रामम् गमिष्यामि। (śvaḥ aham grāmam gamiṣyāmi.)
> "Tomorrow I shall go to the village." — कल मैं गाँव जाऊँगा।

> यदि त्वं पठिष्यसि, ज्ञानं लप्स्यसे। (yadi tvaṃ paṭhiṣyasi, jñānaṃ lapsyase.)
> यदि त्वम् पठिष्यसि, ज्ञानम् लप्स्यसे। (yadi tvam paṭhiṣyasi, jñānam lapsyase.)
> "If you study, you will gain knowledge." — यदि तुम पढ़ोगे, तो ज्ञान पाओगे।

> करिष्यतीत्यत्र स्येति प्रत्ययः। (kariṣyatīty atra syeti pratyayaḥ.)
> करिष्यति इति अत्र स्य इति प्रत्ययः। (kariṣyati iti atra sya iti pratyayaḥ.)
> "In kariṣyati the affix is sya." — करिष्यति में प्रत्यय स्य है।

These are model sentences 1 and 2 and a third line; model sentences 3–6 are in §13. The verbs are
futures of verbs you own: गमिष्यामि of गच्छति, पठिष्यसि of पठति, लप्स्यसे of लभते — the ātmanepada
future, with the ending -से you know from लभसे. The other words are yours: श्वः [v2.c03.l03], अहम्,
ग्रामः [v3.c01.l01], त्वम्, ज्ञानम् [ज्ञान, Volume 1], प्रत्ययः [c02.l01], इति, अत्र; यदि "if" is
today's headword; स्य is a label, the name of the affix.

The joins are Volume 3's: श्वः अहम् → श्वोऽहम् — -अः before अ → -ओऽ; अहम्, ग्रामम्, त्वम्, ज्ञानम्
before a consonant → ं; करिष्यति इति → करिष्यतीति — इ + इ → ई, the quoted form joined to its इति,
then इति अत्र → इत्यत्र — इ before a vowel → य्; स्य इति → स्येति — अ + इ → ए. The comma stops the
seam after पठिष्यसि, which stands in its pause form. In the third line स्य is a quoted name: it is
joined to the इति after it, and its first sound stays as it is.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

तीसरे खण्ड में आपने करिष्यामि पढ़ा था — "मैं करूँगा" — और उसके साथ लिखा था कि इसकी पूरी बात चौथे
खण्ड में होगी। गमिष्यति "जाएगा" का भी वहीं नाम आया था। आज वह बात पूरी होती है।

**लृट् और स्य।** पाणिनि के अनुसार भविष्यत् के अर्थ में धातु के बाद **लृट्** लकार आता है — सूत्र
3.3.13। उसका ल्, लट् के ल् की तरह, अठारह प्रत्ययों में से किसी एक में बदलता है: ति, तः, अन्ति … —
वही प्रत्यय जो आप वर्तमान काल में बोलते हैं। फ़र्क़ यह है कि धातु और प्रत्यय के बीच एक चिह्न आता
है: **स्य** — सूत्र 3.1.33। कृ + स्य + ति।

**आर्धधातुक।** दूसरे पाठ में आपने देखा था कि तिङ् प्रत्यय और जिन प्रत्ययों में श् इत् हो, वे
**सार्वधातुक** कहलाते हैं। धातु के बाद आने वाला बाक़ी हर प्रत्यय **आर्धधातुक** कहलाता है — सूत्र
3.4.114। स्य न तिङ् है, न उसमें श् है; इसलिए वह आर्धधातुक है। और सूत्र 7.3.84 सार्वधातुक और
आर्धधातुक, दोनों प्रकार के प्रत्यय से पहले गुण करवाता है। दूसरे पाठ में उसका पहला आधा चला था, आज
दूसरा: कृ का ऋ अर् बनता है, भू का ऊ ओ।

**इट् आगम।** कई धातुओं में स्य से पहले एक इ और आ जाता है: करिष्यति, पठिष्यति, भविष्यति। यह इ एक
**आगम** है, उसका नाम **इट्** है। इट् का ट् इत् है, और जिस आगम में ट् इत् हो, वह आरम्भ में जुड़ता
है — सूत्र 1.1.46, दूसरे अध्याय से आपका: इ + स्य। सामान्य नियम सूत्र 7.2.35 है: जो आर्धधातुक
प्रत्यय वल् प्रत्याहार के किसी वर्ण से शुरू हो, उसे इट् मिलता है; स वल् में है। ऋ पर समाप्त होने
वाले धातुओं को और हन् को स्य से पहले इट् सूत्र 7.2.70 देता है, और गम् को परस्मैपद में सूत्र 7.2.58।

पर हर धातु इट् नहीं लेता: दास्यति, नेष्यति, स्थास्यति में कोई इ नहीं। कौन-सा धातु इट् लेता है,
यह उस धातु की अपनी बात है; उसे नियम से अनुमान लगाकर मत बनाइए। आपके शब्द-कार्ड पर हर क्रिया का
भविष्यत्-अङ्ग छपा है — वहीं से पढ़िए। नियम उस अङ्ग को समझाते हैं, उसे याद नहीं करवाते।

**दो इट्।** स्वपिति का इ भी यही इट् आगम है; वहाँ उसे सूत्र 7.2.76 ने दिया था, सार्वधातुक प्रत्यय
से पहले — दूसरे पाठ में। पर पहले पाठ के अठारह प्रत्ययों में उत्तम पुरुष एकवचन आत्मनेपद का प्रत्यय भी इट्
कहलाता है — लप्स्ये का अन्तिम ए उसी से है, और वहाँ कोई आगम नहीं। नाम एक है, वस्तुएँ दो।

**ष क्यों?** करिष्यति का ष वही है जो रामेषु का। प्रत्यय का स्, इण् प्रत्याहार के वर्ण के बाद, अगर
पद के अन्त में न हो, ष् बन जाता है — सूत्र 8.3.59, दूसरे अध्याय से आपका। करिष्यति में इ के बाद,
नेष्यति में ए के बाद — ष; दास्यति में आ के बाद — स ही, क्योंकि आ इण् में नहीं है। हिन्दी का शब्द
भविष्य भी इसी ष के साथ आया है — §5 का तथ्य देखिए।

**बाक़ी चार लकार।** लृट् के अलावा चार लकार और हैं, जो आज एक कार्ड पर छपे हैं — केवल पहचानने के
लिए: **लुट्** — दूसरा भविष्यत्, पाणिनि के अनुसार आज के बाद का: भविता "होगा"; **लृङ्** — "होता",
वह काम जो हुआ ही नहीं: अभविष्यत्; **लुङ्** — एक भूतकाल: अभूत्; और **आशीर्लिङ्** — आशीर्वाद:
भूयात् "हो"। कार्ड पर ये रूप देखकर आप उनका लकार बताएँगे; इन्हें बनाएँगे नहीं, और अपने वाक्यों
में नहीं लिखेंगे।

**मा भैषीः।** एक रूप अपवाद है: मा भैषीः — "डरो मत"। यह लुङ् का रूप है, धातु है √भी "डरना";
मा के बाद उसका आरम्भिक अ- नहीं लगता। आप इसे पूरा-का-पूरा सीखते और बोलते हैं, जैसे तीसरे खण्ड में
उवाच सीखा था। मना करने का आपका अपना तरीक़ा वही रहता है: मा + लोट् — मा वद, मा गच्छ।

**परम्परा के दस।** लघुसिद्धान्तकौमुदी दस लकार गिनाती है और कहती है कि उनमें पाँचवाँ, लेट्, केवल
वेद में मिलता है। छठे पाठ की तालिका में भी दस पंक्तियाँ होंगी, पर वे ये दस नहीं: उसमें लिङ् दो
बार है — विधिलिङ् और आशीर्लिङ् — और लेट् नहीं।

**आज के शब्द।** **मा भैषीः** — डरो मत; **नूनम्** — ज़रूर, निश्चय ही; **हृदयम्** — हृदय, दिल;
**भार्या** — पत्नी; **प्रयोजनम्** — प्रयोजन, ज़रूरत: जिस चीज़ की ज़रूरत है वह तृतीया में, जिसे
ज़रूरत है वह षष्ठी में — तस्य हृदयेन मम प्रयोजनम्, "मुझे उसके हृदय से काम है"; **यदि** — अगर;
**वर्तनम्** — रोज़ी, वेतन।

## 8. English Explanation

The recipe of this volume, once more: forms you own, the question they raise, the tools that answer
it, the derivation with the teacher, the tools run by you, then production. Today's one grammar
point: the future (लृट्, 3.3.13) puts the sign स्य between the root and the present's endings
(3.1.33); स्य is ārdhadhātuka (3.4.114), so the root takes guṇa (7.3.84) and, after many roots, the
augment इट् [7.2.35]; and the स of स्य becomes ष after इ [8.3.59, owned].

### Step 1 — the forms you own

| owned form | where owned | its pieces, as far as you can see | what it raises |
|---|---|---|---|
| करिष्यामि | v3.c06.l02, glossed "I will do" | कृ … -मि | an -इष्य- between root and ending |
| गमिष्यति | v3.c03.l03, display: "will go", Volume 4's | गम् … -ति | the same -इष्य-, after गम् |
| रामेषु | v3.c02.l01; derived in c02.l03 | रामे + सु | स of an affix → ष after ए: 8.3.59 |
| पठन्ति, पठथ, पठामः | v3.c03.l01, T6 | the stem पठ- and the endings -अन्ति, -थ, -मः | the endings the future takes over |

Your prompts gave you all four. The ending of करिष्यामि is the present's -मि; what stands between root
and ending is new.

### Step 2 — the question

**What is the -इष्य- of करिष्यामि, and why is its ष the ष of रामेषु?** Lesson 1 gave every finite
verb one frame: a root, an l-affix, its ल् replaced by one of eighteen endings. Lesson 2 put a class
sign between root and ending. But करिष्यामि has no class sign — not the उ of करोति — and its stem
keeps the same shape in every person. Today's tools answer: an l-affix for the future, a sign that
stands where the class sign stood, a name for that sign, an augment where the root takes one, guṇa, and
the ṣ rule you own.

### Step 3 — the tools

Each is printed once, here, with its tier and its bracket line. **D** is the authority for one
derivation step, never recalled by number; **K**, in §15, is on a card, looked up and never assessed.
There is no new **Q** today: the future is made with D tools on top of the B tool 7.3.84 and the
owned 8.3.59.

**1. lṛṭ for the future — D, 3.3.13 लृट् शेषे च** (lṛṭ śeṣe ca).

- Bracket line: [carried (data file): कृत्, 3.1.93 (*preview*, c04.l01); क्रियायाम्, 3.3.10; क्रियार्थायाम्, 3.3.10] [headings (data file): प्रत्ययः, 3.1.1; परश्च, 3.1.2; धातोः, 3.1.91; भविष्यति, 3.3.3 (not a tool; printed by name, as the heading the reading needs)] (further headings: Teacher Notes)
- English: "[In the future,] lṛṭ, in the remaining case too." [REF:ashtadhyayi 3.3.13];
  [REF:kashika 3.3.13]

लृट् is the result; शेषे is a locative of circumstance, "in the remaining case"; च "too". The heading
भविष्यति, printed by name because the reading needs it, is not the verb you will meet in the
Pañcatantra: the data file's padaccheda marks it a locative (सप्तमी), "in [time] to come" — the same
letters, another word. The carried क्रियायाम्, क्रियार्थायाम्, from 3.3.10, name a second use: "when an
action done for the sake of this one stands beside it" — someone goes in order to do something; the च
lets lṛṭ stand there too [Teacher Notes]. Your futures are the plain ones, the "remaining case".
प्रत्ययः and परश्च are Chapter 2's headings [c02.l01], धातोः lesson 1's; कृत् is Chapter 4's.

**2. The sign स्य — D, 3.1.33 स्यतासी लृलुटोः** (syatāsī lṛluṭoḥ).

- Bracket line: [carried (data file): —] [headings (data file): प्रत्ययः, 3.1.1; परश्च, 3.1.2] (further headings: Teacher Notes) [carried (course): धातोः "after a root"]
- English: "Before lṛṭ and luṭ, स्य and तास्, respectively." [REF:ashtadhyayi 3.1.33];
  [REF:kashika 3.1.33]

स्यतासी is a nominative dual, the two results; लृलुटोः a locative dual, "before lṛ and luṭ".
"Respectively" is Chapter 1's "in order" (1.3.10): स्य with the first, तास् with the second. The
rule's लृ carries no marker, and the Kāśikā reads it as both lṛṭ and lṛṅ: its examples are करिष्यति,
the conditional अकरिष्यत् and, for luṭ, श्वः कर्ता [REF:kashika 3.1.33]. That is why the conditional on
today's card is built on the future's स्य. The heading धातोः is printed by the course: the data file
files this rule under an earlier heading the course does not teach (Teacher Notes).

**3. The name ārdhadhātuka — D, 3.4.114 आर्धधातुकं शेषः** (ārdhadhātukaṃ śeṣaḥ).

- Bracket line: [carried (data file): —] [headings (data file): प्रत्ययः, 3.1.1; परश्च, 3.1.2; धातोः, 3.1.91] (further headings: Teacher Notes)
- English: "Every other affix after a root (not a tiṅ, not ś-marked) is called ārdhadhātuka."
  [REF:ashtadhyayi 3.4.114]; [REF:kashika 3.4.114]

It stands right after lesson 2's 3.4.113: the tiṅ and the ś-marked affixes are sārvadhātuka; शेषः,
"the rest", every other affix taught after a root, is ārdhadhātuka. स्य is neither a tiṅ nor
ś-marked: ārdhadhātuka. So is the class-8 sign उ of करोति: lesson 3 printed this rule's number
beside it as a preview (its §8, under 6.4.110), and today the rule is yours. And 7.3.84, whose
sārvadhātuka half lesson 2 used, now works with its other half.

**4. The augment इट् — D, 7.2.35 आर्धधातुकस्येड् वलादेः** (ārdhadhātukasyeḍ valādeḥ).

- Bracket line: [carried (data file): —] [headings (data file): अङ्गस्य, 6.4.1]
- English: "An ārdhadhātuka affix beginning with a वल् sound takes the augment इट्."
  [REF:ashtadhyayi 7.2.35]; [REF:kashika 7.2.35]

वल् = every consonant except य — from the व of वल् to the ल् of हल्: स्य begins with स, a वल् sound.
इट्'s ट् is a marker, and a ṭ-marked augment stands at the beginning of what it is added to — 1.1.46
[D, owned since c02.l02], the rule of the अट् of अगच्छत्. So: इ + स्य. Lesson 2 met this augment
before an ending, after रुद् and the rest (7.2.76 [D], स्वपिति); the इट् and वलादेः carried into
7.2.76 come from this rule, the one lesson 2 marked as a preview.

**5. इट् before स्य after ṛ — D, 7.2.70 ऋद्धनोः स्ये** (ṛddhanoḥ sye).

- Bracket line: [carried (data file): आर्धधातुकस्य, 7.2.35; इट्, 7.2.35; वलादेः, 7.2.35] [headings (data file): अङ्गस्य, 6.4.1]
- English: "After an ṛ-final root, and after हन्, स्य takes इट्." [REF:ashtadhyayi 7.2.70];
  [REF:kashika 7.2.70]

Why a rule of its own, when 7.2.35 already gives इट् before स्य? Because कृ and हृ, like दा and नी,
are roots that refuse इट् — a rule in Teacher Notes reads this from their Dhātupāṭha entries: on
today's card, the luṭ cell कर्ता has no इ. Before स्य, 7.2.70 gives the augment back to a root in ṛ,
and to the हन् of lesson 3's हन्ति: हनिष्यति.

**6. इट् after गम् — D, 7.2.58 गमेरिट् परस्मैपदेषु** (gamer iṭ parasmaipadeṣu).

- Bracket line: [carried (data file): आर्धधातुकस्य, 7.2.35; इट्, 7.2.35; वलादेः, 7.2.35; से, 7.2.57] [headings (data file): अङ्गस्य, 6.4.1]
- English: "After गम्, an s-initial ārdhadhātuka affix takes इट् in the parasmaipada."
  [REF:ashtadhyayi 7.2.58]; [REF:kashika 7.2.58]

गमेः is an ablative, "after गम्" — Chapter 1's case code; परस्मैपदेषु a locative, "in the
parasmaipada"; the carried से, "before an s-initial [affix]", comes from 7.2.57. गम् too is a root
that would refuse इट् [Teacher Notes]; in the parasmaipada this rule gives it back: गमिष्यति, the
first example the Kāśikā gives under the rule.

### Step 4 — करिष्यति, with the teacher

The table is in §13, derivation (a). Work down it with the teacher, saying each line's tool. Step 0
runs lesson 1's frame in one line, with lṛṭ in place of laṭ (3.3.13). Then the sign (3.1.33), its name
(3.4.114), the augment (7.2.70, beside the general 7.2.35), guṇa (7.3.84 with 1.1.51), and last the
ष [8.3.59]: **करिष्यति**. The ष is the ष of रामेषु, made by the same rule in a new form.

### Step 5 — T12, and why the card prints every stem

The future stem plus the present's endings gives every cell: करिष्य- + -मि, -सि, -ति …, and in the
ātmanepada लप्स्य- + -ए, -से, -ते …. That is T12 (§15), the one table of this lesson you learn by
heart, with Volume 3's formula: the singular and the plural in all persons, and the 3rd dual.

**The iṭ line.** Whether a root takes इट् is a fact about that root. The Dhātupāṭha records it, and
a rule in Teacher Notes reads it there; Whitney lists the usage root by root [REF:whitney1889 §935]. The
tradition's names are seṭ "with iṭ" (भू, पठ्) and aniṭ "without iṭ" (दा, नी, लभ्, स्था). You do not
work this out: from today every owned verb's row on your vocabulary card prints its future stem
(§15), and you read the stem from there. Today's rules explain the stem you read; they do not replace
the card.

**The homograph line.** The -इ- of पठिष्यति and the -इ- of lesson 2's स्वपिति are the same augment,
इट्: 7.2.35 gives it before an ārdhadhātuka affix, 7.2.76 before a sārvadhātuka ending. The 1st
singular ātmanepada ending of lesson 1's eighteen is also called इट् — a different item with the same
name: लप्स्ये is लभ् + स्य + that ending, its ṭi → ए [3.4.79], and there is no augment in it.

### Step 6 — your runs

Now run the tools yourself (§18 exercise 3, at home). गम् → गमिष्यति, the future of the 1.x book's
गच्छ, takes its इट् from 7.2.58. पठ् → पठिष्यति and भू → भविष्यति take it from the general 7.2.35; भू
also has guṇa, and its ओ becomes अव् before the इ [6.1.78, owned]. नी → नेष्यति and दा → दास्यति take
no इट् — the card says so: नी has guṇa, and its स् becomes ष् after ए; दा has neither guṇa, since आ is no
इक्, nor ष, since आ is no इण् sound. हृ → हरिष्यति is करिष्यति again: the Kāśikā's second example under
7.2.70.

### Step 7 — the card of the other four, मा भैषीः, and the tradition's ten

The card of §15 prints the other four lakāras of the classical verb, each with its rules [K], its
sign, the √भू and √कृ cells, and its use in the two registers. You read the forms there and name
their lakāra (§18 exercise 2): you never produce them, and they never stand in your own sentences.

One aorist is yours to say: **मा भैषीः** "do not be afraid!", the aorist of √भी "fear" without its
augment after मा. It is taught whole and counted as one word, as उवाच was in Volume 3; formulaic
chunks are taught whole and analysed later [REF:nation2013]. The pattern मा + aorist stays ◐: your
own prohibitive is मा + imperative, owned since v3.c03.l04 — मा वद, मा गच्छ.

The tradition counts ten l-affixes; the √भू table of lesson 6 has ten rows; they are not the same
ten (§4).

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the step from स to ष that 8.3.59 makes, the ऋ that guṇa turns into अर्, and the क्ष of
द्रक्ष्यति. The future image (`planned:assets/images/volume-4/v4c03l05-the-future.svg`) shows कृ +
स्य + ति with the इ slipping in, the guṇa arrow on ऋ and the स curling to ष; say each stage as you
point to it.

1. **स and ष.** For स /s/ the tongue tip rests just behind the upper teeth and the air hisses past
   it. For ष /ʂ/ the tip curls back to the roof of the mouth, behind the ridge, and the hiss turns
   darker. Say दास्यति, then करिष्यति: the tongue that stays down for स curls back for ष.
2. **ऋ and अर्.** कृ has the syllabic /r̩/, a vowel held as long as a short अ. Its guṇa is two sounds:
   the open /ɐ/, then one light tap of the tongue tip, /ɾ/ — कर्.
3. **क्ष in द्रक्ष्यति.** The back of the tongue closes for क /k/, then the tip curls back for ष /ʂ/,
   with no vowel between: /kʂ/.
4. **ऐ in भैषीः.** Start from the open /ɐ/ and glide to /i/; then the tip curls back for ष.

Mirror check: say करिष्यति — दास्यति. The jaw hardly moves; watch the tongue tip rise and curl for
the ष and stay forward for the स.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; they feed the quiz distractors.

1. **Guessing iṭ by rule (\*दासिष्यति, \*स्थासिष्यति).** *Fix:* the future stem is a vocabulary fact
   (the card); the rule only explains it.
2. **\*करस्यति / \*करिस्यति.** *Fix:* इट् by 7.2.70; then स् after इ → ष् [8.3.59].
3. **Taking the -इ- of पठिष्यति for the 1st singular ātmanepada ending इट्.** *Fix:* it is the
   augment इट् [7.2.35] — the same augment as in स्वपिति [7.2.76, lesson 2]; लप्स्ये ends in the
   ending इट्, turned to ए, with no augment.
4. **Producing a ◐ form (अभूत्, भविता) in your own sentences.** *Fix:* the four on the card are
   recognised and named only; मा भैषीः is the one chunk.
5. **मा with a future, or \*मा भवसि.** *Fix:* the prohibitive you own is मा + imperative (मा वद); मा
   भैषीः is a chunk.
6. **Reading भविष्यति in the Pañcatantra passage only as "will be".** *Fix:* it can state a
   supposition, "must be" (Whitney §948).

> **According to Pāṇini, the future (lṛṭ) is made thus** (3.3.13): its ल् becomes one of the
> eighteen, as laṭ's does; between root and ending stands the sign स्य [3.1.33]; स्य is ārdhadhātuka

> of स्य after an इण् sound becomes ष् [8.3.59]. Which roots take इट् is read from the vocabulary
> card. The tools' English lines and bracket lines are in §8 Step 3.

## 20. Revision

पुनरावृत्तिः — one screen to remember.

> **The future.** According to Pāṇini, lṛṭ is the future (3.3.13); its ल् becomes the present's
> endings, and the sign स्य stands between root and ending (3.1.33). स्य is ārdhadhātuka (3.4.114):
> guṇa before it (7.3.84) and, where the root takes it, the augment इट् [7.2.35; after ṛ and हन्
> 7.2.70; after गम् in the P 7.2.58]. The स् after इ or ए becomes ष् [8.3.59]: **करिष्यति**,
> **भविष्यति**, **नेष्यति**; after आ it stays: **दास्यति**. **Which roots take इट्:** the card, never a
> guess. **Two इट्:** the augment (पठिष्यति, स्वपिति); the ending (लप्स्ये). **The card of the other
> four:** luṭ भविता, lṛṅ अभविष्यत्, luṅ अभूत्, āśīrliṅ भूयात् — name them, never produce them; the one
> aorist you say is **मा भैषीः**. **The tradition's ten** include leṭ, Vedic only; lesson 6's table
> has ten other rows. **The ledger:** seven new — 565; cells drilled: 14; tools added: 6; toolbox: 89.
