## 1. Hook

In v3.c01.l02 you met तिष्ठति "stands", and its vocabulary row made a promise: "why the stem is
तिष्ठ- is v4.c03's". Today the promise falls due. Line up seven more verbs you own beside their
roots: गच्छति from गम्, पश्यति from दृश्, नयति from नी, and नृत्यति, शृणोति, करोति, गृह्णाति.
Seven verbs, seven ways of making a stem from a root: गच्छ-, पश्य-, नय-, नृत्य-, शृणो-, करो-,
गृह्णा-. Volume 2 said of three of them only that each "builds its stem
differently" (शृणोति in v2.c04.l01, करोति in v2.c04.l03, गृह्णाति in v2.c06.l02).

Lesson 1 took पठति apart as far as पठ् + ति, and marked the last line of its table for today.
But नयति is not नी + ति. What stands between the root and the ending — and why does नी become
नय-?

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — where today's rules stand in the grammar, and how the tradition names the
ten classes.

Today's rules stand far apart in the Aṣṭādhyāyī; a derivation brings them together. The class sign
is given in the first quarter of Book 3: 3.1.68 कर्तरि शप्, with the signs of the other classes
after it (3.1.69 to 3.1.81) and the tenth class's णिच् a little before it (3.1.25). The name
sārvadhātuka is defined at the end of the same book (3.4.113). The deletion of शप् after two of
the classes is in Book 2 (2.4.72, 2.4.75). Guṇa before the sign (7.3.84, 7.3.86) and the
substitute stems (7.3.77–7.3.79) are in Book 7, under the heading अङ्गस्य, 6.4.1, that you met in
c02.l03. The marker rule 1.3.8 and the two rules on substitutes, 1.1.51 and 1.1.52, are in Book 1,
among the definitions and the rules on how other rules are read.

The roots themselves are not in the sūtras. They are listed in the Dhātupāṭha, the tradition's list
of roots, in ten lists, and a rule names a list by its first root.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** The tradition names the ten classes by the first root of each list in
> the Dhātupāṭha — भ्वादिः 'the class beginning with भू', अदादिः, जुहोत्यादिः … — and the
> Laghusiddhāntakaumudī (c. 17th century) presents the verbs class by class in that order: भ्वादयः,
> अदादयः, जुहोत्यादयः, दिवादयः, स्वादयः, तुदादयः, रुधादयः, तनादयः, क्र्यादयः, चुरादयः.
> [REF:laghukaumudi]; [REF:dhatupatha]

Read the names as compounds. भू + आदि "beginning with भू" gives भ्वादि, with the yaṇ join of
6.1.77: ऊ before a vowel becomes व्. The plural भ्वादयः is "भू and the rest", the words of 1.3.1
भूवादयो धातवः, which lesson 1 read. Each name begins with the first root of its list, or, in
जुहोत्यादि, with a verb form of it: जुहोति, from the root हु. None of these roots is a word you
need: the class names are labels, and they add nothing to the ledger.

One class has no verb of yours yet. रुधादि, the seventh, gets one in lesson 3 (युनक्ति); the other
nine have verbs you own, and §15 puts them on one card.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — what the language does: present stems made from the root in a few distinct
ways, which Whitney sorts into two families.

Whitney describes the same stems without the tradition's rules, and he sorts the classes before he
lists them: "the classes being divided … into two more general classes or conjugations"
[REF:whitney1889 §602].

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "The classes of the FIRST or NON-*a*-CONJUGATION are as follows: I. The
> root-class (second class, or *ad*-class, of the Hindu grammarians) …"; and "In the classes of
> the SECOND or *a*-CONJUGATION, the present-stem ends in *a*". [REF:whitney1889 §603];
> [REF:whitney1889 §605]

His first conjugation is the tradition's classes 2, 3, 5, 7, 8 and 9 (§603). His second is 1, 6
and 4 (§606), and the tradition's tenth stands beside them, a stem in *áya* with "an inflection
like that of the other *a*-stems" (§607). That is the sort you will make in §8 Step 5: a present
stem that ends in अ, or not. [REF:whitney1889 §606]; [REF:whitney1889 §607]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** On the first class: "The present-stem of this class is made by adding अ *a*
> to the root, which has the accent, and, when that is possible …, is strengthened to *guṇa*.
> Thus, भव *bháva* from √भू *bhū*; जय *jáya* from √जि *ji* … — but वद *váda* from √वद् *vad*".
> [REF:whitney1889 §734]

That is the language, observed: an अ after the root, and guṇa where guṇa can be made — and not in
वद्. Today's derivations give Pāṇini's account of the same facts, which is TRADITION: शप् for the
अ, by 3.1.68; guṇa by 7.3.84; and no guṇa where the root has no vowel for guṇa to replace (§18
exercise 3, items 5–6).

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** On the fourth class: "The present-stem of this class adds य *ya* to the
> accented but unstrengthened root." [REF:whitney1889 §759]

So नृत्य-, मन्य-: a य, and no guṇa. Whitney's marks over *bháva*, *jáya* show where the tone fell.
Accent is Volume 5's; the course names it and does not show it.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the day's idea in three lines, each printed joined, its pause form beneath,
then the gloss.

> भ्वादिगणे शबिति विकरणः। (bhvādigaṇe śab iti vikaraṇaḥ.)
> भू-आदि-गणे शप् इति विकरणः। (bhū-ādi-gaṇe śap iti vikaraṇaḥ.)
> "In the bhū class the class sign is śap." — भ्वादि गण में शप् विकरण है।

> तिष्ठतीत्यत्र स्थेति धातुः। (tiṣṭhatīty atra stheti dhātuḥ.)
> तिष्ठति इति अत्र स्था इति धातुः। (tiṣṭhati iti atra sthā iti dhātuḥ.)
> "In tiṣṭhati the root is sthā." — तिष्ठति में धातु स्था है।

> गच्छतीत्यत्र गमिति धातुः, गच्छेत्यङ्गम्। (gacchatīty atra gam iti dhātuḥ, gacchety aṅgam.)
> गच्छति इति अत्र गम् इति धातुः, गच्छ इति अङ्गम्। (gacchati iti atra gam iti dhātuḥ, gaccha iti aṅgam.)
> "In gacchati the root is gam, the stem gaccha." — गच्छति में धातु गम् है, और अङ्ग गच्छ।

These are model sentences 1 and 5 and a third line; model sentences 2, 3, 4 and 6 are in §13. The
first line uses today's upgrade, गणः, in its grammar sense, "a class of roots": गण "group" has been
yours since v1.c03.l03, and the compound भ्वादिगणे is "in the class beginning with भू". विकरणः
(vikaraṇaḥ) is this volume's term for the class sign; like the class name भ्वादि, it is a label,
display only, and adds nothing to the ledger. धातुः is lesson 1's headword. अङ्गम्, which you have
owned as "limb" since v1.c06.l01, is here the grammar label aṅga, "the base before an affix"
(c02.l01).

The joins are Volume 3's: शप् इति → शबिति — a final stop voiced before a vowel, and written
together; तिष्ठति इति → तिष्ठतीति and गच्छति इति → गच्छतीति — two like vowels, one long; इति
अत्र → इत्यत्र and गच्छेति अङ्गम् → गच्छेत्यङ्गम् — इ before a vowel → य्; स्था इति → स्थेति and गच्छ
इति → गच्छेति — आ or अ with इ → ए; गम् इति → गमिति — a consonant-final word written together with
the vowel after it; भू + आदि → भ्वादि inside the compound. The comma stops the seam after धातुः:
it stands in its pause form. Each quoted form — शप्, तिष्ठति, स्था, गच्छति, गम्, गच्छ — is joined to
the इति after it, and none has its first sound fused with the word before it.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

नयति, तिष्ठति, गच्छति — ये क्रियाएँ आप बहुत पहले से बोलते आ रहे हैं। पिछले पाठ में आपने पठति को
खोला था: पहले धातु पठ्, फिर लकार लट्, और लट् के ल् के स्थान पर अठारह प्रत्ययों में से एक — यहाँ
ति। पर नयति को उसी तरह खोलिए, तो नी + ति नहीं निकलता। धातु और प्रत्यय के बीच कुछ और खड़ा है,
और नी बदलकर नय- हो गया है। आज का पाठ इन्हीं दो बातों का उत्तर है।

**सार्वधातुक।** पाणिनि तिङ् प्रत्ययों को — ति, तः, अन्ति जैसे सभी अठारह को — और उन प्रत्ययों को
भी जिनमें श् इत् हो, एक नाम देते हैं: सार्वधातुक। यह सूत्र 3.4.113 है।

**विकरण।** सूत्र 3.1.68 कहता है: जब सार्वधातुक प्रत्यय कर्ता का बोध कराए, तो धातु के बाद और उस
प्रत्यय से पहले शप् आता है। धातु और प्रत्यय के बीच आने वाले इस छोटे प्रत्यय को **विकरण** कहते
हैं। शप् का श् इत् है, क्योंकि प्रत्यय के आरम्भ का श् इत् होता है — सूत्र 1.3.8; और प् भी इत्
है, क्योंकि वह अन्तिम व्यञ्जन है — सूत्र 1.3.3। दोनों का लोप हो जाता है — सूत्र 1.3.9। बचता है
केवल अ। यही अ आप पठति में सुनते हैं: पठ् + अ + ति।

**विकरण से पहले गुण।** अब नी + अ + ति लीजिए। सार्वधातुक प्रत्यय से पहले अङ्ग के अन्तिम वर्ण का गुण
होता है — सूत्र 7.3.84। नी का अन्तिम वर्ण ई है, और गुण के स्वरों — अ, ए, ओ — में ई के निकट ए है।
तो ने + अ + ति। फिर ए के बाद स्वर आया, और ए के स्थान पर अय् हो गया — सूत्र 6.1.78, जिसे आप
तीसरे खण्ड से जानते हैं: नय् + अ + ति = नयति। स्मृ में ऋ का गुण अर् होता है, और र् गुण के साथ आता
है — सूत्र 1.1.51: स्मरति। पर चर् और वद् में इ, उ, ऋ में से कोई स्वर नहीं, तो गुण किसका हो?
इसलिए चरति, वदति — धातु जैसी थी, वैसी ही।

**दस गण।** हर धातु के साथ शप् नहीं आता। परम्परा धातुओं को दस गणों में बाँटती है, और हर गण का
नाम उसकी पहली धातु से रखती है — भ्वादि, यानी भू से आरम्भ होने वाला गण; फिर अदादि, जुहोत्यादि,
दिवादि, स्वादि, तुदादि, रुधादि, तनादि, क्र्यादि, चुरादि। हर गण का अपना विकरण है, या कोई विकरण
नहीं। इन दस को दस अलग तथ्यों की तरह रटिए मत। पहले वह देखिए जो कान से सुनाई देता है: क्या
वर्तमान-अङ्ग अ पर समाप्त होता है? भव-ति, नृत्य-ति, पृच्छ-ति, कथय-ति — हाँ; ये गण 1, 4, 6 और 10
हैं। अस्-ति, ददा-ति, शृणो-ति, करो-ति, गृह्णा-ति — नहीं; ये बाक़ी छह गण हैं: 2, 3, 5, 7, 8 और 9।
शृणोति इनमें केवल अपने विकरण के कारण आता है, क्योंकि उसकी धातु श्रु पहले गण में गिनी जाती है।
सूत्रों के क्रमांक कार्ड पर रहते हैं — उन्हें देखना है, याद नहीं करना।

**आदेश-अङ्ग।** कुछ धातुएँ शित् प्रत्यय से पहले, यानी ऐसे प्रत्यय से पहले जिसमें श् इत् हो — जैसे
शप् — अपना रूप छोड़ देती हैं, और उनकी जगह दूसरा अङ्ग आ जाता है। गम् से गच्छ, इष् से इच्छ, यम् से
यच्छ — सूत्र 7.3.77; स्था से तिष्ठ, पा से पिब, दृश् से पश्य, सद् से सीद — सूत्र 7.3.78। तीसरे खण्ड
में तिष्ठति के साथ लिखा था कि अङ्ग तिष्ठ- क्यों है, यह बात चौथे खण्ड में आएगी — वह बात यही है।
गच्छति, तिष्ठति, पश्यति और पिबति "अनियमित" नहीं हैं; दो सूत्र इन्हें बनाते हैं।

**स्वपिति का इ।** स्वपिति दूसरे गण की क्रिया है — उसमें कोई विकरण नहीं। फिर भी स्वप् और ति के
बीच एक इ सुनाई देता है: स्वपिति। यह इ एक आगम है, जिसका नाम इट् है — सूत्र 7.2.76। ध्यान रहे: पिछले पाठ के अठारह
प्रत्ययों में भी एक इट् है, उत्तम पुरुष एकवचन आत्मनेपद का प्रत्यय। नाम एक है, पर वस्तुएँ दो।

आज के शब्द: **गण** — समूह; व्याकरण में धातुओं का वर्ग, जैसे भ्वादिगण। **विकरण** — धातु और
प्रत्यय के बीच आने वाला चिह्न। **सार्वधातुक** — तिङ् और शित् प्रत्ययों का नाम। और आज की छह नई
क्रियाएँ — जयति, तरति, हरति, सीदति, उत्तिष्ठति, चरति — सब पहले गण की हैं, सब में शप् है।

### Step 2 — the question

**What stands between the root and the ending?** If the ending of नयति is ति, as lesson 1 showed,
then नय- comes from नी, and two things have happened to it: an अ has been added, and the ई has
become अय्. Volumes 2 and 3 handed you every one of these stems whole. Today's tools answer: a class sign,

root outright.

### Step 3 — the tools

**1. The name sārvadhātuka — B, 3.4.113 तिङ्शित्सार्वधातुकम्** (tiṅśit sārvadhātukam).

Padaccheda: तिङ्-शित् [प्रथमा: "a tiṅ, or [an affix] with the marker श्"] · सार्वधातुकम् [प्रथमा:
the name].

Bracket line: [carried (data file): —] [headings (data file): प्रत्ययः, 3.1.1; परश्च, 3.1.2; धातोः, 3.1.91] (further headings: Teacher Notes)

English: "The tiṅ endings and the ś-marked affixes are called sārvadhātuka." [REF:ashtadhyayi

A definition: it gives a name, and both its words are nominatives. Every one of lesson 1's eighteen

**2. The class sign — B, 3.1.68 कर्तरि शप्** (kartari śap).

Padaccheda: कर्तरि [सप्तमी: "when [the ending] denotes the doer"] · शप् [प्रथमा: the affix] ·
[सार्वधातुके, carried from 3.1.67: "before a sārvadhātuka affix"].

Bracket line: [carried (data file): सार्वधातुके, 3.1.67] [headings (data file): प्रत्ययः, 3.1.1; परश्च, 3.1.2] (further headings: Teacher Notes) [carried (course): धातोः "after a root" (the heading word of 3.1.91)]

English: "Before a sārvadhātuka affix that denotes the doer, शप् [comes after the root]."
[REF:ashtadhyayi 3.1.68]; [REF:kashika 3.1.68]

Read the two locatives apart. सार्वधातुके, the carried word, is the case code's locative: "before

**3. The marker श् — B, 1.3.8 लशक्वतद्धिते** (laśakv ataddhite).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): उपदेशे, 1.3.2; इत्, 1.3.2; आदिः, 1.3.5; प्रत्ययस्य, 1.3.6] [headings (data file): —]

English: "At the beginning of an affix that is not a taddhita, ल्, श् and a k-row sound are

You met this number once before, as a preview, in v4.c02.l01: the initial श् of शस् is a marker

not one, since it follows a root. So its first sound, श्, is a marker; its last, प्, is a marker by
the rule you own, 1.3.3 हलन्त्यम्; and 1.3.9 तस्य लोपः drops both. What is left is अ. Two markers,
two jobs: the श् makes शप् a ś-marked affix, so 3.4.113 calls it sārvadhātuka too — the Kāśikā
says, under 3.1.68, that is what its श् is for [REF:kashika 3.1.68]; what its प् does is lesson 3's

### Step 4 — नयति, with the teacher

The table is in §13, derivation (a). Work down it with the teacher, one line at a time, saying each
line's tool as you go. Step 0 is the root as the Dhātupāṭha lists it, णीञ् प्रापणे, "णीञ्, in the
sense of leading": its ञ् is a marker, the one that lets नी take both sets by lesson 1's 1.3.72, and
its ण् becomes न् by a rule in Teacher Notes. Step 1 runs lesson 1's steps 2–7 in one line: लट्,
its ल्, तिप्, ति. Then today's four B tools, in order: ति is sārvadhātuka (3.4.113); शप् comes before
it (3.1.68); its श् and प् are markers, dropped (1.3.8, 1.3.3, 1.3.9). At step 5 नी is the aṅga
before अ, itself sārvadhātuka because it is ś-marked, and 7.3.84 gives guṇa in place of the aṅga's
last sound (1.1.52), an इक् vowel (1.1.3), the nearest grade (1.1.50): ई → ए — your prompt 1. Step 6
is your prompt 2: ए before a vowel → अय्, by 6.1.78. Then **नयति**.

जयति, today's headword, takes the steps of नयति from step 1 on, with इ in place of ई: §18
exercise 3, item 2. भवति takes them with ऊ: guṇa ओ, then अव् by the same 6.1.78. भवति is Whitney's
भव of §5, and lesson 1's exercise 3 took it to step 7, leaving शप् and guṇa for today; deriving it
whole, every step with its sūtra, is the volume's last task (v4.c06.l04).

Why can guṇa happen here? Because शप् allows it. Not every class sign does: लिखति has no guṇa,
though the इ of लिख् is light and stands just before the root's last consonant, as the उ of रुच्
does. Which signs and endings block guṇa, and how, is lesson 3's [1.2.4 and 1.1.5, preview:
c03.l03].

**Lesson 1's रोचते, finished.** Lesson 1's table for रुच → रोचते, its exercise 3, item 5, ended
with two lines marked for today: the class sign, and guṇa. रुच् ends in a consonant, so 7.3.84 finds
no final इक् to change. But its उ is light and penultimate, and 7.3.86 gives it guṇa, ओ: रोच् + अ +
ते → **रोचते**. The table is in §13, derivation (b).

### Step 5 — ten families: the sort first, then the card

शप् is the class sign of one class. Other roots take other signs, or none, and the tradition sorts
the roots into ten classes by their sign. Before you look at the card, sort the verbs you own by
something you can hear: **does the present stem end in अ?**

- भव-ति, नृत्य-ति, पृच्छ-ति, कथय-ति — yes: an अ stands before the ending. These are **thematic**
  stems — the word Volume 3 put in the rows of नयति and तिष्ठति — and they belong to classes 1, 4,
  6 and 10.
- अस्-ति, ददा-ति, शृणो-ति, करो-ति, गृह्णा-ति, स्वप्-इ-ति — no. These are **athematic**, and they
  belong to the other six classes: 2, 3, 5, 7, 8 and 9 — शृणोति by its sign only, since its
  root श्रु is listed in class 1 (§12, mistake 6).

Do §18 exercise 5 now, in class. The point of the sort is that you never learn ten signs as ten
unconnected facts: the four thematic classes all end their stems in अ — शप्, श्यन्, heard as य,
श, and णिच् with शप् — and the athematic stems are the ones whose cells do not follow the pattern
of गच्छति; why, is lesson 3's.

Then the card, §15: ten rows in the tradition's order, each with its first root, its sign, the rule
that gives it, the verbs you own and the stem. The rule numbers stay on the card [K]: you look them
up; nothing asks you to recall them. The card has one more column, the place in Whitney's grammar
where the class is described.

The tenth class is where Pāṇini's account and Whitney's part company.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** According to Pāṇini, the roots fall into ten classes, each named by the
> first root of its list; the tenth class adds णिच् and शप् after it, together the -अय of कथयति
> (3.1.25).
> [REF:ashtadhyayi 3.1.25]; [REF:dhatupatha]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "The Hindu grammarians reckon a tenth class or *cur*-class, having a
> class-sign *áya* added to a strengthened root … Since, however, this stem is not limited to the
> present-system, but extends also into the rest of the conjugation — while it also has to a great
> extent a causative value … it will be best treated in full along with the derivative
> conjugations". [REF:whitney1889 §607]

Both are printed, and each is asked only as its source's: the classes "According to Pāṇini…"
(§19, Q9), Whitney's point as his (§19, Q10). Whitney's point is about what the language
does: the -अय stem of the tenth class is not confined to the present. The ten classes are the
tradition's arrangement of the roots, and this course asks about them only in the form "According
to Pāṇini…".

### Step 6 — stems that replace the root

Look again at गच्छति, तिष्ठति, पश्यति, पिबति. Their stems do not look like their roots at all. Two
sūtras make them, and both work only before a ś-marked affix — before शप्, that is, in the present
system.

**8. छ for the last sound — D, 7.3.77 इषुगमियमां छः** (iṣugamiyamāṃ chaḥ).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): शिति, 7.3.75] [headings (data file): अङ्गस्य, 6.4.1]

English: "Before a ś-marked affix, छ [replaces the final] of इष्, गम् and यम्." [REF:ashtadhyayi
7.3.77]; [REF:kashika 7.3.77]

गम् → गच्छ, इष् → इच्छ, यम् → यच्छ: the owned गच्छति, इच्छति and प्रयच्छति. The carried word शिति,
"before a ś-marked [affix]", is the condition, and शप् is ś-marked. The त् before छ is an augment;
its rule is in Teacher Notes.

**9. Whole substitutes — D, 7.3.78 पाघ्राध्मास्थाम्नादाण्दृश्यर्त्तिसर्त्तिशदसदां

pibajighradhamatiṣṭhamanayacchapaśyarcchadhauśīyasīdāḥ).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): शिति, 7.3.75] [headings (data file): अङ्गस्य, 6.4.1]

[REF:ashtadhyayi 7.3.78]; [REF:kashika 7.3.78]

…", and one compound of substitutes in the nominative; the two lists pair off in order. Four of the

**10. स्वपिति's इ — D, 7.2.76 रुदादिभ्यः सार्वधातुके** (rudādibhyaḥ sārvadhātuke).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): इट्, 7.2.35 (*preview*, c03.l05); वलादेः, 7.2.35] [headings (data file): अङ्गस्य, 6.4.1]

English: "After रुद् and the rest, a sārvadhātuka affix that begins with a वल् sound takes the
augment इट्." [REF:ashtadhyayi 7.2.76]; [REF:kashika 7.2.76]

स्वपिति is class 2: no sign at all. So स्वप् + ति — and yet you say स्वपिति. The -इ- is an augment,
इट्, by this rule: स्वप् is one of "रुद् and the rest", and ति begins with त्, a वल् sound. Open
वल् as Chapter 1 taught you: from the व of हयवरट् to the marker ल् of हल् — every consonant but य.
Two notes. This इट् is not the 1st singular ātmanepada ending इट् of lesson 1: same name, two
things. And the same augment returns before the future's स्य in lesson 5 [7.2.35, preview:
c03.l05].

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the curl of ष्ठ, the held त्त्, the two r-sounds of स्मृ and स्मरति, and the voiced ह of
हरति. The ten-families image (`planned:assets/images/volume-4/v4c03l02-ten-families.svg`) shows
the first door, भ्वादिः, with नयति, तरति and चरति behind it; say each verb as you point to it.

1. **ष्ठ in तिष्ठति.** The tongue tip curls back behind the ridge for ष /ʂ/, then closes there for
   ठ /ʈʰ/ and releases with a puff of breath. One position for both: the tip does not move forward
   between them.
2. **त्त् in उत्तिष्ठति.** The tip of the tongue presses the back of the upper teeth and holds;
   count "one" before the release. It is not the English t, made on the ridge behind the teeth.
3. **ऋ and र in स्मृ and स्मरति.** ऋ /r̩/ is a vowel: the tongue tip makes a light tap or two and
   the voice runs on through it, with no इ after it. In स्मरति it becomes अर् /ɐɾ/: open the mouth
   for /ɐ/, then one light tap /ɾ/.
4. **ह in हरति.** /ɦ/ is voiced: the vocal folds hum through the breath, as in Hindi हर.

Mirror check: say नी — नय — नयति. The jaw opens for /ɐ/ and closes a little for /j/; the tongue tip
touches the teeth for न and त, and the blade rises toward the palate for य.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; they feed the quiz distractors.

1. **Memorising the ten class signs as ten facts.** *Fix:* sort by what you see: a thematic -अ-
   stem (1, 4, 6, 10) or not (2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 9); the numbers stay on the card.
2. **Lengthening the अ of चर्, वद्: \*चार-, \*वाद-.** *Fix:* guṇa replaces an इक् vowel only (1.1.3),
   and चर्, वद् have none (§18 exercise 3, items 5–6); आ for अ would be vṛddhi (1.1.1), not guṇa.
   Their अ stays.
3. **Guṇa in class 6 (\*लेखति, \*क्षेपति).** *Fix:* class 6's sign श has no प्: why that blocks
   guṇa is lesson 3's (1.2.4 with 1.1.5, preview). लिखति and क्षिपति keep their इ.
4. **Taking गच्छ- etc. as "irregular".** *Fix:* two sūtras make them: 7.3.77 (गच्छ, इच्छ, यच्छ) and
   7.3.78 (पिब, तिष्ठ, पश्य, सीद …).
5. **The -इ- of स्वपिति taken for the 1sg ātmanepada ending इट् of 3.4.78.** *Fix:* it is the
   augment इट्, by 7.2.76; the same augment returns before स्य in lesson 5; the ending इट् only shares
   the name.
6. **Calling शृणोति a class-5 root.** *Fix:* its sign is class 5's, श्नु; the root श्रु is listed
   among the bhvādi roots (the Laghusiddhāntakaumudī) and gets श्नु by 3.1.74 (lesson 3).

### The class sign

> **According to Pāṇini, between the root and a sārvadhātuka ending stands a class sign**,
> विकरणः (vikaraṇaḥ). The sārvadhātuka affixes are the tiṅ endings and the ś-marked affixes
> (3.4.113). Before them, after the root, comes शप्, by 3.1.68; in the other classes a sign of their
> own stands in its place — in the tenth, before it — or none at all (the card). शप्'s श् and प् are markers (1.3.8, 1.3.3,
> 1.3.9): what remains is अ. Before शप् the aṅga's last sound, if it is an इक् vowel, takes guṇa
> (7.3.84, with 1.1.52, 1.1.3 and 1.1.50, and 1.1.51 for the र् of अर्); a light penultimate इक्
> vowel takes it too (7.3.86). A root with neither — चर्, वद्, whose only vowel is अ — keeps
> its vowel.

The English lines, padaccheda and bracket lines of the tools are in §8 Steps 3 and 6.
