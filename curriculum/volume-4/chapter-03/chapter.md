# Chapter 3 — धातुः तिङ्विस्तारश्च · Roots & the Full Verb

Chapter 2 opened the machine on nouns: a base, an affix after it, markers that vanish, and rules that
replace, add and delete. This chapter runs the same machine on the verb, starting from forms the
learner has said since Volume 1. The first lesson takes पठति apart: a root (धातुः) as the Dhātupāṭha
lists it (1.3.1), an abstract affix लट् for present time (3.2.123), and its ल् replaced by one of
eighteen endings (3.4.77–78) — the endings the learner already owns, now with their markers. Which set
of nine, parasmaipada or ātmanepada, is decided by the root's own marker (1.3.12, 1.3.72, 1.3.78); which
person and number, by the sentence (1.4.101–102, 1.4.108, 1.4.22). Volume 3's "second family of
endings" turns out to be Pāṇini's ātmanepada, करोति and कुरुते turn out to be one verb, and the 1.x
book's claim that the ātmanepada means "Action benefits oneself" is corrected in the open: that is true
only where the fruit of the action goes to the doer (पचते, यजते, कुरुते). The second lesson puts a class
sign (विकरणः) between root and ending (3.4.113, 3.1.68): शप् or one of its replacements, ten present
classes on one card, guṇa before the sign (7.3.84; which signs and endings block it is lesson 3's), and the stems Volume 3 called irregular —
गच्छ, तिष्ठ, पश्य, पिब — made by two sūtras (7.3.77, 7.3.78); नयति is derived step by step. The third
lesson explains शृणोति beside शृण्वन्ति: a sārvadhātuka ending or sign without the marker p counts as ṅ-marked (1.2.4), and
before such an ending guṇa does not apply (1.1.5); the athematic verbs learned whole in Volume 3 become
tables the learner can build, the markers met so far are collected on one card, and the 1.x card that
gave ङ् and क् different effects is corrected. The fourth lesson is the perfect: the root doubled
(6.1.8) and its own endings (3.4.82); जगाम and ददौ are derived, उवाच and आह become cells, the 1.x label
"remote, witnessed" is corrected by the sūtra's परोक्षे, and Whitney's statement of how the later
language uses the perfect is printed beside Pāṇini's. A passage of the Hitopadeśa (its opening, and Book 3's Vīravara) is read. The
fifth lesson is the future: the sign स्य, by 3.1.33, the augment इट् where a root takes it (7.2.35, 7.2.70,
7.2.58), and the ष of रामेषु once more; करिष्यति is derived, the four other lakāras are met on one card
for recognition, and मा भैषीः "do not be afraid" is the one aorist the learner uses. A passage of the
Pañcatantra is read. The sixth lesson is the Chapter 3 exam, one closed-book sitting of 45 minutes, on
the √भू table of the ten rows of the classical tense-moods. The serial "the monkey and the crocodile,
continued" runs through the chapter. Every quoted sūtra is printed as the data file gives it; every
commentary line is "text of the GRETIL e-text".

## Goals

- Take any owned finite verb back to its root as the Dhātupāṭha lists it, and say from the root's
  marker which set of endings it takes
- Classify every owned verb into its present class by its class sign, and form its present system
- Explain strong and weak stems by the p-marker, and build the athematic present, imperfect and
  imperative of the owned verbs
- Use the perfect (3rd persons) and the future; recognise and name the other four lakāras
- Assemble the √भू table of the classical tense-moods, ten rows, and tell it apart from the tradition's
  own count of ten l-affixes
- The 1.x errors corrected in the open (IR-019, IR-020, IR-022; the laṅ labels "recent past" and
  "past habitual"; "Action benefits oneself"); TRADITION and FACT side by side on the perfect, on आह and
  on the tenth class
- Read a short Hitopadeśa and a short Pañcatantra passage, printed joined, with marginal glosses

## Can-do statements

By the end of this chapter the learner can:

- [ ] Map the eighteen endings of 3.4.78 onto the present endings, naming the marker each one drops, if any
- [ ] Say, according to Pāṇini, whether an owned verb takes parasmaipada, ātmanepada or both, and why
- [ ] Name the class, class sign and present stem of any owned verb, and give the rule of the
      substitute stems (गच्छ, तिष्ठ, पश्य, पिब, सीद, जा) from the substitute-stem card
- [ ] Fill the strong and weak cells of the owned athematic verbs and say why, according to Pāṇini
- [ ] Derive, according to Pāṇini, in table form, पठति, रोचते, नयति, स्मरति, शृणोति, शृण्वन्ति, करोति,
      कुरुतः, जगाम, ददौ, करिष्यति and गमिष्यति
- [ ] Narrate in the 3rd person with the perfect, and say what will happen with the future
- [ ] Read 28 ledger-new words (565 cumulative; Volume 4 so far: 57)
- [ ] Pass the Chapter 3 exam in one 45-minute sitting

## Assessment

Lesson 6 is the Chapter 3 exam: one closed-book sitting of 45 minutes, 2 of them for instructions;
Station 0 (about 8 minutes) runs before it and outside it. A separate sheet prints the chapter's toolbox card
(89 sūtras in number order, each with its text and one English line); it is handed out at the start of task C and collected after it. **A** — the √भू
table: the eighteen 3rd-person cells of the six productive rows (laṭ, laṅ, loṭ, vidhiliṅ, liṭ, lṛṭ)
filled, and the four rows printed filled (luṭ, lṛṅ, luṅ, āśīrliṅ) named with their use, with no credit
for producing them; then 3.2.111 अनद्यतने लङ् read cold in the case code with its bracket line, and the
1.x labels "recent past" and "past habitual" corrected (11 min; 26 points, pass 19). **B** — class
detective: the class, class sign and present stem of twelve owned verbs, three of them traps (गच्छति,
पृच्छति, जानाति) (7 min; 36, pass 27). **C** — strong and weak: eight cells of शृणोति, करोति, अस्ति and
ब्रवीति, each with its reason "according to Pāṇini" (7 min; 16, pass 11). **D** — an unseen Hitopadeśa
dialogue (Vīravara and King Śūdraka, Book 3), printed joined with four glosses, and six questions,
among them the lakāra of उवाच and देहि, and of आह "according to Pāṇini" (10 min; 12, pass 8). **E** —
