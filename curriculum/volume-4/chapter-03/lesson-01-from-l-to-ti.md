## 1. Hook

You have said पठति since Volume 1 (v1.c06.l03) and रोचते since v2.c05.l03. Volume 3 sorted
your verbs into two families of endings — गच्छति and पठति in the first, रोचते and लभते in the
second — and told you to learn each verb's family with the word, as you learn a noun's gender
(v3.c03.l02). It also said that the verbs which take both families are Volume 4's. Here is the
first of them: करोति, yours since v2.c04.l03, has a second form, कुरुते.

Three questions, then. Why does रोचते take the second family and पठति the first? Why does
करोति take both? And where in पठति is the "present" — which part of the word says *now*?
Pāṇini answers all three with one small affix, ल्, and with a letter on the root.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — where today's rules stand in the grammar, and the list of roots they read.

Today's rules come from three places in the Aṣṭādhyāyī. 1.3.1, which names the root, and the
three rules that choose a verb's set of endings, 1.3.12, 1.3.72 and 1.3.78, stand in the third
quarter of Book 1; the names of the sets, persons and numbers — 1.4.99 to 1.4.102, 1.4.108 and
1.4.22 — in its fourth quarter. The l-affix is Book 3's: the heading 3.1.91, then 3.2.123 for
the present, then 3.4.77, 3.4.78 and 3.4.79 in the book's last quarter. As in Chapter 2, the
order of the grammar is not the order of a derivation: you will read a rule of Book 1, then of
Book 3, then of Book 1 again. The rules read a second text, too: the list of roots.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** The roots are listed in the Dhātupāṭha, the tradition's list of
> roots, each with a meaning and with markers that the rules read; the Kāśikā calls the word
> dhātu a name "of earlier teachers" (its line is in Teacher Notes). This course quotes the
> entries as the Laghusiddhāntakaumudī (c. 17th century) prints them; that text does not show the
> accents the markers carry. [REF:dhatupatha]; [REF:laghukaumudi]; [REF:kashika 1.3.1]

Each entry gives the root with its markers, then its meaning in the locative, "in the sense of
…". Today you meet seven; they are labels, read and not counted as words.

| Entry | IAST | Meaning | Root of |
|---|---|---|---|
| भू सत्तायाम् | bhū sattāyām | "in the sense of being" | भवति — the root has no marker |
| पठ व्यक्तायां वाचि | paṭha vyaktāyāṃ vāci | "in clear speech" | पठति — quoted by the Laghusiddhāntakaumudī as an example root, outside its main list |
| रुच दीप्तावभिप्रीतौ च | ruca dīptāv abhiprītau ca | "in shining and in pleasing" | रोचते |
| डुकृञ् करणे | ḍukṛñ karaṇe | "in doing" | करोति |
| डुपचष् पाके | ḍupacaṣ pāke | "in cooking" | पचति |
| यज देवपूजासङ्गतिकरणदानेषु | yaja devapūjāsaṅgatikaraṇadāneṣu | "in worship of the gods, in bringing together, in giving" | यजति |
| णीञ् प्रापणे | ṇīñ prāpaṇe | "in leading" | नयति — why its ण् is न् in the verb is lesson 2's |

The markers are part of each entry: the डु and the ञ् of डुकृञ्; the ञ् of णीञ्; the डु and the ष्
of डुपचष्; and a final अ in पठ, रुच, यज and in the पच of डुपचष्. That अ is nasal, and so a marker —
the tradition says so, as it says of the उ of सुँ in c02.l01; the Laghusiddhāntakaumudī prints no
nasal sign over it, and neither does this course.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — the same parts named from outside the tradition: root, present-stem,
ending.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Whitney describes the finite verb by its personal ending, appended to a
> tense-stem "rather than to the pure root" [REF:whitney1889 §541]; in his chapter on the present
> he writes: "In a small minority of verbs, the present-stem is identical with the root."
> [REF:whitney1889 §602]

So for Whitney पठति is a root, पठ्, made into a present-stem, पठ-, with an ending, -ति. The
tradition has the same root, its dhātu; the stem is lesson 2's; today's lesson is the ending.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Whitney prints the endings of the present as one table of "Primary
> Endings", the active and the middle side by side, each in three persons and three numbers:
> eighteen cells, with the 1st person in the top row, from the active singular *mi* to the middle
> plural *máhe* (the acute
> is his accent mark; accent is Volume 5's). [REF:whitney1889 §553]

Line his table up with 3.4.78 and the eighteen are the same. His active column holds the first
nine of the sūtra with their markers gone — तिप्, तस्, झि … as -ति, -तः, -अन्ति …; his middle
column holds the last nine as the present has them, with the -ए of 3.4.79 (§8 Step 5) — त,
आताम्, झ … as -ते, -आते, -अन्ते …. Two differences are only of writing. Whitney starts from the
1st person, where the tradition starts from the 3rd; and he writes the final -s that the course
prints as visarga: his -tas is your -तः of पठतः.

What Sanskrit does here you can see in your own forms. It marks on the verb both the person and
number of the doer and one of two sets of endings: पठति and लभते differ in their set, पठति and
पठामि in their person, पठति and पठन्ति in their number. The tradition calls the two sets
parasmaipada and ātmanepada; Whitney's table calls them active and middle. Why a verb has the
set it has is what the tradition explains today, from the root.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Whitney renders the two names as "a word for another" (parasmaipada) and "a
> word for one's self" (ātmanepada), and adds that "in the recorded condition of the language,
> however, the antithesis of transitive and reflexive meaning is in no small measure blurred, or
> even altogether effaced". [REF:whitney1889 §529]

So the sense of the two names, for another and for oneself, cannot tell you a verb's set; the
tradition finds the set in the root's marker (§8 Step 4).

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the day's idea in four lines, each printed joined, its pause form beneath,
then the gloss.

> पठेति धातुः, पठतीति तिङन्तं पदम्। (paṭheti dhātuḥ, paṭhatīti tiṅantaṃ padam.)
> पठ इति धातुः, पठति इति तिङन्तम् पदम्। (paṭha iti dhātuḥ, paṭhati iti tiṅantam padam.)
> "paṭha is the root; paṭhati is a word ending in a tiṅ ending." — पठ धातु है; पठति तिङन्त पद है।

> रुचेति धातुः, रोचत इति रूपम्। (ruceti dhātuḥ, rocata iti rūpam.)
> रुच इति धातुः, रोचते इति रूपम्। (ruca iti dhātuḥ, rocate iti rūpam.)
> "ruca is the root, rocate the form." — रुच धातु है, रोचते रूप।

> तवाभिप्रायः कः? (tavābhiprāyaḥ kaḥ?)
> तव अभिप्रायः कः? (tava abhiprāyaḥ kaḥ?)
> "What is your intention?" — तुम्हारा अभिप्राय क्या है?

> कुरुत इत्यात्मनेपदम्, करोतीति परस्मैपदम्। (kuruta ity ātmanepadam, karotīti parasmaipadam.)
> कुरुते इति आत्मनेपदम्, करोति इति परस्मैपदम्। (kurute iti ātmanepadam, karoti iti parasmaipadam.)
> "kurute is ātmanepada, karoti parasmaipada." — कुरुते आत्मनेपद है, करोति परस्मैपद।

These are model sentences 1, 4 and 5 and the रुच line; model sentences 2, 3 and 6 are in §13.
Today's headwords stand as free words: धातुः "root" and अभिप्रायः "intention"; रूपम् "form" is
yours since v1.c05.l04. तिङन्तम् "ending in a tiṅ" is a label, and so are the names of the two
sets, परस्मैपदम् and आत्मनेपदम्: you use them, they are not counted as words. कुरुते is a display
form: the second-set cells of करोति are lesson 3's.

The joins are Volume 3's: पठ इति → पठेति and रुच इति → रुचेति — अ + इ → ए; पठति इति → पठतीति
and करोति इति → करोतीति — two i-vowels, one long; तिङन्तम् पदम् → तिङन्तं पदम् — म् → ं before a
consonant; रोचते इति → रोचत इति and कुरुते इति → कुरुत इति — -ए before a vowel → -अ and a gap;
इति आत्मनेपदम् → इत्यात्मनेपदम् — इ before a vowel → य्; तव अभिप्रायः → तवाभिप्रायः — two
a-vowels, one long. A comma and a daṇḍa stop every seam: धातुः and आत्मनेपदम् stand in their pause
form before the comma. The quoted forms पठ, रुच, कुरुते, करोति are joined to the इति after them,
as Chapter 1 taught.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

पठति आप पहले खण्ड से बोल रहे हैं, और रोचते दूसरे खण्ड से। तीसरे खण्ड ने बताया था कि क्रिया

**ल् के अठारह आदेश।** यह ल् बने हुए शब्द में कभी दिखता नहीं। सूत्र 3.4.77 लस्य और 3.4.78 के
अनुसार उसके स्थान पर अठारह में से कोई एक प्रत्यय आता है — तिप्, तस्, झि, सिप्, थस्, थ, मिप्,
वस्, मस्, और फिर त, आताम्, झ, थास्, आथाम्, ध्वम्, इट्, वहि, महिङ्। घबराइए नहीं: ये सब आपके
पुराने परिचित हैं। पठति का -ति तिप् है, पठामि का -मि मिप्, लभते का -ते त। नया केवल इतना है कि
पाँच प्रत्ययों के साथ इत् लगा है: तिप्, सिप्, मिप् का प्, इट् का ट् और महिङ् का ङ्। ये हट जाते
हैं। तस्, थस्, वस्, मस् और थास् का अन्तिम स्, और आताम्, आथाम्, ध्वम् का अन्तिम म् इत् नहीं हैं —
प्रत्यय का अन्तिम स् और म् बना रहता है, यह आप दूसरे अध्याय में देख चुके हैं। तिप् से महिङ् के
ङ् तक का पूरा समूह **तिङ्** कहलाता है — ठीक वैसे, जैसे सुँ से सुप् के प् तक सुप्।

**नौ और नौ।** सूत्र 1.4.99 के अनुसार पहले नौ प्रत्ययों का नाम **परस्मैपद** है, और 1.4.100 के
अनुसार बाद के नौ का **आत्मनेपद**। सूत्र 1.4.101 हर नौ को तीन-तीन के तीन समूहों में बाँटता है —
**प्रथम पुरुष**, **मध्यम पुरुष**, **उत्तम पुरुष** — और 1.4.102 हर समूह के तीन प्रत्ययों को क्रम
से **एकवचन**, **द्विवचन**, **बहुवचन** कहता है। ध्यान रहे: परम्परा का प्रथम पुरुष "वह" है,
"मैं" नहीं; "मैं" उत्तम पुरुष है। सूत्र 1.4.108 के अनुसार, जब कर्ता न "तुम" हो, न "मैं", तब
प्रथम पुरुष; और 1.4.22 के अनुसार कर्ता एक हो तो एकवचन, दो हों तो द्विवचन।

**कौन-सा परिवार? धातु का इत् तय करता है।** इसका उत्तर क्रिया के अर्थ में नहीं, धातु में है।
धातुपाठ धातु को उसके इत् के साथ लिखता है — भू जैसी कुछ धातुओं का कोई इत् नहीं होता — और
तीन सूत्र तय करते हैं कि कौन-सा परिवार आएगा:

- **1.3.12** — जिस धातु का इत् ङ् हो, या जिसके इत्-स्वर पर वह स्वराघात हो जिसका नाम सूत्र का
  पहला पद लेता है, उसके बाद केवल आत्मनेपद। रुच ऐसी धातु है, इसलिए रोचते।
- **1.3.72** — जिस धातु का इत् ञ् हो, या जिसके इत्-स्वर पर वह दूसरा स्वराघात हो जिसका नाम यह
  सूत्र लेता है, उसके बाद आत्मनेपद तब, जब क्रिया का फल कर्ता को मिले; फल किसी और को मिले, तो
  परस्मैपद।
- **1.3.78** — बाकी सब धातुओं के बाद परस्मैपद। पठ ऐसी धातु है, इसलिए पठति।

स्वराघात धातुपाठ में दर्ज है, पर इस पाठ्यक्रम का धातुपाठ — लघुसिद्धान्तकौमुदी का पाठ — उसे
नहीं दिखाता; स्वराघात पाँचवें खण्ड का विषय है। इसलिए हम हर धातु के साथ यह बताते हैं कि
लघुसिद्धान्तकौमुदी उसे किस समूह में रखती है: रुच को आत्मनेपदी धातुओं में, पच को उभयपदी
धातुओं में।

**उभयपदी धातु।** जिस धातु के बाद दोनों परिवार आ सकें, वह **उभयपदी** कहलाती है। कृ धातुपाठ में
डुकृञ् है; उसका ञ् उसे 1.3.72 के अधीन लाता है: फल कर्ता का हो तो कुरुते, नहीं तो करोति। पच्
और यज् भी उभयपदी हैं। माँ अपने लिए खाना पकाती है — पचते; बेटे के लिए पकाती है — पचति। काशिका
का उदाहरण भी यही है: यजमान अपने फल के लिए यज्ञ करता है — यजते; पुरोहित दूसरे के लिए यज्ञ करते
हैं, दक्षिणा लेकर — यजन्ति।

**पहली पुस्तक की भूल।** संस्कृतम् 1.x के एक कार्ड ने लिखा था कि आत्मनेपद का अर्थ है "Action
benefits oneself", यानी क्रिया का लाभ स्वयं को; और कृ को एक जगह "ONLY parasmaipada" वाली
धातुओं में गिना, तो अगले पृष्ठ पर उभयपदी में। कृ उभयपदी है — अपने ञ् के कारण। "अपने लिए"
वाला अर्थ केवल वहाँ है जहाँ 1.3.72 लगता है: कुरुते, पचते, यजते। रोचते, लभते, मन्यते और जायते
1.3.12 से आत्मनेपद हैं — इत् के कारण; क्रिया का फल किसे मिलता है, इससे इनका परिवार नहीं
बदलता। मह्यं फलं रोचते में कर्ता फल है, और अच्छा लगने से फल का कोई लाभ नहीं।

## 8. English Explanation

The recipe of this volume, once more: forms you own, the question they raise, the tools that
answer it, the derivation with the teacher, the tools run by you, then production. Today's one
grammar point: according to Pāṇini, every finite verb begins as a root (धातुः) plus an abstract
affix ल्, whose name says the time or mood (3.2.123); the ल् is replaced by one of eighteen
endings (3.4.77–78); which set, parasmaipada or ātmanepada, is decided by the root's own marker
in the Dhātupāṭha (1.3.12, 1.3.72, 1.3.78), and which person and number by the sentence
(1.4.101–102, 1.4.108, 1.4.22).

You have also taken one step on trust. In Chapter 2 (c02.l02) the ल् of अगच्छत् became तिप्,
marked "3.4.78 [preview: c03.l01]"; in its lesson 3 सिप् stood behind करोषि and मस् behind
अगच्छाम, with the same mark. Today is that lesson.

### Step 2 — the question

**Where does a verb's set of endings come from?** Volume 3's answer was the dictionary:
"the dictionary says which family a verb takes: Apte marks P. (parasmaipada), A. (ātmanepada) or
U. (both) after the root" (v3.c03.l02). That is true, and it is what you will go on doing with a
new verb. But the dictionary records a result. Pāṇini states the cause, and the cause is in the
root itself: a marker letter in its Dhātupāṭha entry. And one more question waits inside पठति:
the ending -ति says "he, she, it" — but, according to Pāṇini, not "now": the same ending, तिप्,
stands behind the optative भवेत्, as you will see. Where, then, is the present?

| | 3rd · प्रथमः | 2nd · मध्यमः | 1st · उत्तमः |
|---|---|---|---|

None of them is new to you: -ति, -तः, -अन्ति, -सि, -थ, -मि, -मः are the first family's endings,
-ते, -एते, -अन्ते, -से, -ध्वे, -ए, -महे the second's (v3.c03.l01–l02). What is new is the
markers, and you have the line for them already (c02.l01): the final स् and म् of an ending are
kept, so तस्, थस्, वस्, मस्, थास् keep their स् and आताम्, आथाम्, ध्वम् their म्. Only five lose a
marker: the प् of तिप्, सिप्, मिप्; the ट् of इट्; the ङ् of महिङ् [1.3.3, 1.3.9]. In झि and झ the
झ् becomes अन्त् by a rule in Teacher Notes (lesson 3 prints it). §15 maps all eighteen, cell by
cell; exercise 1 has you do it.

same ending, its इ dropped as in अगच्छत्, after the optative's own sign (Teacher Notes). For him
the ending is the same in the present and in the optative, so the present is not in the ending:
it is in लट्. Described from outside, the ending does share that work: Whitney writes that it
fixes person and number "and in part also" mode and tense, which "are mainly made by the
formation of tense and mode-stems" — his primary -ti, used in the present indicative, in पठति;
his secondary -t, used in the optative, in भवेत् [REF:whitney1889 §541]; [REF:whitney1889 §551];

tells them apart is the accent the Dhātupāṭha records on them. Accent is Volume 5's, and the
course does not show it: the Laghusiddhāntakaumudī, the text this course quotes, prints no
accents. So the table cites the group the Laghusiddhāntakaumudī puts each root in: it lists its
roots in runs, closed by "iti parasmaipadinaḥ" and "ityātmanepadinaḥ", and a third run for the
roots of both sets, where पच, नी and यज stand. bhvādi and tanādi are class names, lesson 2's. The
pada card of §15 gives twenty owned verbs their set, each as Apte marks it: the Dhātupāṭha
records the marker, the dictionary records the result.

**The 1.x card, corrected in the open.** The 1.x book's card on the two sets (p. 75) said that
the ātmanepada means "Action benefits oneself", and listed कृ among the roots that take "ONLY

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — the sounds inside today's rules: a derivation read aloud with its markers named and
not pronounced, the eighteen recited in their threes, and the -ति/-ते contrast. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-4/v4c03l01-derivation-read.mp3` (drills 1–3, each item twice).

**Drill 1 — the पठति table, read aloud.** Name each marker; do not pronounce it in the result.

1. "पठ — its final अ is a marker; what is left is पठ्."
2. "लट् — the अ and the ट् are markers; what is left is ल्."
3. "तिप् — the प् is a marker; what you hear is ति."
4. "पठ् + अ + ति — पठति."

The name तिप् /t̪ip/ is said with its प्; the ending you hear is ति /t̪i/. The name लट् /lɐʈ/ is
said whole; in the word it is ल् /l/, and then not even that: ति has replaced it.

**Drill 2 — the eighteen in their threes.** The tradition recites a list as it is set out, in
groups; say each three in one breath:

1. तिप् तस् झि /t̪ip t̪ɐs d͡ʑʱi/
2. सिप् थस् थ /sip t̪ʰɐs t̪ʰɐ/
3. मिप् वस् मस् /mip ʋɐs mɐs/
4. त आताम् झ /t̪ɐ ɑːt̪ɑːm d͡ʑʱɐ/
5. थास् आथाम् ध्वम् /t̪ʰɑːs ɑːt̪ʰɑːm d̪ʱʋɐm/
6. इट् वहि महिङ् /iʈ ʋɐɦi mɐɦiŋ/

Then read the sūtra itself, joined, as it is printed: the list runs on, and you hear मिब्वस् and
इड्वहि, the stops voiced before व, and the anusvāra of तातां and थासाथां said as the nasal of the
stop after it.

**Drill 3 — -ति and -ते.** Say each pair slowly, then at speed:

1. पचति /pɐt͡ɕɐt̪i/ — पचते /pɐt͡ɕɐt̪eː/
2. यजति /jɐd͡ʑɐt̪i/ — यजते /jɐd͡ʑɐt̪eː/
3. करोति /kɐɾoːt̪i/ — कुरुते /kuɾut̪eː/

In the first two pairs the whole difference is the last vowel: a short /i/ against a long /eː/;
in the third the stem changes too (करो-, कुरु-: lesson 3). ए is a pure long vowel,
held twice as long as the इ and steady to its end (§11).

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the sounds that tell पठति from पचते. The from-ल्-to-ति image
(`planned:assets/images/volume-4/v4c03l01-from-l-to-ti.svg`) shows the two rows of nine; say each
ending as you point to it.

1. **ठ in पठति.** The tip of the tongue curls back behind the ridge and releases with a puff of
   breath: /ʈʰ/. The त of -ति, a moment later, is made with the tip on the back of the upper
   teeth: /t̪/. Feel the tip travel forward between the two.
2. **च in पचति, ज in यजति.** The blade of the tongue presses the hard palate and releases with a
   short hiss: /t͡ɕ/, voiceless; /d͡ʑ/, the same with the voice on.
3. **-इ and -ए.** For the -इ of -ति the lips are spread and the tongue high and front, short. For
   the -ए of -ते the jaw drops a little, the tongue stays front, and the vowel is held twice as
   long, with no glide at its end: /eː/, not English "hey".

Mirror check: say पचति पचते पचति पचते. Only the last vowel moves: watch the jaw open slightly for
-ते and hold.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; they feed the quiz distractors.

1. **"Ātmanepada = for oneself" for every second-set verb — the 1.x card.** *Fix:* only under
   1.3.72 (कुरुते, पचते, यजते); रोचते, लभते are ātmanepada by 1.3.12, because of a marker,
   whoever gains by the action.
2. **Calling पठति "first person" because the tradition's list starts with it.** *Fix:* प्रथमः is
   the tradition's name for he, she, it; "I" is उत्तमः — 1.4.101, 1.4.108.
3. **Learning the eighteen as a new table.** *Fix:* they are your present endings with their
   markers; only the markers are new (exercise 1).
4. **Dropping the स् of तस् or the म् of आताम् as markers.** *Fix:* a final स् or म् of an ending
   is kept (c02.l01's ending-marker line; its rule: Teacher Notes); the markers are प्, ट्, ङ्.
5. **Writing \*पठते for "he reads for himself".** *Fix:* पठ has no marker for the second set:
   parasmaipada only (1.3.78); only a root that 1.3.72 covers — by its marker vowel or by ञ् —
   has the choice.
6. **Reading 3.2.123's वर्तमाने as "before the present".** *Fix:* a locative of meaning, "for
   present time" — like 1.3.72's कर्त्रभिप्राये.

### The Kāśikā's three pairs

According to the Kāśikā, on 1.3.72, the second set is used when the main fruit of the action
goes to the doer — "heaven and the rest", for one who sacrifices — and the first set when it does
not. It gives three pairs, read here in IAST as display: *yajate* "he sacrifices [for his own
fruit]" against *yajanti yājakāḥ* "the priests sacrifice"; *pacate* against *pacanti pācakāḥ*
"the cooks cook"; *kurute* against *kurvanti karmakarāḥ* "the workmen work". The priests, cooks
and workmen earn a fee or a wage, the Kāśikā adds; but they do not begin the action for its
sake, so the first set. [REF:kashika 1.3.72]

> "Mother cooks food for her son, and cooks for her own sake." — माँ बेटे के लिए खाना पकाती है,
> और अपने लिए भी पकाती है।

> ऋषयो देवान्यजन्ति। (ṛṣayo devān yajanti.)
> ऋषयः देवान् यजन्ति। (ṛṣayaḥ devān yajanti.)
> "The seers worship the gods." — ऋषि देवताओं की पूजा करते हैं।

> सिंहाद्भयं जायते। (siṃhād bhayaṃ jāyate.)
> सिंहात् भयम् जायते। (siṃhāt bhayam jāyate.)
> "Fear arises from the lion." — शेर से डर पैदा होता है।

Sentence 2 is the 1.3.72 contrast in one line: पचति for her son, पचते for herself. स्वार्थम्
"for one's own sake" is स्व + अर्थम्, two owned words — स्वः "own" [v3.c05.l02] and अर्थः — used as
an adverb, like your किमर्थम् "for what purpose, why"; its pause form shows the member seam with a
hyphen. In sentence 3 the seers act for others, as the Kāśikā's priests do, so the first set:
यजन्ति; for themselves it would be यजन्ते, by 1.3.72. Sentence 6 has the owned जायते, ātmanepada by
1.3.12 like रोचते, and भयम् with the ablative of what it comes from. The joins: भोजनम् पचति →
भोजनं पचति, स्वार्थम् च → स्वार्थं च, भयम् जायते → भयं जायते — म् → ं before a consonant; स्व
अर्थम् → स्वार्थम् — two a-vowels, one long; ऋषयः देवान् → ऋषयो देवान् — -अः before a voiced
consonant → ओ; देवान् यजन्ति → देवान्यजन्ति — न् written together with the य after it; सिंहात् भयम् →
सिंहाद्भयम् — त् → द् before a voiced sound. The comma of sentence 2 stops the seam: पचति stands as
it is before it.

### The anatomy of a verb

> **According to Pāṇini, a finite verb is a root plus an l-affix.** The root (धातुः) is an entry
> of the Dhātupāṭha (1.3.1); after it (3.1.91) comes an affix whose name begins with ल — **लट्**
> for present time (3.2.123). Its markers dropped, **ल्** is replaced (3.4.77) by one of
> **eighteen** endings (3.4.78), the tiṅ. **Which set:** the root's marker decides — ātmanepada
> by 1.3.12; both, by 1.3.72, ātmanepada when the fruit goes to the doer; parasmaipada for the
> rest, by 1.3.78. **Which person and number:** the sentence decides (1.4.101–102, 1.4.108,
> 1.4.22). In laṭ the ātmanepada endings take -ए, by 3.4.79.

The English line, padaccheda and bracket line of each tool are in §8 Steps 3–5.

## 20. Revision

पुनरावृत्तिः — one screen to remember.

> **From ल् to -ति.** According to Pāṇini, a verb is a **root** (धातुः, 1.3.1) and, after it
> (3.1.91), an **l-affix**: **लट्** for present time (3.2.123), its अ and ट् markers. **In place of
> ल्**, by 3.4.77, comes one of **eighteen** (3.4.78): तिप् तस् झि · सिप् थस् थ · मिप् वस् मस् · त
> आताम् झ · थास् आथाम् ध्वम् · इट् वहि महिङ् — your own present endings; the markers that drop: the
> प् of तिप्, सिप्, मिप्, the ट् of इट्, the ङ् of महिङ्. **Which set:** the root's marker — Ā by
> 1.3.12 (रोचते); both by 1.3.72, Ā when the fruit goes to the doer (कुरुते, पचते, यजते); P for the
> rest by 1.3.78 (पठति, भवति). The first nine are parasmaipada (1.4.99), the last nine ātmanepada
> (1.4.100). **Which person:** prathama when the doer is neither "you" nor "I" (1.4.101, 1.4.108) —
> the tradition's prathama is your third person. **Which number:** one — singular, two — dual
> (1.4.102, 1.4.22). **laṭ's -ए:** 3.4.79. **The 1.x card:** कृ takes both; "Action benefits
> oneself" holds only under 1.3.72. **The ledger:** five new — 542; cells drilled: 18; tools
> added: 15; toolbox: 58.

**What students find hard.** The names: प्रथमः sounds like "first person", and the tradition's
list does start with it. Say "प्रथमः — he, she, it" every time it comes up, and never let "first"
stand alone. The second hard point is the 1.x gloss, which learners of the 1.x book bring with
them: ask for a reason why रोचते or जायते would be "for oneself", and let the question fail.
Hindi-medium learners may hear -ते as the Hindi plural "-ते हैं": Sanskrit -ते is a singular.
