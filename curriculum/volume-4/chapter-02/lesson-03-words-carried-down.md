## 1. Hook

Three forms you own: देवेन, रामेण, रामान्. Two have न, one has ण. Volume 3 wrote of the ण of
रामेण: "an observed spelling; the rule behind it waits for Volume 4" (v3.c01.l04). And in
Volume 2, beside गच्छसि, the ष of करोषि "comes with the whole word; the reason waits for
Volume 4" (v2.c04.l03).

Today both reasons arrive — and with them a new way of reading. The rule that turns the न of
रामेन into ण has four words. The rule that lets it happen across the म is one long compound and
the word अपि, and in it there is no न, no ण and no र at all. Where are they? A sūtra does not
always say everything it means.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — where today's rules stand in the grammar, and how the commentators mark a
word that has come down from above.

Today's rules stand at the far end of the Aṣṭādhyāyī. 8.4.1, 8.4.2 and 8.4.37 are in the last
quarter of the eighth and last book; 8.3.55, 8.3.57 and 8.3.59 in the quarter before it. 6.4.1
stands at the head of the last quarter of Book 6, and 7.1.12 and 7.3.103 in Book 7. The order is
not the order of the textbook: rules stand in groups, and a rule can lend its words to the
rules after it. A rule further down the list may then be very short — 8.4.37 is one word,
पदान्तस्य — because the rest of what it means stands in rules above it.

The commentators write that way of reading down.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** The Kāśikā marks a word that continues from an earlier rule with
> phrases such as *iti vartate*, "is still in force". Under 8.3.59 it writes *mūrdhanyaḥ iti
> vartate, sa iti ca*: "mūrdhanyaḥ continues, and saḥ". In Chapter 6 you will find such words
> yourself; until then the course prints them in the bracket line. [REF:kashika 8.3.59]

The phrase is quoted in IAST only, inside an English sentence: whole commentary lines are
Chapter 6's. Some rules do more than lend a word to the next one. According to the Kāśikā,
6.4.1 अङ्गस्य is a heading that stands over every rule to the end of Book 7 [REF:kashika
6.4.1]: whatever those rules do, they do to the aṅga. You have met headings already — lesson
1's 3.1.1 and 3.1.2, lesson 2's 6.1.84; today the tradition's name for them, अधिकारः, becomes a
word you use.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — the same two changes described from outside the tradition, as laws of
the tongue.

Whitney, describing the language, states the ṇ change as one law:

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "The dental nasal न् n, when immediately followed by a vowel or by न् n
> or म् m or य् y or व् v, is turned into the lingual ण् ṇ if preceded in the same word by …
> ष् ṣ, र् r, or ऋ ṛ or ॠ ṝ … unless … there intervene … a palatal (except य् y), a lingual, or a
> dental." [REF:whitney1889 §189]

His reason, in one sentence of ours: the tip of the tongue, once curled back for a retroflex
sound, tends to stay there for the next nasal, unless a sound that moves the front of the
tongue comes between [REF:whitney1889 §189]. That is why रथेन keeps न — थ is a dental, and the
tongue has to come forward to the teeth for it — and why रामेण does not: the म is said with
the lips, and the tongue stays curled.

The ṣ change is a second law:

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "The dental sibilant स् s is changed to the lingual ष् ṣ, if immediately
> preceded by any vowel save अ a and आ ā, or by क् k or र् r—unless the स् s be final, or
> followed by र् r." [REF:whitney1889 §180]

So रामेषु has ष after ए, and बालिकासु keeps स after आ.

Set the two accounts side by side. Whitney states each change as a tendency of the tongue (FACT
about the language). Pāṇini states each as rules with words carried down from rule to rule
(TRADITION). One detail of Whitney's wording matters for the tradition's account too: the न्
changes "when immediately followed by a vowel or by न् n or म् m or य् y or व् v". A न् that
ends a word is followed by none of these, so the final न् of रामान् stays. The tradition says
the same in its own way, with a rule of one word: 8.4.37.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the day's idea in four lines, each printed joined, its pause form beneath,
then the gloss.

> रामेणेत्यत्र रेफो णत्वस्य निमित्तम्। (rāmeṇety atra repho ṇatvasya nimittam.)
> रामेण इति अत्र रेफः णत्वस्य निमित्तम्। (rāmeṇa iti atra rephaḥ ṇatvasya nimittam.)
> "In rāmeṇa the r is the cause of the ṇ." — रामेण में रेफ णत्व का निमित्त है।

> अङ्गस्येत्यधिकारः। (aṅgasyety adhikāraḥ.)
> अङ्गस्य इति अधिकारः। (aṅgasya iti adhikāraḥ.)
> "'aṅgasya' is a heading." — 'अङ्गस्य' अधिकार है।

> पूर्वसूत्रात्पदान्यनुवर्तन्ते। (pūrvasūtrāt padāny anuvartante.)
> पूर्व-सूत्रात् पदानि अनुवर्तन्ते। (pūrva-sūtrāt padāni anuvartante.)
> "Words continue from the previous sūtra." — पूर्व सूत्र से पद अनुवृत्त होते हैं।

> अत्र न इति पदस्यानुवृत्तिः। (atra na iti padasyānuvṛttiḥ.)
> अत्र नः इति पदस्य अनुवृत्तिः। (atra naḥ iti padasya anuvṛttiḥ.)
> "Here the word naḥ is carried down." — यहाँ 'नः' पद की अनुवृत्ति है।

These are model sentences 1–3 and the अनुवृत्तिः line; model sentences 4–6 are in §13. Today's
headwords stand as free words: निमित्तम् "cause", अधिकारः "heading", अनुवृत्तिः "carrying
down". रेफः is the tradition's name for the sound र, a label like the -कार names; णत्वम्, "the
ṇ-change", is a glossed term, a label too. अनुवर्तन्ते, "they continue", is display: a verb of
the same root as अनुवृत्तिः, read with its gloss, not taught. पूर्वसूत्रम् is a compound of two
owned words, पूर्वः [v4.c01.l03] and सूत्रम् [v4.c01.l01], so its pause form shows the member
seam with a hyphen.

The joins are Volume 3's: रामेण इति → रामेणेति — अ + इ → ए; इति अत्र → इत्यत्र — इ before a
vowel → य्; रेफः णत्वस्य → रेफो णत्वस्य — -अः before a voiced consonant → ओ; अङ्गस्य इति →
अङ्गस्येति; इति अधिकारः → इत्यधिकारः; पूर्वसूत्रात् पदानि → पूर्वसूत्रात्पदानि — a consonant-final
word written together with the next; पदानि अनुवर्तन्ते → पदान्यनुवर्तन्ते; नः इति → न इति — -अः
before a vowel other than अ → -अ and a gap; पदस्य अनुवृत्तिः → पदस्यानुवृत्तिः — two a-vowels, one
long. A daṇḍa stops every seam. The quoted forms रामेण, अङ्गस्य and नः are joined to the इति
after them, as Chapter 1 taught; अङ्गस्य is a sūtra, printed as the data file has it.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

देवेन और रामेण — दोनों "से" वाले रूप हैं, दोनों में एक ही प्रत्यय है, फिर भी एक में न है और दूसरे
में ण। तीसरे खण्ड ने रामेण के ण को "देखी हुई वर्तनी" कहकर छोड़ दिया था, और दूसरे खण्ड ने
करोषि के ष के बारे में कहा था कि उसका कारण चौथे खण्ड में मिलेगा। आज दोनों के नियम मिलते
हैं — और साथ में सूत्र पढ़ने का एक नया ढंग भी।

**अनुवृत्ति।** पाणिनि का कोई सूत्र अक्सर अकेले में अधूरा होता है। उसके कुछ पद ऊपर के सूत्र
से नीचे चले आते हैं — इसी को **अनुवृत्ति** कहते हैं। उदाहरण लीजिए। सूत्र 8.4.1 रषाभ्यां नो णः
समानपदे कहता है: र् या ष् के बाद, एक ही पद के भीतर, न् के स्थान पर ण्। अगला सूत्र 8.4.2
अट्कुप्वाङ्नुम्व्यवायेऽपि कहता है: "बीच में अट् वर्ण, कवर्ग, पवर्ग, आङ् या नुम् हों, तब भी।"
पर इस सूत्र में न र है, न ष, न न, न ण — ये सब 8.4.1 से नीचे आते हैं। रामेण में र और न के
बीच आ, म् और ए हैं: आ और ए अट् में हैं, म् पवर्ग में — इसलिए ण बनता है। रथेन में बीच में थ
है, जो तवर्ग का है; 8.4.2 उसकी छूट नहीं देता, इसलिए न बना रहता है। देवेन में र या ष है ही नहीं।
और रामान् में न् पद के अन्त में है: सूत्र 8.4.37 पदान्तस्य कहता है कि पद के अन्त का न् ण् नहीं
बनता। इस छोटे-से सूत्र में "नहीं" शब्द भी ऊपर से आया है।

**अधिकार।** कुछ सूत्र शीर्षक की तरह होते हैं — वे अपने नीचे के बहुत-से सूत्रों पर लागू रहते हैं।
इन्हें **अधिकार** कहते हैं। सूत्र 6.4.1 अङ्गस्य ऐसा ही अधिकार है: यहाँ से सातवें अध्याय के अन्त
तक हर सूत्र अङ्ग के बारे में है। और 8.3.55 अपदान्तस्य मूर्धन्यः भी: उस पाद के अन्त तक, पद के
अन्त में न हो तो, स् के स्थान पर मूर्धन्य।

**णत्व और षत्व।** न का ण बनना **णत्व** कहलाता है, स का ष बनना **षत्व**। दोनों के निमित्त अलग
हैं — यह याद रखिए:

- णत्व के निमित्त: र, ष — और काशिका के अनुसार ऋ भी — पितॄणाम्, मातॄणाम्। कोई और स्वर नहीं।
- षत्व के निमित्त: इण् प्रत्याहार के वर्ण — अ और आ को छोड़कर हर स्वर, और ह य व र ल — और कवर्ग।
  सूत्र 8.3.59 के अनुसार प्रत्यय का या आदेश का स् इनके बाद ष् बनता है, अगर वह पद के अन्त में
  न हो। रामेषु में ए के बाद, करोषि में ओ के बाद — ष। बालिकासु में आ के बाद — स, क्योंकि आ इण् में
  नहीं है।

करोषि का ष "कृ" धातु से नहीं आता: वह -सि प्रत्यय का स् है, जो ओ के बाद ष बन गया। शृणोषि में भी
यही बात है।

**हिन्दी में।** हिन्दी ने बहुत-से संस्कृत शब्द ज्यों-के-त्यों, उनके संस्कृत रूप में लिए हैं
[REF:kachru2006]। इसलिए कारण, हरण और मरण का ण — र, फिर केवल स्वर, फिर न, एक ही शब्द में:
8.4.1–2 — और भविष्य का ष — इ के बाद प्रत्यय स्य का स्: 8.3.59 — हिन्दी में शब्द के साथ बने-बनाए
आए हैं। जिस नियम ने इन्हें बनाया, वह संस्कृत का नियम है। ध्यान रहे: रामायण और प्रमाण इसके
उदाहरण नहीं हैं — उनके र और न एक ही पद में नहीं हैं, और उनका ण दूसरे नियम बनाते हैं।

आज के पाँच शब्द: **अनुवृत्ति** — पद का आगे के सूत्रों में चलते रहना; **अधिकार** — व्याकरण में
शीर्षक-सूत्र, और रोज़ की हिन्दी में हक़ या अधिकार भी; **समान** — एक-सा, बराबर; **निमित्त** —
कारण, वजह; और **कारण** — वजह; इसका अपना ण भी णत्व से है: र, फिर अ, फिर न।

## 8. English Explanation

The recipe of this volume, once more: forms you own, the question they raise, the tools that
answer it, the derivations with the teacher, the tools run by you, then production. Today's
one grammar point: a sūtra is often incomplete on its own — words of earlier sūtras continue
into it (anuvṛtti), and some sūtras are headings that stand over a long stretch (adhikāra). The
material is Volume 3's unfinished business: the ṇ and the ṣ inside a word.

### Step 1 — the forms you own

Your four §3 prompts gave these:

| owned form | where owned | what Volume 3 or 2 said |
|---|---|---|
| देवेन, रामेण, रथेन | v3.c01.l03 | the ṇ of रामेण: an observed spelling; रथेन keeps न |
| रामेषु, बालिकासु | v3.c02.l01 | the ṣ of रामेषु: an observed spelling; बालिकासु keeps स |
| रामान् | v3.c02.l01 | the accusative plural: its final न् stays |
| करोषि, शृणोषि | v2.c04.l03, v2.c04.l02 | "the reason waits for Volume 4" |

Prompt 3 named all of these as changes inside a word — "named here, taught in Volume 4"
(v3.c04.l04) — together with the guṇa inside गुरवे and भवति = भो + अति, which lesson 2 and
Chapter 1 have already redeemed.

### Step 2 — the question

**Why ण here and न there?** देवेन, रामेण and रथेन share one ending. Only one has ण. And
रामान् has the same र as रामेण, yet keeps its न्. In the same way, रामेषु has ष where बालिकासु
has स. Volume 3 described the pattern; today the rules that state it. But the rules have a
surprise: the second of them does not mention the sounds it is about.

### Step 3 — words carried down: the ṇ rules

Each tool is printed once, here, with its tier. **Q** is read with its padaccheda and
recognised by its gist; **B** is a step of the भवति derivation; **D** is the authority for one
derivation step, never recalled by number. From this lesson on, every sūtra also carries its
**bracket line**: what the Aṣṭādhyāyī data file records as carried into it, and the headings it
stands under.

**1. The ṇ rule — Q, 8.4.1 रषाभ्यां नो णः समानपदे** (raṣābhyāṃ no ṇaḥ samānapade).

Padaccheda: रषाभ्याम् [पञ्चमी, dual: "after र् or ष्"] · नः [षष्ठी: "in place of न्"] · णः
[प्रथमा: the result, ण्] · समानपदे [सप्तमी: "within one and the same word" — a locative of place,
not "before"; the Kāśikā's *samānapadasthau*, "both standing in the same word"].

Bracket line: [carried (data file): — · (further headings: Teacher Notes)]

English: "After र् or ष्, ण् replaces न् when both are in one word." [REF:ashtadhyayi 8.4.1];
[REF:kashika 8.4.1]

One line beside it: the Kāśikā adds ऋ to र and ष: पितॄणाम्, मातॄणाम्. You met both forms in
Volume 3 (v3.c02.l04); their ण is made by the same rule.

Read the case code of Chapter 1 into it: रषाभ्याम्, the ablative — "after"; नः, the genitive —
"in place of"; णः, the nominative — the result. The fourth word is the trap. समानपदे is a
locative, and in Chapter 1 the locative of a sound meant "before it". But समानपदे names no
sound: it is "in one and the same word", the place where both sounds stand.

**2. Across what may come between — D, 8.4.2 अट्कुप्वाङ्नुम्व्यवायेऽपि**
(aṭkupvāṅnumvyavāye 'pi).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): रषाभ्याम्, 8.4.1 · carried (course): नः णः समानपदे, 8.4.1 ·
(further headings: Teacher Notes)]

English: "Even when अट् sounds, the k-row, the p-row, the preverb आङ् or num stand between [the
र्/ष् and the न्]." [REF:ashtadhyayi 8.4.2]; [REF:kashika 8.4.2]

अट् is a pratyāhāra you own from Chapter 1: from the अ of अइउण् to the ट् of हयवरट् — every vowel,
and ह य व र. कु and पु are the k-row and the p-row (उदित्, lesson 2 of Chapter 1). आङ् is the
preverb आ; num is an augment of c05.l03.

Now look at what 8.4.2 says and what it does not. It names what may stand between. It does
not name the र or ष, the न, or the ण. Those continue into it from 8.4.1: that is
**anuvṛtti**, अनुवृत्तिः, "carrying down". Without them 8.4.2 is not a rule at all.

**The bracket line.** From this lesson on, the bracket line under a quoted sūtra prints the data
file's anuvṛtti and adhikāra fields as the file gives them, with the two exceptions: headings not
yet taught, and a carried word the rule does not use, such as 7.3.103's दीर्घः. Under 8.4.2 the
file carries one word, रषाभ्याम्; the rest of what continues is printed on a separate course
line, so that you see which words the file records and which the reading supplies (the Kāśikā
writes all of them out, Chapter 6).

Read a bracket line in three parts. "carried (data file)" lists each word the file records, with
the sūtra it comes from. "carried (course)" — only where it is needed — lists what the reading
needs beyond the file. "headings (data file)" lists the headings the rule stands under; a heading
this course has not taught yet is not printed by name but as "(further headings: Teacher
Notes)". You do not have to find the carried words yourself until Chapter 6. You read them from
the bracket line and say the completed rule.

**3. Not at the end of a word — D, 8.4.37 पदान्तस्य** (padāntasya).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): रषाभ्याम्, 8.4.1; न "not", 8.4.34 · carried (course): नः णः,
8.4.1 · (further headings: Teacher Notes)]

English: "[न्] at the end of a word does not [become ण्]." [REF:ashtadhyayi 8.4.37];
[REF:kashika 8.4.37]

One word, and a bracket line longer than the rule. Read the न the file carries from 8.4.34 with
care: it is the word न "not", not the sound न्. The Kāśikā's own gloss says *ṇakārādeśo na
bhavati*, "the ण substitute does not come". The course line adds what is to be done — or rather,
not done: नः णः, from 8.4.1. So: "After र् or ष्, न् at the end of a word does not become ण्."
That is the rule of रामान्.

The three ṇ rules form a staircase: 8.4.1 at the top, and 8.4.2 and 8.4.37 below it, each taking
its missing words from the step above. The words-carried-down image draws it so.

**(a) Derive, according to Pāṇini, राम + टा → रामेण.** 7.1.12 replaces टा by इन; अ + इ → ए is

**(b) Derive, according to Pāṇini, राम + सुप् → रामेषु.** The प् of सुप् is a marker; 7.3.103

**(c) Derive, according to Pāṇini, करो- + सि → करोषि.** Take the stem करो- as given [c03.l03]
and the ending सि from सिप् [its प् a marker; 3.4.78, preview: c03.l01]. One step: 8.3.59, after

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — where the tongue tip is for the four sounds of today's rules. The words-carried-down
image (`planned:assets/images/volume-4/v4c02l03-words-carried-down.svg`) shows the two
staircases; the curl itself you feel in your own mouth.

1. **र.** The tip taps once on the ridge behind the upper teeth: /ɾ/. It leaves the tongue tilted
   back and up.
2. **ण.** The tip curls back behind the ridge and closes there while air goes through the nose:
   /ɳ/. After र, the tip has only to stay where it is.
3. **न and थ.** The tip touches the back of the upper teeth: /n̪/, /t̪ʰ/. Coming from र, the tip
   has to travel forward — the curl is undone.
4. **ष and स.** For ष the tip curls back as for ण and the air hisses past it: /ʂ/. For स the tip
   rests near the teeth: /s/.

Mirror check: say रामेण and रथेन in turn, mouth slightly open. On रामेण your tongue tip hides
behind the ridge to the end; on रथेन you see it come forward to the teeth at थ.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; they feed the quiz distractors.

1. **"In रामेण the इ triggers ण"** — the 1.x card. *Fix:* र triggers (8.4.1); the इ of राम +
   इन is no trigger for ṇ (among the vowels only ऋ is), and by then it is inside the ए [6.1.87].
2. **One trigger list for ṇ and ṣ.** *Fix:* ṇ: र, ष — and ऋ, the Kāśikā's addition; ṣ: the इण्
   sounds and the k-row.
3. **Reading समानपदे as "before the same word".** *Fix:* a locative of place: "within one word"
   — the Kāśikā: both stand in the same word.
4. **Writing *रामाण्.** *Fix:* a word-final न् stays: 8.4.37.
5. **Reading the न carried into 8.4.37 as the sound न्.** *Fix:* it is "not", from 8.4.34:
   "[न्] at a word's end does not [become ण्]".
6. **Tying the ष of करोषि to the root कृ.** *Fix:* it is the स् of the ending सि after ओ, an
   इण् sound: 8.3.59; शृणोषि the same.

— छात्रा: गुरो, रामेणेत्यत्र णकारस्य निमित्तं किम्? (guro, rāmeṇety atra ṇakārasya nimittaṃ kim?)

— गुरुः: रेफः। रषाभ्यां नो णः समानपदे। (rephaḥ. raṣābhyāṃ no ṇaḥ samānapade.)
  रेफः। रषाभ्यां नो णः समानपदे। (rephaḥ. raṣābhyāṃ no ṇaḥ samānapade.)

  स्थान पर ण्।

— छात्रा: मकारोऽपि तत्रास्ति। (makāro 'pi tatrāsti.)

  "But the m is there too." — पर वहाँ मकार भी है।

— गुरुः: मकारः पवर्गे, तेन णत्वं भवत्येव। (makāraḥ pavarge, tena ṇatvaṃ bhavaty eva.)

  में नकार का कोई पद नहीं है।

— गुरुः: तत्पूर्वसूत्रादनुवर्तते। एषानुवृत्तिः। (tat pūrvasūtrād anuvartate. eṣānuvṛttiḥ.)
  तत् पूर्व-सूत्रात् अनुवर्तते। एषा अनुवृत्तिः। (tat pūrva-sūtrāt anuvartate. eṣā anuvṛttiḥ.)
  "It continues from the previous sūtra. This is anuvṛtti." — वह पिछले सूत्र से आता है। यही
  अनुवृत्ति है।

— छात्रा: रामानित्यत्र णकारः किं नास्ति? (rāmān ity atra ṇakāraḥ kiṃ nāsti?)

— गुरुः: नकारः पदान्तेऽस्ति, पदान्तस्येति सूत्रम्। (nakāraḥ padānte 'sti, padāntasyeti sūtram.)

  "The n is at the end of the word: the sūtra is 'padāntasya'." — नकार पद के अन्त में है; सूत्र
  है पदान्तस्य।

— छात्रा: करोषीत्यत्र षकारस्य कारणं किम्? (karoṣīty atra ṣakārasya kāraṇaṃ kim?)

  "In karoṣi, what is the reason for the ṣ?" — करोषि में षकार का कारण क्या है?

— गुरुः: ओकार इण्प्रत्याहारे, सकारश्च प्रत्ययस्य। (okāra iṇpratyāhāre, sakāraś ca pratyayasya.)
  ओकारः इण्-प्रत्याहारे, सकारः च प्रत्ययस्य। (okāraḥ iṇ-pratyāhāre, sakāraḥ ca pratyayasya.)
  "The o is in iṇ, and the s belongs to the affix." — ओकार इण् प्रत्याहार में है, और सकार प्रत्यय
  का है।

— छात्रा: बालिकास्वित्यत्र तु न? (bālikāsv ity atra tu na?)

  "But not in bālikāsu?" — पर बालिकासु में नहीं?

न are in two members of a name, राम-अयन, and the ण is made by 8.4.3 पूर्वपदात् संज्ञायामगः; in
प्रमाण the र is in the preverb प्र, and the ण is made by 8.4.29 कृत्यचः. Nothing in this lesson
claims that Hindi itself has or lacks a ṇ rule.

**The ष of करोषि.** The course's standing decision holds: the ष is the स् of the ending सि after
the stem's ओ — 8.3.59 —, the same in शृणोषि; no line ties it to the root कृ. Volume 2's "the reason
waits for Volume 4" is redeemed here without the root.

**What students find hard.** The main risk is anuvṛtti as guesswork. The course gives every
carried word in the bracket line until Chapter 6: the learner applies the completed rule
(exercise 5) and is never asked to find the words. The second hard point is the two trigger
lists: learners who have read the 1.x card hold one list for both. Put the two lists side by
side on the board and leave them there all lesson. Hindi-medium learners may hear [n] for ण in
everyday Hindi; drill 1 of §9 makes the difference deliberate.

**Differentiation.** Fast learners: ask why 8.4.37 needs a course line at all (without नः णः the
"not" has nothing to deny); or have them read the Kāśikā line on 8.4.2 above and find every word
the bracket line supplied. Learners who struggle: give them the two staircases on one card and
let them run exercise 1 with the card open.
