## 1. Hook

You have written रामः since Volume 1, and you learned its ः there as a breath after the vowel
(v1.c02.l06). Volume 3 showed you the whole list of noun endings once, as a picture: one long
word, sūtra 4.1.2, which begins with सुँ — v3.c01.l05. So where in रामः is that ending — and
why does सुँ come out as a breath?

A second puzzle. Volume 3 gave you नृपः for "king", an a-stem, "usable before राजन्'s consonant
declension" (v3.c01.l04). Today राजा arrives, and with it three forms of one word that do not
look alike: राजा, राजभिः, राज्ञा. Three stems to learn? No: one base with three names.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — where an affix is taught, and how the commentators read the rules that
teach it.

The rules that make a word out of a base and an ending stand far apart in the Aṣṭādhyāyī. The
names — aṅga, pada, bha — are defined together in the fourth quarter of the first book (1.4.13,
1.4.17, 1.4.18), next to 1.4.14, the rule that calls a word that ends in a *sup* or *tiṅ* ending a
पदम्, which you met in Volume 3. The affixes themselves are taught from the opening of the third
book on: 3.1.1 and 3.1.2 stand at its head. The case endings are listed in the fourth book (4.1.2,
the picture of v3.c01.l05). And the rules that change the end of a word — the स् that becomes रुँ,
the र् that becomes a visarga, the न् that is dropped — stand in the eighth book (8.2.7, 8.2.66,
8.3.15).

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** In Pāṇini's grammar an affix is taught by a heading: from 3.1.1
> प्रत्ययः on, what the rules introduce is an affix, and by 3.1.2 परश्च it comes after the root or
> base it is added to. The Kāśikā, a commentary on the whole Aṣṭādhyāyī (c. 7th century CE), calls
> both rules headings. [REF:kashika 3.1.1]; [REF:kashika 3.1.2]

A heading is a rule that stands over a stretch of later rules and is understood in each of
them; how that works is lesson 3's subject. Today it is enough that every affix a later rule
introduces is, by these two headings, an affix, and comes after its base.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — what a descriptive grammar says about the same forms: a stem that changes
shape before its endings.

Many languages build their word forms by adding affixes to a base. Sanskrit's case endings are
affixes added after the base, and with some bases the base itself changes shape before some of the
endings. Whitney, describing the language rather than Pāṇini's rules, says this of the stems in
-अन्, such as राजन्:

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "The stem has a triple form. In the strong cases of the masculine, the vowel of
> the ending is prolonged to आ ā; in the weakest cases it is in general struck out altogether; in
> the middle cases, or before a case-ending beginning with a consonant, the final न् n is dropped.
> The न् n is also lost in the nom. sing. …" [REF:whitney1889 §421]

Whitney's "strong", "weakest" and "middle" cases and Pāṇini's three names — strong, bha and pada —
pick out the same endings for a masculine: the two accounts name them differently. Whitney speaks of
a stem with three forms; Pāṇini keeps one base and gives it a different name before each set of
endings, and lets each name set off its own rule. The language does it (FACT); Pāṇini's names and
rules describe it (TRADITION). This lesson prints both, side by side.

One more point of description. Whitney prints the final -स् of the endings (राजभिस्, राज्ञस्); this
course prints the visarga at a pause (राजभिः, राज्ञः), as Volume 3 did. Today you see why the two
are the same: the visarga is what a pada-final स् becomes at a pause (§13, derivation (a)).

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the day's idea in five lines, each printed joined, its pause form beneath,
then the gloss.

> रामशब्दे राम इति प्रकृतिः, सुरिति प्रत्ययः। (rāmaśabde rāma iti prakṛtiḥ, sur iti pratyayaḥ.)
> राम-शब्दे रामः इति प्रकृतिः, सुः इति प्रत्ययः। (rāma-śabde rāmaḥ iti prakṛtiḥ, suḥ iti pratyayaḥ.)
> "In the word rāmaḥ, rāma is the base and su the affix." — राम शब्द में राम प्रकृति है, सु
> प्रत्यय।

> प्रत्ययः प्रकृतेः परो भवति। (pratyayaḥ prakṛteḥ paro bhavati.)
> प्रत्ययः प्रकृतेः परः भवति। (pratyayaḥ prakṛteḥ paraḥ bhavati.)
> "The affix comes after the base." — प्रत्यय प्रकृति के बाद आता है।

> राजा नृपश्चैकार्थौ। (rājā nṛpaś caikārthau.)
> राजा नृपः च एक-अर्थौ। (rājā nṛpaḥ ca eka-arthau.)
> "राजा and नृपः have one meaning." — राजा और नृप एकार्थक हैं।

> आत्मा नरस्य मित्रम्। (ātmā narasya mitram.)
> आत्मा नरस्य मित्रम्। (ātmā narasya mitram.)
> "One's self is a man's friend." — आत्मा मनुष्य का मित्र है।

> प्रत्ययात्पूर्वं प्रकृतिः। (pratyayāt pūrvaṃ prakṛtiḥ.)
> प्रत्ययात् पूर्वम् प्रकृतिः। (pratyayāt pūrvam prakṛtiḥ.)
> "Before the affix: the base." — प्रत्यय से पहले प्रकृति।

Four of today's six headwords stand in these lines as free words: प्रकृतिः, प्रत्ययः, राजा and
आत्मा — प्रत्ययः also as प्रत्ययात्, its ablative, an a-stem cell you own. The other two, स्वामी and
भृत्यः, are in §13.

Three notes on the words. In the first line the base and the affix are named as words: in the
pause form each stands with a case ending, रामः and सुः, as a label; the base itself is राम, with no
ending. In §16 राजन् is named bare instead, राजन्निति. In the second line प्रकृतेः is the ablative
of an f. i-stem — the cell of Volume 3's मतिः card that looks like the masculine one; it is ◐,
read here, not drilled. In the third line एकार्थौ, "having one meaning", is a masculine dual
nominative agreeing with the two nouns, ✓; its pause form shows it as one word, members hyphenated.
पूर्वम् in the last line is the neuter of lesson 3's पूर्वः, used as an adverb, "before" — a cell of
an owned adjective.

The joins are Volume 3's: -अः before इ → -अ and a gap: राम इति; -उः before a vowel → -उर्: सुरिति;
-अः before a voiced sound → -ओ: परो भवति; -अः before च → -श्: नृपश्च; अ + ए → ऐ: चैकार्थौ; त् + प
written together: प्रत्ययात्पूर्वम्; म् before a consonant → ं: पूर्वं प्रकृतिः. No seam at
आत्मा नरस्य, -आ + न, or नरस्य मित्रम्, -अ + म. A comma and a daṇḍa stop every seam: प्रकृतिः,
stands in its pause form. रामशब्दे is a compound, one word, hyphenated in the pause form.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

पहले खण्ड से आप रामः लिखते आ रहे हैं, और उसके अन्त का ः — विसर्ग — आपने स्वर के बाद की हल्की
साँस के रूप में सीखा था। तीसरे खण्ड में संज्ञा के सारे प्रत्ययों की सूची एक बार चित्र की तरह
दिखाई गई थी — सूत्र 4.1.2, जो सुँ से शुरू होता है। आज प्रश्न यह है: रामः में वह प्रत्यय कहाँ है,
और सुँ आख़िर ः कैसे बन गया?

पाणिनि के अनुसार हर शब्द-रूप के दो हिस्से हैं: **प्रकृति** — मूल, जिस पर कुछ जोड़ा जाता है — और
**प्रत्यय** — वह जो जोड़ा जाता है। सूत्र 3.1.1 और 3.1.2 बताते हैं कि प्रत्यय प्रकृति के
**बाद** आता है। रामः में प्रकृति राम है और प्रत्यय सुँ। पर सुँ के उ पर चन्द्रबिन्दु है — वह
अनुनासिक स्वर है, और सूत्र 1.3.2 के अनुसार उपदेश में, यानी पहली बार सिखाए गए रूप में, अनुनासिक
स्वर **इत्** होता है: एक चिह्न-वर्ण, जो काम बताने के लिए लगा है, शब्द में रहने के लिए नहीं।
सूत्र 1.3.9 उसका लोप करता है — वह हट जाता है। बचा: राम + स्। फिर पद के अन्त का स् रुँ बनता है —
8.2.66; रुँ का उँ भी इत् है और हट जाता है, और विराम पर र् विसर्ग बन जाता है — 8.3.15।
तो रामः का ः उसी प्रत्यय का बचा हुआ अंश है।

हर प्रत्यय के इत् एक जैसे नहीं होते। आज की **अन्त्य-चिह्न पंक्ति** याद रखिए: जस् का ज् और टा
का ट् इत् हैं; शस् का श् और ङे, ङसिँ, ङस्, ङि का ङ् भी इत् हैं; पर भिस्, भ्यस्, जस्, ङस्, ओस्
का अन्तिम स् और अम्, भ्याम्, आम् का म् इत् **नहीं** हैं — विभक्ति के प्रत्यय में अन्त का दन्त्य,
स् या म् बना रहता है। इसलिए अम् में म् बचा रहता है, भिस् भिस् ही रहता है, और सुप् का केवल प्
जाता है। सुप् अपने-आप में एक प्रत्याहार है: 4.1.2 के पहले प्रत्यय सुँ से अन्तिम इत् प् तक —
सारे इक्कीस विभक्ति-प्रत्यय — सात विभक्तियाँ × तीन वचन।

अब नाम। सूत्र 1.4.13 के अनुसार जिस प्रकृति के बाद कोई भी प्रत्यय आता है, वह उस प्रत्यय से पहले
**अङ्ग** कहलाती है। विभक्ति के प्रत्ययों से पहले उसके दो और नाम हैं। पहले पाँच प्रत्यय — सुँ, औ,
जस्, अम्, औट् — **सर्वनामस्थान** कहलाते हैं, नपुंसक को छोड़कर; इन्हें हम "सबल" प्रत्यय भी कहेंगे।
बाकी प्रत्ययों में से जो व्यञ्जन से शुरू होते हैं, उनसे पहले प्रकृति **पद** कहलाती है — 1.4.17;
जो स्वर या य से शुरू होते हैं, उनसे पहले **भ** — 1.4.18 यचि भम्।

और यहीं से राजन् खुलता है। राजन् + भिस् में भिस् व्यञ्जन से शुरू होता है, इसलिए राजन् पद है;
पद के अन्त का न् हट जाता है — 8.2.7: राजभिः। राजन् + टा में टा का ट् इत् है, बचा आ — स्वर; इसलिए
राजन् भ है; भ में अन् का अ हट जाता है — 6.4.134: राज्न् + आ, और ज् के साथ न् ञ् बन जाता है:
राज्ञा। एक ही प्रकृति, तीन नाम — और हर नाम अपना अलग नियम चलाता है। तीन अलग प्रकृतियाँ रटनी नहीं
हैं।

नमस्ते भी आज पूरा खुलता है। नमस् अव्यय है; अव्यय के बाद आया सुप् प्रत्यय लुप्त हो जाता है —
2.4.82; फिर भी शब्द पद गिना जाता है, इसलिए स् → रुँ → विसर्ग: नमः। ते से पहले भी विसर्ग बनता
है, और फिर विसर्ग के स्थान पर स् आता है — 8.3.34: नमस्ते। यानी नमस्ते का स् मूल प्रकृति का स्
नहीं, विसर्ग का **आदेश** है।

आज के छह शब्द: **राजा**; **प्रत्यय** — व्याकरण में जोड़ा जाने वाला अंश, और साधारण अर्थ में
विश्वास; **प्रकृति** — व्याकरण में मूल रूप, और साधारण अर्थ में स्वभाव; **आत्मा** — स्वयं, आत्मा;
**स्वामी** — मालिक; और **भृत्य** — सेवक, नौकर।

## 8. English Explanation

The recipe of this volume, once more: forms you own, the question they raise, the tools that
answer it, the derivation with the teacher, the tools run by you, then production. Today's one
grammar point: a word is a base plus an affix, and the base has three names before case endings.

### Step 1 — the forms you own

| owned form | where owned | its pieces | what it raises |
|---|---|---|---|
| रामः | v1.c04.l01; its ः since v1.c02.l06 | राम + the nom. sg. ending | where is the ending? why ः? |
| नृपेण, नृपस्य | v3.c01.l03–l04 [your §3 prompt 2] | नृप + an ending | the base नृप does not change |
| नमः, नमस्ते | v1.c03.l04; split in v1.c05.l02; 8.3.34 in v3.c04.l02 | नमः + ते | is the स् of नमस्ते the स् of नमस्? |
| राजा, राजभिः, राज्ञा | today: राजा as a headword, two cells derived | राजन् + three endings | why three shapes? |

The base नृप of your prompt keeps its shape before every ending you know. The base राजन् does not:
before भिस् it loses its न्, before टा it loses an अ, before सुँ it loses both its न् and the
ending. Volume 3 left this for Volume 4.

### Step 2 — the question

**What is an ending, where does it stand, and why does one base look three ways?** Two puzzles in
one: the ः of रामः, which is all that is left of an ending; and राजा, राजभिः, राज्ञा, three faces of
one base.

### Step 3 — the tools: base, affix, marker

Each tool is printed once, here, with its tier and its padaccheda. The tiers you know from Chapter
1: **Q** is quoted, read word by word, recognised by its gist; **B** is a step of a derivation you
will meet again on the भवति thread; **D** is the authority for one derivation step, never recalled
by number. New today: **K**, a reference card — number, text, one English line — never assessed.

**1. The affix — B, 3.1.1 प्रत्ययः** (pratyayaḥ).

Padaccheda: प्रत्ययः [प्रथमा] the name, "affix".

English: "[What is taught from here on is called] an affix." A heading over Books 3–5
[REF:ashtadhyayi 3.1.1]; [REF:kashika 3.1.1]

**2. After the base — B, 3.1.2 परश्च** (paraś ca).

Padaccheda: परः [प्रथमा] "following, after" · च "and".

English: "And it comes after [the root or nominal base]." [REF:ashtadhyayi 3.1.2];
[REF:kashika 3.1.2]

**3. The nasal vowel is a marker — B, 1.3.2 उपदेशेऽजनुनासिक इत्** (upadeśe 'j anunāsika it).

Padaccheda: उपदेशे [सप्तमी] "in a teaching" — a locative of place, not the case code's "before" ·
अच् [प्रथमा] "a vowel" · अनुनासिकः [प्रथमा] "nasal" · इत् [प्रथमा] the name, "marker".

English: "In a teaching, a nasal vowel is a marker." [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.3.2]; [REF:kashika 1.3.2]

A "teaching" (उपदेशः) is an item as the grammar first states it: सुँ as 4.1.2 lists it, not as it
stands in a word. You own the other marker rule from Chapter 1: 1.3.3 हलन्त्यम्, the final consonant
of a teaching is a marker; and 1.3.9 तस्य लोपः drops every marker.

**4. aṅga — B, 1.4.13 यस्मात् प्रत्ययविधिस्तदादि प्रत्ययेऽङ्गम्** (yasmāt pratyayavidhis tadādi
pratyaye 'ṅgam).

Padaccheda: यस्मात् [पञ्चमी] "from which" — the item after which the affix is enjoined ·
प्रत्ययविधिः [प्रथमा] "an affix is enjoined" · तदादि [प्रथमा] "beginning with that" · प्रत्यये
[सप्तमी] "before the affix" · अङ्गम् [प्रथमा] the name.

English: "That after which an affix is enjoined, [and what begins with it], is called aṅga before
that affix." [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.4.13]; [REF:kashika 1.4.13]

**The anatomy, in one card.** According to Pāṇini, a form = प्रकृतिः "base" + प्रत्ययः "affix",
the affix placed after the base (3.1.1–2), its markers (इत्) dropped (1.3.2, 1.3.3, 1.3.9). Before
any affix the base is called अङ्गम् (aṅgam), aṅga (1.4.13). The base a case ending is added to is a
प्रातिपदिकम् (prātipadikam), "a nominal base" — today's first reference card:

> **K — 1.2.45 अर्थवदधातुरप्रत्ययः प्रातिपदिकम्** (arthavad adhātur apratyayaḥ prātipadikam).
> "A meaningful form that is not a root and not an affix is called prātipadika (a nominal base)."
> [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.2.45]; [REF:kashika 1.2.45] — a card: looked up, never assessed.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Whitney names the same parts without the tradition's names: "stem" and
> "case-ending". [REF:whitney1889 §421]

**The ending-marker line.** Which sounds of a case ending are markers? One line, so that 1.3.3 is
not over-applied:

- the initial ज् of जस् and ट् of टा are markers [Teacher Notes];
- the initial श् of शस् and ङ् of ङे, ङसिँ, ङस्, ङि are markers [1.3.8 लशक्वतद्धिते, preview:
  c03.l02];
- but the **final** स् of भिस्, भ्यस्, जस्, ङस्, ओस् and the म् of अम्, भ्याम्, आम् are **not**
  markers: in a case or verb ending, a final dental, स् or म् is kept [Teacher Notes].

So अम् keeps its म्, भिस् stays भिस्, and सुप् loses only its प्. And one more line, on सुँ itself:
**The उ of सुँ is nasal (उँ): the tradition says so; the printed rule does not show it.**

**सुप् is a pratyāhāra too.** You own the rule that makes a name from a first sound and a marker
(1.1.71, Chapter 1). From सुँ, the first ending of 4.1.2, to the final marker प् of सुप्, the last,
सुप् names all twenty-one case endings: seven cases × three numbers, twenty-one items in the
padaccheda of 4.1.2. Volume 3's warning holds: the प् is a marker, not a syllable.

### Step 4 — the three names

**5. pada — Q, 1.4.17 स्वादिष्वसर्वनामस्थाने** (svādiṣv asarvanāmasthāne).

Padaccheda: स्वादिषु [सप्तमी, plural] "before the endings beginning with सुँ" · असर्वनामस्थाने
[सप्तमी] "before one that is not a sarvanāmasthāna" · [पदम्, carried from 1.4.14] [प्रथमा] the
name given.

English: "Before the endings from सुँ on, except the strong ones, [the base is called] pada."
[REF:ashtadhyayi 1.4.17]; [REF:kashika 1.4.18]; [REF:laghukaumudi on 1.4.17] — the Kāśikā's e-text
has no comment under 1.4.17; this line follows its restatement under 1.4.18 and the
Laghusiddhāntakaumudī's line on 1.4.17.

**6. bha — Q, 1.4.18 यचि भम्** (yaci bham).

Padaccheda: यचि [सप्तमी] "before [an ending that begins with] य or a vowel" — यच् = य + अच् ·
भम् [प्रथमा] the name bha · [स्वादिषु, असर्वनामस्थाने, carried from 1.4.17].

English: "Before [such an ending] beginning with य or a vowel, [the base is called] bha."
[REF:ashtadhyayi 1.4.18]; [REF:kashika 1.4.18] — according to the Kāśikā it is an exception to
1.4.17: where 1.4.17 would give the name pada, 1.4.18 gives bha.

Which endings are "strong"? Today's second reference card:

> **K — 1.1.43 सुडनपुंसकस्य** (suḍ anapuṃsakasya). "The five endings सुँ, औ, जस्, अम्, औट् are
> called sarvanāmasthāna (the strong endings), except after a neuter." [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.43];
> [REF:kashika 1.1.43] — a card: looked up, never assessed.

सुट् is itself a pratyāhāra of 4.1.2: from सु to the ट् of औट्. The Kāśikā's examples under 1.1.43
are राजन्'s own strong cells (§13).

> **According to Pāṇini, one base has three names before the case endings.** Before the five strong
> endings (1.1.43) it is neither pada nor bha. Before the others it is **pada** if the ending begins
> with a consonant (1.4.17), and **bha** if it begins with य or a vowel (1.4.18). It is aṅga before
> every affix (1.4.13). **What a rule does to a base depends on the name it bears.**

For राजन् the three names set off three rules:

- **pada → 8.2.7:** the न् at the end of the base is dropped — राजभिः.
- **bha → 6.4.134:** the अ of the base's -अन् is dropped — राज्ञा.
- **strong → the lengthened अ:** राजानम्, राजा — the rule is 6.4.8 [preview: c05.l03].

Sort a base before an ending by asking, in order: is the ending one of the five strong ones? If not,
what is its first sound once its markers are gone — a consonant, or a vowel (or य)?

**Why is रामः's base not pada?** Before सुँ, a strong ending, the base राम is not called pada
(1.4.17 leaves the strong endings out); the whole word रामस् is a pada because it ends in a sup
(1.4.14). That is the pada 8.2.66 needs in derivation (a).

**The 1.x card, corrected.** The 1.x book printed the rājan card with four errors (p. 98). It
glossed 1.4.18 'Before vowel-initial endings or those starting in sibilant + vowel, the stem is
bha': the य of यचि is the semivowel य, not a sibilant. It said that 'In "weak" cases (3rd-7th cases)
before vowel endings, stems collapse': the second-case plural राज्ञः — शस्, a display form — is bha
too, though it is no 3rd–7th case; and the bha rule drops only the अ, giving राज्न् + आ. It printed
'Case 1 sg: राजा (pada — n drops)' and 'Case 2 sg: राजानम् (aṅga — n stays)': before सुँ and अम्
the base is strong (sarvanāmasthāna), neither pada nor 'just aṅga' — every base is aṅga before an
affix. The न् of राजा drops for another reason: once the ending is gone, the whole word is a pada
(1.4.14; the reason a deleted ending still counts: Teacher Notes), and 8.2.7 drops the न्.

### Step 5 — the derivations, with the teacher

All four are in §13 in table form; work down them with the teacher, one line at a time, in this
order.

**(a) Derive, according to Pāṇini, राम + सुँ → रामः.** The affix comes after the base (3.1.1–2);
its उँ is a marker by 1.3.2, dropped by 1.3.9; राम is aṅga before स् by 1.4.13. रामस् ends in a sup, so
it is a pada (1.4.14); its final स् becomes रुँ by 8.2.66; the उँ of रुँ is a marker, dropped; at a
pause the र् becomes the visarga (8.3.15).

**(b) Derive, according to Pāṇini, राजन् + भिस् → राजभिः.** भिस् begins with a consonant and is not
strong: राजन् is pada (1.4.17), and the pada-final न् of a nominal base is dropped (8.2.7). Then
the final स् goes the way of रामः's.

**(c) Derive, according to Pāṇini, राजन् + टा → राज्ञा.** The ट् of टा is a marker (the
ending-marker line); आ is a vowel and टा is not strong: राजन् is bha (1.4.18). Of a bha base in -अन्
the अ of the अन् is dropped (6.4.134): राज्न् + आ. Then a rule you own, 8.4.40: न् becomes ञ् in contact
with ज्, and ज् + ञ् is written ज्ञ.

**(d) Derive, according to Pāṇini, नमस् + सुँ → नमः, and नमः + ते → नमस्ते.** नमस् is an
indeclinable (1.1.37, owned); after it the case ending is deleted (2.4.82). The word is still a
pada — a deleted ending still counts (the reason: Teacher Notes) — so its स् → रुँ → visarga. Before
ते the visarga arises too, and is replaced by स् — 8.3.34, owned. So the स् of नमस्ते is a
substitute for the visarga, not the stem's स् left untouched.

The new D tools, each printed once here with its English line; their tiers stand beside them in
§13:

- **D — 8.2.66 ससजुषो रुः** (sasajuṣo ruḥ) — "The स् at the end of a pada [and that of सजुष्]
  becomes रुँ." [REF:kashika 8.2.66]
- **D — 8.3.15 खरवसानयोर्विसर्जनीयः** (kharavasānayor visarjanīyaḥ) — "[A pada-final र्] becomes
  visarga before a khar sound or at a pause." [REF:kashika 8.3.15]
- **D — 7.1.24 अतोऽम्** (ato 'm) — "After a neuter stem in short अ, सुँ and अम् are replaced by
  अम्." [REF:kashika 7.1.24]
- **D — 6.1.107 अमि पूर्वः** (ami pūrvaḥ) — "Before अम्, [an अक् vowel and the अ of अम्] become one
  vowel: the first." [REF:kashika 6.1.107]
- **D — 8.2.7 नलोपः प्रातिपदिकान्तस्य** (nalopaḥ prātipadikāntasya) — "The न् at the end of a
  nominal base that is a pada is dropped." [REF:kashika 8.2.7]
- **D — 6.4.134 अल्लोपोऽनः** (allopo 'naḥ) — "Of a bha base ending in अन्, the अ [of the अन्] is
  dropped." [REF:kashika 6.4.134]
- **D — 2.4.82 अव्ययादाप्सुपः** (avyayād āpsupaḥ) — "After an indeclinable (avyaya), the case
  ending [and the feminine आप्] is deleted (luk)." [REF:kashika 2.4.82]
- **D, owned — 8.4.40 स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः** — "स् and the त-row become श् and the च-row in contact

1. **ज्ञ in राज्ञा.** The blade of the tongue rises to the hard palate for ज् /d͡ʑ/, voiced, with a
   short hiss at the release. Do not release it: let the air turn into the nose with the tongue
   still at the palate — that is ञ् /ɲ/. Then open for आ. The back of the tongue never rises; if it
   does, you have said [g].
2. **The visarga at a pause.** रामः, राजभिः, नमः end in a vowel and a breath /h/: hold the mouth in
   the vowel's shape and let the air out through the open throat. No vowel echo is needed in
   speech; the echo of recitation is a TRADITION practice (Volume 1).
3. **भ in राजभिः.** The lips close as for ब, the voice runs, and the release carries a breathy
   puff: /bʱ/. Keep the voice on through the puff.

Mirror check: say राजा, राज्ञा, राजा, राज्ञा. The jaw barely moves; the tongue stays forward for
both ज and ञ.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; they feed the quiz distractors.

1. **Taking aṅga, pada and bha for three different stems to memorise.** *Fix:* one base, three
   names; the name decides which rule acts (the sort, §18 exercise 3).
2. **Calling the base pada before सुँ, as the 1.x card did.** *Fix:* सुँ is one of the five strong endings
   (1.1.43); 1.4.17 leaves them out; the whole word रामस् is a pada by 1.4.14.
3. **Reading the य of यचि as a sibilant, or as "before य" only.** *Fix:* यच् = य + अच्: before an
   ending that begins with य or a vowel.
4. **Dropping the final स् of भिस् or the म् of अम् as markers.** *Fix:* a final dental, स् or म्
   of an ending is kept [Teacher Notes]; only the प् of सुप् goes, and the initial ट् ज् श् ङ् of
   टा, जस्, शस्, ङे ….
5. **Writing \*राजैः by 7.1.9 after राज + भिस्.** *Fix:* after 8.2.7, 7.1.9 does not apply — why is
   8.2.1 [preview: c06.l03]; the form is राजभिः [Whitney §424].
6. **Saying the स् of नमस्ते is the stem's स् kept.** *Fix:* नमः + ते: the visarga arises (8.3.15)
   and 8.3.34 replaces it with स्; the स् is a substitute.
7. **Saying राज्ञा as [ɾɑːgjɑː] — a Hindi speaker's habit from ज्ञान.** *Fix:* in Sanskrit ज्ञ is
   ज् + ञ्, /d͡ʑɲ/ (§9, §11); the habit is a regional variant, not the target.

## 17. Story

कथा — the grammarians' line, 2: the commentators after Pāṇini, and why grammar is studied.

Chapter 1 told how Pāṇini's rules are recited. Today, the line that follows him.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** Pāṇini's rules have long been commented on. Kātyāyana wrote short notes on
> them, the vārttikas; Patañjali's Great Commentary, the Mahābhāṣya (c. 2nd century BCE on the
> usual estimate), discusses Pāṇini's rules and Kātyāyana's notes together [REF:mahabhashya];
> [REF:cardona1997].

The Mahābhāṣya opens with a question: what is grammar for? Its answer, in one line:

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** Patañjali names the purposes of studying grammar. [REF:mahabhashya
> Paspaśāhnika]
>
> Mahābhāṣya, Paspaśāhnika (Kielhorn I, p. 1, l. 14 – p. 2, l. 2) — text of the GRETIL e-text:
>
> रक्षोहागमलघ्वसन्देहाः प्रयोजनम्। (rakṣohāgamalaghvasandehāḥ prayojanam.)
>
> रक्षा-ऊह-आगम-लघु-असन्देहाः प्रयोजनम्। (rakṣā-ūha-āgama-laghu-asandehāḥ prayojanam.)
>
> "The purposes [of grammar] are preservation (of the Veda), adaptation (ūha, of mantras to the
> rite), scripture (āgama), economy (laghu) and freedom from doubt (asandeha)."

The line is display: read it, do not learn it; it is not a headword and not a tool, and प्रयोजनम्
"purpose" becomes a word of yours in c03.l05. The second line is its pause form: one long compound,
its members hyphenated, and प्रयोजनम्. Three of the four seams inside the compound are joins you
own: रक्षा + ऊह → रक्षोह, guṇa; ऊह + आगम → ऊहागम, a long vowel; लघु + असन्देह → लघ्वसन्देह, yaṇ.
The fourth, आगम + लघु, is no join at all.

The last purpose is the one this lesson serves. रामः, राजभिः, राज्ञा, नमस्ते: four forms you
used for years without knowing their parts. Now each has a derivation, and the doubt about where
the ending went, and why राजन् looks three ways, is gone.

## 20. Revision

पुनरावृत्तिः — one screen to remember.

> **One base, three names.** According to Pāṇini, a form = **base** (प्रकृतिः) + **affix**
> (प्रत्ययः) after it (3.1.1–2), its **markers** (इत्) dropped (1.3.2, 1.3.3, 1.3.9). The base is
> **aṅga** before every affix (1.4.13). Before the case endings: **strong** before सुँ औ जस् अम्
> औट् [1.1.43, card]; **pada** before the other consonant-initial ones (1.4.17); **bha** before the
> other य- or vowel-initial ones (1.4.18 यचि भम्). **The name decides the rule:** pada → 8.2.7, the
> न् dropped: **राजभिः**; bha → 6.4.134, the अ dropped: **राज्ञा**; strong → the lengthened अ
> (c05.l03). **The ending-marker line:** ज् of जस्, ट् of टा, श् of शस्, ङ् of ङे … are markers; the
> final स् and म् of an ending are not; सुप् loses only its प्. **The ः of रामः** is what is left of
> सुँ: स् → रुँ by 8.2.66, then the visarga by 8.3.15. **The स् of नमस्ते** is a substitute for the visarga
> (2.4.82, 8.3.34). **The ledger:** six new — 527; cells drilled: 3; tools added: 13; toolbox: 25.

(the K card): a meaningful form, not a root, not an affix. How such stems are themselves made is
Chapter 4's; today they are given.

**Why the vocative is left out.** The vocative singular is सुँ in the sense of address (sambuddhi);
its own rules are not today's. The table prints it; the sort leaves it out.

**What students find hard.** The ending-marker line: learners over-apply 1.3.3 and strike the स् of
भिस्. Have them say aloud, for each ending, "first sound? last sound? which is a marker?" The second
hard thing is that रामस् is a pada though राम is not: the base's name (before सुँ: strong) and the
whole word's name (1.4.14) are two different things. Hindi-medium learners hear ज्ञ as [gj] from
ज्ञान; give §11's tongue position, and accept the variant in speech without teaching it.

**Differentiation.** Fast learners: why does 6.4.134 not apply to राजभिः? (भिस् makes राजन् pada, not
bha.) And why is the base before सुप् pada, though सुप् ends in a marker? (The marker is gone: सु
begins with a consonant.) Learners who struggle: give them the three boxes with the five strong
endings already written in, and let them sort only by the first sound.
