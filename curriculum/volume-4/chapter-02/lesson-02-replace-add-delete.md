## 1. Hook

Put two forms you own side by side: गच्छति "goes", from Volume 2, and अगच्छत् "went", from
Volume 3 (v3.c03.l03). At the front of अगच्छत् stands an अ that गच्छति does not have. At the
back, the इ of -ति is gone. And the ending itself, which you learned as -ति, turns out today to
stand in the place of something else. Three different things happened to one word: a sound was
added, a sound was taken away, and one item was put in place of another.

Now a claim from the 1.x book, printed as an example of adding a sound: in भू + ति → भवति,
"गुण inserts व". Does it? By the end of this lesson you can say what guṇa is, according to
Pāṇini — and what it never does.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — where guṇa and vṛddhi stand in the grammar, and how two traditions use
the same two words.

The Aṣṭādhyāyī opens with definitions. Its first sūtra, 1.1.1, gives a name to three vowels; its
second, 1.1.2, gives a name to three more; its third, 1.1.3, says where the two names apply.
Today you read all three. The rules that use the names stand far later — 6.1.87 and 6.1.88,
which you own from Volume 3, are in the sixth book. As in Chapter 1, Book 1 defines once, and
the later rules use the definitions.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** Pāṇini's grammar gives names to sets of vowels: by 1.1.1 आ ऐ औ are
> vṛddhi, and by 1.1.2 अ ए ओ are guṇa. Volume 3 met गुणः and वृद्धिः as the names of two vowel
> joins; in the Aṣṭādhyāyī they name the vowels a rule puts in. [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.1];
> [REF:kashika 1.1.1]

The second tradition is the one many Hindi-medium learners know from school.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** Hindi school grammar sorts the vowel joins under five names —
> दीर्घ, गुण, वृद्धि, यण्, अयादि. In Pāṇini गुण and वृद्धि are not names of joins but of three
> vowels each; the join is named by the rule that puts them in (6.1.87, 6.1.88). [REF:guru1952]

Neither use is wrong: a school book needs a name for each kind of join, and Pāṇini needs a name
for each set of vowels. This lesson keeps the two apart. When you say "the guṇa join" you mean
6.1.87; when you say "guṇa" in a rule, you mean अ, ए or ओ.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — what the vowel grades look like from outside the tradition.

The vowels इ, ए and ऐ belong together in Sanskrit words: one root shows इ in one form and ए or
ऐ in another, and so do उ, ओ, औ and ऋ, अर्, आर्. Whitney, describing the language, puts it as
a matter of adding an a-element:

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "A guṇa-vowel (guṇa *secondary quality*) differs from the corresponding
> simple vowel by a prefixed a-element which is combined with the other according to the usual
> rules; a vṛddhi-vowel (vṛddhi *growth, increment*), by the further prefixion of a to the
> guṇa-vowel." And: "But in all gunating processes अ a remains unchanged—or, as it is sometimes
> expressed, अ a is its own guṇa; आ ā, of course, remains unchanged for both guṇa and vṛddhi."
> [REF:whitney1889 §235]

These are Whitney's glosses of the two words, quoted here once: guṇa "secondary quality",
vṛddhi "growth, increment". The course leaves guṇa and vṛddhi untranslated.

Set the two accounts side by side. Whitney, the describer, adds an a-element to the simple
vowel (FACT about the language's vowel grades). Pāṇini, the grammarian, replaces a vowel by
one member of a named set (TRADITION). The two say the same thing in two ways, and both yield
ए ओ for guṇa, ऐ औ for vṛddhi, and अर् आर् for ऋ. Neither inserts a व anywhere.

Why should a grammar prefer substitution? Because a substitute is one operation of one kind, and
the grammar already has that kind: the genitive "in place of" of Chapter 1. Whatever stands in
place of something else — a vowel for a vowel, an ending for an ending, a sound for two sounds —
is a substitute, and one tool reads them all.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the day's idea in four lines, each printed joined, its pause form beneath,
then the gloss.

> गुणो वृद्धिश्चादेशौ, आगमौ न। (guṇo vṛddhiś cādeśau, āgamau na.)
> गुणः वृद्धिः च आदेशौ, आगमौ न। (guṇaḥ vṛddhiḥ ca ādeśau, āgamau na.)
> "guṇa and vṛddhi are substitutes, not augments." — गुण और वृद्धि आदेश हैं, आगम नहीं।

> अगच्छदित्यत्राकार आगमः। (agacchad ity atrākāra āgamaḥ.)
> अगच्छत् इति अत्र अकारः आगमः। (agacchat iti atra akāraḥ āgamaḥ.)
> "In agacchat the a is an augment." — अगच्छत् में अकार आगम है।

> इकारस्य लोपः। (ikārasya lopaḥ.)
> इकारस्य लोपः। (ikārasya lopaḥ.)
> "The i is dropped." — इकार का लोप।

> उकारस्य स्थान ओकारो गुणः। (ukārasya sthāna okāro guṇaḥ.)
> उकारस्य स्थाने ओकारः गुणः। (ukārasya sthāne okāraḥ guṇaḥ.)
> "In place of u, o is the guṇa." — उकार के स्थान पर ओकार गुण है।

These are model sentences 1–3 and a fourth line; model sentences 4–6 are in §13. The headwords
stand as free words: आगमः and लोपः, today's; आदेशौ, the dual of आदेशः [v4.c01.l03]; गुणः and
वृद्धिः, today's terms. Chapter 1 read लोपः from 1.3.9 तस्य लोपः as a labelled preview; today it is
yours. अकारः, इकारः, उकारः, ओकारः are -कार sound names, labels as in Chapter 1.

The joins are Volume 3's: गुणः वृद्धिः → गुणो वृद्धिः and ओकारः गुणः → ओकारो गुणः — -अः before a
voiced consonant → ओ; वृद्धिः च → वृद्धिश्च — visarga before च → श्; च आदेशौ → चादेशौ — two a-vowels,
one long; अगच्छत् इति → अगच्छदिति — a final त् voiced before a vowel; इति अत्र → इत्यत्र — इ before
a vowel → य्; अत्र अकारः → अत्राकारः — two a-vowels, one long; अकारः आगमः → अकार आगमः — -अः before a
vowel other than अ → -अ and a gap; स्थाने ओकारः → स्थान ओकारः — -ए before a vowel → -अ and a gap.
A comma and a daṇḍa stop every seam: आदेशौ, stands in its pause form. The quoted form अगच्छत् is
joined to the इति after it, as Chapter 1 taught.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

गच्छति यानी "जाता है", और अगच्छत् यानी "गया" — इन दोनों को साथ रखिए। अगच्छत् के आगे एक अ है, जो गच्छति में नहीं
है। पीछे -ति का इ ग़ायब है। और -ति स्वयं भी किसी और चीज़ के स्थान पर आया है। एक ही शब्द में
तीन अलग-अलग काम हुए — कुछ जोड़ा गया, कुछ हटाया गया, और कुछ किसी के स्थान पर रखा गया।

पाणिनि के अनुसार हर सूत्र इन्हीं तीन में से कोई एक काम करता है:

- **आदेश** — किसी अंश के स्थान पर दूसरा अंश रखना। जिसके स्थान पर रखा जाता है, वह सूत्र में
  षष्ठी विभक्ति में होता है — सूत्र 1.1.49, जो पहले अध्याय से आपका है। जैसे सूर्य + उदयः में अ और उ —
  दोनों के स्थान पर एक ओ: सूर्योदयः।
- **आगम** — कोई नया वर्ण जोड़ना। आगम कहाँ जुड़ेगा, यह उसका अपना **इत्** बताता है: जिस आगम में
  ट् इत् हो, वह आरम्भ में जुड़ता है; जिसमें क् इत् हो, वह अन्त में — सूत्र 1.1.46।
  अगच्छत् का अ ऐसा ही आगम है — अट्; उसका ट् इत् है, इसलिए वह गच्छ के आगे आता है।
- **लोप** — किसी अंश का हट जाना, दिखाई न देना — सूत्र 1.1.60। जिस सूत्र में "लोपः" शब्द हो, वह
  लोप करता है। -ति का इ इसी तरह हटता है, और हर इत् भी — 1.3.9 तस्य लोपः।

अब **गुण** और **वृद्धि**। तीसरे खण्ड में और हिन्दी की स्कूली व्याकरण में ये दो सन्धियों के नाम
थे — "गुण सन्धि", "वृद्धि सन्धि"। पाणिनि के यहाँ ये सन्धियों के नाम नहीं, स्वरों के नाम हैं:

- सूत्र 1.1.1 के अनुसार आ, ऐ, औ — इन तीन स्वरों का नाम **वृद्धि** है।
- सूत्र 1.1.2 के अनुसार अ, ए, ओ — इन तीन का नाम **गुण** है।
- सूत्र 1.1.3 बताता है कि जब कोई सूत्र केवल "गुण" या "वृद्धि" कहे, और यह न बताए कि किसके स्थान
  पर, तो वह इ, उ, ऋ, ऌ के स्थान पर आता है — ह्रस्व हों या दीर्घ।

तो गुण एक आदेश है: इ के स्थान पर ए, उ के स्थान पर ओ, ऋ के स्थान पर अर्। गुण का अर्थ "अ जोड़ना"
नहीं है — इ + अ नहीं, सीधे इ के स्थान पर ए। सबसे पास वाला स्वर चुना जाता है — सूत्र 1.1.50: इ तालु से
बोला जाता है, इसलिए अ ए ओ में से तालु वाला ए।

1.x पुस्तक ने भू + ति → भवति के बारे में लिखा था "where गुण inserts व" — यानी गुण व जोड़ता है। ऐसा नहीं है। यहाँ दो आदेश हैं: पहले
ऊ के स्थान पर ओ, यानी गुण — भू → भो; फिर स्वर से पहले ओ के स्थान पर अव्, यानी अयादि, सूत्र 6.1.78 — भो + अति
→ भव् + अति → भवति। कुछ भी जोड़ा नहीं गया; व अव् का हिस्सा है।

हिन्दी में भी "गुण" और "वृद्धि" अपने रोज़ के अर्थ रखते हैं — गुण यानी विशेषता, वृद्धि यानी बढ़ोतरी।
आज के नए शब्द इन्हीं अर्थों में भी वाक्य बनाते हैं: **वृद्धि** — बढ़ोतरी; उसका उलटा **हानि** — घाटा;
**कोप** — क्रोध; **आगम** — आना, और व्याकरण में जोड़ा गया वर्ण; **लोप** — हट जाना। जैसे —
धनस्य वृद्धिर्हानिश्च: धन की वृद्धि और हानि।

## 8. English Explanation

The recipe of this volume, once more: forms you own, the question they raise, the tools that
answer it, the derivations with the teacher, the tools run by you, then production. Today's one
grammar point: a rule does one of three things — it replaces, it adds, or it deletes.

### Step 1 — the forms you own

Three forms, and your three §3 prompts:

| owned form | where owned | what you already know |
|---|---|---|
| अगच्छत् | v3.c03.l03 | the imperfect of गच्छति: an अ in front, -त् for -ति |
| भवति | v1.c04.l01; भो + अति in v3.c04.l04 and v4.c01.l03 | ओ before a vowel → अव् [6.1.78] |
| सूर्योदयः | v3.c04.l01 | सूर्य + उदयः, joined by the guṇa join (6.1.87) |

Prompt 1 gave अगच्छत्, अपठत्, ऐच्छत्; prompt 2 named the joins of सूर्योदयः and तत्रैव "guṇa" and
"vṛddhi"; prompt 3 read 6.1.87's three words: आत् "after अ", गुणः "the result", [अचि] "before a
vowel".

### Step 2 — the question

**What exactly happened in each?** In अगच्छत् an अ appeared from nowhere and an इ vanished. In
सूर्योदयः two vowels became one. In भवति an ओ became अव्. Are these one kind of thing or
several? And prompt 3's गुणः, "the result" — the result is *a guṇa vowel*; but what is a guṇa
vowel, if not the name of a join?

### Step 3 — the three operations

> **According to Pāṇini, a rule does one of three things.** It **replaces** — the new item is a
> substitute, आदेशः, and what it replaces stands in the genitive (1.1.49, Chapter 1). It **adds**
> — the new sound is an augment, आगमः, and the augment's own marker says where it goes (1.1.46).
> It **deletes** — the item disappears, लोपः, and the rule says so with the word लोपः [1.1.60].

The table of the three is §15's operations table: learn it whole. The tools, each printed once
here with its tier. **Q** is read with its padaccheda and recognised by its gist; **B** is a step
of the भवति derivation; **D** is the authority for one derivation step, never recalled by number;
**K** is a reference card, looked up and never assessed — this lesson has the chapter's first.

**1. Where an augment goes — D, 1.1.46 आद्यन्तौ टकितौ** (ādyantau ṭakitau).

English: "An augment marked with ट् goes at the beginning, one marked with क् at the end [of the
item it is added to]." [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.46]; [REF:kashika 1.1.46]

**2. What deletion is — D, 1.1.60 अदर्शनं लोपः** (adarśanaṃ lopaḥ).

English: "Disappearance [of what would have appeared] is called lopa." [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.60];
[REF:kashika 1.1.60]

**3. One for two — D, 6.1.84 एकः पूर्वपरयोः** (ekaḥ pūrvaparayoḥ).

English: "[From here on:] one [substitute] in place of the earlier and the later." A heading: it
stands over the vowel joins you own, 6.1.87 and 6.1.88 among them. [REF:ashtadhyayi 6.1.84];
[REF:kashika 6.1.84]

Chapter 1 printed this heading as the bracket [पूर्वपरयोः] beside 6.1.101 and promised its sūtra
for today (v4.c01.l03 §15): this is it. पूर्वपरयोः is a genitive dual — "in place of the two, the
earlier and the later" — so the heading too is a genitive doing its Chapter 1 job.

**Re-read [Q, owned]: 1.1.49 षष्ठी स्थानेयोगा** — "a genitive means 'in place of'", worded in
v4.c01.l03 §8. It is today's mark of a substitute.

Two more points. Not every augment is ṭ-marked or k-marked: an augment marked with म् goes after
the last vowel (1.1.47, preview: c05.l03). And deletion has kinds named for affixes; those names
are in Teacher Notes.

### Step 4 — the two grades

**4. vṛddhi — Q, 1.1.1 वृद्धिरादैच्** (vṛddhir ādaic).

Padaccheda: वृद्धिः [प्रथमा: the name] · आत्-ऐच् [प्रथमा: what is named — आत्, long आ written
with त्, tapara, so long आ only (c01.l02); ऐच्, the pratyāhāra ऐ औ].

English: "आ, ऐ and औ are called vṛddhi." [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.1]; [REF:kashika 1.1.1]

**5. guṇa — B, 1.1.2 अदेङ् गुणः** (adeṅ guṇaḥ).

Padaccheda: अत्-एङ् [प्रथमा: what is named — short अ, tapara, and एङ्, the pratyāhāra ए ओ] · गुणः
[प्रथमा: the name].

English: "अ, ए and ओ are called guṇa." [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.2]; [REF:kashika 1.1.2]

**A definition has two nominatives.** In a rule that does something, the nominative names the
result (Chapter 1). In a definition like 1.1.1 or 1.1.2, both words are nominative: one is **the
name** (वृद्धिः, गुणः), the other is **what it names** (आत्-ऐच्, अत्-एङ्). Neither is a result.

**6. In place of what — B, 1.1.3 इको गुणवृद्धी** (iko guṇavṛddhī).

Padaccheda: इकः [षष्ठी: "in place of an इक् vowel": इ उ ऋ ऌ, short or long] · गुणवृद्धी [प्रथमा,
dual: "guṇa and vṛddhi"].

English: "Guṇa and vṛddhi, when a rule names them without saying what they replace, replace an
इक् vowel." [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.3]; [REF:kashika 1.1.3]

So a guṇa vowel is a substitute, and the vowels it replaces are इ उ ऋ ऌ. Which of अ ए ओ comes?
The one nearest to the vowel replaced (1.1.50, your tool since Chapter 1): ए for इ, ओ for उ. The
full ladder is §15's guṇa/vṛddhi table.

**Card — K, 1.1.51 उरण् रपरः** (uraṇ raparaḥ). "Where an अण् vowel replaces ऋ, it comes with र्
[अर्, आर्]." [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.51]; [REF:kashika 1.1.51] It is a reference card: it says why
the guṇa of ऋ is अर्, not a bare अ. It becomes a derivation tool in c03.l02; nothing asks you to
quote it.

**The ladder.** guṇa is a substitute: इ → ए, never इ + अ. Then भू → भो → भव् is two
substitutions, not one insertion:

1. भू → भो — guṇa in place of ऊ [1.1.3 with 1.1.2; the rule that orders this guṇa: 7.3.84, c03.l02].
2. भो + अति → भव् + अति — 6.1.78 एचोऽयवायावः [owned]: ओ → अव् before a vowel. Joined: **भवति**.

### Step 5 — the derivations, with the teacher

All three are in §13 in table form; work down them with the teacher, one line at a time.

**(a) Derive, according to Pāṇini, सूर्य + उदयः → सूर्योदयः.** One substitute stands for both
vowels (6.1.84); after अ, before a vowel, it is a guṇa vowel (6.1.87 with 1.1.2); of अ ए ओ, ओ is
nearest to उ [1.1.50]. A substitute, nothing else.

**(b) Derive, according to Pāṇini, गच्छ- + लङ् → अगच्छत्.** All three operations in one word: the
l-affix ल् is replaced by तिप् — a substitute; the प् of तिप् is a marker and drops, and so does the
इ of ति — two deletions; अट् is added, and its ट् puts it at the beginning — an augment. Two of today's
D tools are written out here:

- **D — 3.4.100 इतश्च** (itaś ca) — "And [the] इ [of a P ending of a ṅ-marked l-affix is
  dropped]." [ङितः, carried from 3.4.99; and, from an earlier rule — Teacher Notes — लोपः "deletion" in the P endings].
  [REF:ashtadhyayi 3.4.100]; [REF:kashika 3.4.100] P is परस्मैपदम्, the active set of endings
  you have used since Volume 3 (-ति, -तः, -अन्ति). लङ्, the l-affix of the imperfect, is
  ṅ-marked: its ङ् is a marker, as the anubandha card of c03.l03 will collect.
- **D — 6.4.71 लुङ्लङ्लृङ्क्ष्वडुदात्तः** (luṅlaṅlṛṅkṣv aḍ udāttaḥ) — "Before luṅ, laṅ and lṛṅ,
  [the aṅga gets the augment] अट्, [which is] accented (accent: Volume 5)." [REF:ashtadhyayi
  6.4.71]; [REF:kashika 6.4.71] Of the three l-affixes you need only laṅ today.

The step ल् → तिप् is 3.4.78 [preview: c03.l01]: the eighteen endings are Chapter 3's. Take it as
given.

**(c) Read ऐच्छत्.** Volume 3 taught ऐच्छत् whole (v3.c03.l03). Today its one difference from
अगच्छत् has rules: इच्छ- begins with a vowel, so the augment is आट्, not अट् [its rule: Teacher Notes]; its ट् still puts it at the
beginning; and आ + इ → ऐ is vṛddhi — a substitute (its rule: Teacher Notes). Read the table in
§13; you are not asked to derive it.

### Step 7 — the 1.x card, corrected in the open

The 1.x book (p. 89) printed a card on the three operations. Read it against today's tools.

> The 1.x book printed "Example: भू + ति → भवति (where गुण inserts व)" as an example of insertion
> (p. 89). guṇa is a substitute: ऊ → ओ; it inserts nothing. The व comes from a second substitute,
> ओ → अव् [6.1.78, which you met in Chapter 1]. Nothing in भवति is an augment. The book's next
> example, the अट् augment of अगच्छत्, is a true augment, and this lesson keeps it. The same card
> gave as its example of deletion "रामस् + अहम् → रामोऽहम्" and said that "for the next word, अ
> underwent lopa (replaced by avagraha ऽ)". In Pāṇini's terms nothing is dropped there: -अः before
> अ gives ओ [Volume 3], and then ओ + अ → ओ is one substitute for both (6.1.109 एङः पदान्तादति,
> under 6.1.84). The ऽ is a printing sign that shows where the अ stood, not an operation. And its
> example of a ट्-marked augment, "नुम् augment", is wrong: नुम् is marked with म्, so it goes after
> the last vowel (1.1.47, preview, c05.l03); अट् is the ट्-marked augment.

Whitney's account (§5) agrees on the substance: both accounts give ओ from ऊ; neither inserts a
व.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — the sounds inside today's rules: the ladder said aloud, the augment heard, the
syllabic ऋ of वृद्धिः. Audio: 🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-4/v4c02l02-ladder.mp3` (drill 1,
each row twice).

**Drill 1 — the ladder.** Say each row slowly, simple vowel, then guṇa, then vṛddhi:

1. इ — ए — ऐ /i eː ɐi̯/
2. उ — ओ — औ /u oː ɐu̯/
3. ऋ — अर् — आर् /r̩ ɐɾ ɑːɾ/

Then, without audio, the fourth row once: ऌ — अल् — आल् /l̩ ɐl ɑːl/. Listen to what changes. ए and
ओ are long and pure: the tongue (for ए) and the lips (for ओ) hold one position from start to end.
ऐ and औ move: each starts from the short अ /ɐ/ and glides. That is guṇa and vṛddhi heard as
quality and length — not as an extra syllable. There is no "इ-अ" in ए.

**Drill 2 — the augment heard.** Say गच्छति, then अगच्छत्:

1. गच्छति /gɐt͡ɕt͡ɕʰɐt̪i/
2. अगच्छत् /ɐgɐt͡ɕt͡ɕʰɐt̪/

The augment is the first syllable you hear, अ-. Then read the table of अगच्छत् [§13] aloud, naming
each marker as you pass it — "लङ्: the ङ् is a marker", "तिप्: the प् is a marker", "अट्: the ट् is a
marker" — and never sounding a marker in the result. What you say at the end is a-gac-chat, with
a short final त्.

**Drill 3 — वृद्धिः.** वृद्धिः /ʋr̩d̪d̪ʱih/: the ऋ is a vowel, the tip of the tongue near the ridge
for its whole length, not "ri". Say वृद्धिः, गुणः, वृद्धिः, गुणः, and hear the ऋ carry the first
syllable as a vowel does.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — what the mouth does on each rung of the ladder. The replace-add-delete image
(`planned:assets/images/volume-4/v4c02l02-replace-add-delete.svg`) shows the three operations;
the ladder itself you feel in your own mouth.

1. **इ → ए.** For इ the tongue is high at the palate. For ए it drops a little, still at the
   palate, and stays put: /eː/. Nothing is added in front of it; the tongue moves to one new place.
2. **ए → ऐ.** For ऐ the jaw opens to the अ position first, then the tongue rises toward इ: a
   glide, /ɐi̯/.
3. **उ → ओ → औ.** The lips are round and tight for उ, round and a little open for ओ /oː/, held.
   For औ the mouth opens to अ, then the lips round: /ɐu̯/.
4. **ऋ → अर्.** For ऋ the tongue tip curls up toward the ridge and the sound is a vowel. For अर्
   the mouth opens for अ /ɐ/, then the tongue tip taps once: /ɾ/.

Mirror check: say ए ऐ ए ऐ. On ए nothing moves; on ऐ you see the jaw drop and close.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; they feed the quiz distractors.

1. **"guṇa = add an अ."** *Fix:* guṇa is a substitute: इ → ए, never इ + अ. Run the substitution
   ladder (§18 exercise 1).
2. **"In भवति guṇa inserts व"** — the 1.x card. *Fix:* two substitutes: ऊ → ओ — guṇa; ओ → अव्
   (6.1.78). Nothing is inserted.
3. **Calling the ऽ of रामोऽहम् a deletion.** *Fix:* ओ + अ → ओ is one substitute for both (6.1.109);
   ऽ is a printing sign.
4. **Placing every augment at the front.** *Fix:* the augment's marker decides: ट् → beginning, क्
   → end (1.1.46); म् → after the last vowel (1.1.47, preview).
5. **Reading 1.1.1's two nominatives as "the result".** *Fix:* in a definition the nominatives
   are the name (वृद्धिः) and what it names (आत्-ऐच्).
6. **Giving ऋ's guṇa as अ or रि.** *Fix:* ऋ → अर्, with र् by 1.1.51 (the card).

## 17. Story

कथा — the grammarians' line, 2 continued: Patañjali names the three operations.

Lesson 1 read, from the opening of Patañjali's Great Commentary, his short list of the purposes
of grammar. Two sentences later the same passage says what one of those purposes, the
preservation of the Veda, needs. Its first three words are today's lesson.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** According to Patañjali, knowing deletion, augment and the change of
> sounds is what lets one guard the Vedas. [REF:mahabhashya Paspaśāhnika]
>
> Mahābhāṣya, Paspaśāhnika (Kielhorn I, p. 1, l. 14 – p. 2, l. 2); text of the GRETIL e-text:
>
> लोपागमवर्णविकारज्ञो हि सम्यग्वेदान्परिपालयिष्यति। (lopāgamavarṇavikārajño hi samyag vedān paripālayiṣyati.)
>
> लोप-आगम-वर्ण-विकार-ज्ञः हि सम्यक् वेदान् परिपालयिष्यति। (lopa-āgama-varṇa-vikāra-jñaḥ hi samyak vedān paripālayiṣyati.)
>

Read the long first word member by member, in the pause line: लोप "deletion" — आगम "augment" —
वर्ण "sound" — विकार "change" — ज्ञः "one who knows". Two of its members are today's headwords; वर्ण
you have owned since Volume 1. Gloss line for the rest: वर्णविकारः "change of sounds"; हि "for";
सम्यक् "properly" (Volume 3); वेदान् "the Vedas"; परिपालयिष्यति "will guard" [a future causative, ○:
read with its gloss, not taught].

The e-text prints the sentence word by word, in pause form; the joined line is the course's: -ज्ञः

## 20. Revision

पुनरावृत्तिः — one screen to remember.

> **Replace, add, delete.** According to Pāṇini, a rule **replaces** — a substitute, आदेशः,
> named in the nominative, what it replaces in the genitive (1.1.49); **adds** — an augment,
> आगमः, placed by its marker: ट् at the beginning, क् at the end (1.1.46); or **deletes** —
> लोपः, "disappearance" (1.1.60). Two vowels become one under the heading एकः पूर्वपरयोः [6.1.84].
> **guṇa and vṛddhi** are names of vowels: vṛddhi = आ ऐ औ [1.1.1], guṇa = अ ए ओ [1.1.2]; named
> alone, they replace इ उ ऋ ऌ [1.1.3], the nearest one coming (1.1.50): इ → ए, ऐ · उ → ओ, औ ·
> ऋ → अर्, आर् · ऌ → अल्, आल्. guṇa is never "add अ". **भवति**: भू → भो by guṇa, then भो + अति → भव् + अति by 6.1.78
> — two substitutes, nothing inserted. **अगच्छत्**: ल् → तिप् — a substitute —, the इ of ति dropped
> (3.4.100), अट् added at the front (6.4.71, 1.1.46). **The ledger:** five new — 532; cells
> drilled: 3; tools added: 8; toolbox: 33.

**What students find hard.** "guṇa = add a" is the main risk: the ladder of exercise 1 is the
cure, and the crossed-out "इ + अ" on the board. Never accept "ए is अ + इ" as an answer in this

avagraha: learners who learned "ऽ = a deleted अ" need to see 6.1.109 as one substitute for two
sounds, the same kind of operation as the guṇa join. Hindi-medium learners know "गुण सन्धि" from
