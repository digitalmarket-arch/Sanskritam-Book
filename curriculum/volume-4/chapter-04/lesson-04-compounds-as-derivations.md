## 1. Hook

You have read जलज "water-born; lotus" since v1.c03.l02, and खग "bird", the "sky-goer", since
v1.c03.l01; Volume 3 filed both, on its recognition card, as upapada compounds (v3.c05.l04). Volume
3 also taught you to build a compound from its vigraha in four kinds — and left for this chapter
"the words that change their last member in a compound (-राज, -सख)" (v3.c05.l04 §15). Its dove-king
was कपोतानां नृपः, in two words.

Put two phrases side by side. राज्ञः पुरुषः, "the king's man", makes one word, राजपुरुषः: the ending
of राज्ञः is gone, and so is the न् of राजन्. कपोतानां राजा, "the king of the doves", should then make
\*कपोतराजा — and it makes कपोतराजः, which ends like रामः. Where did the न् go in the first word, and
where did the -अ come from in the second? A compound, it turns out, is a derivation, with steps and
rules like any other.

## 4. Historical Context

the rule before 2.2.38; 2.1.4, the next rule, opens a second heading inside it; and under both come

of Book 5, among the affixes that come at a compound's end; and 6.3.46 in the third quarter of Book 6.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — what the language does: Whitney on the first member of a compound and on
compound accent, beside Pāṇini's rules; and the 1.x glosses this lesson corrects.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Whitney, on a stem as the first member of a compound: stems in *an* and *in*
> "usually … lose their final *n*, and are combined as if *a* and *i* were their proper finals".
> [REF:whitney1889 §1249]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Whitney: dependent compounds whose last member is an ordinary noun "are
> regularly and usually accented on the final syllable" (§1267); and "The possessive compound is
> distinguished from its substrate, the determinative, generally by a difference of accent … oftenest,
> the possessive has as a compound the natural accent of its prior member" (§1295).
> [REF:whitney1889 §1267]; [REF:whitney1889 §1295]

Read Whitney beside Pāṇini. Whitney's first-member rule is today's 8.2.7 seen from outside: राज- in
राजपुरुषः and जन्म- in जन्मदिनम् are the stems राजन्, जन्मन् with their न् gone. Pāṇini reaches the same
shape by another road: the first member is a pada, and a pada-final न् of a nominal base drops. His
"usually" leaves room for exceptions; the course meets none. Whitney's determinative is the
tradition's tatpuruṣa, his possessive its bahuvrīhi (v3.c05.l04 §5), and the accent he describes is
the Vedic accent: on the last syllable of the tatpuruṣa, on the first member of the bahuvrīhi.
According to Pāṇini the same, in two card rules (§8 Step 6). The two accounts pick out the same
forms. The quiz asks Whitney's as FACT, Pāṇini's only "According to Pāṇini …", and of the accent
only the meaning; no accent mark is printed in this course before Volume 5.

> **The 1.x glosses, corrected in the open (IR-026).** The 1.x book printed: 'With one accent →
> "May Indra's slayer prosper" (Tatpuruṣa) / With different accent → "May the slayer of Indra
> prosper" (Bahuvrīhi) / A single accent change → opposite meaning!' (p. 82). The two glosses say one
> thing twice: "Indra's slayer" is "the slayer of Indra". The contrast is one of kind, and of
> meaning. The tatpuruṣa is इन्द्रस्य शत्रुः, "Indra's enemy" — his slayer; the bahuvrīhi is इन्द्रः
> शत्रुः यस्य सः, "he whose enemy is Indra" — the contrast v3.c05.l04's Teacher Notes already drew,
> "slayer of Indra" against "whose slayer is Indra". The story behind the word is not told here: the
> course's references hold no source for it.

- **Step 0.** The phrase: जल with the locative ending ङि, and the root जन्.
- **Steps 1–2.** 3.2.97 gives the kṛt ड; its ड् is a marker: जन् + अ.
- **Steps 3–4.** 6.4.143 with 1.1.64: the ṭi अन् drops before the ḍ-marked affix — ज् + अ, joined: ज.

- **Step 6.** 2.4.71: the ending inside, ङि, is deleted — जल + ज.
- **Steps 7–8.** The neuter nominative singular, by your rules of c02.l01: **जलजम्**, "lotus".

### Step 4 — a final affix of the compound's own

**9. The compound's own affix — D, 5.4.91 राजाहस्सखिभ्यष्टच्** (rājāhassakhibhyaṣ ṭac).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): तत्पुरुषस्य, 5.4.86] [headings (data file): प्रत्ययः, 3.1.1; परश्च, 3.1.2; तद्धिताः, 4.1.76] (further headings: Teacher Notes)

English: "After a tatpuruṣa ending in राजन्, अहन् or सखि, the samāsānta टच्." [REF:ashtadhyayi
5.4.91]; [REF:kashika 5.4.91]

By its case code: राजाहस्सखिभ्यः, an ablative plural — "after राजन्, अहन्, सखि"; टच्, a nominative —
the affix. तत्पुरुषस्य is carried from 5.4.86: only at the end of a tatpuruṣa. And lesson 3's heading
4.1.76 stands over the rule: टच् is a taddhita, of the kind the tradition calls samāsānta, "at a
compound's end" (that heading is in Teacher Notes). Its च् is a marker by 1.3.3 and its ट् by a rule
in Teacher Notes: what remains is अ. Before that अ the base is bha, and the अन् of राजन् goes — a
labelled step, its rule in Teacher Notes. So कपोतराजन् + अ → कपोतराज् + अ → कपोतराज, a stem in अ:
**कपोतराजः**, declined as रामः. राजपुरुषः takes no टच्: there राजन् is the first member, not the last.

The Kāśikā's first example under the rule is महाराजः, and for सखि it gives राजसखः — राज्ञः सखा, "the
king's friend": before the अ of टच्, the इ of the bha base सखि drops, by lesson 3's 6.4.148, as the
final vowel of a bha base drops before a taddhita.

**With the teacher.** §13, derivation (c), takes कपोतानां राजा to कपोतराजः: the compound and its
inner deletion as in derivation (a); then टच्, its markers, the अन् gone; then the ending.

**10. महत् → महा — D, 6.3.46 आन्महतः समानाधिकरणजातीययोः** (ān mahataḥ samānādhikaraṇajātīyayoḥ).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): —] [headings (data file): —] (further headings: Teacher Notes)

English: "Before a second member in the same case, महत् → महा." [REF:ashtadhyayi 6.3.46];
[REF:kashika 6.3.46]

By its case code: आत्, a nominative — the result, आ; महतः, a genitive — "in place of महत्";
समानाधिकरण-जातीययोः, a locative dual — "before [a second member] in the same case", its second word
an affix the course does not use. By 1.1.52 [D, c03.l02] the substitute replaces the last sound of
महत्, its त्: मह + आ, and the two like vowels make one long one (6.1.101 [owned]): महा. So महान् राजा,
both in the nominative, gives महा + राजन्, and 5.4.91 adds टच्: **महाराजः** — the derivation you write
yourself (§18 exercise 3). Not before a genitive: the Kāśikā's counter-example is महत्पुत्रः [display]
"a great man's son" — महतः पुत्रः, the two cases differ.

### Step 6 — the accent card (IR-026)

**The card lines — K, 6.1.223 समासस्य** (samāsasya) **and K, 6.2.1 बहुव्रीहौ प्रकृत्या पूर्वपदम्**
(bahuvrīhau prakṛtyā pūrvapadam). English: "A compound is accented on its last syllable"; "In a
bahuvrīhi, the first member keeps its own accent." [REF:ashtadhyayi 6.1.223]; [REF:kashika 6.1.223];
[REF:ashtadhyayi 6.2.1]; [REF:kashika 6.2.1] — cards: looked up, never assessed.

So, according to Pāṇini, इन्द्रशत्रुः read as a tatpuruṣa, इन्द्रस्य शत्रुः, is accented on its last
syllable; read as a bahuvrīhi, इन्द्रः शत्रुः यस्य सः, it keeps the accent of इन्द्र. The verse of §4
says what the wrong accent did. The Mahābhāṣya also gives स्थूलपृषती [display], said of a cow for the
sacrifice — "stout and dappled", or "whose spots are big" — as a word whose kind, it says, only the

What is asked is the meaning only: इन्द्रस्य शत्रुः, "Indra's enemy", against इन्द्रः शत्रुः यस्य सः,
"he whose enemy is Indra". No accent mark is printed or asked: accent is Volume 5's.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — one breath for one word, the द्ध of युद्धम्, the retroflex ड of the affix against the
dental द, and the न्म of जन्म. The compound-steps image
(`planned:assets/images/volume-4/v4c04l04-compound-steps.svg`) shows two padas closing into one;
say the vigraha at the open bracket and the compound at the closed one.

1. **A compound is one word.** One breath, no pause at the seam: राज-पुरुषः said as one, with no gap
   between ज and प, as you say रामः. The pause belongs to the vigraha, between राज्ञः and पुरुषः.
2. **द्ध in युद्धम्.** /d̪d̪ʱ/: the tongue tip at the back of the upper teeth for द; hold the closure,
   then release it with a breathy puff for ध. Two consonants, one closure — not \*युधम्.
3. **ड against द.** The affix ड /ɖ/ is retroflex: the tongue tip curled back to the roof of the mouth.
   The द of दिनम् /d̪/ is dental: the tip at the teeth. Say ड — द, ड — द.
4. **न्म in जन्म.** /n̪m/: the tongue tip at the teeth for न्, then the lips close for म while the voice
   runs on through the nose.

Mirror check: say राज्ञः पुरुषः, then राजपुरुषः. In the first the lips part and the breath stops after
the visarga; in the second the lips close for प straight from ज, with no stop of breath.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; they feed the quiz distractors.

1. **\*राजन्पुरुषः.** *Fix:* राजपुरुषः. The first member is a pada, its ending deleted but still
   counting: its final न् drops (8.2.7). So too जन्मदिनम्, never \*जन्मन्दिनम्.
2. **\*राज्ञःपुरुषः, the vigraha's ending kept.** *Fix:* राजपुरुषः. The ending inside a compound is
   deleted (2.4.71); only the whole takes an ending.
3. **\*कपोतराजा.** *Fix:* कपोतराजः. -राजन् at a tatpuruṣa's end takes टच्, by 5.4.91: the compound is a
   stem in अ, declined as रामः.
4. **\*महत्राजः.** *Fix:* महाराजः. महत् → महा before a member in the same case (6.3.46) — and टच् after
   राजन्.
5. **\*जलजन्.** *Fix:* जलजम्. Before ड, the ṭi of जन्, अन्, drops (6.4.143 with 1.1.64): the vowel goes
   too, not only the न्.
6. **"Compounds have no grammar."** *Fix:* every compound has steps — the heading and the pair
   (2.1.3–4), one nominal base (1.2.46), the inner endings deleted (2.4.71), the pada's own rules for
   the first member (8.2.7).

this one.
