## 1. Hook

You have written कृत "done" since v1.c02.l03 and गत "gone" since v1.c03.l01, each as a whole word.
Volume 3 read मया कृतम् "done by me" on its R2 card and told you, in v3.c02.l05: "You read R2; you
do not build it (v4.c04)." This is that lesson. Today you build it.

Put three forms of one verb side by side: गच्छति, गमिष्यति, गत. गमिष्यति keeps the म् of the root
गम्; गच्छति has turned it into छ, in lesson 2 of Chapter 3; गत has nothing in its place at all. Why is
"gone" गत, with no trace of the म्? One letter answers, and you never hear it: the क् of क्त.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — where today's rules stand in the Aṣṭādhyāyī, and the book the reading comes
from.

The rules of today's affixes stand in Book 3. Lesson 1 of Chapter 3 gave you 3.1.91 धातोः, a heading
that, according to the Kāśikā, stands to the end of Book 3: from there on, the affixes come after a
root. Two rules after it, one rule names every such affix that is not a verb ending.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** According to Pāṇini, an affix that comes after a root and is not a verb
> ending is called kṛt (3.1.93 [D]); the participles, the absolutive, the infinitive and the agent
> noun of this chapter are all kṛt. [REF:ashtadhyayi 3.1.93]; [REF:kashika 3.1.93]

The two participle rules stand in Book 3's second quarter. 3.2.102 निष्ठा stands under the heading
भूते, "in past time"; 3.2.124, a little later in the same quarter, under the heading वर्तमाने, "for
present time" — the 3.2.123 वर्तमाने लट् that gave you laṭ in Chapter 3. The name niṣṭhā itself is
given in Book 1, among the names: 1.1.26. The rules that shape the root before क्त stand far from
these. 6.1.15 is in Book 6's first quarter, one rule before Chapter 3's 6.1.16. 6.4.37 is under the
heading अङ्गस्य [6.4.1, c02.l03]. 8.2.30 is in the last three quarters of the grammar, from 8.2 on,
where a rule counts as not yet applied for every rule before it and so acts after them (c03.l03): it
is the last line of the derivation that uses it.

The reading of §13 comes from a book of fables.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** The Hitopadeśa, the book of fables the reading comes from, is dated c.
> 9th–12th century CE on the scholarly estimate. [REF:hitopadesha]

In its first book a king of pigeons, Citragrīva, sees his flock drawn to grain scattered in a lonely
forest and tells them a story. Its first sentence is today's reading; lesson 2 reads the next, and the
Chapter 4 exam a later part of the story.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — what the language does: the -त form looks back on an action, and whom it
describes depends on the verb.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Whitney: the participle in -त, "when coming from transitive verbs, qualifies
> anything as having endured the action expressed by the verb: thus, दत्त dattá given; उक्त uktá
> spoken … When made from an intransitive or neuter verb, the same participle … has no passive but
> only an indefinite past sense: thus, गत gatá gone; भूत bhūtá been; पतित patitá fallen."
> [REF:whitney1889 §952]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The -तवत् form, in the later language, "is chiefly used predicatively, and
> oftenest without copula expressed, or with the value of a personal verb-form in a past tense";
> Whitney's example, *māṁ na kaścid dṛṣṭavān* "no one has seen (or saw) me". [REF:whitney1889 §960]

Read the two together. दत्त, उक्त — "given", "spoken" — come from verbs that take an object, and they
describe the thing that underwent the action. गत, भूत, पतित come from verbs of motion or verbs with no
object, and they describe the one who acted: "gone", "fallen". The -तवत् form is the past of the doer:
Whitney's *dṛṣṭavān* is "has seen", said of the one who saw. These are the two frames of §8 Step 4.

The 1.x book's participle card (p. 104) calls क्त the "past passive participle" — "having been
[verb]ed". That is right for a verb that takes an object —
मया पुस्तकं पठितम् (mayā pustakaṃ paṭhitam) "the book (was) read by me" — and wrong for a verb of
motion or one with no object: गतः is "gone", not "been gone" (Whitney §952a). The lesson keeps the
English name and states its condition. It corrects two more 1.x lines: the "future participle" of
p. 105 (IR-024; §15 (4)), and p. 96's "Sutra 8.2.39: झलां जश् झशि" (IR-017, second half): in the data file 8.2.39 is झलां जशोऽन्ते and
8.4.53 is झलां जश् झशि — the rule that makes the ब् of लब्ध; with 8.2.40, which makes its ध, it
stands on the card [K] (§15, the card rules; §12).

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the day's idea in three lines, each printed joined, its pause form beneath, then
the gloss.

> कृदिति प्रत्ययो धातोः परः, तिङ् न। (kṛd iti pratyayo dhātoḥ paraḥ, tiṅ na.)
> कृत् इति प्रत्ययः धातोः परः, तिङ् न। (kṛt iti pratyayaḥ dhātoḥ paraḥ, tiṅ na.)
> "kṛt: an affix after a root, not a tiṅ." — कृत्: धातु के बाद आने वाला प्रत्यय, तिङ् नहीं।

> क्तस्य ककार इत्, अतः कृत इति रूपे गुणो न भवति। (ktasya kakāra it, ataḥ kṛta iti rūpe guṇo na bhavati.)
> क्तस्य ककारः इत्, अतः कृतः इति रूपे गुणः न भवति। (ktasya kakāraḥ it, ataḥ kṛtaḥ iti rūpe guṇaḥ na bhavati.)
> "The क् of क्त is a marker; so in the form कृत there is no guṇa." — क्त का ककार इत् है, इसलिए
> कृत रूप में गुण नहीं होता।

> गच्छन्बालकः, पठितं पुस्तकम्। (gacchan bālakaḥ, paṭhitaṃ pustakam.)
> गच्छन् बालकः, पठितम् पुस्तकम्। (gacchan bālakaḥ, paṭhitam pustakam.)
> "a boy going; a book read." — जाता हुआ लड़का, पढ़ी हुई पुस्तक।

Words owned: प्रत्ययः, धातुः [धातोः], परः, न, अतः, इति, रूप [रूपे, the locative of रूपम्], गुणः,
भवति, बालक [बालकः], पुस्तकम्; today's forms कृतः, गच्छन्, पठितम्. Labels, display only: कृत्, तिङ्,
क्त [क्तस्य], the -कार name ककारः, इत्.

The joins are Volume 3's: कृत् इति → कृदिति — त् → द् before a vowel, the quoted name joined to its
इति; प्रत्ययः धातोः → प्रत्ययो धातोः, गुणः न → गुणो न — -अः before a voiced sound → ओ; ककारः इत् →
ककार इत्, कृतः इति → कृत इति — -अः before a vowel other than अ drops its visarga, and a gap stays;
पठितम् पुस्तकम् → पठितं पुस्तकम् — म् → ं before a consonant; गच्छन् बालकः → गच्छन्बालकः — न् before
ब, written together. A comma and a daṇḍa stop every seam: परः, इत् and बालकः stand in their pause form
before one. तिङ् stays apart from न, as अच् हल् च stood apart in v4.c01.l01: a quoted list name is left
unjoined.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

कृत "किया हुआ" और गत "गया हुआ" आप पहले खण्ड से जानते हैं — पूरे शब्दों की तरह। तीसरे खण्ड में आपने
मया कृतम् पढ़ा था — "मेरे द्वारा किया गया" — और वहाँ कहा गया था कि इसे बनाना आप चौथे खण्ड में
सीखेंगे। आज वही दिन है। इस पाठ का नाम ही दो बातें कहता है: **किया हुआ** — भूतकाल का कृदन्त, और
**करता हुआ** — वर्तमान काल का कृदन्त।

**कृत् प्रत्यय।** धातु के बाद लगने वाला जो प्रत्यय तिङ् नहीं है, उसे पाणिनि **कृत्** कहते हैं —
सूत्र 3.1.93 कृदतिङ्। तिङ् प्रत्यय से क्रिया बनती है, जैसे गच्छति; कृत् प्रत्यय से ऐसा शब्द बनता है
जो संज्ञा या विशेषण की तरह विभक्ति लेता है, जैसे गतः, गता, गतम्। कृत् से बने शब्द को कृदन्त कहते हैं।

**निष्ठा: क्त और क्तवतु।** सूत्र 1.1.26 कहता है कि क्त और क्तवतु, दोनों का नाम **निष्ठा** है, और
सूत्र 3.2.102 कहता है कि निष्ठा भूतकाल में धातु के बाद आती है। क्त का क् **इत्** है — यानी वह चिह्न
जो काम करके हट जाता है — इसलिए बचता है -त: कृ + त → कृत "किया हुआ"। क्तवतु से बचता है -तवत्:
कृतवान् "जिसने किया"।

**क् के तीन काम।** क्त का क् सुनाई नहीं देता, पर धातु पर तीन काम करता है।
पहला — वह गुण नहीं होने देता, सूत्र 1.1.5 क्ङिति च: कृ का ऋ अर् नहीं बनता, इसलिए कृत; श्रुत भी इसी
तरह।
दूसरा — गम्, मन्, हन् जैसी धातुओं का अनुनासिक, यानी नाक से बोला जाने वाला अन्तिम व्यञ्जन, गिर जाता
है, सूत्र 6.4.37: गत, मत, हत। यही आज के पहले प्रश्न का उत्तर है — गत में गम् का म् क्यों नहीं।

बदल जाता है — उक्त, सुप्त। उक्त में एक नियम और लगता है: त से पहले च् क् बन जाता है, सूत्र 8.2.30
चोः कुः।

**दो ढाँचे।** -त वाला रूप विशेषण है। वह किससे मेल खाता है, यह क्रिया पर निर्भर है।
जिस क्रिया का कर्म होता है, उसमें -त रूप कर्म से मेल खाता है, और कर्ता तृतीया विभक्ति में आता है:
मया पुस्तकं पठितम् — "मेरे द्वारा पुस्तक पढ़ी गई"। यही तीसरे खण्ड का R2 ढाँचा है।
गति वाली क्रिया में, या जिस क्रिया का कर्म नहीं होता, उसमें -त रूप कर्ता से मेल खाता है: रामो वनं
गतः — "राम वन गया"; सीता वनं गता — "सीता वन गई"।
-तवत् वाला रूप सदा कर्ता से मेल खाता है, और कर्ता प्रथमा विभक्ति में रहता है: स कार्यं कृतवान् —
"उसने काम किया"; सा कार्यं कृतवती।

कुर्वाणः में -आन ही है।

### Step 4 — the two frames

With a verb that takes an object, the -त form agrees with the thing done and the doer stands in the
instrumental: **मया पुस्तकं पठितम्** (mayā pustakaṃ paṭhitam) — Volume 3's R2, now built. With a verb
of motion or a verb with no object, the -त form agrees with the doer:
**रामो वनं गतः** (rāmo vanaṃ gataḥ), **सीता वनं गता** (sītā vanaṃ gatā). The -तवत् form always agrees
with the doer: **स कार्यं कृतवान्** (sa kāryaṃ kṛtavān), **सा कार्यं कृतवती** (sā kāryaṃ kṛtavatī) —
Whitney §959: its feminine ends in -वती.

The -त form is an adjective: it takes its noun's gender, number and case, by Volume 3's tables — the
masculine as राम, the feminine in -ता as कमला, the neuter as वन. गतः, गता, गतम्; पठितः, पठिता, पठितम्.
The -तवत् form is ✓ in its four nominatives: -वान्, -वती, -वत्, and the masculine plural -वन्तः.

Card line [K]: 3.4.72 — the -त of a verb of motion or with no object is for the doer (§15, the card
rules); it is read off the card, never asked. What is asked is the frame, a fact of the language. The
feminine -ता: the affix of its -आ is lesson 3's [4.1.4, preview: l03].

- Bracket line: [carried (data file): कृत्, 3.1.93] [headings (data file): प्रत्ययः, 3.1.1; परश्च, 3.1.2; धातोः, 3.1.91; वर्तमाने, 3.2.123] (further headings: Teacher Notes)

सन् ब्राह्मणः (san brāhmaṇaḥ) "a brāhmaṇa who is" [REF:kashika 3.2.124]. The stories' use —

The ending names: शतृ's श् and ऋ are markers (1.3.8, 1.3.2 [B]), leaving -अत्; शानच्'s श् and च् are
markers too, and its -आन is ātmanepada (1.4.100 [D, owned]: "taṅ (the last nine endings) and āna are

card rule [K] — Whitney §584b: "After a tense-stem ending in a, the middle participial suffix is māna
instead of āna". After another stem it is -आन: कुर्वाणः, its ण् by 8.4.2 [D, owned]. The 1.x card's own
example of शतृ, "√पठ् → पठ् + अत् → पठन्त् → पठन्", is kept, with the sign shown: पठ + अत्, शप्'s अ
between root and affix.

A shortcut for the -अत् stem: take the 3rd plural present and drop its -इ — गच्छन्ति → गच्छन्त्-:
गच्छन् [m. nom. sg.], गच्छन्तः [m. nom. pl.]; the weak stem without न्, गच्छत्-, gives the locative
singular गच्छति — the same letters as the verb गच्छति, another word. The shortcut holds for every verb
on the card; ददति → ददत्- is listed, ◐ — Whitney §444: the class-3 participle has no न्. Derivation
(c) of §13 shows गच्छन् step by step; no task asks it (its अ + अ → अ is c06.l04's rule).

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the cluster of लब्ध, the curl of दृष्ट, the held त of दत्त. The done-and-doing image
(`planned:assets/images/volume-4/v4c04l01-done-and-doing.svg`) shows one root and two arrows, क्त to
"done" and शतृ to "doing", the क् of क्त lit with three small doors behind it; say each form as you
point to its door.

1. **ब्ध in लब्ध.** The lips close for ब and open on a breathy ध at the teeth — no vowel between:
   /lɐbd̪ʱɐ/. The breath comes with the ध, not with the ब.
2. **ष्ट in दृष्ट.** The tongue curls back for ष and stays curled for ट: /d̪r̩ʂʈɐ/. Do not let it
   flatten to a dental त between them.
3. **त्त in दत्त.** Hold the त, do not release it twice: the tongue tip stays on the teeth for both
   halves, /d̪ɐt̪t̪ɐ/.
4. **ण in कुर्वाणः.** After the long आ the tongue tip curls back for the retroflex nasal /ɳ/ — not the
   dental न of लभमानः: /kuɾʋɑːɳɐh/ against /lɐbʱɐmɑːn̪ɐh/.

Mirror check: say लब्ध — दत्त. In the first the lips meet; in the second they stay apart and only the
tongue works.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; they feed the quiz distractors.

1. **\*अहं पुस्तकं पठितः.** *Fix:* मया पुस्तकं पठितम् (mayā pustakaṃ paṭhitam), or
   अहं पुस्तकं पठितवान् (ahaṃ pustakaṃ paṭhitavān). With an object, -त agrees with the thing done;
   -तवत् agrees with the doer (§8 Step 4).
2. **\*सीता वनं गतः.** *Fix:* सीता वनं गता (sītā vanaṃ gatā). The -त form is an adjective: it takes
   the doer's gender.
3. **\*गम्तः.** *Fix:* गतः. The nasal of गम् drops before क्त: 6.4.37 [D].
4. **\*वक्तः.** *Fix:* उक्तः. व् → उ before a k-marked affix (6.1.15 [D]).
5. **\*करितः.** *Fix:* कृतः. कृ takes no इट् before क्त, and the क् blocks guṇa (1.1.5 [Q]).
6. **\*बालका ग्रामं गच्छन्हसन्ति.** *Fix:*
   बालका ग्रामं गच्छन्तो हसन्ति (bālakā grāmaṃ gacchanto hasanti). Many doers: the participle in the
   plural, गच्छन्तः.

**And one 1.x error, corrected in the open (IR-017, second half).** The 1.x book's pratyāhāra page
(p. 96) printed: "Sutra 8.2.39: झलां जश् झशि / → "झल् changes to जश् (voiced) before झश्" / → Final
stops voice when followed by another voiced stop / → लभ् + त = लब्ध (b+t → b+dh) / Sutra 8.4.53: झलां
जश् जशि / → Similar: voicing assimilation". In the data file 8.2.39 is झलां जशोऽन्ते, "at the end of a
pada" — owned since v3.c04.l03 — and the text the page gives it, झलां जश् झशि, "before a jhaś", is
8.4.53. लब्ध is not a pada's end: it takes 8.2.40 for its ध and 8.4.53 for its ब्, both on the card
[K] (§15, the card rules).

> **Done and doing.** According to Pāṇini, an affix after a root that is not a tiṅ is a kṛt (3.1.93).
> **The niṣṭhā** — क्त and क्तवतु, by 1.1.26 — comes for past time (3.2.102): -त "done, gone", -तवत् "has
