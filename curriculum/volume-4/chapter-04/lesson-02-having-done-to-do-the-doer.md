## 1. Hook

Since v3.c06.l01 you have read उक्त्वा "having said" and आगत्य "having come" on the R5 card, and
that lesson said of the card: "you read it, you find its doer, you never build it" (v3.c06.l01 §8).
Today you build them.

Put two forms of one verb side by side: गत्वा "having gone" and गन्तुम् "to go". Both start from
गम्, and both add an affix that begins with त्. Why does गत्वा lose the म् of गम्, while गन्तुम्
keeps its nasal, as न्? Lesson 1 met the answer in गत: a letter you never hear. Today it is the क्
of क्त्वा — and what happens where an affix has no क्.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — where today's rules stand in the grammar, how the Kāśikā tests the first of
them, and the 1.x lesson this one restores.

Today's nine tools stand in five books of the Aṣṭādhyāyī. 3.4.21 समानकर्तृकयोः पूर्वकाले is in the
last quarter of Book 3, three rules after 3.4.18, from which it carries the affix क्त्वा itself.
3.3.10 तुमुन्ण्वुलौ क्रियायां क्रियार्थायाम् is in the quarter before it, under the heading भविष्यति
"in the future" (3.3.3), which gives तुमुन् its time. 7.1.37 stands in Book 7, under c02.l03's
heading 6.4.1, अङ्गस्य; 6.4.38 stands right after lesson 1's 6.4.37 and carries its words;
6.1.71 is in the first quarter of Book 6. 1.1.39 and 1.1.40 stand in Book 1, a few rules after
1.1.37, which named the avyaya in v3.c05.l03, and both carry its word अव्ययम्. 8.3.24 and 8.4.58
stand in the last three quarters of the grammar, whose rules run after every rule before them
(Teacher Notes), as lesson 1's 8.2.30 does.

The first of them is a rule of meaning, and the Kāśikā tests it by asking what each of its words
excludes.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** According to Pāṇini, क्त्वा comes after a root when two actions have
> one doer, for the earlier one (3.4.21 [Q]). The Kāśikā gives two counter-examples. With two
> doers — "Devadatta goes when the brahmin has eaten" — the rule does not apply; and with no
> "earlier" — "he walks and talks", two actions at once — it does not apply either. Their Sanskrit
> stands in Teacher Notes. [REF:ashtadhyayi 3.4.21]; [REF:kashika 3.4.21]

**The 1.x lesson, restored.** The 1.x book taught today's forms in one lesson, "Lesson 11.23 —
Gerund & Infinitive / क्त्वा-तुमुन्" (p. 106). Its forms of कृ and गम् — कृत्वा, गत्वा, आगत्य,
उपगम्य, कर्तुम्, गन्तुम् — are cells of today's card, and its gloss of 3.4.21, "For two acts with
the same agent, the earlier one takes ktvā" (p. 106), says what the rule says: it is kept. Two of
its lines are corrected in the open, each quoted from the page image: its gloss of 7.1.37 in §8
Step 3, and its use of 3.3.10 in §8 Step 4. Three of its claims, two on p. 106 and one on p. 107,
are not repeated here: none of them has a count, a method or a source behind it (Teacher Notes
quote them). In place of the last, which is about long chains of absolutives before one verb, the
Kāśikā gives a chain of its own under 3.4.21 — *snātvā pītvā bhuktvā vrajati*, "having bathed,
drunk and eaten, he goes" (the e-text's *bhukvā* read as *bhuktvā*) — and adds that the rule's
dual, "of two", does not limit the chain to two.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — what the language does: Whitney on the gerund, the infinitive and the agent
noun, beside Pāṇini's rules.

Whitney's name for the -त्वा / -य form is the *gerund*. The course calls it the absolutive, as the
R5 card did, and the 1.x book's "Gerund" is the same form.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Whitney: the gerund is "used generally as adjunct to the logical subject of
> a clause, denoting an accompanying or (more often) a preceding action to that signified by the
> verb of the clause". It "is made in the later language by one of the two suffixes त्वा *tvā* and
> य *ya*, the former being used with a simple root, the latter with one that is compounded with a
> prepositional prefix"; and "A root ending in a short vowel takes त्य *tya* instead of य *ya*". Of
> the infinitive: "The root has the *guṇa*-strengthening". [REF:whitney1889 §989];
> [REF:whitney1889 §990]; [REF:whitney1889 §992]; [REF:whitney1889 §968]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The agent noun in -तृ: "the root itself being strengthened by *guṇa* … the
> roots in *ṛ* take no *i*: thus, *kartṛ* (against *kariṣya*); roots *han* and *gam* show the same
> difference". [REF:whitney1889 §943]

Read Whitney beside Pāṇini. Whitney's gerund is the adjunct of "the logical subject": the doer of
the main verb, which is 3.4.21's one doer. His "accompanying or (more often) a preceding" is wider
than the rule's "earlier": this lesson teaches the earlier action, as the rule does, and reads an
accompanying one where a text has it. His -tvā "with a simple root" and -ya "with one that is
compounded with a prepositional prefix" are 7.1.37's two cases; his -tya after a short vowel is
6.1.71's augment. His guṇa in the infinitive is the guṇa of 7.3.84, which nothing blocks before
तुमुन्, because तुमुन् has no क्. And his last line on the agent noun is about गम्: गमिष्यति, the
future of c03.l05, has an इ, and गन्ता has none.

The two accounts pick out the same forms; they explain them differently. The quiz asks Whitney's as
FACT, and Pāṇini's only in the form "According to Pāṇini …".

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the day's idea in two lines, each printed joined, its pause form beneath, then
the gloss.

> स गृहं गत्वा खादति। पूर्वं गच्छति, पश्चात्खादति। (sa gṛhaṃ gatvā khādati. pūrvaṃ gacchati, paścāt khādati.)
> सः गृहम् गत्वा खादति। पूर्वम् गच्छति, पश्चात् खादति। (saḥ gṛham gatvā khādati. pūrvam gacchati, paścāt khādati.)
> "He goes home and eats. First he goes, afterwards he eats." — वह घर जाकर खाता है। पहले जाता है,
> बाद में खाता है।

> स स्नातुं गच्छति, स्नात्वा च गृहमागच्छति। (sa snātuṃ gacchati, snātvā ca gṛham āgacchati.)
> सः स्नातुम् गच्छति, स्नात्वा च गृहम् आगच्छति। (saḥ snātum gacchati, snātvā ca gṛham āgacchati.)
> "He goes to bathe, and having bathed he comes home." — वह नहाने जाता है, और नहाकर घर आता है।

The first line is 3.4.21 said in Sanskrit: one doer, सः, for both verbs, and the earlier action,
the going, in -त्वा; its second sentence says the order aloud, पूर्वम् "first", पश्चात्
"afterwards". The second line has both of today's forms: स्नातुम् names what the going is for, and
स्नात्वा the action done before the coming. पूर्वम् is the neuter of पूर्वः, a word of v4.c01.l03,
used as an adverb, as in v4.c02.l01; पश्चात् and स्नाति are today's.

The joins are Volume 3's: सः before a consonant → स; गृहम्, पूर्वम्, स्नातुम् before a consonant → ं;
पश्चात् खादति → पश्चात्खादति — त् before a voiceless sound, no change, written together; गृहम्
आगच्छति → गृहमागच्छति — म् written together with the vowel after it. A daṇḍa and a comma stop every
seam: खादति and गच्छति stand as they are before them.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

तीसरे खण्ड में आपने उक्त्वा, गत्वा, आगत्य जैसे रूप केवल पढ़े थे — "कहकर", "जाकर", "आकर" — और
उनका कर्ता ढूँढ़ा था। आज आप इन्हें बनाना सीखेंगे, और साथ में दो और रूप: स्नातुम् — "नहाने के
लिए", और कर्ता-शब्द — "करने वाला"।

**पूर्वकालिक कृदन्त: -त्वा।** जब दो क्रियाओं का कर्ता एक ही हो और एक क्रिया पहले हो, तो पहली
क्रिया की धातु के बाद क्त्वा प्रत्यय आता है — सूत्र 3.4.21 समानकर्तृकयोः पूर्वकाले। क्त्वा का क्
इत् है, इसलिए बचता है -त्वा: गम् से गत्वा, कृ से कृत्वा। स गृहं गत्वा खादति — "वह घर जाकर खाता
है": जाना पहले, खाना बाद में, और दोनों का कर्ता एक — वह। इस रूप को हमारा पाठ्यक्रम पूर्वकालिक
कृदन्त कहता है; हिन्दी स्कूली व्याकरण में इसे पूर्वकालिक क्रिया कहते हैं।

**कर्ता एक है या नहीं — यही जाँच है।** हर बार पूछिए: क्या दोनों क्रियाओं का एक ही कर्ता बताया जा
सकता है? मकरो वानरं दृष्ट्वा वदति — देखना भी मगरमच्छ का, बोलना भी: ठीक। पर "मेरे आने पर वह जाता
है" में दो कर्ता हैं — मैं और वह; यहाँ -त्वा नहीं लग सकता। आज ऐसी बात दो वाक्यों में कहिए; दो
कर्ताओं वाली रचना पाँचवें अध्याय में आएगी।

**उपसर्ग के बाद -य।** आगच्छति में आ उपसर्ग है। सूत्र 7.1.37 के अनुसार, जिस समास का पहला पद नञ्
यानी निषेध वाला अ- न हो, उसमें क्त्वा के स्थान पर ल्यप् आता है; ल् और प् इत् हैं, बचता है -य।
उपसर्ग और धातु मिलकर समास बनाते हैं — यह नियम चौथे पाठ में आएगा। इसलिए गत्वा, पर आगत्य; और
प्रविश्य, आलोच्य, आकर्ण्य। धातु के अन्त में ह्रस्व स्वर हो, तो -य से पहले त् जुड़ता है — यह तुक्
आगम है, सूत्र 6.1.71: आ + ग + त् + य = आगत्य। दीर्घ स्वर के बाद यह त् नहीं जुड़ता: प्रदाय। और
गम् जैसी धातु, जिसके अन्त में म् है, -य से पहले अपना म् रख भी सकती है — सूत्र 6.4.38: आगत्य भी
ठीक, आगम्य भी।

**तुमुन्: "करने के लिए"।** स स्नातुं गच्छति — "वह नहाने जाता है": जाना नहाने के लिए है। सूत्र
3.3.10 के अनुसार, जिस धातु की क्रिया किसी दूसरी क्रिया का प्रयोजन हो, उसके बाद तुमुन् प्रत्यय
आता है; उ और न् इत् हैं, बचता है -तुम्: स्ना से स्नातुम्, गम् से गन्तुम्। इच्छति और शक्नोति के
साथ भी -तुम् आता है, पर वह दो दूसरे नियमों से आता है, जो शिक्षक-टिप्पणी में हैं: अहं
गन्तुमिच्छामि — "मैं जाना चाहता हूँ"; स वक्तुं न शक्नोति — "वह बोल नहीं सकता"।

**ये रूप बदलते नहीं।** -त्वा, -य और -तुम् वाले शब्द अव्यय हैं — सूत्र 1.1.40 और 1.1.39 इन्हें
अव्यय कहते हैं, और अव्यय के बाद आया विभक्ति-प्रत्यय लुप्त हो जाता है। इसलिए गत्वा हर वाक्य में
गत्वा ही रहता है — कर्ता पुरुष हो या स्त्री, एक हो या अनेक।

**क् क्या करता है।** पहले पाठ में आपने देखा कि क्त का क् इत् होकर भी धातु पर तीन काम करता है।
क्त्वा में वही क् है; तुमुन् में, और कर्ता-शब्द बनाने वाले तृच् प्रत्यय में, क् नहीं है। इसीलिए
ये जोड़े बनते हैं —

- कृत्वा, पर कर्तुम्, कर्ता: क् गुण रोकता है; जहाँ क् नहीं, वहाँ ऋ को गुण मिलता है और वह अर्
  हो जाता है।
- गत्वा, पर गन्तुम्, गन्ता: क् वाले प्रत्यय से पहले गम् का म् गिर जाता है; क् न हो तो नासिक्य
  बना रहता है, न् के रूप में।
- उक्त्वा, पर वक्तुम्, वक्ता: क् वाले प्रत्यय से पहले वच् का व् उ बन जाता है — यह सम्प्रसारण है।

**कर्ता-शब्द: -तृ।** उसी पूरी धातु के बाद -तृ लगाने से करने वाले का नाम बनता है: गन्ता — जाने
वाला, वक्ता — बोलने वाला, द्रष्टा — देखने वाला, श्रोता — सुनने वाला। इनके रूप दाता की तरह चलते
हैं, जो आप तीसरे खण्ड में सीख चुके हैं: वक्ता, वक्तारम्, वक्तारः।

**आज के सात शब्द:** स्नाति — नहाता है; शक्नोति — सकता है; आकर्णयति — सुनता है, कान देता है;
लोभः — लालच; पङ्कः — कीचड़; आलोचयति — विचार करता है; पश्चात् — बाद में, पीछे।

The recipe of this volume, once more: forms you own, the question they raise, the tools that answer

point: two kṛt forms that do not decline — -त्वा [क्त्वा] for the earlier of two actions with one
doer (3.4.21), -य [ल्यप्] in its place after a prefix (7.1.37), and -तुम् [तुमुन्] after a root that
names the purpose of another action (3.3.10). क्त्वा is k-marked like lesson 1's क्त; तुमुन् and
तृच् are not — so कृत्वा, but कर्तुम् and कर्ता.

### Step 1 — one doer, the earlier action

**1. One doer, the earlier action — Q, 3.4.21 समानकर्तृकयोः पूर्वकाले** (samānakartṛkayoḥ pūrvakāle).

Padaccheda, as the data file gives it: समानकर्त्तृकयोः [सप्तमी, dual: "when two have the same
doer"] · पूर्वकाले [सप्तमी, singular: "for the earlier"]. The file spells the first word -र्त्तृ-,
with a doubled त् after र्; Monier-Williams prints *kartṛ* and *samāna-kartṛka*, Apte *kartṛ* and
*kartṛka*, each with one t; the sūtra text has -र्तृ-.

Bracket line: [carried (data file): कृत्, 3.1.93; क्त्वा, 3.4.18] [headings (data file): प्रत्ययः, 3.1.1; परश्च, 3.1.2; धातोः, 3.1.91] (further headings: Teacher Notes)

English: "[After a root,] when two actions have the same doer, [क्त्वा] for the earlier."
[REF:ashtadhyayi 3.4.21]; [REF:kashika 3.4.21]

The affix itself is carried: क्त्वा comes from 3.4.18, three rules before, and कृत् from lesson 1's
3.1.93 — so क्त्वा is a kṛt. Both words of the rule are locatives. The case code of c01.l03 reads a
locative as "before" (1.1.66); here the two locatives do not mean "before": they name the
circumstance, as the Kāśikā reads them — of two root-meanings with the same doer, in the earlier
one (its line is in Teacher Notes). The 1.x book's gloss, "For two acts with the same agent, the
earlier one takes ktvā" (p. 106), says the same, and is kept.

**The test.** Before you join two actions with -त्वा, name the doer of each verb.

- मकरो वानरं दृष्ट्वा वदति। (makaro vānaraṃ dṛṣṭvā vadati.) — pause: मकरः वानरम् दृष्ट्वा वदति।
  (makaraḥ vānaram dṛṣṭvā vadati.) "Having seen the monkey, the crocodile speaks." One doer, मकरः,
  for both: yes.
- \*मय्यागत्य स गच्छति (mayy āgatya sa gacchati) — pause: मयि आगत्य सः गच्छति (mayi āgatya saḥ
  gacchati) — meant as "when I have come, he goes". Two doers, "I" and "he": no. Say it in two
  sentences today; c05.l04 gives that pattern its own construction.

### Step 2 — -त्वा, and what its क् does

क्त्वा → -त्वा: its क् is a marker, as the क् of क्त was (1.3.8, 1.3.9 [B]). And it does the same
work as the क् of क्त: before a k-marked affix the root takes the shape it takes before -त. Whitney
says the same of the language: with regard to the auxiliary vowel *i*, "as well as to the form of
the root before it, the formation nearly agrees with that of the participle in *ta*"
[REF:whitney1889 §991]. So: कृत, कृत्वा; गत, गत्वा; उक्त, उक्त्वा; दृष्ट, दृष्ट्वा; श्रुत, श्रुत्वा;
दत्त, दत्त्वा — of दा "only *dattvā́*", Whitney adds (§991). गम्, कृ and वच् take no इट् here, as
before क्त: a labelled step, whose rule is in Teacher Notes.

**2. A word in -त्वा does not decline — D, 1.1.40 क्त्वातोसुन्कसुनः** (ktvātosunkasunaḥ).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): अव्ययम्, 1.1.37] [headings (data file): —]

English: "A word ending in क्त्वा, तोसुन् or कसुन् is called avyaya." [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.40];
[REF:kashika 1.1.40]

अव्ययम् is carried from 1.1.37, which named the avyaya in v3.c05.l03; तोसुन् and कसुन् are two other
affixes, whose forms the course does not teach. After an avyaya the case ending is deleted (2.4.82
[D, owned: c02.l01]), as the ending of नमः was in c02.l01: गत्वा never changes, whoever the doer.

### Step 3 — after a prefix: -य

आगत्य, the R5 card's other form, has a prefix, and no -त्वा. Three D tools make it.

**3. -य in a compound — D, 7.1.37 समासेऽनञ्पूर्वे क्त्वो ल्यप्** (samāse 'nañpūrve ktvo lyap).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): अन्यतरस्याम्, 7.1.35 (the file carries it; this rule does not use it)] [headings (data file): अङ्गस्य, 6.4.1]

English: "In a compound whose first member is not नञ्, क्त्वा is replaced by ल्यप्."
[REF:ashtadhyayi 7.1.37]; [REF:kashika 7.1.37]

The ल् and the प् of ल्यप् are markers (1.3.8, 1.3.3, 1.3.9 [B]): what remains is -य. नञ् is the
negative particle अ- "not". The Kāśikā's pairs make the two conditions plain: कृत्वा [no compound]
against प्रकृत्य [प्र + कृ, a compound]; अकृत्वा "without doing" [a compound, but its first member is
नञ्] against प्रकृत्य. Why is आ + गम्-क्त्वा a compound at all? A prefix compounds with a kṛt-final
word: 2.2.18, lesson 4's rule — in today's derivation, one marked line.

> **The 1.x gloss, corrected in the open.** The 1.x book printed: 'Sutra: समासेऽनञ्पूर्वे क्त्वो
> ल्यप् (7.1.37) → "After a prefix (other than नञ्), क्त्वा becomes ल्यप् → -य"' (p. 106). The sūtra
> does not say "after a prefix": it says "in a compound whose first member is not नञ्". नञ् is not
> a prefix but the negative अ-, and the compound it heads keeps -त्वा, as the Kāśikā's अकृत्वा
> shows. A prefix brings -य only because a prefix and a root make a compound, by 2.2.18 (lesson 4).

**4. The nasal, either way — D, 6.4.38 वा ल्यपि** (vā lyapi).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): नलोपः, 6.4.23 (the file carries it; this rule does not use it); अनुदात्तोपदेश-वनति-तनोति-आदीनाम्, 6.4.37; अनुनासिक, 6.4.37; लोपः, 6.4.37] [headings (data file): अङ्गस्य, 6.4.1] (further headings: Teacher Notes)

English: "Before ल्यप् the nasal loss [of 6.4.37] is optional for a root in म्; for the others the
loss is obligatory." [REF:ashtadhyayi 6.4.38]; [REF:kashika 6.4.38]

The rule carries three words of lesson 1's 6.4.37 and adds two of its own: ल्यपि "before ल्यप्" and
वा "optionally". The
Kāśikā fixes the reach of the option: it holds for the roots that end in म्, and for the others the
loss stays obligatory. गम् ends in म्: before ल्यप् it may lose its nasal or keep it — आगत्य or
आगम्य, the Kāśikā's own pair.

**5. The augment त् — D, 6.1.71 ह्रस्वस्य पिति कृति तुक्** (hrasvasya piti kṛti tuk).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): —] [headings (data file): —]

English: "After a short vowel, before a p-marked kṛt, the augment तुक्." [REF:ashtadhyayi 6.1.71];
[REF:kashika 6.1.71]

तुक् is त् with two markers, उ and क्; being k-marked, it goes at the end of what it is added to
(1.1.46 [D, owned: c02.l02]). ल्यप् is p-marked; and it counts as a kṛt, as the क्त्वा it stands for
did — a labelled step, whose rule is read in c06.l03. So, after a root that ends in a short vowel,
-त्य: आगत्य [ग, once its nasal is gone], उपकृत्य [कृ]. Not after a long vowel: प्रदाय [दा]; nor after a
consonant: आगम्य, प्रविश्य.

**With the teacher.** §13, derivation (a), takes आ + गम् + क्त्वा to आगत्य and to आगम्य, the two
routes side by side. Work down it one line at a time.

- **Step 0.** Two actions, one doer: the mouse comes, then cuts — मूषक आगत्य जालमकृन्तत् (mūṣaka
  āgatya jālam akṛntat), §13's sentence 2. 3.4.21 gives क्त्वा after the root of the earlier action,
  आ + गम्.
- **Step 1.** आ and गम् make a compound — 2.2.18, lesson 4's rule, one marked line — and its first
  member is a prefix, not नञ्: 7.1.37 puts ल्यप् in place of क्त्वा.
- **Step 2.** ल् and प् are markers, 1.3.8 for the one at the beginning and 1.3.3 for the one at the
  end, and 1.3.9 drops them: -य.
- **Step 3.** Before ल्यप् the nasal loss of lesson 1's 6.4.37 is optional for a root in म्
  (6.4.38): two routes, ग or गम्.
- **Step 4.** On the first route ग ends in a short vowel, and ल्यप् is p-marked and counts as a kṛt:
  6.1.71 adds तुक्, and 1.1.46 sets the k-marked augment at the end of ग — ग + त्. On the second
  route गम् ends in म्, not a short vowel: no तुक्.
- **Step 5.** The word is an avyaya (1.1.40): the ending after it is deleted (2.4.82).
- **Step 6.** आगत्य, or आगम्य. Either is right, and every key accepts both.

### Step 4 — -तुम्, the infinitive

**6. For the purpose of another action — D, 3.3.10 तुमुन्ण्वुलौ क्रियायां क्रियार्थायाम्**
(tumunṇvulau kriyāyāṃ kriyārthāyām).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): कृत्, 3.1.93] [headings (data file): प्रत्ययः, 3.1.1; परश्च, 3.1.2; धातोः, 3.1.91; भविष्यति, 3.3.3 (not a tool; printed by name, as the heading the reading needs)] (further headings: Teacher Notes)

English: "After a root that names the purpose of another action, तुमुन् or ण्वुल्."
[REF:ashtadhyayi 3.3.10]; [REF:kashika 3.3.10]

स स्नातुं गच्छति (sa snātuṃ gacchati) "he goes to bathe": the going is for the bathing, so the root
of the bathing, स्ना, takes तुमुन्. Its markers are the nasal उ and the final न्, by 1.3.2, 1.3.3 and
1.3.9 [B]: what remains is -तुम्. The heading भविष्यति "in the future" gives the purpose its time, as the
Kāśikā reads it: the bathing is still to come. ण्वुल्, the rule's second affix, is another kṛt,
not taught in this lesson.

**With इच्छति and शक्नोति.** अहं गन्तुमिच्छामि (ahaṃ gantum icchāmi) "I want to go"; स वक्तुं न
शक्नोति (sa vaktuṃ na śaknoti) "he cannot speak". Here the -तुम् does not name the purpose of the
wanting: it completes it, with the same doer, and it rests on two more rules, kept in Teacher
Notes.

> **The 1.x gloss, made exact.** The 1.x book printed: 'Sutra: तुमुन्ण्वुलौ क्रियायां क्रियार्थायाम्
> (3.3.10) → "When the action is for the sake of another action, use tumun"', and as its examples
> 'रामः ग्रामं गन्तुम् इच्छति।' and 'सः वेदं पठितुम् आगच्छति।' (p. 107). The gloss does not say
> which of the two actions takes तुमुन्. It goes after the root that names the purpose — पठितुम् —
> while the action done for its sake, आगच्छति, stands beside it: सः वेदं पठितुम् आगच्छति is 3.3.10's
> own kind. रामः ग्रामं गन्तुम् इच्छति rests on another rule, the -तुम् with a verb of wishing
> (Teacher Notes).

**7. It does not decline either — D, 1.1.39 कृन्मेजन्तः** (kṛn mejantaḥ).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): अव्ययम्, 1.1.37] [headings (data file): —]

English: "A kṛt ending in म् or in an एच् vowel is called avyaya." [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.39];
[REF:kashika 1.1.39]

-तुम् ends in म्, so a word in -तुम् is an avyaya, and its ending is deleted (2.4.82). The Kāśikā's
example under this rule is a form of another kind (Teacher Notes); the rule's own words are enough
here: a kṛt that ends in म्.

**Two sound rules for गन्तुम्.** गम् + तुम् gives गम्तुम्, and no क् stands in तुमुन् to take the
nasal away: 6.4.37 needs one. Two D tools finish the word.

**8. D, 8.3.24 नश्चापदान्तस्य झलि** (naś cāpadāntasya jhali).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): मः, 8.3.23; अनुस्वारः, 8.3.23] [headings (data file): —] (further headings: Teacher Notes)

English: "न् or म् not at the end of a pada becomes anusvāra before a jhal." [REF:ashtadhyayi
8.3.24]; [REF:kashika 8.3.24]

The rule before it, 8.3.23 मोऽनुस्वारः, is yours since Volume 3: the anusvāra of a म् at the end of
a word, before a consonant (गृहं गच्छति). 8.3.24 adds न्, by its च "and", and its own place: inside
a word, before a jhal. त is a jhal: गम्तुम् → गंतुम्.

**9. D, 8.4.58 अनुस्वारस्य ययि परसवर्णः** (anusvārasya yayi parasavarṇaḥ).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): —] [headings (data file): —] (further headings: Teacher Notes)

English: "anusvāra before a yay sound becomes the nasal of that sound's row." [REF:ashtadhyayi
8.4.58]; [REF:kashika 8.4.58]

यय्, read from the fourteen sūtras as c01.l01 taught you to read a pratyāhāra: from the य of हयवरट्
to the marker य् of कपय् — every consonant but श, ष, स and ह. त is one of them, so the anusvāra
becomes the nasal of the t-row, न्: गंतुम् → गन्तुम्. Both rules stand in the last three quarters of
the grammar and run last. Your own derivations, गत्वा and गन्तुम्, are §18 exercise 3, items 1–2;
their keys are in §19.

### Step 5 — the k-marker's three jobs, in pairs

क्त्वा has a क्; तुमुन् has none, and neither has तृच्, the affix of the doer-noun of Step 6. Put the
pairs side by side, and each of lesson 1's three jobs shows twice:

| with क् [-त्वा] | without क् [-तुम्, -तृ] | the job of the क् |
|---|---|---|
| कृत्वा | कर्तुम् · कर्ता | it stops guṇa and vṛddhi: 1.1.5 [Q, owned: c03.l03]. Without it, 7.3.84 [B] gives guṇa, ऋ → अर् |
| गत्वा | गन्तुम् · गन्ता | it takes the nasal away: 6.4.37 [D, lesson 1]. Without it, the nasal stays, as न् |
| उक्त्वा | वक्तुम् · वक्ता | it brings samprasāraṇa, व् → उ: 6.1.15 [D, lesson 1]. Without it, none |
| दृष्ट्वा | द्रष्टुम् · द्रष्टा | without क्, दृश् takes an augment that gives द्रष्- [Teacher Notes]; with it, none |

गम् has no इक् vowel, so in the second pair there is no guṇa to block: गत्वा and गन्तुम् differ only
in the nasal. The pairs are the k-marker card of §15, which lesson 1 began and today completes.

### Step 6 — the doer in -तृ

The same full root, with -तृ, names the doer: कर्ता, गन्ता, वक्ता, द्रष्टा, श्रोता, दाता, प्रवेष्टा —
each checked in Monier-Williams under its stem in -tṛ, with Whitney's account of §5 (§943). Two of
them are yours already, दाता and कर्ता, since v3.c02.l04, and they stay counted; the rest are built by
today's pattern and add nothing to the ledger. The affix is तृच्; its च् is a marker (1.3.3), and it
has no क्: so the root has its full grade, as before -तुम् — गन्ता beside गन्तुम्, वक्ता beside वक्तुम्,
श्रोता beside श्रोतुम्.

Its cells are दाता's in T5 (v3.c02.l04), and the ones you produce are the ones T5 made yours: the
singular, all eight cells, and the nominative and accusative plural — वक्ता, वक्तारम्, वक्त्रा …,
वक्तारः, वक्तॄन्. No rule number is given for its affix in this lesson: you meet that rule unseen in
the Volume 4 exam.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the व of -त्वा, the न्त of गन्तुम्, the ष्ट of द्रष्टुम् and the त्य of आगत्य. The having-done
image (`planned:assets/images/volume-4/v4c04l02-having-done.svg`) shows one root and two arrows,
गत्वा with the क् lit and गन्तुम् with the nasal kept; say each form as you point to it.

1. **त्व in गत्वा.** The tongue tip is at the upper teeth for त /t̪/; while it is still there, the
   lower lip rises lightly to the upper teeth for व /ʋ/, lips unrounded — not English "tw", with
   rounded lips.
2. **न्त in गन्तुम्.** The anusvāra is not a separate sound: the tongue is already at the teeth for न
   /n̪/ and stays there for त /t̪/ — 8.4.58 in the mouth.
3. **ष्ट in द्रष्टुम्.** The tip curls back for ष /ʂ/ and stays curled for ट /ʈ/; then it drops for उ.
4. **त्य in आगत्य.** /t̪j/: the tongue at the teeth for त, then the front of the tongue rises
   towards the hard palate for य, with no vowel between.

Mirror check: say गत्वा — गन्तुम्. In the first the lips meet the teeth for व; in the second they
stay apart until the म्.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; they feed the quiz distractors.

1. **Two doers joined: \*मय्यागत्य स गच्छति** (mayy āgatya sa gacchati) — pause: मयि आगत्य सः
   गच्छति (mayi āgatya saḥ gacchati) — meant as "when I have come, he goes". *Fix:* today, two
   sentences; the construction for two doers, मय्यागते स गच्छति (mayy āgate sa gacchati) — pause:
   मयि आगते सः गच्छति (mayi āgate saḥ gacchati) — is c05.l04's [preview]. *Why:* -त्वा and -य need
   one doer for both actions (3.4.21).
2. **\*गन्त्वा.** *Fix:* गत्वा. *Why:* the nasal drops before a k-marked affix, and क्त्वा is one
   (6.4.37).
3. **\*वच्त्वा.** *Fix:* उक्त्वा. *Why:* samprasāraṇa before a k-marked affix (6.1.15).
4. **\*आगत्वा.** *Fix:* आगत्य. *Why:* after a prefix — in a compound — the absolutive ends in -य
   (7.1.37).
5. **\*कृतुम्.** *Fix:* कर्तुम्. *Why:* तुमुन् has no क्: guṇa (7.3.84).
6. **\*प्रदत्य.** *Fix:* प्रदाय. *Why:* तुक् only after a short vowel (6.1.71).

### (3) The absolutive's choice

- **No prefix → -त्वा:** गत्वा, कृत्वा, उक्त्वा, दृष्ट्वा, श्रुत्वा, दत्त्वा, स्नात्वा, गृहीत्वा.
- **A prefix → -य:** आगत्य or आगम्य, उपगम्य or उपगत्य, उपकृत्य, प्रविश्य, प्रदाय, आलोच्य [its णिच्
  dropped: Teacher Notes], आकर्ण्य.
- **A short root vowel before -य → -त्य:** आगत्य, उपगत्य, उपकृत्य.
- **The negative अ- is no prefix:** अकृत्वा keeps -त्वा [the Kāśikā's counter-example; ○].

उपकृत्य is उप + कृ "help" [Monier-Williams s.v. upa-kṛ], from a Pañcatantra verse [Panc 1.366];
प्रदाय is प्र + दा "give". Neither verb is on your list: they stand on the card for their shapes.

### (4) The same-doer test

Before you join two sentences with -त्वा or -य, name the doer of each verb. **One doer:** join, the
earlier action in the absolutive, the later in the finite verb (मकरो वानरं दृष्ट्वा वदति). **Two
doers:** do not join; say two sentences — c05.l04 gives that pattern its own construction. **No
"earlier":** two actions at once are not 3.4.21's case either. The Kāśikā's two counter-examples,
in English: "Devadatta goes when the brahmin has eaten" — two doers; "he walks and talks" — no
earlier action. Their Sanskrit is in Teacher Notes.

पुनरावृत्तिः — one screen to remember.

> **Having done, to do, the doer.** According to Pāṇini, क्त्वा comes after a root when two actions
> have one doer, for the earlier (3.4.21): **गत्वा, कृत्वा, उक्त्वा, दृष्ट्वा**. In a compound not
> headed by नञ् — a prefix and the root — क्त्वा → ल्यप्, **-य** [7.1.37]: प्रविश्य, आलोच्य; after a
> short vowel, the augment तुक्, by 6.1.71: **आगत्य**, and for गम् also आगम्य, by 6.4.38; after a
> long vowel, none: प्रदाय. **-तुम्** after a root that names the purpose of another action (3.3.10):
> स स्नातुं गच्छति; and with इच्छति, शक्नोति. Both are avyaya (1.1.40, 1.1.39). **The क्:** क्त्वा has
> it, तुमुन् and तृच् do not — कृत्वा / कर्तुम्, कर्ता; गत्वा / गन्तुम्, गन्ता; उक्त्वा / वक्तुम्, वक्ता.
> **The doer** in -तृ declines as दाता. **The test:** one doer, or no joining. **The ledger:** seven
> new — 580; cells drilled: 19; tools added: 9; toolbox: 105.
