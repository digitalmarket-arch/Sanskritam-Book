## 1. Hook

In its first weeks the course gave you three words whole: लौकिक "worldly" (v1.c02.l05), एकता
"oneness" (v1.c02.l04) and ममता "mine-ness" (v1.c02.l01). Volume 3 read पक्षी "bird" on its R1 card
(v3.c02.l03) and श्रीमान् on its R6 card (v3.c05.l01), and its three verses read पात्रता and पात्रत्व
whole: "Read whole; the formation is v4.c04's" (v3.c06.l04 §8). And this volume has a name of the
same kind: पाणिनीयम्.

Put three of them beside the words they come from: एकता beside एक, पक्षी beside पक्ष, लौकिक beside
लोक. What do the -ता of एकता, the -ई of पक्षी and the औ of लौकिक share? Each is the work of
an affix added not to a root but to a word — and the औ of लौकिक is the work of a letter you never
hear.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — where today's rules stand in the Aṣṭādhyāyī, and how the Kāśikā reads the name
of this volume.

The kṛt affixes of lessons 1 and 2 stand in Book 3, under the heading धातोः "after a root" [3.1.91,
B, c03.l01]. Today's affixes stand in Books 4 and 5. Book 4 begins with the case endings — 4.1.2, the
sup list of c02.l01 — and the feminine affixes, 4.1.4 among them, the rule that gives -ता its आ.
Then one rule names the affixes that fill the rest of Book 4 and all of Book 5.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** According to Pāṇini, the affixes taught from 4.1.76 to the end of Book 5
> are called taddhita (4.1.76 तद्धिताः [D]); the Kāśikā reads the heading's reach as
> *āpañcamādhyāyaparisamāpteḥ*, "up to the completion of the fifth book". [REF:ashtadhyayi 4.1.76];
> [REF:kashika 4.1.76]

Inside that stretch the rules go by sense. 4.1.92 तस्यापत्यम्, "offspring", stands in Book 4's first
quarter; 4.2.114, after a vṛddha word, in its second; 5.1.44 and 5.1.119 in Book 5's first quarter;
5.2.94 and 5.2.115, "who has it", in its second; the rules of today's card stand among them. The
rules that change the base stand elsewhere. 7.2.117 is in Book 7, under the heading अङ्गस्य [6.4.1,
c02.l03], the next rule after c03.l04's 7.2.116, which gave जगाम its आ; 7.1.2 and 7.3.50 stand under

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** According to the Kāśikā, पाणिनीयम् is "taught by Pāṇini" (*proktam*),

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — what the language does: Whitney on secondary derivation, beside Pāṇini's
markers.

Whitney's name for today's words is *secondary*: "Words of secondary derivation are made by the
addition of further suffixes to stems already ending in evident suffixes" (§1202) — a word made from
a word, where the kṛt forms of lessons 1 and 2 are made from a root.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Whitney on secondary derivation: "Before a suffix beginning with a vowel or
> with y … final a- and i-vowels are regularly lost" (§1203a); the vṛddhi of the first syllable is a
> mark of many secondary derivatives (§1204); -tā and -tva make abstract nouns, "mamátā selfishness"
> among them (§1237c, §1239); -in is made "almost unlimitedly from stems in अ a or आ ā" — *dhanín,
> pakṣín, balín* (§1230). [REF:whitney1889 §1203]; [REF:whitney1889 §1204]; [REF:whitney1889 §1230];
> [REF:whitney1889 §1237]; [REF:whitney1889 §1239]

Read Whitney beside Pāṇini. Whitney states what happens to the base: before a vowel or y its final a
or i goes, and the first syllable of a secondary word may take vṛddhi. His example of a -tā word is
your own ममता, which he files as "of special formation", made from a pronoun's form. Pāṇini says when
each thing happens. The final vowel goes where the base is bha (6.4.148), and a base is bha before an
affix that begins with a vowel or य्, by 1.4.18 — the same line that Whitney draws, "a vowel or y". The
first vowel takes vṛddhi where the affix carries a marker.

**The marker decides.** ठञ् and अञ् carry ञ्, अण् carries ण् — vṛddhi: लौकिक; दैवम्, whose अञ् a
vārttika gives (Teacher Notes). तल्, त्व, मतुप्, इनि and छ carry neither — no vṛddhi: एकता, not
\*ऐकता; पक्षी. A k-marked taddhita brings vṛddhi too, by a rule in Teacher Notes. A plain -क —
बालकः, yours since Volume 1 — brings none: its क् is the affix's own sound, not a marker, because
1.3.8 लशक्वतद्धिते [B] exempts a taddhita ("at the beginning of an affix that is not a taddhita").
The -तः of ततः, इतः, अतः and कुतः — yours since v1.c02.l06, whose "suffix story" this is — is a
taddhita too, on today's card (◐).

The two accounts pick out the same forms and explain them differently: Whitney by what the language
does, Pāṇini by a marker and a name. The quiz asks Whitney's as FACT, and Pāṇini's only in the form
"According to Pāṇini …".

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the day's idea in two lines, each printed joined, its pause form beneath, then
the gloss.

> तद्धितः शब्दात्परः, न धातोः। (taddhitaḥ śabdāt paraḥ, na dhātoḥ.)
> तद्धितः शब्दात् परः, न धातोः। (taddhitaḥ śabdāt paraḥ, na dhātoḥ.)
> "A taddhita comes after a word, not after a root." — तद्धित प्रत्यय शब्द के बाद आता है, धातु के
> बाद नहीं।

> एकस्य भाव एकता, बलमस्यास्तीति बली। (ekasya bhāva ekatā, balam asyāstīti balī.)
> एकस्य भावः एकता, बलम् अस्य अस्ति इति बली। (ekasya bhāvaḥ ekatā, balam asya asti iti balī.)
> "The being of one: एकता; 'he has strength': बली." — एक होने का भाव: एकता; "इसके पास बल है": बली।

The first line sets today beside lesson 1's §6 line on kṛt: there धातोः परः, "after a root", here
शब्दात् परः, "after a word". The second gives two senses of today's table as their phrases —
एकस्य भावः, the sense of 5.1.119, and बलमस्यास्ति, the sense of 5.2.94 — each with the word that
its affix makes.

Words owned: शब्दः [शब्दात्, the ablative], परः, न, धातुः [धातोः], एक [एकस्य], बलम्, अयम् [अस्य], अस्ति,
इति; एकता, yours since v1.c02.l04; बली, a form of today's table. Glossed: भावः "being". Label: तद्धितः,
the name of a class of affix, as कृत् was in lesson 1.

The joins are Volume 3's: शब्दात् परः → शब्दात्परः — त् before a voiceless sound, written together;
भावः एकता → भाव एकता — -अः before a vowel other than अ drops its visarga, and a gap stays; बलम् अस्य →
बलमस्य — म् written together with the vowel after it; अस्य अस्ति → अस्यास्ति — two like vowels, one
long; अस्ति इति → अस्तीति — the same. A comma and a daṇḍa stop every seam: परः and एकता stand in their
pause form before one.

तीसरे खण्ड में आपने पक्षी, सुखी, श्रीमान् और धीमान् पढ़े, और तीसरे खण्ड के तीन श्लोकों वाले पाठ में
पात्रता — यह कहकर कि इसकी रचना चौथे खण्ड में आएगी। आज वह रचना सामने है।

**तद्धित प्रत्यय।** पिछले दो पाठों के कृत् प्रत्यय धातु के बाद आते थे: कृ से कृत, गम् से गत्वा। आज
के प्रत्यय धातु के बाद नहीं, शब्द के बाद आते हैं — यानी प्रातिपदिक के बाद: एक से एकता, लोक से
लौकिक। पाणिनि के अनुसार सूत्र 4.1.76 तद्धिताः से पाँचवें अध्याय के अन्त तक जितने प्रत्यय कहे गए हैं,
उन सबका नाम **तद्धित** है। हर तद्धित किसी एक अर्थ में आता है, और वह अर्थ उसका सूत्र बताता है।

**तीन प्रश्न।** किसी तद्धित को पहचानना हो, तो उसका अर्थ पूछिए —

- **"होने का भाव"?** — सूत्र 5.1.119: तल् और त्व। तल् का ल् इत् है; स्त्रीलिङ्ग का आ जुड़कर -ता
  बनता है — सूत्र 4.1.4। त्व से -त्वम् बनता है। एक से एकता, मम से ममता, गुरु से गुरुत्वम्, बाल से
  बालत्वम्। -ता वाले शब्द स्त्रीलिङ्ग हैं और कमला की तरह चलते हैं; -त्व वाले नपुंसकलिङ्ग हैं, वन
  की तरह।
- **"जिसके पास है"?** — सूत्र 5.2.94: मतुप्, यानी -मत्। अ, आ या म् में समाप्त होने वाले शब्द के बाद
  इसका म् व् बन जाता है — सूत्र 8.2.9, जिसकी पूरी शर्त §8 में है: धन से धनवान्, बल से बलवान्, गुण से
  गुणवान्; पर श्री से श्रीमान्, धी से धीमान्। इसी अर्थ में अ वाले शब्द के बाद इनि भी आता है, जिससे -इन् बनता है — सूत्र 5.2.115:
  पक्ष से पक्षी, बल से बली, सुख से सुखी, धन से धनी। ये शब्द आज केवल प्रथमा में आपके हैं।
- **"किसकी सन्तान"?** — सूत्र 4.1.92। दशरथ से दाशरथिः — राम। यह आज केवल दिखाया जाता है।

**पहले स्वर की वृद्धि।** लोक से लौकिक बना, तो पहला स्वर ओ औ बन गया। एक से एकता बना, तो ए वैसा ही
रहा। अन्तर प्रत्यय के इत् में है। जिस तद्धित में ञ् या ण् इत् हो, उससे पहले अङ्ग के पहले स्वर के
स्थान पर वृद्धि होती है — सूत्र 7.2.117 तद्धितेष्वचामादेः। लौकिक का प्रत्यय ठञ् है, और उसमें ञ् है;
एकता का प्रत्यय तल् है, और उसमें न ञ् है, न ण्। जिस तद्धित में क् इत् हो, उससे पहले भी वृद्धि होती
है — वह नियम शिक्षक-टिप्पणी में है। पर बालकः के -क का क् इत् नहीं है: तद्धित के आरम्भ का क्
प्रत्यय का अपना वर्ण रहता है — सूत्र 1.3.8।

**भ और पद।** जो तद्धित स्वर या य् से शुरू होता है, उससे पहले शब्द **भ** कहलाता है — सूत्र 1.4.18,
जो आपने दूसरे अध्याय में सीखा था। भ शब्द का अन्तिम इ या अ गिर जाता है — सूत्र 6.4.148: पाणिनि +
ईय से पाणिनीय, लौक + इक से लौकिक। जो तद्धित व्यञ्जन से शुरू होता है, उससे पहले शब्द **पद** रहता है
— सूत्र 1.4.17 — और कुछ नहीं गिरता: एक + ता से एकता, बल + वत् से बलवत्। इसलिए \*एक्ता नहीं, एकता।

**पाणिनीयम्।** इस खण्ड का नाम भी एक तद्धित है। पाणिनि का पहला स्वर आ है, जो वृद्धि स्वर है; ऐसा
शब्द **वृद्ध** कहलाता है — सूत्र 1.1.73। वृद्ध शब्द के बाद छ प्रत्यय आता है — सूत्र 4.2.114 — और
छ ईय बन जाता है — सूत्र 7.1.2। फिर, भ होने के कारण, पाणिनि का इ गिरता है, और बनता है पाणिनीयम्।
काशिका के अनुसार इसका अर्थ है "पाणिनि द्वारा पढ़ाया गया" — "बनाया गया" नहीं।

**कार्ड।** कुछ तद्धित आज एक कार्ड पर हैं। -तर और -तम: प्रियतर "अधिक प्रिय", प्रियतम "सबसे प्रिय",
बलवत्तर "अधिक बलवान्" — ये रूप आपके हैं। यावत् … तावत् — "जब तक … तब तक" — यह जोड़ी भी आपकी है।
-मय "से बना", -वत् "जैसा", -मात्र, -तः और -क केवल पढ़ने के लिए हैं। कार्ड के सूत्र कार्ड पर देखे जाते
हैं, पूछे नहीं जाते।

**आज के छह शब्द:** पक्षः — पंख, पक्ष; वेदः — ज्ञान, वेद; शास्त्रम् — शास्त्र; व्याकरणम् — व्याकरण;
पण्डितः — विद्वान्, पंडित; दैवम् — भाग्य, दैव। पक्षः से ही पक्षी बनता है — "जिसके पंख हों"।

## 8. English Explanation

The recipe of this volume, once more: forms you own, the question they raise, the tools that answer
it, the derivation with the teacher, the tools run by you, then production. Today's one grammar
point: a taddhita is added after a word — a nominal base — not after a root (4.1.76), in a sense its
rule names: "the being of", "who has it", "known in", "descendant of". A ñ- or ṇ-marked taddhita gives
the base's first vowel vṛddhi (7.2.117); before a taddhita beginning with a vowel or य् the base is
bha (1.4.18), and its final इ or अ drops (6.4.148).

Each new tool is printed once, here, with its tier, its bracket line and its English line. **Q** is
read with its padaccheda and recognised by its gist ("According to Pāṇini, …"); **D** is the
authority for one derivation step, never recalled by number; **K**, in §15, is on a card, looked up
and never assessed.

### Step 1 — taddhita

**1. The name — D, 4.1.76 तद्धिताः** (taddhitāḥ).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): अनुपसर्जनात्, 4.1.14 (the file carries it; this rule does not use it)] [headings (data file): प्रत्ययः, 3.1.1; परश्च, 3.1.2; तद्धिताः, 4.1.76 (the heading begins here)] (further headings: Teacher Notes)

English: "[From here to the end of Book 5, the affixes are called] taddhita." [REF:ashtadhyayi
4.1.76]; [REF:kashika 4.1.76]

A heading, like c02.l03's अङ्गस्य: it stands over every rule to the end of Book 5, and so the data
file lists it under itself, "the heading begins here". प्रत्ययः and परश्च [3.1.1, 3.1.2, c02.l01] make
each of those rules add an affix after something; what it comes after is said by a heading at the head
of Book 4, in Teacher Notes: after a nominal base, a word, not a root. That is the line between today
and lessons 1–2. A kṛt (3.1.93, lesson 1) comes after a root: कृ → कृत. A taddhita comes after a
word: लोक → लौकिक, एक → एकता.

**Three questions name the senses.** A taddhita comes in a sense its rule names. Three of the rules
that name senses are today's tools; answer the question, not the number.

**2. "-ness?" — D, 5.1.119 तस्य भावस्त्वतलौ** (tasya bhāvas tvatalau).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): —] [headings (data file): प्रत्ययः, 3.1.1; परश्च, 3.1.2; तद्धिताः, 4.1.76] (further headings: Teacher Notes)

English: "In the sense 'the being of X', त्व and तल्." [REF:ashtadhyayi 5.1.119]; [REF:kashika
5.1.119]

तस्य is a genitive, "of it": the base stands in the genitive in the phrase the new word replaces —
एकस्य भावः "the being of one" → एकता. The ल् of तल् is a marker (1.3.3 [B]); त्व has none.

**3. "Has?" — D, 5.2.94 तदस्यास्त्यस्मिन्निति मतुप्** (tad asyāsty asminn iti matup).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): —] [headings (data file): प्रत्ययः, 3.1.1; परश्च, 3.1.2; तद्धिताः, 4.1.76] (further headings: Teacher Notes)

English: "In the sense 'X is his' or 'X is in it', मतुप्." [REF:ashtadhyayi 5.2.94]; [REF:kashika
5.2.94]

The rule is its own phrase: तत् अस्य अस्ति "that is his", अस्मिन् "in it", इति "so". The Kāśikā gives
one example of each sense: *gāvo 'sya santi gomān devadattaḥ*, "he has cows: Devadatta is gomān",
and *vṛkṣāḥ asmin santi vṛkṣavān parvataḥ*, "there are trees on it: the mountain is vṛkṣavān"
[REF:kashika 5.2.94]. The उ and the प् of मतुप् are markers (1.3.2, 1.3.3 [B]): -मत्.

**4. "Descendant of?" — D, 4.1.92 तस्यापत्यम्** (tasyāpatyam).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): —] [headings (data file): प्रत्ययः, 3.1.1; परश्च, 3.1.2; तद्धिताः, 4.1.76] (further headings: Teacher Notes)

English: "In the sense 'X's offspring'." [REF:ashtadhyayi 4.1.92]; [REF:kashika 4.1.92]

This rule names a sense and no affix; the affix comes from the rules after it, by the shape of the
base. Step 4 shows one, once.

### Step 2 — the table

§15 (1) is the table of today's words. Read it by the three questions.

**-ता and -त्व: "-ness".** एकता, ममता, पात्रता; गुरुत्वम्, बालत्वम्, पात्रत्वम्. -ता makes feminines,
-त्वम् neuters, "of the same value" (Whitney §1237, §1239): a -ता word declines as कमला and a -त्व word
as वन, in every cell. पात्र here is not "vessel" but "a fit or worthy person", as v3.c06.l04 read it
with Apte: पात्रता, पात्रत्वम् "worthiness". The -ता is feminine and takes the feminine -आ by one more
tool:

**5. D, 4.1.4 अजाद्यतष्टाप्** (ajādyataṣ ṭāp).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): —] [headings (data file): प्रत्ययः, 3.1.1; परश्च, 3.1.2] (further headings: Teacher Notes)

English: "After a base in अ, in the feminine, टाप्." [REF:ashtadhyayi 4.1.4]; [REF:kashika 4.1.4]

"In the feminine" is a heading, in Teacher Notes. The rule also names अज and the words listed with
it; -ता needs only its second part, "after a base in अ": एकत → एकता. The ट् and the प् of टाप् are
markers — the प् by 1.3.3, the ट् by a rule in Teacher Notes — and the आ joins the अ before it, अ + आ
→ आ, by the 6.1.101 अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः you own.

**-मत् and -वत्: "who has it".** श्रीमान्, धीमान्; धनवान्, बलवान्, गुणवान् — in the nominative, as
lesson 1's -तवत्: masculine singular -मान्/-वान्, feminine singular -मती/-वती, neuter singular
-मत्/-वत्, masculine plural -मन्तः/-वन्तः. Whitney says of -mant and -vant words: "inflected precisely
alike, and very nearly like the participles in अन्त् ant" (§452). Why -वत् after धन and -मत् after
श्री? One D tool:

**6. The म् of मतुप् becomes व् — D, 8.2.9 मादुपधायाश्च मतोर्वोऽयवादिभ्यः** (mād upadhāyāś ca mator vo
'yavādibhyaḥ).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): —] [headings (data file): —] (further headings: Teacher Notes)

English: "After a base ending in म् or अ/आ, or with म् or अ/आ before its last sound, the म् of मतुप्
becomes व् — not after यव and the rest." [REF:ashtadhyayi 8.2.9]; [REF:kashika 8.2.9]

धन, बल and गुण end in अ: धनवान्, बलवान्, गुणवान् — the Kāśikā's own example is *vṛkṣavān*. श्री and धी
end in ई, so the म् stays: श्रीमान्, धीमान्. 8.2.9 stands in the last three quarters of the grammar, so
it acts after every rule before it (c03.l03): in बलवान् it comes inside the nominative's work, as
exercise 3 (2) shows.

**-इन्: "who has it", again.** पक्षी, बली, सुखी, धनी — the nominative singular, -ई, as पक्षी has been
since Volume 3's R1 card.

**7. D, 5.2.115 अत इनिठनौ** (ata iniṭhanau).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): तत्, 5.2.94; अस्य, 5.2.94; अस्ति, 5.2.94; अस्मिन्, 5.2.94; इति, 5.2.94; मतुप्, 5.2.94] [headings (data file): प्रत्ययः, 3.1.1; परश्च, 3.1.2; तद्धिताः, 4.1.76] (further headings: Teacher Notes)

English: "After a base in अ, in the same sense, इनि or ठन्." [REF:ashtadhyayi 5.2.115]; [REF:kashika
5.2.115]

The sense is carried from 5.2.94, all of its words — and so is मतुप् itself: the Kāśikā adds *matub
api bhavati*, "matup comes too", with *daṇḍī* beside *daṇḍavān*, "who has a staff" [REF:kashika 5.2.115].
So बली and बलवान् both mean "strong". The Kāśikā sets its own बलवान्, बली and सुखी under other rules
(Teacher Notes). ठन्, the rule's second affix, is not taught here.

### Step 3 — vṛddhi, and the bha base

Now the two changes to the base. First, the first vowel.

**8. vṛddhi of the first vowel — Q, 7.2.117 तद्धितेष्वचामादेः** (taddhiteṣv acām ādeḥ).

Padaccheda (data file): तद्धितेषु [सप्तमी, plural: "before taddhitas"] · अचाम् [षष्ठी, plural: "of
the vowels"] · आदेः [षष्ठी, singular: "in place of the first"].

Bracket line: [carried (data file): वृद्धिः, 7.2.114; अचः, 7.2.115; ञ्णिति, 7.2.115] [headings (data file): अङ्गस्य, 6.4.1]

English: "Before a ñ- or ṇ-marked taddhita, the first vowel of the aṅga is replaced by vṛddhi."
[REF:ashtadhyayi 7.2.117]; [REF:kashika 7.2.117]

Read it with the case code of c01.l03. The locative तद्धितेषु is the context: by 1.1.66 [Q, owned],
the operation is on what precedes it — "before taddhitas". आदेः is a genitive, and by 1.1.49 [Q,
owned] a genitive means "in place of": "in place of the first". अचाम् is a genitive of the group, "of
the vowels". The Kāśikā puts the three together: *parato 'ṅgasya acām ādeḥ acaḥ sthāne vṛddhir
bhavati*, "before [such an affix], in place of the first vowel among the vowels of the aṅga, vṛddhi
comes" [REF:kashika 7.2.117]. The bracket line holds the rest: the result, वृद्धिः, comes from
7.2.114, and the condition, ञ्णिति "before a ñ- or ṇ-marked [affix]", from 7.2.115 — the run of rules
that gave जगाम its आ in c03.l04. The ñ/ṇ condition is carried, not written in the rule.

The vṛddhi vowels are prompt 1's, by 1.1.1: आ ऐ औ. लोक → लौक-: ओ → औ. देव → दैव-: ए → ऐ. A first vowel
that is a vṛddhi vowel already stays as it is: the आ of पाणिनि.

One more marker brings the same vṛddhi: a k-marked taddhita, by a rule in Teacher Notes. And one sound
that looks like a marker brings none: the क् of the -क in बालकः is the affix's own sound, because 1.3.8
[B] makes a क् a marker only "at the beginning of an affix that is not a taddhita". §15 (3) is the box.

Second, the last vowel.

**9. D, 6.4.148 यस्येति च** (yasyeti ca).

Bracket line: [carried (data file): तद्धिते, 6.4.144; लोपः, 6.4.147] [headings (data file): अङ्गस्य, 6.4.1] (further headings: Teacher Notes)

English: "Of a bha base ending in इ/ई or अ/आ, the final vowel is dropped before ई and before a
taddhita." [REF:ashtadhyayi 6.4.148]; [REF:kashika 6.4.148]

The rule needs a bha base, and you know when a base is bha: before an affix that begins with य or a
vowel, by 1.4.18 यचि भम् [Q, owned: c02.l01]; before one that begins with a consonant it is pada, by
1.4.17 [Q, owned]. In c02.l01 those affixes were case endings; the stretch the two rules carry, "from
सुँ on", runs on through the taddhitas (Teacher Notes). So before -ईय and -इक the base is bha, and its
last vowel goes: पाणिन्-ईय, लौक्-इक. Before -ता, -त्व, -मत् and -वत् it is pada, and nothing goes:
एकता keeps its अ, बलवान् its अ. Whitney, in §5, said the same of the language: before a vowel or y,
final a and i are lost.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the vṛddhi diphthongs, the क्ष of पक्षी, the ण्ड of पण्डितः and the त्त of बलवत्तरः. The
words-from-words image (`planned:assets/images/volume-4/v4c04l03-words-from-words.svg`) shows a word,
an affix sliding onto it, and the first vowel lighting up when the affix carries ञ् or ण्; say each
word as you point to it.

1. **औ in लौकिक.** Open on अ /ɐ/, jaw half open, lips neutral; then round the lips and raise the back
   of the tongue towards उ /u/ — one smooth glide, one syllable: /lɐu̯/.
2. **ऐ in दैवम्.** Open on अ /ɐ/ and glide the front of the tongue up towards इ /i/: /d̪ɐi̯/ — not a
   long ए.
3. **क्ष in पक्षी.** The back of the tongue closes on the soft palate for क /k/, then the tip curls
   back for ष /ʂ/ with no vowel between: /kʂ/.
4. **ण्ड in पण्डितः.** The tip curls back and stays there for both ण /ɳ/ and ड /ɖ/.
5. **त्त in बलवत्तरः.** The tip at the upper teeth for त /t̪/, held for twice its length: the त् of
   the stem बलवत् and the त of -तर.

Mirror check: say लोक — लौकिक. In the first the lips round once, for ओ, and stay round; in the second
they move, from neutral into the round.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; they feed the quiz distractors.

1. **\*ऐकता.** *Fix:* एकता. *Why:* तल् has no ञ् or ण्: no vṛddhi (7.2.117).
2. **\*एक्ता.** *Fix:* एकता. *Why:* before -ता the base is pada, not bha: nothing drops (6.4.148 needs
   bha).
3. **\*बलमान्.** *Fix:* बलवान्. *Why:* by 8.2.9, after a base in अ, the म् of मतुप् → व्.
4. **\*लोकिक.** *Fix:* लौकिक. *Why:* ठञ् is ñ-marked: vṛddhi (7.2.117).
5. **\*पाणिनियम्.** *Fix:* पाणिनीयम्. *Why:* by 7.1.2, छ → ईय, with a long ई.
6. **\*यदा गुरुर्वदति, तावत् …** *Fix:* यावद्गुरुर्वदति, तावत् … *Why:* the pair is यावत् … तावत्; यदा
   pairs with तदा, as v3.c05.l03 taught.

> इनि, छ — leaves the first vowel as it is: एकता, गुरुत्वम्, धनवान्, पक्षी, पाणिनीयम्. The -क of
> बालकः and कुटुम्बकम् is no k-marker: in a taddhita, 1.3.8 [B] leaves an initial क् alone, and
> कुटुम्बकम् keeps the उ of कुटुम्बम्.

### (4) Bha or pada before a taddhita

> **The first sound of the affix decides the last vowel.** A taddhita that begins with a vowel or य्
> makes the base before it bha (1.4.18 [Q, owned]), and its final इ/ई or अ/आ drops (6.4.148):
> पाणिन्-ईय, लौक्-इक, दाशरथ्-इ. A taddhita that begins with a consonant leaves the base pada (1.4.17
> [Q, owned]), and nothing drops: एक-ता, बल-वत्, गुरु-त्व.
