## 1. Hook

You met भवति in v1.c04.l01, among your first Sanskrit verbs. Volume 3 opened it up as भो + अति and left a
note: named here, taught in Volume 4 (v3.c04.l04). You also own the rule that turns the ओ into
अव्: 6.1.78 एचोऽयवायावः, "ए, ओ, ऐ, औ before a vowel become अय्, अव्, आय्, आव्" [v3.c04.l01].

Now read the rule itself, not its English. It names four sounds and four results. But where
does it say "before a vowel"? And which of the four does ओ become, and why not अय्? Today the
rule answers in its own grammar — in cases.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — where today's three definitions stand, and how the commentators read
every rule through them.

Today's three Q tools stand together in the first quarter of the first book of the
Aṣṭādhyāyī: 1.1.49, 1.1.66 and 1.1.67. The rules they explain stand far later — 6.1.77 and
6.1.78 in the sixth book, 7.1.9 and 7.3.101 in the seventh, 8.4.40 in the eighth. Book 1
says once how a genitive, a locative and an ablative are to be read; every later rule then
writes its cases and leaves the reading to those definitions. 1.3.10, the rule of "in order",
stands in the third quarter of the same first book.

The commentators read the rules this way, and you can watch them do it.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** The commentators read the rules through this code. The Kāśikā
> glosses इको यणचि as *aci parataḥ iko yaṇādeśo bhavati* 'when a vowel follows, the yaṇ
> substitute comes in place of an ik' — the locative turned into 'when … follows', the
> genitive kept, the nominative called the substitute. [REF:kashika 6.1.77]
>
> Kāśikā, on 6.1.77 — text of the GRETIL e-text:
>
> अचि परतः इको यणादेशो भवति। (aci parataḥ iko yaṇādeśo bhavati.)
>
> अचि परतः इकः यणादेशः भवति। (aci parataḥ ikaḥ yaṇādeśaḥ bhavati.)
>
> "When a vowel follows, the yaṇ substitute comes in place of an ik."

The second line is the pause form. Read the gloss word by word against the sūtra: अचि is the
sūtra's own locative, and परतः, "following", spells out what that locative means; इको is the
sūtra's genitive इकः, unchanged; and यण्, the sūtra's nominative, has become यणादेशः, "the
yaṇ substitute" — the result named as a substitute. The line is printed as the e-text writes
it. It is here to be read, not learned: nothing in this course asks you to reproduce it.
Chapter 6 reads such glosses with you.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why a rule of three words can say so much: Sanskrit marks a word's job
with its ending.

Sanskrit marks each noun's role with an ending, so its words can change places and still be
read, and Pāṇini's rules rely on it: इको यणचि
is three words, and the endings alone tell you which word is replaced (इकः), which word is
the result (यण्) and which names the context (अचि). Swap the words around and the rule says
the same thing.

There is one place where the endings do not settle it. In nouns, the genitive and the ablative
singular share one form outside the masculine and neuter a-stems.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "The ablative has a special ending … only in a-stems, masc. and neut. …
> Everywhere else, the ablative is identical with the genitive." [REF:whitney1889 §307]

In the a-stems you own the two apart: रामस्य against रामात्, गृहस्य against गृहात्. But the
names of sounds in Pāṇini's rules are not a-stems. अच्, इक्, अत्, भिस् are consonant stems, and
स्तु in स्तोः is a u-stem: their genitive and ablative singular are one form — इकः, अचः, अतः,
भिसः, स्तोः. So in a rule the form alone cannot tell you whether अतः is "of short a" or
"after short a". §8 Step 4 gives the test the course uses.

One more reason the grammar needs a definition at all. In ordinary Sanskrit a genitive has
many senses — the owner, the thing near, the whole of which something is a part, and more.
The Kāśikā says so in its comment on 1.1.49 (the line is in Teacher Notes). A rule cannot
afford that range, so 1.1.49 fixes one sense for a genitive in the rules whose link is not
otherwise fixed: "in place of".

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the day's idea in seven lines, each printed joined, its pause form
beneath, then the gloss.

> इक इत्यत्र षष्ठी, स्थान इत्यर्थः। (ika ity atra ṣaṣṭhī, sthāna ity arthaḥ.)
> इकः इति अत्र षष्ठी, स्थाने इति अर्थः। (ikaḥ iti atra ṣaṣṭhī, sthāne iti arthaḥ.)
> "In 'ikaḥ' there is a genitive, meaning 'in place of'." — 'इकः' में षष्ठी है, अर्थ है 'के
> स्थान पर'।

> अचीति सप्तमी, पूर्वस्य कार्यम्। (acīti saptamī, pūrvasya kāryam.)
> अचि इति सप्तमी, पूर्वस्य कार्यम्। (aci iti saptamī, pūrvasya kāryam.)
> "'aci' is a locative: the operation is on what comes before." — 'अचि' सप्तमी है: कार्य पहले
> वाले पर होता है।

> अत इति पञ्चमी, उत्तरस्य कार्यम्। (ata iti pañcamī, uttarasya kāryam.)
> अतः इति पञ्चमी, उत्तरस्य कार्यम्। (ataḥ iti pañcamī, uttarasya kāryam.)
> "'ataḥ' [in 7.1.9] is an ablative: the operation is on what comes after." — (7.1.9 में)
> 'अतः' पञ्चमी है: कार्य बाद वाले पर होता है।

> अतो दीर्घो यञीत्यत्र अत इति षष्ठी। (ato dīrgho yañīty atra ata iti ṣaṣṭhī.)
> अतो दीर्घो यञि इति अत्र अतः इति षष्ठी। (ato dīrgho yañi iti atra ataḥ iti ṣaṣṭhī.)
> "In अतो दीर्घो यञि, 'ataḥ' is a genitive." — अतो दीर्घो यञि में 'अतः' षष्ठी है।

> दीर्घ आदेशः। (dīrgha ādeśaḥ.)
> दीर्घः आदेशः। (dīrghaḥ ādeśaḥ.)
> "The long vowel is the substitute." — दीर्घ स्वर आदेश है।

> पूर्वो वर्ण इक्, उत्तरोऽच्। (pūrvo varṇa ik, uttaro 'c.)
> पूर्वः वर्णः इक्, उत्तरः अच्। (pūrvaḥ varṇaḥ ik, uttaraḥ ac.)
> "The earlier sound is an ik, the later an ac." — पहला वर्ण इक् है, बाद वाला अच्।

> सूत्रस्य विधिः कः? (sūtrasya vidhiḥ kaḥ?)
> सूत्रस्य विधिः कः? (sūtrasya vidhiḥ kaḥ?)
> "What is the sūtra's operation?" — सूत्र की विधि क्या है?

Five of the lines use today's headwords as free words: पूर्वस्य and उत्तरस्य — the genitive of
पूर्वः and उत्तरः, "of the preceding", "of the following" — then पूर्वः and उत्तरः themselves, in
the sixth line; आदेशः "substitute"; विधिः "operation". The case names षष्ठी, सप्तमी, पञ्चमी are labels, as in Volume 3; so are the
pratyāhāra names इक्, अच्. The joins are Volume 3's: -अः before इ or आ → -अ and a gap: इक इति, अत
इति, वर्ण इक्, दीर्घ आदेशः; -ए before इ → -अ and a gap: स्थान इति; -अः before a voiced sound → -ओ: पूर्वो
वर्णः; -अः before अ → -ओऽ: उत्तरोऽच्; इ before a vowel → य्: इत्यत्र, इत्यर्थः, यञीत्यत्र;
two like vowels → one long: अचीति. A comma and a daṇḍa stop every seam: षष्ठी, and इक्, stand in
their pause form. A quoted sūtra is printed as the data file has it, joined to its इति — यञि +
इति → यञीति — and stays as printed in the pause line. The fourth line keeps the quoted word
अतः apart from the अत्र before it: a word quoted as a form is joined to the इति after it, but
its first sound is never merged into the word before it, just as a quoted name's is not
(lesson 1).

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

भवति आपकी शुरुआती संस्कृत क्रियाओं में से एक है — पहले खण्ड से। तीसरे खण्ड में आपने देखा कि उसके भीतर भो + अति है, और
कि अगले स्वर से पहले ओ अव् बन जाता है — अयादि सन्धि, सूत्र 6.1.78 एचोऽयवायावः। पर उस सूत्र
में "स्वर से पहले" कहाँ लिखा है? और ओ चारों में से अव् ही क्यों बनता है, अय् क्यों नहीं? आज
इसका उत्तर सूत्र की अपनी भाषा देती है — **विभक्ति**, यानी शब्द के अन्त का वह रूप जो वाक्य में
उसका काम दिखाता है।

हिन्दी में हम यह बात परसर्ग के साथ एक और शब्द जोड़कर कहते हैं: "इक् **के स्थान पर** यण्", "स्वर **से
पहले**" (हिन्दी माध्यम में संस्कृत पढ़ाते समय "स्वर **परे होने पर**" भी कहा जाता है), "ह्रस्व अ **के बाद**"। संस्कृत
यही बात केवल विभक्ति के प्रत्यय से कहती है — इकः, अचि, अतः; उसे न "स्थान" शब्द चाहिए, न
"पहले", न "बाद" [REF:kachru2006]। और पाणिनि के सूत्रों में हर विभक्ति का काम तय है:

- **षष्ठी — के स्थान पर।** षष्ठी में रखा शब्द वह है जिसकी जगह कुछ और आता है — सूत्र 1.1.49।
- **सप्तमी — से पहले।** सप्तमी में रखा शब्द वह है जो आगे आता है; कार्य उसके ठीक पहले वाले
  वर्ण पर होता है — सूत्र 1.1.66।
- **पञ्चमी — के बाद।** पञ्चमी में रखा शब्द वह है जो पहले आता है; कार्य उसके ठीक बाद वाले पर
  होता है — सूत्र 1.1.67।
- **प्रथमा — परिणाम।** प्रथमा में रखा शब्द वह है जो नया आता है — उसे **आदेश** कहते हैं।

अब इको यणचि पढ़िए: इकः षष्ठी है — इक् के स्थान पर; यण् प्रथमा है — यण् आता है; अचि सप्तमी
है — अच् से पहले। यानी "स्वर से पहले इ, उ, ऋ, ऌ के स्थान पर य्, व्, र्, ल्"। इति + अवदत् में इ
के बाद अ है, इसलिए इ के स्थान पर य् आता है: इत्यवदत्।

यहाँ एक जाल है। इकः, अकः, अतः जैसे शब्दों में षष्ठी और पञ्चमी एकवचन का रूप एक ही है। तो
विभक्ति रूप से नहीं, सूत्र के अर्थ से तय होती है — और अब से हर सूत्र के नीचे उसका
**पदच्छेद** छपेगा, हर पद की विभक्ति के नाम के साथ। पहचानने का प्रश्न यह है: "परिणाम किसके
स्थान पर आ सकता है?" 7.1.9 अतो भिस ऐस् में परिणाम ऐस् एक प्रत्यय है, इसलिए वह प्रत्यय भिस्
के स्थान पर आता है: भिसः षष्ठी है, और अतः पञ्चमी — "ह्रस्व अ के बाद"। 7.3.101 अतो दीर्घो
यञि में परिणाम दीर्घ एक स्वर है, इसलिए वह ह्रस्व अ के स्थान पर आता है: यहाँ अतः षष्ठी है।

दो और नियम। सूत्र 1.3.10 के अनुसार, जब बदलने वाले और आने वाले वर्ण गिनती में बराबर हों, तो वे क्रम से
जुड़ते हैं: ए ओ ऐ औ के स्थान पर अय् अव् आय् आव् — पहले के स्थान पर पहला, दूसरे के स्थान पर
दूसरा। ओ दूसरा है, इसलिए अव्: भो + अति → भव् + अति → भवति। और सूत्र 1.1.50 के अनुसार, जहाँ कई
परिणाम सम्भव हों, वहाँ सबसे मिलता-जुलता आता है — यह पहले पाठ में आपने इ → य् में देखा था।

देवैः भी आज खुलता है। देव + भिस् में 7.1.9 से भिस् के स्थान पर ऐस् आता है: देव + ऐस्; फिर अ
और ऐ मिलकर ऐ बनते हैं — वृद्धि, सूत्र 6.1.88: देवैस्; और अन्त के स् का विसर्ग होता है — उसका नियम
अगले अध्याय में। तीसरे खण्ड ने -ऐः को अपवाद कहकर छोड़ दिया था; आज उसका नियम मिल गया।

आज के चार शब्द: **आदेश** — परिणाम, जो किसी के स्थान पर आता है; **पूर्व** — पहला, पहले वाला,
पूर्व दिशा भी; **उत्तर** — बाद वाला, उत्तर दिशा भी, और "जवाब" भी; और **विधि** — कार्य, जो सूत्र
करवाता है — और वह सूत्र भी जो उसे बताता है; तरीका; और भाग्य भी।

### Step 4 — the code, and the test for -अः

> **According to Pāṇini, a sūtra speaks in cases.** The **genitive** names what is replaced:
> "in place of X" (1.1.49). The **locative** names what follows: the operation applies before
> it (1.1.66). The **ablative** names what precedes: the operation applies after it (1.1.67).
> The **nominative** names the result.

Read इको यणचि with it: इकः [षष्ठी: in place of] · यण् [प्रथमा: the result] · अचि [सप्तमी:
before]. "In place of an इक् sound, before an अच् sound: a यण् sound." The locative does not
mean "in", as it does in वने "in the forest": in a rule, the locative of a sound means "before
it".

**One form, two cases.** In these words the genitive and the ablative singular have one form
(इकः, एचः, अकः, अतः, भिसः; स्तोः too): the form does not decide the case, the rule's sense does,
and the padaccheda printed under each sūtra names it. The test the course uses is one
question: **what can the result stand in place of?**

- In 7.1.9 अतो भिस ऐस् the result ऐस् is an ending, so it stands in place of the ending: भिसः is
  the genitive and अतः the ablative ("after short a").
- In 7.3.101 अतो दीर्घो यञि the result दीर्घः is a vowel, so it stands in place of the short a:
  अतः is the genitive.

When there are as many replaced items as results, they pair off in order (1.3.10). Where one
item could take several results, the most similar one wins (1.1.50).

**The trap box.**

- **The अतः pair.** Same form, two cases: ablative in 7.1.9, genitive in 7.3.101. Ask what the
  result can replace; the padaccheda confirms.
- **The bracket [पूर्वपरयोः].** In 6.1.101 अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः no word is in the genitive: अकः is
  ablative, "after an अक् sound". What is replaced is both sounds, the earlier and the later, by
  a heading that stands over the rule; the course prints it as a bracket, [पूर्वपरयोः: in place
  of both, the earlier and the later; carried from a heading, c02.l02] — a labelled preview.
  The heading's own sūtra and number are Chapter 2's.

उच्चारणम् — the sounds inside today's rules: a sūtra read aloud joined, then word by word, so

**Drill 1 — one sūtra, two readings.** Read इको यणचि as printed, in one breath; then in pause
form, a small stop after each word:

1. इको यणचि /ikoː jɐɳɐt͡ɕi/
2. इकः यण् अचि /ikɐh jɐɳ ɐt͡ɕi/

In the pause form you hear the endings: the -अः of इकः — the genitive, with its breath at the

1. इको यणचि
2. एचोऽयवायावः /eːt͡ɕoːjɐʋɑːjɑːʋɐh/
3. अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः /ɐkɐh sɐʋɐɾɳeː d̪iːɾgʱɐh/
4. अतो भिस ऐस् /ɐt̪oː bʱisɐ ɐi̯s/
5. अतो दीर्घो यञि /ɐt̪oː d̪iːɾgʱoː jɐɲi/
6. स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः /st̪oːh ɕt͡ɕun̪ɑː ɕt͡ɕuh/

**Drill 3 — अचि and अति.** The rule's word and the verb's ending sound alike and are not:

1. अचि /ɐt͡ɕi/ — the locative of अच्, "before a vowel"

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the two sounds that tell अचि from अति, and the ending you hear in every
genitive. The rule-speaks image (`planned:assets/images/volume-4/v4c01l03-a-rule-speaks.svg`)
prints इको यणचि with its three cases marked; say each word as you point to it.

1. **च in अचि.** The blade of the tongue presses the hard palate and releases with a short
   hiss: /t͡ɕ/. The tongue tip stays down behind the lower teeth.
2. **त in अति.** The tip of the tongue touches the back of the upper teeth and releases
   cleanly, with no hiss: /t̪/ — not the English t, made on the ridge behind the teeth.
3. **The -अः of a genitive at a pause.** इकः, अतः, भिसः end in a short /ɐ/ and a breath /h/:
   open the mouth for the vowel, then let the air out through the open throat. Joined before a
   voiced sound, the same ending is ओ /oː/, the lips rounded (इको यणचि, अतो भिस).

Mirror check: say अचि अति अचि अति. The jaw does not move; only the tongue moves, back and up for
च, forward to the teeth for त.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; they feed the quiz distractors.

1. **Reading a sūtra's locative as "in" (वने "in the forest").** *Fix:* in a rule the locative
   of a sound means "before it": अचि "before a vowel" (1.1.66).
2. **Taking every -अः word for a genitive: अतः in 7.1.9 "in place of a".** *Fix:* ask what the
   result can stand in place of: ऐस् is an ending, so it replaces भिस्; अतः is ablative, "after
   a".
3. **Taking every -अः word for an ablative: अतः in 7.3.101 "after a".** *Fix:* दीर्घः is a
   vowel, so it replaces the short a: अतः is genitive.
4. **Looking in 6.1.101 for a genitive word.** *Fix:* the replaced item is both sounds, by the
   heading [पूर्वपरयोः]; अकः is ablative.
5. **Pairing 6.1.78's results by sound, not by order (ओ → अय्).** *Fix:* 1.3.10: ओ is second of
   ए ओ ऐ औ, so अव्.
6. **Writing यण् आदेशः as two free words in joined text.** *Fix:* a consonant-final word joins
   the next vowel; the compound यणादेशः is the course's form (§13, model sentence 4).

## 17. Story

कथा — the grammarians' line, 3: the code in the commentators' hands.

The case code is not a modern invention. The commentators' glosses turn each rule into a
sentence with "in place of" and "when … follows" — you read one in §4, the Kāśikā's line on इको
यणचि, where the locative अचि became अचि परतः and the nominative यण् became यणादेशः, "the yaṇ
substitute". The code you learned today is the one their sentences already use.

Do one thing with it, aloud. Take the four rules of §15's table that have a genitive word but no ablative —
6.1.77, 6.1.78, 7.3.101, 8.4.40 — and for each make the commentators' kind of sentence in
English: "In place of …, before/after …, … comes." For 6.1.77: "In place of an इक् sound,
before a vowel, a यण् sound comes." Then do 7.1.9 and see why it reads "after short a, in place
of भिस्, ऐस् comes". Chapter 6 reads such glosses with you.

पुनरावृत्तिः — one screen to remember.

> **How a rule speaks.** According to Pāṇini, a sūtra speaks in cases: **genitive** (षष्ठी) —
> in place of (1.1.49); **locative** (सप्तमी) — before: the operation is on what precedes it
> (1.1.66); **ablative** (पञ्चमी) — after: the operation is on what follows it (1.1.67);
> **nominative** (प्रथमा) — the result, the substitute (आदेशः). **One form, two cases:** इकः,
> अतः, अकः are genitive or ablative; ask "what can the result stand in place of?" — 7.1.9's अतः
> is ablative, 7.3.101's genitive. **Equal sets pair in order** (1.3.10): ओ → अव्, so भो + अति →
> **भवति**; ए → अय्, so **नयति**. **देव + भिस्** → देव + ऐस् by 7.1.9 → देवैस् by 6.1.88 →
> **देवैः**. **Brackets:** [अचि] is carried (c02.l03); [पूर्वपरयोः] is a heading (c02.l02).

**Why the derive-it-yourself items start from अग्ने, गुरो, ने.** How the final vowel of the stem
or root became ए or ओ is guṇa (c02.l02), and ने's ए stands before the class sign (c03.l02);
today the learner takes those strings as given and runs only 6.1.78 and 1.3.10. Each item says
so. The two owned guṇa joins of exercise 5, परोपकारः and गणेशः, run 6.1.87 with 1.1.50 choosing
the vowel; नेति is not used here (it is a retake item of the exam's task D).

**What students find hard.** The locative read as "in": exercise 1 is the cure — do it fast, the
two columns side by side, and say the contrast aloud each time. The second hard thing is the
अतः pair: never let a learner answer "genitive" or "ablative" without saying what the result
replaces. Hindi-medium learners know "स्वर परे होने पर" from school Sanskrit; show that it is the
locative's job put into Hindi.

**Differentiation.** Fast learners: ask why 6.1.101 cannot use a genitive word for what it
replaces (it would have to name two sounds at once; the heading does it — see 6.1.84 above).
Learners who struggle: give them only the four-line code on a card and the six rules on the
worksheet, and let them circle and box before they name any case.
