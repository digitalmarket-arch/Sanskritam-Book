## 1. Hook

Since v3.c04.l01 you have said इत्यवदत् (ity avadat), "… he said", and you have been told that
one sūtra of Pāṇini states it: 6.1.77 इको यणचि (iko yaṇ aci). Five syllables, *i-ko-ya-ṇa-ci*,
and nobody has yet taught you to read them. Put the two side by side:

| what you say | what the rule says |
|---|---|
| इति + अवदत् → इत्यवदत् | इको यणचि |

**Which of these five syllables says "i", which says "y", which says "before a vowel"?** By the
end of this lesson you will read the rule as a sentence: "ik before ac: yaṇ". The word इक् is not
a word of Sanskrit; it is a name made from a list. Today's list is fourteen lines long.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — where the fourteen sūtras stand, the story told about them, how many names
they make, and what the 1.x book got wrong.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** The fourteen sūtras are handed down with Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī, at its
> head, but they are not among its numbered rules: the Aṣṭādhyāyī's own rules start at 1.1.1.
> The Kāśikā, a commentary on the whole Aṣṭādhyāyī (c. 7th c. CE), explains them first, 'for the
> sake of the pratyāhāras' [REF:maheshvarasutras]; [REF:kashika].

So every number you read in this volume — 1.1.71, 6.1.77 — is a rule of the eight books, and the
fourteen lines are not numbered among them. They are the list the numbered rules point into.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** Legend. A legend tells that when Śiva danced, his drum sounded
> fourteen times and gave these sūtras. The 1.x book printed this under the label 'Legend', and
> so does this course: it is a story the tradition tells, not an account of where the list came
> from, and nothing in this course asks you to repeat it.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** How many pratyāhāras do Pāṇini's rules use? The Kāśikā answers sūtra
> by sūtra: for each marker it names the pratyāhāras made with it and a rule that uses each, and
> it sums them up in a verse. By its count there are 41. It notes one more, ञम्, used in a rule
> that is not among the Aṣṭādhyāyī's numbered sūtras, and, under लण्, that the र of 1.1.51 names
> both र and ल. So the answer depends on what is counted: 41 by its list, more if these are added.
> The 1.x book wrote: 'Panini built ~42 pratyāhāras from the 14 Maheshvara Sutras' (p. 95). This
> course gives no count without saying how it was made. [REF:kashika]

You will not learn 41 names. You learn the ones the rules you own use (§15), and you learn to
expand any of them from the fourteen lines — which is the point of the list.

### The 1.x errors, corrected in the open

The 1.x book's chapter on Pāṇini printed three errors in today's territory. Each is quoted with
its page and corrected here; §12 and §18 exercise 4 drill the corrections.

1. **चय्, झश्, जश् — p. 96.** The 1.x book printed: "• चय् (cay) = voiceless aspirated" ·
   "• झश् (jhaś) = voiced unaspirated stops (ज ब ग ड द)" · "• जश् (jaś) = also voiced
   unaspirated". **Corrected:** चय् = च ट त क प, voiceless and **unaspirated**; झश् names ten
   voiced stops, aspirated and unaspirated (झ भ घ ढ ध ज ब ग ड द); जश् names the five unaspirated
   ones (ज ब ग ड द). They are not the same set.
2. **The memory aid — p. 70.** The 1.x book printed: "Sutras 1-4: VOWELS (अच्) / Sutra 5-6:
   SEMI-VOWELS (य् व् र् ल्) / Sutra 7: NASALS (ञ म ङ ण न) / Sutras 8-11: STOPS (झ भ घ ढ ध ज ब ग
   ड द ख फ छ ठ थ च ट त क प) / Sutras 13-14: SIBILANTS + ह (श ष स ह)". **Corrected:** sūtra 12,
   कपय्, has no line there — its क प are put under "8-11" — and the ह of sūtra 5 is missing from
   "5-6". The rebuilt aid, with all fourteen, is in §15.
3. **इको यणचि's number — p. 71.** The 1.x book printed: "This lets Panini write rules like
   "इको यणचि" (1.1.45) = "इक् becomes यण् before अच्"". **Corrected:** इको यणचि is 6.1.77, as
   v3.c04.l01 said; 1.1.45 is a different rule (its text: इग्यणः सम्प्रसारणम्)
   [REF:ashtadhyayi 6.1.77]; [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.45].

The 1.x gloss of 6.1.77 itself — "इक् becomes यण् before अच्" — is right. What was wrong was the
address, and the two sound-sets and the memory aid around it.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — the grid behind the order: why the fourteen lines do not follow the
varṇamālā you learned in Volume 1.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The stops of Sanskrit form five series by place. "In each series there are
> two surd members, two sonant, and one nasal"; "The members are by the Hindu grammarians called
> respectively *first, second, third, fourth*, and *last* or *fifth*"; "The first and third
> members of each series are the ordinary corresponding surd and sonant mutes"; "The second and
> fourth of each series are aspirates". [REF:whitney1889 §32] [REF:whitney1889 §33]
> [REF:whitney1889 §34] [REF:whitney1889 §35] [REF:whitney1889 §37]

Whitney's "surd" is our voiceless, his "sonant" our voiced. That is the grid you built in Volume
1: five rows of place (throat, palate, roof of the mouth, teeth, lips), five columns of manner
(voiceless, voiceless aspirated, voiced, voiced aspirated, nasal). The varṇamālā reads the grid
**row by row**: क ख ग घ ङ, then च छ ज झ ञ. The fourteen sūtras read the same grid **column by
column**:

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The fourteen sūtras list the vowels first, in sūtras 1–4; then the
> semivowels with ह, 5–6; the nasals, 7; the voiced aspirated stops, 8–9; the voiced unaspirated
> stops, 10; the voiceless stops, 11–12 — aspirated ख फ छ ठ थ and unaspirated च ट त in 11,
> unaspirated क प in 12; the sibilants, 13; and ह again, 14. [REF:maheshvarasutras 1–14]

Why by column? Because the grammar's rules speak of manner groups. A rule about voicing needs one
name for "every voiced stop", and the column order lets a single stretch of the list be that
name. You already own such a rule: according to Pāṇini, 8.2.39 झलां जशोऽन्ते, the rule behind
षट् → षड् in v3.c04.l03, says that at a word's end a झल् becomes a जश् — one stretch of the list
in place of another. §15 expands both names.

**The two properties, in modern terms.** Voicing is the vocal folds vibrating while the sound is
made: put a hand on your throat and say ज, then च — the buzz is there in ज and not in च.
Aspiration is a puff of breath after the release: hold a strip of paper before your lips and say
फ, then प — it moves for फ. Sanskrit's voiced aspirates (घ झ ढ ध भ) are breathy-voiced, written
with a raised ʱ (STYLE_GUIDE §2): voiced, with breath after. So चय्, च ट त क प, names the **first** member of each
of the five series — voiceless and unaspirated. That one fact is the 1.x book's error on p. 96, put right.

The course principle behind the lesson, with its one source: formulaic chunks are taught whole and
analysed later [REF:nation2013]. You were given इको यणचि whole in Volume 3; today it is analysed.
The spacing of today's two bookings (§20) follows the course's one claim for it: spaced review
helps retention [REF:cepeda2006].

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the lesson's idea in five lines of Sanskrit, each printed joined, its pause
form beneath, then the gloss. Every word is one you own, one of today's five headwords, or a label
(a sūtra, a pratyāhāra, इत्).

> सूत्राणामादिरइउण्। (sūtrāṇām ādir aiuṇ.)
> सूत्राणाम् आदिः अइउण्। (sūtrāṇām ādiḥ aiuṇ.)
> "The first of the sūtras is अइउण्." — सूत्रों में पहला अइउण् है।

> सूत्रस्यान्त्यो वर्ण इत्। (sūtrasyāntyo varṇa it.)
> सूत्रस्य अन्त्यः वर्णः इत्। (sūtrasya antyaḥ varṇaḥ it.)
> "The last sound of each of the fourteen sūtras is a marker." — चौदह सूत्रों में से हर सूत्र
> का अन्तिम वर्ण इत् है।

> अजिति स्वराणां प्रत्याहारः। (aj iti svarāṇāṃ pratyāhāraḥ.)
> अच् इति स्वराणाम् प्रत्याहारः। (ac iti svarāṇām pratyāhāraḥ.)
> "अच् is the abbreviation for the vowels." — अच् स्वरों का प्रत्याहार है।

> अच् हल् च द्वौ प्रत्याहारौ। (ac hal ca dvau pratyāhārau.)
> अच् हल् च द्वौ प्रत्याहारौ। (ac hal ca dvau pratyāhārau.)
> "अच् and हल् are two pratyāhāras." — अच् और हल् दो प्रत्याहार हैं।

> इतो लोपः। (ito lopaḥ.)
> इतः लोपः। (itaḥ lopaḥ.)
> "A marker disappears." — इत् का लोप होता है।

**सूत्रम्** in line 2 is one of the fourteen. In इको यणचि the last sound is no marker: 1.3.3
speaks of a final consonant, and इको यणचि ends in a vowel. In line 4 the two names stand unjoined,
as quoted names: joined, अच् हल् would become अज्झल्, and the names would no longer be seen. च
and द्वौ are owned (द्वौ is the masculine nominative dual of द्वि), and प्रत्याहारौ is today's
headword in the dual. **लोपः** in line 5 is Chapter 2's word; here it is read from 1.3.9's तस्य
लोपः, printed in §15: a labelled preview, not a word you are asked to use yet. इतः is the label इत् with
its genitive ending, "of a marker".

The joins are the ones Volume 3 taught: आदिः + अ → आदिरइउण् — -इः before a vowel → र्; सूत्रस्य +
अन्त्यः → सूत्रस्यान्त्यः — two a-vowels, one long; अन्त्यः वर्णः → अन्त्यो वर्णः and इतः लोपः →
इतो लोपः — -अः before a voiced consonant → ओ; वर्णः इत् → वर्ण इत् — -अः before a vowel other
than अ → अ, with a gap; अच् इति → अजिति — a final stop voiced before a vowel; स्वराणाम्
प्रत्याहारः → स्वराणां प्रत्याहारः — a final म् before a consonant → ं.

## 8. English Explanation

No new sound and no new letter: every sound of the fourteen sūtras has been on your chart since
Volume 1. The lesson's load is one list and one way of naming stretches of it. The recipe is the
volume's: a form you own, the question it raises, the tools that answer it, the derivation done
with the teacher, the same tools run by you, production.

### Step 1 — the form you own

इत्यवदत् "… he said", from v3.c04.l01, where you split it as इति + अवदत् and named the join यण्.
v3.c04.l01 also told you which sūtra states the join:

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** According to Pāṇini's sūtra 6.1.77 इको यणचि (iko yaṇ aci): "इ, उ, ऋ
> become य्, व्, र् before a vowel". [REF:ashtadhyayi 6.1.77]

That was a paraphrase. The sūtra itself has three words, and you could not read them.

### Step 2 — the question

What are इक्, यण् and अच्? They are not in any dictionary as words of the language. Each is a
**name** for a set of sounds, made from a list that stands before Pāṇini's numbered rules (§4).
To read the rule you need the list and the way names are made from it.

### Step 3 — the tools: the list, the marker, the name

**The list (T9).** Fourteen short lines, अइउण् to हल्, given in full in §15. Write them into the
Volume 1 grid before you recite them — the main risk of this lesson is learning them as a chant
with no grid behind it. On the grid, the consonant lines run down the **columns** (§5):

| place ↓ · manner → | voiceless | voiceless aspirated | voiced | voiced aspirated | nasal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| कण्ठ्य throat | क · 12 | ख · 11 | ग · 10 | घ · 9 | ङ · 7 |
| तालव्य palate | च · 11 | छ · 11 | ज · 10 | झ · 8 | ञ · 7 |
| मूर्धन्य roof | ट · 11 | ठ · 11 | ड · 10 | ढ · 9 | ण · 7 |
| दन्त्य teeth | त · 11 | थ · 11 | द · 10 | ध · 9 | न · 7 |
| ओष्ठ्य lips | प · 12 | फ · 11 | ब · 10 | भ · 8 | म · 7 |

The number in each cell is the sūtra that lists the sound. Outside the stops: य व र in 5, ल in 6;
श ष स in 13; ह in 5 **and** 14. The nasals are one line (7); the voiced aspirates two lines (8–9);
the voiced unaspirated one line (10); the voiceless two lines (11–12).

**The marker.** Each line ends in a consonant that is not one of its sounds: the ण् of अइउण्, the
ट् of हयवरट्. It is a marker, **इत्** (it). Two sūtras of the grammar say so (§15): 1.3.3 — the
final consonant of a teaching is a marker; 1.3.9 — the marker is dropped. "A teaching" means a
list as first taught, such as these fourteen lines; the last consonant of an ordinary word, the
त् of अवदत्, is no marker.

**The name.** A sound of the list followed by a marker names every sound from that sound up to
the marker, and the sound itself: that pair is a **pratyāhāra** (प्रत्याहारः, today's headword).
1.1.71 states it (§15). The markers passed on the way are not among the sounds named. So:

- अच् — from अ to the च् of ऐऔच्: अ इ उ ऋ ऌ ए ओ ऐ औ, all the vowels;
- इक् — from इ to the क् of ऋऌक्: इ उ ऋ ऌ;
- यण् — from य to the ण् of लण्: य व र ल.

Now 6.1.77 reads word by word: इकः "of an ik" · यण् "a yaṇ" · अचि "before an ac" — **"ik
before ac: yaṇ"**. (Why "of" and "before": the case code, v4.c01.l03.)

### Step 4 — the derivation, with the teacher

§15 sets out इति + अवदत् → इत्यवदत् in five steps, each with the sūtra that does it. Steps 1–2
find the sounds in their sets (इ is an इक् sound, अ an अच् sound); step 3 applies 6.1.77; step 4
chooses which यण्: य्, because इ and य् are both made at the palate — Volume 1 filed इ with
the palatal row (v1.c03.l02) and made य at the ई position (v1.c04.l01). The sūtra behind step 4 is printed as a preview; you read it in
v4.c01.l03. Every derivation is Pāṇini's analysis of a form, so every derivation item in this
volume is headed "Derive, according to Pāṇini, …".

### Step 5 — the same tools, run by you

With the teacher still beside you, run the same steps on three more owned joins from
v3.c04.l01: इति + आह → इत्याह — Whitney's own example of the join —, अपि + अस्ति → अप्यस्ति, सु +
आगतम् → स्वागतम्. In the third, the replaced sound is उ, and the यण् nearest to उ is व्: both use
the lips (§18 exercise 5).

### Step 6 — production

You now say and write Sanskrit about the sound system: "अच् is the abbreviation for the
vowels", "अइउण्. Its last sound is ṇ.", "how many syllables are in इको यणचि? Five." §16 is a
teacher and a student saying exactly that; §18 exercise 6 asks you to write six such sentences,
joined, with the pause form beneath, and to say them aloud.

Then the 1.x corrections (§4): चय् is unaspirated, झश् is not जश्, the memory aid gains sūtra 12,
and इको यणचि is 6.1.77. And the payoff: the five syllables you could not read in Volume 3 now
read "ik before ac: yaṇ".

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — the sounds inside the rules: reciting the fourteen. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-4/v4c01l01-fourteen-recited.mp3` (the fourteen, slowly and then at a
steady pace; each line once alone, then the whole list).

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** Traditional recitation sounds each consonant with its helping अ and
> ends each line on its marker — the Kāśikā says the अ is "for pronunciation" (§15).
> [REF:kashika]

**Drill 1 — the recited line.** Say it line by line after the track, then alone:

*a i u ṇ · ṛ ḷ k · e o ṅ · ai au c · ha ya va raṭ · laṇ · ña ma ṅa ṇa nam · jha bhañ · gha ḍha
dhaṣ · ja ba ga ḍa daś · kha pha cha ṭha tha ca ṭa tav · ka pay · śa ṣa sar · hal*

Three things to hear. The vowel lines are said as written, *a i u ṇ*, with no helping vowel
added. Every consonant of lines 5–14 carries its helping अ, *ha ya va*, and only the marker is
bare: *raṭ*, *nam*, *bhañ*. The marker is sounded — it closes the line — but it is not counted
among the line's sounds.

**Drill 2 — voicing at the throat.** Hand flat on the throat. Say ज … च … ज … च: the buzz comes
and goes. Then down sūtras 10 and 11: *ja ba ga ḍa da* buzz all the way; *ca ṭa ta* do not.

**Drill 3 — aspiration at the lips.** A strip of paper an inch before the lips. प … फ … प … फ:
the strip moves only for फ. Then sūtra 12, *ka pa*: the strip stays still — these are the
unaspirated voiceless stops, the first members of their rows (§5). Then sūtra 8, *jha bha*: the
voice runs on into a breathy release.

**Drill 4 — the two हs.** Say हयवरट् and हल् one after the other. The ह is the same sound, /ɦ/,
in both; the list simply names it twice. Ask yourself where each one stands on the grid — nowhere
among the stops: it is one of the ūṣman sounds of Volume 1.

**Drill 5 — counting syllables.** Clap इको यणचि: *i · ko · ya · ṇa · ci* — five claps, five
syllables, five अक्षराणि. A syllable is a vowel with whatever consonants lean on it; the word
अक्षरम्, today's headword, is the tradition's name for it.

**Drill 6 — the anusvāra before प.** In अजिति स्वराणां प्रत्याहारः the anusvāra stands before प,
so it is said as the lip nasal: स्वराणां प्रत्याहारः /sʋɐɾɑːɳɑːm pɾɐt̪jɑːɦɑːɾɐh/. The same in
व्यञ्जनानां प्रत्याहारः.

**Schwa deletion.** A Hindi speaker's habit is to drop the अ inside a line: *hayavarṭ*, *jabagaḍdaś*.
The tradition sounds every helping अ: §12 mistake 6.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the grid felt in the mouth. The grid image
(`planned:assets/images/volume-4/v4c01l01-fourteen-on-the-grid.svg`) shows the fourteen lines
written into the Volume 1 grid, the markers in a second colour.

1. **The palate check (इ, य्).** Say इ, hold it, and let it slide into य्: the tongue does not
   move from the hard palate; only the vowel shortens into a glide. That is step 4 of today's
   derivation in your mouth — the यण् "most like" इ is the one made in the same place.
2. **The lips check (उ, व्).** Mirror: say उ, then व्. Both use the lips — rounded for उ, the
   lower lip against the upper teeth for /ʋ/. That is why सु + आगतम् gives स्वागतम्, not
   \*स्यागतम्.
3. **The throat check (voicing).** Hand on the throat for sūtras 8–10: the buzz never stops.
   For sūtras 11–12 it never starts.
4. **The breath check (aspiration).** Paper strip for sūtra 11 (*kha pha cha ṭha tha*: it moves)
   against the end of the same line and sūtra 12 (*ca ṭa ta*, *ka pa*: it does not).

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; these feed the quiz distractors.

| # | Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Counting the marker among the sounds: अच् "includes च्" | the marker closes the stretch; it is never one of the sounds named (1.1.71 names "the sounds between, and itself") |
| 2 | चय् read as "voiceless aspirated" (the 1.x gloss) | चय् = च ट त क प: the first member of each row — voiceless, unaspirated |
| 3 | झश् taken for जश् | झश् starts at झ [sūtra 8]: ten voiced stops; जश् starts at ज [sūtra 10]: five |
| 4 | हल् started from the ह of sūtra 14 ("हल् = ह") | a pratyāhāra starts at the first sound named; हल् is formed with the ह of हयवरट्, so it covers every consonant |
| 5 | Reciting the sūtras as a chant with no grid: unable to say where क or श stands | write each line into the Volume 1 grid before reciting; say the row and the manner of each consonant line |
| 6 | Hindi-speaker schwa deletion in the recitation: *hayavarṭ* for *ha ya va raṭ* (or dropping the helping अ altogether) | every consonant of sūtras 5–14 is sounded with its अ; only the marker is bare |

   "अच् is the abbreviation for the vowels." — अच् स्वरों का प्रत्याहार है।

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — the fourteen sūtras (T9), the marker, the pratyāhāra, and the rule you could not read,
read. First, the conventions of the volume, said once.

### Conventions, said once

**Tiers.** Every sūtra printed in this volume carries a tier letter beside its number. **Q** —
quoted: read with its word-by-word division (padaccheda) and a gloss of each word; you say what
it does, by gist. **B** — one of the 26 sūtras of the भवति derivation that the volume builds step
by step: read with its padaccheda; at the volume's end you know it by number and gist. **D** —
the authority for one step of a derivation: number, text, one English line; never recalled by
number. This chapter has no **K** (a sūtra on a reference card) and no **R** (a sūtra read with
its commentary in Chapter 6). A sūtra you own from Volume 3 is marked "owned"; a step that belongs
to a later lesson is marked with that lesson.

**Quoted sounds stay unjoined.** When a line quotes sounds or a list of sounds (इ उ ऋ ऌ; अ आ),
they are printed apart, never joined: joined, अ आ would merge into one आ and इ उ ऋ ऌ into
something else — the very joins being named. The Kāśikā writes them the same way. A quoted sūtra
is printed as the Aṣṭādhyāyī text has it, joined (इको यणचि), and is never re-joined or split.

**Quoted forms join the इति after them.** A quoted form — a name, a sūtra, a word — is joined
to the इति that follows it: हलिति, अजिति, इको यणचीति. Its first sound is never fused with the
word before it, so that the form can still be seen: अत्र अत इति, not अत्रात इति. One limit, for
your own sentences: a name that ends in ण् after a short vowel — अण्, इण्, यण्, अइउण् — is not
set before इति or any other vowel, because by rule the ण् would double there, and this chapter
does not teach that seam; say the name alone, before a daṇḍa, or inside a compound
(इण्प्रत्याहारे). The Laghusiddhāntakaumudī prints अणिति, without the doubling
[REF:laghukaumudi on 1.1.71]; the course does not copy that form into its own sentences
(Teacher Notes). Every other seam of a line is joined as Volume 3 taught. These conventions hold in every lesson of the chapter.

**Status marks.** ✓ productive — "you produce it: exercises, quiz and exam may ask for it";
◐ recognition — "you read it when you meet it; nothing asks you to produce it, and nothing scores
it"; ○ display — "shown once, with its address; not yours yet".

— छात्रः: गुरो, सूत्राणामादिः कः? (guro, sūtrāṇām ādiḥ kaḥ?)
  गुरो, सूत्राणाम् आदिः कः? (guro, sūtrāṇām ādiḥ kaḥ?)
  "Teacher, which is the first of the sūtras?" — गुरुजी, सूत्रों में पहला कौन-सा है?

— गुरुः: अइउण्। तस्यान्त्यो वर्णो णकारः। (aiuṇ. tasyāntyo varṇo ṇakāraḥ.)
  अइउण्। तस्य अन्त्यः वर्णः णकारः। (aiuṇ. tasya antyaḥ varṇaḥ ṇakāraḥ.)
  "अइउण्. Its last sound is ṇ." — अइउण्। उसका अन्तिम वर्ण णकार है।

— छात्रः: णकारोऽपि वर्णः? (ṇakāro 'pi varṇaḥ?)
  णकारः अपि वर्णः? (ṇakāraḥ api varṇaḥ?)
  "Is ṇ also one of the sounds [of the list]?" — क्या णकार भी सूची का वर्ण है?

— गुरुः: न। स इत्। इतो लोपः। (na. sa it. ito lopaḥ.)
  न। सः इत्। इतः लोपः। (na. saḥ it. itaḥ lopaḥ.)
  "No. It is a marker. A marker disappears." — नहीं। वह इत् है। इत् का लोप होता है।

— छात्रः: अजिति कति वर्णाः? (aj iti kati varṇāḥ?)
  अच् इति कति वर्णाः? (ac iti kati varṇāḥ?)
  "How many sounds does अच् name?" — अच् में कितने वर्ण हैं?

— गुरुः: नव स्वराः। अ इ उ ऋ ऌ ए ओ ऐ औ। (nava svarāḥ. a i u ṛ ḷ e o ai au.)
  नव स्वराः। अ इ उ ऋ ऌ ए ओ ऐ औ। (nava svarāḥ. a i u ṛ ḷ e o ai au.)
  "Nine vowels: a i u ṛ ḷ e o ai au." — नौ स्वर: अ इ उ ऋ ऌ ए ओ ऐ औ।

  इ उ ऋ ऌ इति चत्वारः वर्णाः। (i u ṛ ḷ iti catvāraḥ varṇāḥ.)
  "The four sounds i u ṛ ḷ." — चार वर्ण: इ उ ऋ ऌ।

**The seams.** Two like vowels, one long: तस्यान्त्यः, यणचीति, पञ्चाक्षराणि. इ before an unlike
vowel → य्: इत्यत्र, कत्यक्षराणि. -अः before a voiced consonant → ओ: अन्त्यो वर्णः, वर्णो
णकारः, इतो लोपः, चत्वारो वर्णाः. -अः before अ → ओऽ: णकारोऽपि, हकारोऽस्ति, इकोऽर्थः. -अः before
another vowel → अ, with a gap: क आदिः; and सः before a vowel the same way: स इत्. ए before अ → एऽ:
सूत्रेऽपि. A final stop voiced before a vowel: अजिति, हयवरडिति. A consonant-final word written
together with what follows: हलिति. In turn 9 the quoted sūtra is printed as the Aṣṭādhyāyī text
has it and joined to इति, यणचि + इति → यणचीति, as every quoted form is joined to इति [§15]; the
pause line keeps the quoted sūtra as printed.

**Not joined.** गुरो, — the comma is a stop. Every daṇḍa and "?" is a stop. The quoted sounds of
turns 6 and 12 stay apart (§15).

names in -कार from Volume 1, इत् and the pratyāhāras are labels, and लोपः is the labelled preview of
§15. गुरो is the vocative of the owned गुरुः. Every turn is a course sentence, written for this
dialogue; no source is claimed for any.

## 17. Story

कथा — the grammarians' line, 1: who Pāṇini was among the grammarians, and the book that teaches
beginners his rules today.

Volume 4 follows one line of people through its six chapters: Pāṇini, the teachers he names, and
the commentators who read him. The first two stations are told here, each once.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī is placed around the 4th century BCE or earlier;
> estimates vary. According to Cardona, the evidence allows no date later than the early 4th
> century BCE [REF:cardona1997]. In his own rules Pāṇini names other teachers of grammar — among
> them Āpiśali, Śākaṭāyana and Śākalya (the rules that name them: Teacher Notes).

So the grammar you are reading names, inside its own rules, other teachers of the same craft.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** The Laghusiddhāntakaumudī, the beginner's grammar of the tradition
> (17th c. [REF:laghukaumudi]), opens with three of today's tools: its first rule is 1.3.3
> हलन्त्यम्, its third 1.3.9 तस्य लोपः, its fourth 1.1.71 आदिरन्त्येन सहेता. Its second, the
> definition of the word लोपः, is Chapter 2's. [REF:laghukaumudi on 1.3.3];
> [REF:laghukaumudi on 1.1.71]

A student of the tradition, opening that book, starts where you started today: the marker, its
