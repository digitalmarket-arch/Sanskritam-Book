## 1. Hook

Put two lines you own side by side. From v3.c04.l01: न अस्ति → नास्ति, two short अ become one
long आ; and विद्या आलयः → विद्यालयः, two long आ become one long आ. Both joins, Volume 3
said, are the work of one sūtra, 6.1.101 अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः. Now that lesson 1 has taught you
to read its first word, look again: अक् is अ इ उ ऋ ऌ — five short vowels. So how can a rule
about a short अ join two *long* आ?

And a second puzzle, from Volume 2's "stretch": गच्छामि has a long आ, गच्छसि a short अ, and
Volume 3 gave the rule, 7.3.101 अतो दीर्घो यञि. Why, then, not \*गच्छाथ? Today's answer to both
is one fact about how a rule is written: one letter in a rule can stand for a whole family —
or for itself alone.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — where today's three rules stand, and one question the tradition itself
had to settle: which ण् does अण् run to?

All three of today's tools stand in the first quarter of the first book of the Aṣṭādhyāyī:
1.1.9, 1.1.69 and 1.1.70. The rules you re-read with them stand much later — 6.1.101 in the
sixth book, 7.3.101 in the seventh — and lean on them: a later rule writes a short letter,
and the first book says how far that letter reaches. You read a later rule's letters through
the first book's definitions; that is how the grammar is built to be read.

Lesson 1 left you with a fact from the list: ण् closes two sūtras: अइउण्, sūtra 1, and लण्,
sūtra 6. So a name ending in ण् could run to either. The tradition settles it.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** According to the Kāśikā, every अण् in Pāṇini's rules is formed
> with the first ण् — except in one rule, 1.1.69, where it runs to the second; every इण् is
> formed with the second. [REF:kashika] (on लण्)

So अण् is अ इ उ everywhere but in 1.1.69, the rule you read today; there it is अ इ उ ऋ ऌ ए ओ
ऐ औ ह य व र ल. And इण् is always इ … ल. Two later rules show the use, and their numbers are
printed here only as pointers, not tools: the Kāśikā on अइउण् names 1.1.51 उरण् रपरः as its
example of the first ण्, and 8.3.57 इण्कोः, which Chapter 2 reads (c02.l03), uses the second —
which is why ए and ओ, and the semivowels, are among the sounds that change a following स् to
ष् there. The Kāśikā's own lines on the two ण् stay in the teacher's notes until Chapter 6
reads commentary with you.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — what "the same family" means in the mouth: the place where a sound is
made, and how the tongue or lips make it.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "In each series there are two surd members, two sonant, and one nasal":
> every stop row is made at one place, and its members differ in voicing, breath and nasality.
> [REF:whitney1889 §34]

Modern phonetics describes a speech sound by its place (where the air is narrowed or stopped:
throat, palate, roof, teeth, lips) and its manner (how: a full stop, a near-closure, an open
channel). Pāṇini's 1.1.9 names the first with -आस्य-, "of the mouth", which the Kāśikā explains
as the place, "the palate and the rest"; the course says स्थानम्, "place", a word you own. The
second it names with -प्रयत्न-, "effort": प्रयत्नः is today's headword. The tradition's own map
of the places is a short list in the Laghusiddhāntakaumudī, printed under the very rule you
read today, 1.1.9.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** The Laghusiddhāntakaumudī, under 1.1.9, gives the places of the
> mouth and the sounds made at each. Laghusiddhāntakaumudī, under 1.1.9 — text of the GRETIL
> e-text [REF:laghukaumudi on 1.1.9]:
>
> अकुहविसर्जनीयानां कण्ठः। इचुयशानां तालु। ऋटुरषाणां मूर्धा। ऌतुलसानां दन्ताः। उपूपध्मानीयानामोष्ठौ। ञमङणनानां नासिका च।
> (akuhavisarjanīyānāṃ kaṇṭhaḥ. icuyaśānāṃ tālu. ṛṭuraṣāṇāṃ mūrdhā. ḷtulasānāṃ dantāḥ. upūpadhmānīyānām oṣṭhau. ñamaṅaṇanānāṃ nāsikā ca.)
>
> अकुहविसर्जनीयानाम् कण्ठः। इचुयशानाम् तालु। ऋटुरषाणाम् मूर्धा। ऌतुलसानाम् दन्ताः। उपूपध्मानीयानाम् ओष्ठौ। ञमङणनानाम् नासिका च।
> (akuhavisarjanīyānām kaṇṭhaḥ. icuyaśānām tālu. ṛṭuraṣāṇām mūrdhā. ḷtulasānām dantāḥ. upūpadhmānīyānām oṣṭhau. ñamaṅaṇanānām nāsikā ca.)
>
> "The throat for अ, the क-row, ह and the visarga; the palate for इ, the च-row, य and श; the
> roof for ऋ, the ट-row, र and ष; the teeth for ऌ, the त-row, ल and स; the lips for उ, the
> प-row and the upadhmānīya; the nasals [ञ म ङ ण न] use the nose as well."
>
> The upadhmānīya is a breath-sound before प and फ that this course does not teach.

The second line is the pause form: each compound is one word; the anusvāra of each -आनां is
म् again, and -आनाम् + ओष्ठौ stands apart. Read the list with today's tools and you
will see that it is written the way a rule is written. It names the vowels by their short
letters, अ इ उ ऋ ऌ, and the stop rows by the names with उ, कु चु टु तु पु: the list's अ means
the whole a-family and its चु the whole च-row — exactly what 1.1.69 says a short vowel and a
letter with उ do (§8 Step 3). According to the Laghusiddhāntakaumudī, in the lines that follow
this list, ए and ऐ use the throat and the palate together, and ओ and औ the throat and the lips.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Whitney, on this very list: "The Paninean scheme (commentary to Pāṇini's
> grammar i. 1. 9) classes a as guttural, but apparently only to give that series as well as
> the rest a vowel". [REF:whitney1889 §20]

So the place list is the tradition's scheme, and a modern grammarian reads it as a scheme:
अ has no closure anywhere in the mouth, and the list files it with the throat so that every
row has its vowel. Today's rules use the list for one purpose only — to say which sounds are
one family.

Length, the other half of today's story, is duration. As Volume 1 taught (v1.c02.l01), a long
vowel (दीर्घः) lasts about twice as long as a short one (ह्रस्वः), and the pairs इ/ई, उ/ऊ,
ऋ/ॠ differ in nothing else. अ and आ differ in openness as well (/ɐ/ against /ɑː/), and the
grammar still counts them one family; why, is in the teacher's notes. The course principle
behind the lesson, with its one source: formulaic chunks are taught whole and analysed later
[REF:nation2013]. गच्छामि came to you whole in Volume 2; today its long आ is analysed.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the day's idea in five lines, each printed joined, its pause form beneath,
then the gloss.

> अ आ च सवर्णौ। (a ā ca savarṇau.)
> अ आ च सवर्णौ। (a ā ca savarṇau.)
> "अ and आ are savarṇa (of one family)." — अ और आ सवर्ण हैं।

> इकारो दीर्घो न, ह्रस्वः। (ikāro dīrgho na, hrasvaḥ.)
> इकारः दीर्घः न, ह्रस्वः। (ikāraḥ dīrghaḥ na, hrasvaḥ.)
> "इ is not long but short." — इ दीर्घ नहीं, ह्रस्व है।

> स्थानं तुल्यम्, प्रयत्नोऽपि तुल्यः। (sthānaṃ tulyam, prayatno 'pi tulyaḥ.)
> स्थानम् तुल्यम्, प्रयत्नः अपि तुल्यः। (sthānam tulyam, prayatnaḥ api tulyaḥ.)
> "The place is the same, and the effort is the same too." — स्थान भी समान है, प्रयत्न भी।

> गच्छामीत्यत्र मकारो यञ्। (gacchāmīty atra makāro yañ.)
> गच्छामि इति अत्र मकारः यञ्। (gacchāmi iti atra makāraḥ yañ.)
> "In gacchāmi, the m is a yañ sound." — गच्छामि में मकार यञ् है।

> गच्छथेत्यत्र दीर्घो नास्ति। (gacchathety atra dīrgho nāsti.)
> गच्छथ इति अत्र दीर्घः न अस्ति। (gacchatha iti atra dīrghaḥ na asti.)
> "In gacchatha there is no long vowel." — गच्छथ में दीर्घ स्वर नहीं है।

The quoted sounds अ आ stay unjoined: joined, they would merge into one आ, the very join this
lesson explains. सवर्णः is today's headword, an adjective used of two sounds; सवर्णौ is its
masculine dual, a cell you own, agreeing with the two sound-names. दीर्घः and ह्रस्वः are
adjectives too, said of a vowel; in the fifth line दीर्घः stands alone, "a long [vowel]". The
joins are Volume 3's: -अः before a voiced sound → -ओ [इकारो दीर्घो, मकारो यञ्, दीर्घो नास्ति];
-अः before अ → -ओऽ [प्रयत्नोऽपि]; म् before त → ं [स्थानं तुल्यम्]; two like vowels → one long
[गच्छामीति, नास्ति]; अ + इ → ए [गच्छथेति]; इ before a vowel → य् [इत्यत्र]. A comma is a stop:
the word before it stands in its pause form (न, ह्रस्वः; तुल्यम्). The -कार names (इकारः,
मकारः) are labels, as in lesson 1; यञ् is a pratyāhāra name, a label too.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

नास्ति और विद्यालयः आप बरसों से बोलते आ रहे हैं, और तीसरे खण्ड में आपने सीखा कि दोनों में
एक ही जोड़ है — दीर्घ सन्धि: न + अस्ति में दो ह्रस्व अ मिलकर एक दीर्घ आ बनते हैं, और विद्या +
आलयः में दो दीर्घ आ मिलकर एक दीर्घ आ। इस जोड़ का सूत्र 6.1.101 अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः है। पर पिछले
पाठ में आपने अक् को खोलकर देखा: अक् = अ इ उ ऋ ऌ — पाँचों ह्रस्व। तो जिस सूत्र में केवल ह्रस्व
अ लिखा है, वह दो दीर्घ आ को कैसे जोड़ता है?

इसका उत्तर आज का शब्द है — **सवर्ण**। पाणिनि के सूत्र 1.1.9 के अनुसार दो वर्ण सवर्ण होते हैं जब
उनका **स्थान** एक हो — यानी मुँह की वह जगह जहाँ से वे बोले जाते हैं — और **प्रयत्न** भी एक
हो — यानी बोलते समय जीभ का काम: पूरा छूना, थोड़ा छूना, या खुला रास्ता छोड़ना। अ और आ दोनों
कण्ठ से बोले जाते हैं: वे सवर्ण हैं। इ और ई तालु से: सवर्ण। इनमें अन्तर केवल **मात्रा** का है —
**ह्रस्व** स्वर एक मात्रा का, **दीर्घ** दो मात्रा का। तीन मात्रा वाला स्वर भी होता है,
**प्लुत** — आ३; आज केवल उसका नाम जानिए। पर इ और ए सवर्ण नहीं: ए कण्ठ और तालु दोनों से बोला
जाता है। और इ और श, दोनों तालु के होते हुए भी, सवर्ण नहीं: स्वर और व्यञ्जन कभी सवर्ण नहीं होते।

अब नियम, सूत्र 1.1.69: सूत्र में लिखा ह्रस्व स्वर अपने पूरे सवर्ण-परिवार के लिए आता है। सूत्र में अ
लिखा हो तो अ भी और आ भी; इ लिखा हो तो इ भी और ई भी। इसी तरह जिस वर्ण के साथ उ का चिह्न लगा
हो — उसे **उदित्** कहते हैं — वह अपने पूरे वर्ग के लिए आता है: चु = च छ ज झ ञ, तु = त थ द ध न।
इसीलिए अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः का अ, आ को भी पकड़ लेता है, और विद्यालयः उसी सूत्र से बनता है।

पर इस नियम की एक सीमा है, सूत्र के अन्तिम शब्द में: अप्रत्ययः। जो वर्ण सूत्र स्वयं लाता है, वह
अपने परिवार के लिए नहीं आता। 6.1.77 इको यणचि जिस यण् को लाता है, वह केवल सादा य व र ल है,
उनके अनुनासिक रूप नहीं।

और दूसरी सीमा, सूत्र 1.1.70: जिस स्वर के बाद त् लिखा हो, वह केवल अपनी ही मात्रा के लिए आता है।
ऐसे स्वर को **तपर** कहते हैं — त्-परक, यानी "जिसके बाद त् लिखा हो"। अत् = केवल ह्रस्व अ; आत् =
केवल दीर्घ आ। आपका परिचित 1.1.11 भी ऐसा ही है: उसका ईत् केवल ई है, ऊत् केवल ऊ।

यहीं दूसरे खण्ड का "खिंचाव" खुलता है। गच्छामि में आ दीर्घ है, गच्छसि में अ ह्रस्व। सूत्र 7.3.101
अतो दीर्घो यञि कहता है: क्रिया के अङ्ग का ह्रस्व अ दीर्घ हो जाता है जब आगे का प्रत्यय यञ् के किसी
वर्ण से शुरू हो। यहाँ अत् तपर है, इसलिए केवल ह्रस्व अ। और यञ् = य व र ल ञ म ङ ण न झ भ। -मि का
म यञ् में है, इसलिए गच्छामि; -थ का थ यञ् में नहीं, इसलिए गच्छथ — गच्छाथ नहीं।

हिन्दी से एक जोड़। हिन्दी व्याकरण की पुस्तकें दीर्घ सन्धि को दो सवर्ण स्वरों का मेल बताती हैं —
विद्या + आलय = विद्यालय, हिम + आलय = हिमालय। हिन्दी की पाठशाला में यह नियम
जिस शब्द से समझाया जाता है, वही सवर्ण आज आपका संस्कृत शब्द है।

अन्त में अण् की बात। चौदह सूत्रों में ण् दो बार आता है — अइउण् में और लण् में। काशिका के अनुसार
पाणिनि के सूत्रों में हर अण् पहले ण् तक जाता है [अ इ उ], केवल एक सूत्र को छोड़कर — 1.1.69,
जहाँ वह दूसरे ण् तक जाता है [अ से ल तक]; और इण् हमेशा दूसरे ण् तक जाता है [इ से ल तक]।

## 8. English Explanation

The recipe of this volume, once more: forms you own, the question they raise, the tools that
answer it, the derivation with the teacher, the tools run by you, then production. Today's
one grammar point: in a rule, a short vowel, and a letter marked with उ, stands for its whole
family — its savarṇas — unless the rule itself puts it in; a vowel written with त् after it
stands for its own length only.

### Step 1 — the forms you own

Four forms, from your §3 prompts:

| owned form | where owned | its pieces | what it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| नास्ति | v2.c01.l04; seam named in v3.c04.l01 | न + अस्ति | short अ + short अ → long आ |
| विद्यालयः | v1.c06.l01; seam named in v3.c04.l01 | विद्या + आलयः | long आ + long आ → long आ |

| गच्छथ | v3.c03.l01 | गच्छ + थ | the stem's अ short before -थ |

Volume 3 gave both pairs a sūtra: 6.1.101 for the first two (v3.c04.l01 §15 Step 1), 7.3.101
for the second two (v3.c03.l01 §15 Step 2). That lesson also said of अतो दीर्घो यञि: "The name
of the set of sounds is not analysed here; Volume 4 reads such names." Today it is read.

### Step 2 — the question

**Can अ mean आ?** 6.1.101 begins with अकः, and अक् is अ इ उ ऋ ऌ, all short. Yet the rule joins
विद्या + आलयः, where both vowels are long. And 7.3.101 begins with अतः — the same letter अ,
written with त्. Does that अ take in आ as well? Two letters, both "अ", may reach differently.
Today's three tools say how far each reaches.

### Step 3 — the tools

Each is printed once, here, with its tier: **Q** is quoted, read word by word and recognised by
its gist; **D** is the authority for one derivation step, never recalled by number (lesson 1
gave you the tiers, in its §15; this chapter uses Q, B and D only).

**1. Savarṇa — D, 1.1.9 तुल्यास्यप्रयत्नं सवर्णम्** (tulyāsyaprayatnaṃ savarṇam).
English line: "Sounds made at the same place in the mouth with the same effort are savarṇa."
[REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.9] [REF:kashika 1.1.9]

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** According to the Kāśikā, the effort that counts for savarṇa is the
> inner effort of the mouth, of four kinds: full contact (spṛṣṭatā), slight contact
> (īṣatspṛṣṭatā), closed (saṃvṛtatā) and open (vivṛtatā). Voicing and the puff of breath are
> not among them. [REF:kashika 1.1.9]

So अ and आ are savarṇa: one place, the throat; one effort, open. इ and ई are savarṇa: the
palate. त and थ are savarṇa too: the teeth, full contact — the puff of breath in थ is not an
inner effort. That is why one name can cover a whole stop row. Two limits, both for the
teacher's notes in their detail: according to Pāṇini, a vowel and a consonant are never
savarṇa, even where they share a place — इ and श both use the palate, and still they are not
one family (the rule that says so: Teacher Notes); and ए is not savarṇa with इ, nor with अ: it
uses the throat and the palate together (§5).

**2. The family — Q, 1.1.69 अणुदित् सवर्णस्य चाप्रत्ययः** (aṇudit savarṇasya cāpratyayaḥ).

Word by word: अणुदित् "an aṇ sound, or one marked with उ" · सवर्णस्य "[names] its savarṇa" ·
च "too" · अप्रत्ययः "when not something [the rule] introduces".

English line: "An aṇ sound or a sound marked with उ stands for its savarṇas as well as itself —
unless it is something the rule introduces." [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.69] [REF:kashika 1.1.69]

Note: here अण् is अ … ल [Step 7]. Read the rule in three parts:

- **An aṇ sound** — a vowel or a semivowel, written short in the rule — stands for its whole
  family: अ for अ आ, and for the three-mora आ३, which is named here only; इ for इ ई; उ for उ
  ऊ; ऋ for ऋ ॠ.
- **A letter marked with उ** — called **udit** — stands for its whole row: चु for च छ ज झ ञ, तु
  for त थ द ध न. You own a rule that uses both: 8.4.40 स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः, the rule of जगच्च
  and तच्च [v3.c04.l03], whose स्तोः is "स् or the त-row" and whose श्चु is "श् or the च-row".
- **Unless the rule introduces it** (अप्रत्ययः). A letter the rule itself puts in does not
  cover its family. 6.1.77 इको यणचि puts in a यण् sound, and that यण् is the plain य व र ल only —
  never their nasalised forms.

**3. The length — D, 1.1.70 तपरस्तत्कालस्य** (taparas tatkālasya).
English line: "A vowel followed by त् stands only for [the savarṇas] of its own length."
[REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.70] [REF:kashika 1.1.70]

A vowel written with त् after it is called **tapara**, "with त् after it". It reaches no
further than its own length: अत् is short अ only, आत् is long आ only. You own two rules that
use it. 7.3.101's अतः is अत्: short अ only. And 1.1.11 ईदूदेद्द्विवचनं प्रगृह्यम्, from
v3.c04.l01, is three tapara letters, ईत् ऊत् एत्, "a dual ending in ई, ऊ or ए": its ईत् is ई only,
its ऊत् ऊ only.

### Step 4 — the derivations, with the teacher

Both derivations are in §13 in table form; work down them with the teacher, one line at a
time, saying each step aloud before you read its gloss.

**(a) Derive, according to Pāṇini, विद्या + आलयः → विद्यालयः.** Step 1 asks whether the left
आ is an अक् sound at all: it is, because the अ of अक् stands for its savarṇa आ [1.1.69]. Step
2 asks whether the two आ are savarṇa: yes, by 1.1.9. Step 3 is the rule you already own: after
an अक्, before a savarṇa vowel, one long vowel for both (6.1.101). That answers Step 2's
question: अ can mean आ, because 6.1.101 writes a plain अ, with no त् after it.

**(b) Derive, according to Pāṇini, गच्छ + मि → गच्छामि, and गच्छ + थ → गच्छथ.** Step 1: the stem
ends in short अ, and 7.3.101's अत् [short अ only, 1.1.70] fits it. Step 2: is the ending's
first sound in यञ्? Expand यञ् from the fourteen sūtras by lesson 1's 1.1.71: य … झभञ् = य व र
ल ञ म ङ ण न झ भ. म is in it; थ is not. Step 3: 7.3.101 lengthens the अ before म, and does
nothing before थ.

### Step 7 — the two ण्, and what they are for

Last, the question §4 raised: which ण् does a name in ण् run to? According to the Kāśikā,

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — the sounds inside today's rules: length you can hear, a place you can feel, and the
yañ test by ear. Audio: 🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-4/v4c01l02-length-pairs.mp3`
(drills 1 and 3, each item twice).

**Drill 1 — length pairs, by duration.** Clap once for a short vowel and twice for a long one,
and hold the long vowel for both claps:

1. अ /ɐ/ — आ /ɑː/
2. इ /i/ — ई /iː/
3. उ /u/ — ऊ /uː/
4. ऋ /r̩/ — ॠ /r̩ː/

The pairs 2–4 differ in length only; अ and आ differ in openness as well. The three-mora आ३ is
named today and never drilled.

**Drill 2 — the same place, felt.** Say इ, then य, then च, then श, slowly, and feel where the
tongue goes each time: always to the hard palate. That is the "same place" of 1.1.9. Now feel
the difference in effort: for च the tongue presses the palate fully; for य it comes close
without touching; for इ it leaves an open channel; for श it leaves a narrow one through which
the air hisses. Same place, different effort: so इ, य, च and श are not one family (and इ and
श, a vowel and a consonant, never are).

**Drill 3 — the yañ test by ear.** Say each pair twice and hear where the stem's vowel
stretches:

1. गच्छामि /gɐt͡ɕt͡ɕʰɑːmi/ — गच्छथ /gɐt͡ɕt͡ɕʰɐt̪ʰɐ/
2. गच्छामः /gɐt͡ɕt͡ɕʰɑːmɐh/ — गच्छसि /gɐt͡ɕt͡ɕʰɐsi/
3. पठामि /pɐʈʰɑːmi/ — पठथ /pɐʈʰɐt̪ʰɐ/

Before म the vowel takes two beats; before थ and स it takes one. Your ear already knew this
from Volume 2; today your rule knows it too.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the tradition's five places, on your own mouth. The sound-families diagram
(`planned:assets/images/volume-4/v4c01l02-sound-families.svg`) draws the place list of §5 as a
side view of the mouth, with each place's family written beside it.

1. **The throat (कण्ठः).** Say अ, then आ: nothing closes anywhere; the jaw opens wider for आ and
   stays open twice as long. Then ह: the air rubs in the throat. These three are filed with the
   throat.
2. **The palate (तालु).** Say इ, ई, य, च, श [§9 Drill 2]: the tongue rises to the hard palate
   each time.
3. **The roof (मूर्धा).** Say ऋ, ट, ष: the tongue tip curls back to the roof of the mouth.
4. **The teeth (दन्ताः).** Say त, थ, द, ध, न: the tongue tip touches the back of the upper teeth
   — not the ridge behind them, as English t and d do. One place, full contact for all five:
   this is the row तु names.
5. **The lips (ओष्ठौ).** Say उ, ऊ, प, ब: the lips round or close.

Mirror check: say इ ई, then इ ए. For इ ई the jaw does not move, only the time changes; for इ ए
the jaw drops a little and the vowel moves towards the throat. That is the difference between
a savarṇa pair and an unlike pair.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; they feed the quiz distractors.

1. **Reading every letter in a rule as covering its long partner: "अत् covers आ".** *Fix:* a
   vowel followed by त् stands for its own length only (1.1.70): अत् = short अ.
2. **Thinking the यण् that 6.1.77 puts in covers nasal य̃ व̃ ल̃ too.** *Fix:* a letter the rule
   introduces covers no family (1.1.69's अप्रत्ययः).
3. **अण् always read with the second ण्.** *Fix:* the first ण् [अ इ उ] everywhere but in 1.1.69.
4. **इण् read as इ उ only.** *Fix:* इण् always runs to the second ण्: इ … ल.
5. **Lengthening before every ending: \*गच्छाथ, \*गच्छासि.** *Fix:* only before a yañ sound: म,
   व [7.3.101].
6. **Taking इ and श [or अ and ह] as savarṇa because they share a place.** *Fix:* savarṇa needs
   the same place **and** the same effort; a vowel and a consonant never are (Teacher Notes).

— गुरुः: विद्यालय इत्यत्र कति पदानि? (vidyālaya ity atra kati padāni?)
  विद्यालयः इति अत्र कति पदानि? (vidyālayaḥ iti atra kati padāni?)
  "How many words are there in vidyālayaḥ?" — विद्यालयः में कितने पद हैं?

— छात्रा: द्वे पदे, विद्या आलयश्च। (dve pade, vidyā ālayaś ca.)
  द्वे पदे, विद्या आलयः च। (dve pade, vidyā ālayaḥ ca.)
  "Two words, vidyā and ālayaḥ." — दो पद, विद्या और आलयः।

— गुरुः: किमाकारौ सवर्णौ? (kim ākārau savarṇau?)
  किम् आकारौ सवर्णौ? (kim ākārau savarṇau?)
  "Are the two ā savarṇa?" — क्या दोनों आ सवर्ण हैं?

— छात्रा: आम्। स्थानं तुल्यम्, प्रयत्नोऽपि तुल्यः। (ām. sthānaṃ tulyam, prayatno 'pi tulyaḥ.)
  आम्। स्थानम् तुल्यम्, प्रयत्नः अपि तुल्यः। (ām. sthānam tulyam, prayatnaḥ api tulyaḥ.)
  "Yes. The place is the same; the effort is the same too." — हाँ। स्थान समान है, प्रयत्न भी
  समान है।

— गुरुः: अत एको दीर्घः, विद्यालयः। (ata eko dīrghaḥ, vidyālayaḥ.)
  अतः एकः दीर्घः, विद्यालयः। (ataḥ ekaḥ dīrghaḥ, vidyālayaḥ.)
  "So: one long vowel, vidyālayaḥ." — इसलिए एक दीर्घ स्वर: विद्यालयः।

— छात्रा: गच्छामीत्यत्र दीर्घः कुतः? (gacchāmīty atra dīrghaḥ kutaḥ?)
  गच्छामि इति अत्र दीर्घः कुतः? (gacchāmi iti atra dīrghaḥ kutaḥ?)
  "Where does the long vowel in gacchāmi come from?" — गच्छामि में दीर्घ स्वर कहाँ से आया?

— गुरुः: मकारो यञ्। अतो दीर्घो यञीति सूत्रम्। (makāro yañ. ato dīrgho yañīti sūtram.)
  मकारः यञ्। अतो दीर्घो यञि इति सूत्रम्। (makāraḥ yañ. ato dīrgho yañi iti sūtram.)
  "म is a yañ sound. The rule is अतो दीर्घो यञि." — मकार यञ् है। सूत्र है अतो दीर्घो यञि।

— छात्रा: गच्छथेत्यत्र दीर्घो नास्ति। किमर्थम्? (gacchathety atra dīrgho nāsti. kimartham?)
  गच्छथ इति अत्र दीर्घः न अस्ति। किमर्थम्? (gacchatha iti atra dīrghaḥ na asti. kimartham?)
  "In gacchatha there is no long vowel. Why?" — गच्छथ में दीर्घ स्वर नहीं है। क्यों?

— गुरुः: थकारो न यञ्। (thakāro na yañ.)
  थकारः न यञ्। (thakāraḥ na yañ.)
  "थ is not a yañ sound." — थकार यञ् नहीं है।

— छात्रा: अदित्यत्र तकारः किमर्थम्? (ad ity atra takāraḥ kimartham?)
  अत् इति अत्र तकारः किमर्थम्? (at iti atra takāraḥ kimartham?)
  "In 'at', what is the t for?" — अत् में तकार किसलिए है?

— गुरुः: अकार एव, नाकारः। (akāra eva, nākāraḥ.)
  अकारः एव, न आकारः। (akāraḥ eva, na ākāraḥ.)
  "Short a only, not ā." — केवल अकार, आकार नहीं।

— छात्रा: गच्छामीत्यत्र दीर्घः, गच्छसीत्यत्र ह्रस्वः। (gacchāmīty atra dīrghaḥ, gacchasīty atra hrasvaḥ.)

## 17. Story

कथा — the grammarians' line, 2: the map of the mouth.

Pāṇini defines savarṇa in one rule; the commentators supply the map of places that the rule
leaves to its readers [REF:laghukaumudi on 1.1.9]. You have read that map already, in §5: the
Laghusiddhāntakaumudī's list of the throat, the palate, the roof, the teeth, the lips and the
nose, printed under 1.1.9. Lesson 1 met the same book at its opening; today it is the place
where one piece of a rule, the -आस्य- "place in the mouth" of 1.1.9, is spelled out sound by
sound.

Read the list in §5 once more, aloud, and do one thing with it: for each row of the table in
§15 — अ, इ, उ, ऋ, चु, तु — find the place the list gives it. अ: the throat. इ and चु: the palate.
ऋ: the roof. तु: the teeth. उ: the lips. Then say, for each, one sound of its family in a
Sanskrit word you own: आ in विद्यालयः, ई in इतीव, ऊ in सूर्यः, छ in गच्छति, थ in गच्छथ (ऋ's long
ॠ is in no word you own). A rule writes one letter; the map tells you where its family lives.

## 20. Revision

पुनरावृत्तिः — one screen to remember.

> **How far a letter in a rule reaches.** A short vowel stands for its whole family, its
> savarṇas: अ for अ आ, इ for इ ई, उ for उ ऊ, ऋ for ऋ ॠ [1.1.69]. A letter with उ — udit — stands
> for its row: चु the च-row, तु the त-row (1.1.69). Not a letter the rule itself puts in: the यण्
> of 6.1.77 is plain य व र ल [अप्रत्ययः]. A vowel with त् after it (tapara) stands for its own
> length only: अत् short अ, आत् आ, ईत् ई [1.1.70]. **Savarṇa** = same place + same effort
> (1.1.9); a vowel and a consonant never are. **The two ण्:** अण् = अ इ उ, except in 1.1.69 (अ …
> ल); इण् = इ … ल, always. **यञ्** = य व र ल ञ म ङ ण न झ भ. **The stretch:** गच्छ + मि →
> गच्छामि [म is yañ]; गच्छ + थ → गच्छथ [थ is not]. **The ledger:** four new — 517; cells
> drilled: 7; tools added: 3; toolbox: 7.

marker that produces a meaningful group." It is wrong on both counts: 1.1.71 says nothing of
which ण्, and the Kāśikā's rule is the first ण् for every अण् but one. It stays here, not in
learner text (the editor's decision).

**What students find hard.** Reading every letter as covering its long partner — the tapara
drill, exercise 1, is the cure; do it at speed. The second hard thing is the two ण्: draw the
arrows (board work) before anyone expands anything. Hindi-medium learners know दीर्घ सन्धि and
सवर्ण from school; let them say the Hindi rule first, then show that 6.1.101 is the same rule
with a short अ written in it.

**Differentiation.** Fast learners: ask why 8.4.40 can write चु and not "च छ ज झ ञ" (the udit
name lets one letter name a whole row). Learners who
struggle: give them only the five letters of the §15 table and the two answers, "family" and
"this length only", on cards (the family-or-length worksheet,
`planned:assets/worksheets/volume-4/v4c01l02-family-or-length.pdf`).
