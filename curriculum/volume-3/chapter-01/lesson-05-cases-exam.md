## 1. Hook

Four lessons ago रामः had two shapes, रामः and रामम्. Today it has eight, and you built
every one of them from forms you already owned: रामः, रामम्, रामेण, रामाय, रामात्, रामस्य,
रामे, हे राम. That is the singular column, and today you hear it, write it, produce it
against the clock, and find it in the two stories Volume 2 gave you. Since Volume 2 you have
read those stories with घटे, वने, सिंहस्य and माम् glossed whole. Today you read them
without one gloss, noun by noun. The title, **विभक्तयः** (vibhaktayaḥ) "the cases", is the
plural of विभक्तिः, read whole as a title: the plural is v3.c02.l01's and the i-stems
v3.c02.l03's. 387 words. Pass, and Chapter 1 is complete.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — the tradition's own order for this table, and where the two
thread lines come from.

Chapter 1 began with two numbered names, प्रथमा and द्वितीया, and the honest note that the
tradition numbers its cases instead of describing them. Lesson 2 added the place where and
the point of departure, lesson 3 the means and the receiver, lesson 4 the two forms that
name no part in the action, the genitive and the call. Each pair arrived as the answer to
a question word, and each came from forms you owned before you knew their names. Today the
pieces stand in one column, and the column has a long history as a teaching device.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** In the Laghukaumudī, Varadarāja's beginner's grammar (17th
> c.), the twenty-one *sup* endings are listed in seven groups of three, one group for each
> numbered case, and the first noun worked through them is राम, case by case: रामः with
> the calling form हे राम beside it, then रामम्, रामेण, रामाय, रामात्, रामस्य, रामे. So a
> case-by-case table of राम is the tradition's own teaching order. Chapter 1 takes only the
> singular column. [REF:laghukaumudi on 4.1.2]

Two lines you have carried since earlier volumes turn out to be case lessons too, and you
can now parse them (§17).

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** उद्यमेन हि सिध्यन्ति कार्याणि न मनोरथैः… is the Hitopadeśa,
> introduction (prastāvikā), verse 36; the collection is dated, on the scholarly estimate,
> to c. 9th–12th c. CE. सत्यम् एव जयते is from the Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad, 3.1.6.
> [REF:hitopadesha prastāvikā 36]; [REF:mundaka 3.1.6]

One line of course history. The 1.x book printed the singular column of राम correctly, and
the 2.0 audit kept it. This chapter restores it with two changes: the ṇ cell is marked, and
देवेन stands beside रामेण, so that the ending is learned as -एन and the ण is seen as a
spelling.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why the exam is five tasks in one sitting, and why the last one is
scored on being understood first.

No new fact about Sanskrit enters today; every grammatical claim of the lesson is the
chapter's, gathered in §15. What this section explains is the shape of the exam.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Retrieval practice beats re-reading for long-term retention.
> [REF:roediger2006]

Each task retrieves the column by a different route. Task A goes in by ear: the question
word at the front of each question (केन? कस्मै? कुतः?) tells you which ending to listen
for. Task B goes from ear to hand, with every ending spelled. Task C goes from a noun and a
question word to a form, under time pressure, which is the route the column takes in
speech. Task D goes from a printed form back to its name and its job. Task E starts from
a picture and asks for whole sentences.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spaced practice, with gaps between sessions, is retained longer than
> the same practice massed together. [REF:cepeda2006]

That is why the exam opens with Station 0, which runs the +1-day item of lesson 4 and the
+1-week item of lesson 3, and why §20 books two more items for Chapter 2.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Formulaic chunks are taught whole and analysed later.
> [REF:nation2013]

Volume 2 handed you घटे, वने, सिंहस्य, माम्, मह्यम् and भोः राम as whole chunks. This
chapter analysed each of them, one case pair at a time, and task D is where you read the
stories they came from with the analysis in hand.

The risk in a grammar chapter is that the drills are passed and free speech freezes. So
task E is scored on communication first and endings second (rubric in §18 and §22): a
clear sentence with one wrong ending scores higher than a silence.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the column in Sanskrit, the sūtra that names the chapter, and one
traveller who needs every case (pause form throughout):

> **प्रथमा, द्वितीया, तृतीया, चतुर्थी, पञ्चमी, षष्ठी, सप्तमी; सम्बोधनम् च।**
> (prathamā, dvitīyā, tṛtīyā, caturthī, pañcamī, ṣaṣṭhī, saptamī; sambodhanam ca.) "First,
> second, third, fourth, fifth, sixth, seventh; and the calling." — प्रथमा, द्वितीया,
> तृतीया, चतुर्थी, पञ्चमी, षष्ठी, सप्तमी; और सम्बोधन।

> **कः? — रामः। कम्? — रामम्। केन? — रामेण। कस्मै? — रामाय।**
> (kaḥ? — rāmaḥ. kam? — rāmam. kena? — rāmeṇa. kasmai? — rāmāya.) "Who? — Rāma. Whom? —
> Rāma. By whom? — by Rāma. To whom? — to Rāma." — कौन? — राम। किसे? — राम को। किससे? —
> राम से। किसको? — राम को।

> **कस्मात्? — रामात्। कस्य? — रामस्य। कस्मिन्? — रामे। हे राम!**
> (kasmāt? — rāmāt. kasya? — rāmasya. kasmin? — rāme. he rāma!) "From whom? — from Rāma.
> Whose? — Rāma's. In whom? — in Rāma. O Rāma!" — किससे? — राम से। किसका? — राम का।
> किसमें? — राम में। हे राम!

> **सुप्तिङन्तं पदम्।**
> (suptiṅantaṃ padam.) "A word is what ends in a *sup* or a *tiṅ* ending." — पद वह है
> जिसके अन्त में सुप् या तिङ् प्रत्यय हो।

> **रामः पदम्। रामे पदम्। पठति पदम्।**
> (rāmaḥ padam. rāme padam. paṭhati padam.) "रामः is a word. रामे is a word. पठति is a
> word." — रामः पद है। रामे पद है। पठति पद है।

> **प्रभाते नृपस्य सेवकः ग्रामात् नगरम् रथेन गच्छति। सः नृपाय फलम् ददाति।**
> (prabhāte nṛpasya sevakaḥ grāmāt nagaram rathena gacchati. saḥ nṛpāya phalam dadāti.)
> "In the morning the king's servant goes by chariot from the village to the city. He
> gives the king a fruit." — सुबह राजा का सेवक रथ से गाँव से नगर जाता है। वह राजा को फल
> देता है।

> **हे सेवक! कुत्र गच्छसि?**
> (he sevaka! kutra gacchasi?) "Servant! Where are you going?" — सेवक! कहाँ जा रहे हो?

Seven lines. The first names the column (a list of names, so nominatives); the next two
run it through its askers; the fourth is the one sūtra of the day, Pāṇini 1.4.14 (§15),
and the fifth applies it: रामः and रामे end in a noun ending, पठति in a verb ending, so all
three are पदानि. The last two put all eight forms into one short scene.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

आज कोई नया शब्द नहीं, कोई नया रूप नहीं। आज पूरे पहले अध्याय की परीक्षा है — एक बैठक में,
पैंतालीस मिनट, पाँच कार्य। पहले पुनरावृत्ति (Station 0), फिर परीक्षा।

पहले एक बात याद कर लीजिए: विभक्ति यानी संज्ञा का वह रूप जो बताता है कि वाक्य में वह संज्ञा
क्या काम कर रही है। चार पाठों में आपने अकारान्त पुल्लिंग और नपुंसक संज्ञा के एकवचन के आठों
रूप बनाए — रामः, रामम्, रामेण, रामाय, रामात्, रामस्य, रामे, हे राम। परम्परा इन्हें गिनती के
नाम देती है: प्रथमा (पहली), द्वितीया (दूसरी), तृतीया (तीसरी), चतुर्थी (चौथी), पञ्चमी
(पाँचवीं), षष्ठी (छठी), सप्तमी (सातवीं); और पुकारने के रूप को सम्बोधन कहती है।

हर विभक्ति एक प्रश्न का उत्तर है — यही इस अध्याय की कुंजी है। कः? (कौन?) — रामः। कम्?
(किसे?) — रामम्। केन? (किससे?) — रामेण। कस्मै? (किसको, किसके लिए?) — रामाय। कस्मात् या
कुतः? (कहाँ से?) — रामात्। कस्य? (किसका?) — रामस्य। कस्मिन् या कुत्र? (कहाँ?) — रामे। और
पुकारना कोई प्रश्न नहीं, एक काम है: हे राम! परीक्षा में प्रश्न-शब्द को सबसे पहले पकड़िए; वही
बता देता है कि उत्तर किस अन्त पर खत्म होगा।

हिन्दी आपकी मदद भी करती है और धोखा भी देती है। "से" के दो काम हैं — "छड़ी से" में साधन
(दण्डेन) और "पेड़ से" में अलगाव (वृक्षात्); संस्कृत इन्हें दो अलग विभक्तियों से कहती है।
"को" के भी दो काम हैं — "हिरन को देखता है" में कर्म (मृगम्) और "राम को फल देता है" में पाने
वाला (रामाय)। "में" और "पर" दो शब्द हैं, पर संस्कृत में एक ही सप्तमी है: वने, वृक्षे।
"सोमवार को" संस्कृत में सप्तमी है — सोमवासरे। और का, के, की बदलते हैं, पर -स्य कभी नहीं:
रामस्य गृहम्, रामस्य माता, रामस्य पिता।

हिन्दी स्कूल-व्याकरण के अनुसार कारक आठ हैं — कर्ता, कर्म, करण, सम्प्रदान, अपादान, सम्बन्ध,
अधिकरण और सम्बोधन। नाम संस्कृत के हैं, पर यह गिनती पाणिनि की नहीं। पाणिनि के अनुसार कारक
छह हैं; षष्ठी (सम्बन्ध) और सम्बोधन कारक नहीं, क्योंकि ये क्रिया में किसी की भूमिका नहीं
बताते। परीक्षा में यह बात हमेशा "किसके अनुसार" के साथ कहिए।

आज का सूत्र एक ही है: **सुप्तिङन्तं पदम्** — "पद वह है जिसके अन्त में सुप् या तिङ् हो"।
सुप् संज्ञा के सब प्रत्ययों का परम्परागत नाम है, तिङ् क्रिया के प्रत्ययों का। इसीलिए यह
अध्याय सुबन्तप्रवेशः कहलाता है — "सुप् से अन्त होने वाले शब्दों में प्रवेश" — और तीसरा
अध्याय तिङन्तप्रवेशः।

अब परीक्षा की सलाह। कार्य E में आप एक चित्र देखकर छह वाक्य बोलेंगे और रिकॉर्ड करेंगे, ताकि
आठों रूप कम-से-कम एक बार आएँ। यहाँ अंक पहले समझ के मिलते हैं, फिर शुद्धता के। छोटा, साफ़
वाक्य बोलिए; अगर एक अन्त ग़लत भी हो जाए, तो रुकिए मत, आगे बढ़िए। और कार्य D में पहली बार
आप दूसरे खण्ड की दोनों कथाएँ — प्यासा कौआ और शेर-चूहा — बिना किसी अर्थ-सहायता के पढ़ेंगे,
हर संज्ञा की विभक्ति और उसका काम बताते हुए। घटे, वने, सिंहस्य, माम् — जो रूप तब पूरे शब्द
की तरह याद किए थे, आज उनका नाम भी आपके पास है।

## 8. English Explanation

Five tasks, one sitting, the whole chapter. The recipe of the chapter's grammar lessons
runs one last time, because today's table is assembled from four lessons instead of one.

### Step 1 — the scene

Look at the scene picture (`planned:assets/images/volume-3/v3c01l05-scene-picture.svg`):
a hunter on a road near a village, a tree on a river bank, a dove in the tree, a mouse at
a hole by its roots, a well. Task E asks you to describe it. Before you do, count how many
different questions you could ask about it: who comes, what he carries, with what, for
whom, from where, whose hole, where the dove is, and how the mouse would call out.

### Step 2 — the questions

Every one of those questions has an asker you own, and each asker wants one ending:

- **कः?** "who?" and **कम्?** "whom?" — with **किम्?** "what?" for a neuter, both jobs;
- **केन?** "with what? by whom? with whom?" and **कस्मै?** "to whom? for whom?";
- **कस्मात्?** or **कुतः?** "from where?" and **कस्मिन्?** or **कुत्र?** "where?";
- **कस्य?** "whose?";
- and **हे …!**, which is not a question but an act: calling.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — exam-day checks; the reference values are in the four lessons' §9–§10. Audio:
🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-3/v3c01l05-listening-scene.mp3` (task A) and
`planned:assets/audio/volume-3/v3c01l05-dictation-set.mp3` (task B).

**The nine endings by ear.** Task A is read at natural pace, pause form, so every ending
is there to be heard; nothing is swallowed by a seam. Listen for the finish of each noun:
a breath (-ः), closed lips (-म्), the -न or curled -ण after a long /eː/ (-एन, -एण), a long
/ɑː/ and a full syllable (-आय), a long /ɑː/ stopped on the teeth (-आत्), the /sjɐ/ cluster
(-स्य), a long pure /eː/ alone (-े), and nothing at all after a full final /ɐ/ (the call).

**The pairs that sound alike.** रामे and रामेण both carry the long /eː/; one stops there,
the other goes on to a curled nasal. रामात् and रामाय share /ɾɑːmɑː/; one closes on the
teeth, the other opens into a full यa. कस्मात् and कस्मै share कस्म-; listen for the held
/t̪/ against the glide /ɐi̯/ (l03 §12).

**The call.** हे + name rises and is followed by a pause; the final अ of the name is full
(l04 §9 drills 2–3). In task E, a call is the easiest way to bring in the vocative: one
character calls another.

**Warm-up before the sitting (2 min):** the column of रामः chanted once top to bottom,
slowly, then at speaking pace, with देवेन said after रामेण; then the askers once, कः to
कस्मिन्.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the exam circuit, one check per task.

- **Task A (ears):** catch the asker, then listen for one word's last sound. The questions

- **Task B (pen):** every ending its letters: ः, म्, ण or न after े, आय, आत्, स्य, े, and

- **Task C (head, then pen):** read the asker first, then the noun. For केन, look along the

- **Task E (mouth):** plan the picture's six things — the hunter, the village, the road,

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — three worked models, none of them from the exam itself.

**A task C item, worked aloud.** Prompt: पुष्पम् + केन? The asker केन wants the
instrumental, ending -एन. Look along the stem पुष्प for a trigger: ष is one. Look between
the ष and the ending for a blocker: only प, of the प-row, which does not block. So the
ending is spelled -एण: **पुष्पेण** (puṣpeṇa) "with a flower". Ten seconds, three steps:
asker, trigger, blocker. Compare पुस्तकम् + केन? → **पुस्तकेन** (pustakena): the स of
पुस्तक is the dental स, not ष, so there is no trigger.

**A task D line, worked aloud.** From lesson 4's court:

> **रथः गृहस्य समीपे अस्ति।**
> (rathaḥ gṛhasya samīpe asti.) "The chariot is near the house." — रथ घर के पास है।

रथः — प्रथमा · कर्ता: the doer of अस्ति, the thing that is. गृहस्य — षष्ठी · सम्बन्धः: the
relation to समीपे, "the house's nearness". समीपे — सप्तमी · अधिकरणम्: the place where. And
from lesson 1, a predicate: **एषः व्याधः।** (eṣaḥ vyādhaḥ.) "This is a hunter." —
व्याधः is प्रथमा, but it does no action; it names what एषः is. In task D such a word is
marked "nom. · predicate": the "is" sentence names it, and it is not a kāraka.

**A task E model, for a different picture** (lesson 4's court scene,
`planned:assets/images/volume-3/v3c01l04-court-scene.svg`). Six sentences, all eight forms:

1. सोमवासरे नृपः गृहात् वनम् गच्छति। — "On Monday the king goes from the house to the
   forest."
2. सः रथेन गच्छति। — "He goes by chariot."
3. नृपस्य सेवकः अपि आगच्छति। — "The king's servant comes too."
4. वृक्षस्य समीपे सिंहः स्वपिति। — "Near the tree a lion is sleeping."
5. सेवकः नृपाय कथयति — हे नृप! तत्र सिंहः अस्ति। — "The servant tells the king: 'O king!
   There is a lion there.'"
6. नृपः सिंहम् पश्यति। — "The king sees the lion."

Coverage: प्रथमा नृपः, सेवकः, सिंहः · द्वितीया वनम्, सिंहम् · तृतीया रथेन · चतुर्थी नृपाय ·
पञ्चमी गृहात् · षष्ठी नृपस्य, वृक्षस्य · सप्तमी सोमवासरे, समीपे · सम्बोधनम् हे नृप.
Every sentence says one thing in the picture, every verb is a he-form you own, and a
listener could draw the scene from them. That is the bar for task E.

### The sūtra of the day — why the chapter is called सुबन्त-

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** Pāṇini's sūtra 1.4.14 सुप्तिङन्तं पदम्
> (suptiṅantaṃ padam) says: a word (पदम्) is what ends in a *sup* or a *tiṅ* ending.
> *sup* is the tradition's name for the whole set of noun endings, and *tiṅ* its name for
> the verb endings. Hence सुबन्तम् "ending in a sup", a noun form, and तिङन्तम्, a verb
> form. [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.4.14]

So this chapter, सुबन्तप्रवेशः, is "the entry into sup-words", and Chapter 3,
तिङन्तप्रवेशः, will be the entry into tiṅ-words. रामः, रामेण and हे राम are all पदानि for
the grammar, and so is पठति.

### The Hindi bridge, the whole chapter

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Hindi को marks a definite or animate object and also the receiver;
> Sanskrit separates them (मृगम्, रामाय). Hindi से marks the instrument and also the point
> of departure; Sanskrit separates them (दण्डेन, वृक्षात्). Company is के साथ, as सह +
> instrumental is (मित्रेण सह). Hindi में and पर are two postpositions where Sanskrit has one
> locative (वने, वृक्षे); a day takes को in Hindi and the locative in Sanskrit (सोमवासरे).
> Hindi का/के/की agree with the possessed noun; Sanskrit -स्य never changes. हे राम is the
> same bare name in both. [REF:kachru2006]

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** Hindi school grammar teaches eight "kārakas" with their
> postpositions: कर्ता (ने), कर्म (को), करण (से, के द्वारा), सम्प्रदान (को, के लिए), अपादान
> (से), सम्बन्ध (का, के, की), अधिकरण (में, पर), सम्बोधन (हे, अरे). The names are the
> Sanskrit names; the system is not Pāṇini's: Hindi school grammar counts सम्बन्ध and
> सम्बोधन as कारक, Pāṇini does not. [REF:guru1952]

### Two thread lines, parsed

The verse you have recited since v2.c06.l03 and the motto you have owned since v1.c06.l04
both carry the chapter's cases:

> **उद्यमेन हि सिध्यन्ति कार्याणि न मनोरथैः।**
> **न हि सुप्तस्य सिंहस्य प्रविशन्ति मुखे मृगाः॥**
>
> (udyamena hi sidhyanti kāryāṇi na manorathaiḥ. na hi suptasya siṃhasya praviśanti
> mukhe mṛgāḥ.)
> "Tasks succeed through effort, not through wishing; deer do not walk into the mouth of
> a sleeping lion." — कार्य उद्यम से सिद्ध होते हैं, मनोरथों से नहीं; सोते हुए सिंह के मुँह
> में मृग प्रवेश नहीं करते। (Hitopadeśa, prastāvikā 36; §4)

- **उद्यमेन** — तृतीया · करणम्: "by effort", the means, like जालेन in l03.
- **सिंहस्य** — षष्ठी · सम्बन्धः: "of the lion", the mouth's owner (l04).
- **मुखे** — सप्तमी · अधिकरणम्: "into the mouth", the place reached, like कूपे पतति in l02.

> **सत्यम् एव जयते।**
> (satyam eva jayate.) "Truth alone prevails." — सत्य ही जीतता है। (Muṇḍaka 3.1.6; §4)

- **सत्यम्** — प्रथमा, neuter · कर्ता: the doer of जयते, in the form a neuter wears for
  both jobs (l01).

The rest of the verse stays glossed whole: कार्याणि, मनोरथैः and मृगाः are plurals, सिध्यन्ति
and प्रविशन्ति plural verbs (v3.c02–c03). सुप्तस्य is a genitive too, agreeing with सिंहस्य;
why a word of that kind looks like a noun is Chapter 2's business.

**Scoring notes.** *Task A:* a right word in the wrong case does not score (the task tests
the endings by ear); a right noun in English or Hindi scores only for Q7. *Task C:* a form
with the right case but the wrong ṇ spelling scores 0 for that item; tell the learner which
column it belongs to. *Task D:* accept English case names; accept "doer of अस्ति" for
कर्ता; for predicates, "nom." + "predicate" or "says what X is". *Task E:* score
communication first, with the picture in front of you, before you count endings. A learner
who stays with six plain sentences and gets them right has done the task better than one
who reaches for plurals.

**Accept, never star.** (1) **ग्रामाय गच्छति** or a dative of the goal with a verb of
motion: Pāṇini allows it beside the accusative (2.3.12 गत्यर्थकर्मणि
द्वितीयाचतुर्थ्यौ चेष्टायामनध्वनि); the course teaches only the accusative. (2) **विना**
with the accusative or the ablative (जलम् विना, जलात् विना): correct Sanskrit (Apte s.v.
विना; 2.3.32 makes the instrumental an option); the course drills the instrumental. (3)
**व्याधः कपोताय अन्नम् क्षिपति** "throws food at the dove": classical (Whitney §286f) but
untaught, so it is not offered as a model; accept it in task E. (4) **रामाद्** for रामात्:
the Laghukaumudī also gives the -द् at pause (8.4.56 वाऽवसाने); the course prints -आत्, as
Whitney's table does.

**Sūtras (Teacher Notes only; 1.4.14 is the one quoted to learners).** *sup* is a
pratyāhāra: it runs from the first ending सु of 4.1.2 to the final marker प्, and so names
all twenty-one nominal endings; *tiṅ* names the eighteen verb endings the same way (3.4.78).
The pratyāhāra mechanism is v4.c01's; do not teach it now, and do not say "from its last
syllable". The chapter's other sūtras are listed
in the four lessons' Teacher Notes (the kāraka definitions 1.4.24–1.4.54, the case
assignments of 2.3, 2.3.47 and 2.3.50, the ṇ rule 8.4.1–2 and 8.4.37, 6.1.69). The
Laghukaumudī's walk through राम follows its ending list [REF:laghukaumudi on 4.1.2]; its
order and the 2.0 table agree, except that it takes the vocative up beside the first case
and the course prints it last, as Whitney does.

**The 4.1.2 picture.** Show it once, on the wall chart, and move on. If someone tries to
read it, say: "those are the grammar's code names for the endings, with marker letters
added; Volume 4 reads them." Its IAST is printed in §15 only so that nobody guesses.

**The Hindi angles.** The Hindi bridge is the strongest hook of the chapter; let the class
build the §15 bridge table from the Station 0 pictures before you show it. The Hindi points
follow Kachru [REF:kachru2006], cited bare until Pass C has located each; the eight कारक of
Hindi school grammar are TRADITION, attributed [REF:guru1952], and are assessed only as
"According to Hindi school grammar …" — this exam does not assess them.

**What students find hard.** Task C's clock (rehearse §13's three steps — asker,
trigger, blocker — before the sitting); the predicate in task D; and starting task E. Give
§11's six things as a prompt card only after a long stall; communication then drops a band.

**The completion moment.** Booklets closed, the class stands and chants the column once
together, रामः to हे राम, with देवेन after रामेण. Say the counts aloud: 27 words in the
chapter, 387 in all, eight cells, two stories read without a gloss. Chapter 1 is closed.
