## 1. Hook

विद्या ददाति विनयम् — in a verse the words stand where the metre wants them. Today you put
them back where a sentence wants them. It takes seven steps, and the first one you already
know: पदच्छेदः.

You met those first three words in Volume 1, as "Your first line of real literature", and
that lesson promised that the rest of the verse, its chain from learning to happiness,
"awaits your sandhi (Volume 3)". The wait is over. Today you read the whole verse, and two
more beside it: a jackal's line about the whole earth as one family, and a verse that says
in sixteen syllables what the course's own moral बुद्धिः एव बलम् says in three words.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — where the three verses stand in their books, who speaks them, and how
readers have taken a verse apart.

The first verse stands among the Hitopadeśa's introductory verses, before the first story
begins: the book's own opening, not a character's speech. The other two stand inside stories,
in a character's mouth, and that changes how you hear them. A line can travel on its own,
away from its book; today you read each one whole, in its book, with its speaker.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** In the Hitopadeśa (Book 1, Mitralābha, verse 71 in the GRETIL numbering) this
> verse is spoken by a jackal who has made friends with a deer in order to eat him; when a crow
> distrusts the newcomer, the jackal answers angrily, and this is his second verse
> [REF:hitopadesha mitralābha 71]. The same verse stands in the Pañcatantra, Book 5, in the story of the
> brahmins who made a lion, where the third brahmin speaks it, arguing that their unlearned friend should
> share in what they earn [REF:panchatantra tantra 5]. The course makes no claim about which text has it
> first.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** In the Pañcatantra (Book 1) the verse यस्य बुद्धिर्बलं तस्य introduces the story
> of the lion Bhāsuraka and the hare [REF:panchatantra tantra 1]. The Hitopadeśa has it too (Book 2,
> verse 122 in the GRETIL numbering), beginning बुद्धिर्यस्य बलं तस्य and with पश्य "look!" for वने; there a
> crow speaks it [REF:hitopadesha suhṛdbheda 122].

Read the first callout twice. The line "for those whose conduct is noble, the earth itself is
family" is, in the Hitopadeśa, the argument of someone who means to eat his new friend; in
the Pañcatantra it is a generous brahmin's plea for a friend who has no learning. The words do
not change. What changes is who is speaking, and to whom: the source line tells you that, and
§17 prints it above the verse.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** Sanskrit readers and teachers read a verse by first setting out its
> अन्वयः — its words in prose order, with any word the verse leaves understood supplied — and
> translate from that.

The course takes the practice and gives it fixed steps (§8). Which commentators used it, and
how, is Volume 5's subject (v5.c01); today no commentary is named, because none is quoted.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why a verse can be put back into prose order at all, and what holds its
shape.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** A śloka has four quarters of eight syllables each, thirty-two in all;
> Apte describes the anuṣṭubh as "a class of metres consisting of four Pādas of 8 syllables
> each". [REF:apte1890 s.v. अनुष्टुभ्]

Inside those thirty-two syllables the words stand where the verse puts them. Look at the
first verse: its second clause opens with the ablative विनयात्, its verb याति comes next, and
the person who "comes to worthiness" is never printed at all. The course's own prose would
write the doer first and the verb last.

Why can a reader move the words back without changing the sense? Because you found the
answer in Volume 3's first lesson (v3.c01.l01): the endings carry the roles, whatever the
order. विद्या is the doer of ददाति by its nominative, विनयम् the thing given by its
accusative, wherever each stands. An anvaya does nothing but use that fact: it reads the
roles off the endings and lays the words out in one fixed order. It never changes a form.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Formulaic chunks are taught whole and analysed later. [REF:nation2013]

विद्या ददाति विनयम् has been a whole line in your hands since Volume 1, and उद्यमेन हि
सिध्यन्ति since Volume 2. Today they are analysed, word by word and role by role. Station 0
and the bookings of §20 bring the new procedure back after one day and after one week,
because spaced review helps retention [REF:cepeda2006].

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the lesson's idea in two lines, each printed joined, its pause form
beneath, then the gloss:

> श्लोके पदानि न क्रमेण तिष्ठन्ति। (śloke padāni na krameṇa tiṣṭhanti.)
> श्लोके पदानि न क्रमेण तिष्ठन्ति। (śloke padāni na krameṇa tiṣṭhanti.)
> "In a verse the words do not stand in order." — श्लोक में पद क्रम से नहीं खड़े होते।

> अन्वये तु क्रमेण तिष्ठन्ति। (anvaye tu krameṇa tiṣṭhanti.)
> अन्वये तु क्रमेण तिष्ठन्ति। (anvaye tu krameṇa tiṣṭhanti.)
> "In the anvaya, though, they do." — अन्वय में तो वे क्रम से खड़े होते हैं।

No seam changes anything: every word ends in a vowel before a consonant, so joined and pause
form are the same — the method of v3.c04.l04 proves it. पदानि is the plural of पदम्
(v3.c04.l04); क्रमेण is the instrumental of the owned क्रमः "order" (v1.c05.l04), "in order",
its ण the change inside a word named in v3.c04.l04; तिष्ठन्ति is the plural of तिष्ठति; तु
stands second in its clause, as v3.c05.l03 taught. श्लोके and अन्वये are locatives of the
grammar terms श्लोकः and अन्वयः, glossed, not vocabulary.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी व्याख्या

श्लोक में शब्द वहाँ खड़े होते हैं जहाँ छन्द की लय उन्हें रखती है — आठ-आठ अक्षरों के चार
चरणों में। वाक्य में वे वहाँ खड़े होते हैं जहाँ अर्थ उन्हें माँगता है। श्लोक के शब्दों को फिर
से वाक्य के क्रम में रख देना अन्वय कहलाता है। हिन्दी में भी यही शब्द, अन्वय, इसी काम के
लिए चलता है, और हिन्दी कविता भी गद्य के क्रम में खोलकर पढ़ी जाती है। अगर आपने स्कूल में
संस्कृत पढ़ी है, तो हो सकता है कि अन्वय से आपकी मुलाक़ात एक अभ्यास के रूप में हुई हो।

अन्वय कोई पहेली नहीं है। यह एक तय विधि है, सात चरणों की:

1. **पदच्छेद।** जुड़े हुए शब्दों को अलग कीजिए — चौथे अध्याय के चौथे पाठ के छह चरणों से।
2. **क्रिया ढूँढ़िए।** हर उपवाक्य की क्रिया पहचानिए। जिस उपवाक्य में क्रिया छपी ही न हो, वहाँ
   अस्ति या भवति जोड़िए, या पिछले उपवाक्य की क्रिया।
3. **कर्ता ढूँढ़िए।** हर क्रिया का कर्ता वह प्रथमा वाला शब्द है जो पुरुष और वचन में क्रिया से
   मेल खाए। कर्ता छपा न हो तो जोड़िए — जैसे नरः, यानी "मनुष्य"।
4. **बाक़ी विभक्तियाँ जोड़िए।** द्वितीया कर्म है; पञ्चमी "से" — जिससे कुछ निकलता या होता है;
   षष्ठी "का" — या "जिसके पास है"। हर शब्द को उसकी क्रिया या उसके संज्ञा-शब्द से जोड़िए।
5. **विशेषण को उसके विशेष्य से जोड़िए** — लिंग, वचन और विभक्ति के मेल से।
6. **सम्बन्धवाचक उपवाक्य पहले।** यः वाला उपवाक्य सः वाले उपवाक्य से पहले रखिए।
7. **गद्य-क्रम में पढ़िए:** कर्ता — बाक़ी विभक्तियाँ — कर्म — क्रिया।

एक नियम सबसे ऊपर: अन्वय किसी शब्द का रूप नहीं बदलता, केवल उसका स्थान बदलता है। विनयम् को
विनयः बना देना अन्वय नहीं, ग़लती है। जो शब्द आप जोड़ते हैं, वह वर्गाकार कोष्ठक [ ] में लिखा जाता है,
ताकि पता रहे कि वह श्लोक में नहीं था।

अब पहला श्लोक। विद्या ददाति विनयम् — क्रिया ददाति, कर्ता विद्या, कर्म विनयम्: विद्या विनय
देती है। अगले उपवाक्य में क्रिया याति है, पर उसका कर्ता छपा नहीं। विद्या पुरुष और वचन में तो
मेल खाती है, पर अर्थ नहीं बैठता: विनय से चलकर पात्रता तक विद्या नहीं, मनुष्य पहुँचता है।
इसलिए हम जोड़ते हैं — [नरः] विनयात् पात्रताम् याति, "मनुष्य विनय से पात्रता पाता है"। यहाँ
विनयात् पञ्चमी है। पहले अध्याय के दूसरे पाठ में आपने पञ्चमी को "से" के रूप में सीखा था —
पेड़ से, गाँव से; यहाँ वही "से" कारण बताता है: विनय से, यानी विनय के कारण। आख़िरी उपवाक्य,
धनाद्धर्मं ततः सुखम्, में कोई क्रिया नहीं; पिछले उपवाक्य की क्रिया आप्नोति वहाँ दो बार जुड़ती
है।

## 8. English Explanation

One procedure, three verses, three source lines, and the end of a thread. The work: take a
verse, printed joined as its book prints it, and lay its words out as a sentence, without
changing a single form.

### The word

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** अन्वयः (anvayaḥ) is the dictionaries' word for this. Apte gives, as its
> third sense, "The natural order or connection of words in a sentence, construing,
> grammatical order or relation"; Monier-Williams, "the natural order or connection of words
> in a sentence, syntax, construing". [REF:apte1890 s.v. अन्वयः]; [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v.
> anvaya]

In this course अन्वयः is a grammar term, like पदच्छेदः: you use it, it is not a vocabulary
row. Its Hindi is अन्वय.

### The seven steps

Taught on the first verse; run in this order on every verse.

1. **Split the verse** (पदच्छेदः, v3.c04.l04). You own this step: the six steps of that
   lesson take any joined line back into its pause form.
2. **Find the finite verbs; where a clause has none, supply अस्ति / भवति or the verb of the
   clause before.** You own the verbless clause: the crow story's moral, बुद्धिः एव बलम्, has
   "two nouns and एव, no verb" (v2.c06.l01).
3. **Find each verb's subject — the nominative that agrees with it in person and number
   (supply one if none is printed: नरः "a person").** You own agreement of verb and doer from
   v3.c03.l01, where every person of the verb was taught.
4. **Attach each other case to its verb** — the accusative, the ablative "from, through", the
   genitive "of / who has". You own the cases from Chapters 1–2 and their roles map
   (v3.c02.l05).
5. **Attach each adjective to its noun by agreement in gender, number and case.** You own it
   from v3.c02.l02, "She, and the Words that Follow Her".
6. **Put the relative clause (यः …) before its correlative (सः …).** You own it from
   v3.c05.l03: the relative clause first.
7. **Read it out in prose order: subject — other cases — object — verb.** The course's own
   prose writes the doer first (v3.c01.l01) and the verb last.

Two rules hold the steps together. **An anvaya never changes a form, only the order.** And
**a supplied word stands in square brackets**, so that anyone can see it was not printed in
the verse. §15 prints the steps as a card and works the first verse down it, step by step.

### One reading note: the ablative of "through"

In v3.c01.l02 you learned the ablative (पञ्चमी) as the "from"-case, the answer to कुतः?:
वृक्षात् "from the tree". The first verse uses it three times — विनयात्, पात्रत्वात्, धनात् — and its ततः "from that"
carries the same sense: what something comes *out of*, not a place but a cause. "From
discipline" is "through discipline". Nothing new is formed; the ending is the one you know,
read one step further.

### Three labels for words you read whole

- **पात्रताम्, पात्रत्वात्** — -ता and -त्व make "-ness" words, as in the owned एकता "oneness"
  (v1.c02.l04) and ममता "mine-ness" (v1.c02.l01); -त्व is the neuter of the pair. पात्र here
  is not "vessel" but, in Apte's words, "a fit or worthy person, a person fit or worthy to
  receive gifts" [REF:apte1890 s.v. पात्रम्]: पात्रता "worthiness". Read whole; the formation
  is v4.c04's.
- **कुटुम्बकम्** — the owned कुटुम्बम् "household" (v2.c02.l02) with -क added, which adds
  nothing to the sense here: "family".
- **निर्बुद्धेः** — निर् "without" + बुद्धिः, the kind of word that v3.c05.l04 labels in
  निरामयाः and asks you to read whole: "one without intelligence". It is a genitive singular
  masculine in -एः, like अग्नेः [v3.c02.l03].

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — verse rhythm: four quarters of eight syllables, one breath per half-verse, and
every seam spoken without a pause. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-3/v3c06l04-three-verses-recited.mp3` (the three verses, in the
order of §17, each first in pause form, slowly, then joined at a steady pace, then joined at
natural pace).

**Drill 1 — three speeds, the first verse.** (1) Pause form, slowly, word by word, a small
stop after each: विद्या · ददाति · विनयम् · विनयात् · याति · पात्रताम्. (2) Joined, at a steady
pace, no stop inside a half-verse: विद्या ददाति विनयं विनयाद्याति पात्रताम्। Then (3) joined, at
natural pace, timed. The joined half-verse is one breath-group; the space after विनयं is a
word boundary, not a breath.

**Drill 2 — count the eight.** Tap the table once per syllable on the first quarter:
vi · dyā · da · dā · ti · vi · na · yaṃ — eight. Then on the last quarter of the third verse:
śa · śa · ke · na · ni · pā · ti · taḥ — eight. A syllable is a vowel with what precedes it;
a consonant cluster splits between two syllables, so विद्या is vid-yā by ear, vi-dyā on paper,
and counts two either way. That is all of metre this lesson needs: its long–short rules are
v5.c04.

**Drill 3 — one breath per half-verse.** Recite each verse in two breaths, one per half,
with a light break after the eighth syllable of each quarter and a breath at the daṇḍa:
विद्या ददाति विनयं ‖ विनयाद्याति पात्रताम्। — breath — पात्रत्वात् धनमाप्नोति ‖ धनाद्धर्मं ततः
सुखम्॥ The break is a lift of the voice, not a pause for air.

**Drill 4 — seams inside the quarter.** Say these as single stretches, three times each:
विनयाद्याति · धनमाप्नोति · धनाद्धर्मं · बुद्धिर्बलं · निर्बुद्धेस्तु · वसुधैव. Each holds one or
two seams of Chapter 4; none of them is a place to breathe.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — four checks for the verses, mirror in hand.

1. **प्न in आप्नोति — the tongue gets ready while the lips hold.** Close the lips for प;
   while they are still closed, put the tongue tip on the back of the upper teeth. Then open
   the lips, and the air goes out through the nose for न. No vowel between them: not
   \*āpanoti, and not \*āpmoti.
2. **द्ध in धनाद्धर्मं and बुद्धिः — hold at the teeth, release with breath.** The tongue tip
   closes on the back of the upper teeth for the first द, holds, and releases into a
   breathy ध. One closure, two sounds.
3. **सिंहो — the mouth stays open.** The anusvāra before ह is a nasalised vowel: no [ŋ], no
   [m]. The lips do not close, the tongue does not rise; the vowel goes through the nose and
   the ह follows.
4. **न्म in मदोन्मत्तः — the tongue holds while the lips close.** The tongue is at the teeth
   for न; while it is still there the lips close for म. The nasal hum runs on through both.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real reading errors with verses, each with its fix. These feed the quiz
distractors.

1. **Translating word by word in verse order.** "Learning gives discipline, from discipline
   goes worthiness …" *Fix:* find the verb first, then its subject by case (steps 2–3).
2. **Taking the first nominative as the subject of every verb.** विद्या … याति read as
   "learning comes to worthiness". *Fix:* विद्या agrees with याति in person and number, so
   agreement alone does not decide; the sense does — the chain runs forward, and it is not
   learning that goes from discipline to worthiness. याति's subject is not printed: supply
   [नरः] "a person" (step 3).
3. **Changing a form to make the prose "sound right".** \*विनयः ददाति, \*विद्या विनयः ददाति.
   *Fix:* the anvaya moves words; it never changes them. विनयम् stays accusative.
4. **Splitting वेति wrongly** — \*वा + एति, \*वे + इति. *Fix:* an ए at a seam hides अ/आ + इ/ई,
   the guṇa of v3.c04.l01: वा + इति. (वा + ए would have given ऐ.) इति closes the quoted thought.
5. **Reading लघुचेतसाम् as two words**, "of the light minds". *Fix:* one compound, a
   bahuvrīhi, glossed: "of the small-minded".
6. **The Hindi-speaker habit with वसुधैव कुटुम्बकम्** — quoting it as a free-standing saying,
   detached from its speaker. *Fix:* the callout of §4: in the Hitopadeśa a jackal says it,
   to a crow, about a deer he means to eat. Quote the line with its source.

### The anvaya card — the first verse, step by step

| Step | What you do | Result on the first verse |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Split the verse (पदच्छेदः) | विद्या ददाति विनयम् विनयात् याति पात्रताम्। पात्रत्वात् धनम् आप्नोति धनात् धर्मम् ततः सुखम्॥ |

(vidyā vinayam dadāti. [naraḥ] vinayāt pātratām yāti. [saḥ] pātratvāt dhanam āpnoti. dhanāt dharmam [āpnoti], tataḥ sukham [āpnoti].)

worthiness gains wealth. Through wealth [he gains] dharma, and from that, happiness." —

धर्म [पाता है], और उससे सुख [पाता है]।

### The supply box

> **What may be supplied, in square brackets:** the copula, [अस्ति] or [भवति] — the two are
> interchangeable here; [भवेत्] in a question about what could be; the verb of the clause
> before; a subject, [नरः] "a person" or [सः] "he". **What is never done:** a form changed,
> a word dropped, a word moved inside a compound. The anvaya is written in pause form, one
> word at a time, like the padapāṭha of v3.c04.l04: it is a list of words in order, not a
> new joined text.

### Where the three verses differ

- **The second verse has no verb at all.** Both its clauses supply [भवति]. Its first clause is
  a quoted thought closed by इति — "this one is ours, that one an outsider" — and that whole
  thought is what the small-minded "count": गणना is the subject, लघुचेतसाम् its genitive.
  तु stands second, as always (v3.c05.l03).
- **The third verse begins with a relative.** यस्य … तस्य is v3.c05.l03's relative sentence in
  the genitive, "whose … his", "who has … has" (the cells are v3.c05.l02's). It already stands
  first: step 6 changes nothing. Its last clause is an "X-ed by Y" sentence of v3.c02.l05:
  सिंहः … शशकेन निपातितः "the lion … by a hare was brought down" — the -त form is the verb, and
  मदोन्मत्तः agrees with सिंहः in gender, number and case (step 5).

### Verse 2 — who counts as one of us

You may have met the second half of this verse on its own. Read it whole, in its book, with
its speaker.

*The scene* (§4): in the first book of the Hitopadeśa a jackal has made friends with a deer,
meaning to eat him. A crow, the deer's friend, distrusts the newcomer. The jackal answers
angrily, and this is his second verse. Ask as you read: what is the jackal asking the crow to
accept?

Hitopadeśa 1, verse 71 (GRETIL numbering) — text of the GRETIL e-text
[REF:hitopadesha mitralābha 71]:

> अयं निजः परो वेति गणना लघुचेतसाम्। उदारचरितानां तु वसुधैव कुटुम्बकम्॥
> (ayaṃ nijaḥ paro veti gaṇanā laghucetasām | udāracaritānāṃ tu vasudhaiva kuṭumbakam ||)

*Pause form (ours):*

> अयम् निजः परः वा इति गणना लघुचेतसाम्। उदारचरितानाम् तु वसुधा एव कुटुम्बकम्॥

*Our gloss:* "'This one is one of us, that one an outsider' — that is how the small-minded
count; but for those whose conduct is noble, the earth itself is family." — "यह अपना है, वह
पराया" — ऐसी गिनती छोटे मन वालों की होती है; उदार आचरण वालों के लिए तो पूरी धरती ही परिवार है।

*Our anvaya:*

("ayam nijaḥ, [ayam] paraḥ vā" iti gaṇanā laghucetasām [bhavati]. udāracaritānām tu vasudhā eva kuṭumbakam [bhavati].)

Twelve words, two glossed: गणना "reckoning, counting" (an ā-stem like सभा) and लघुचेतसाम् "of
the small-minded" (a bahuvrīhi on चेतस् "mind", genitive plural; its stem class is v4.c05's).

अयं [म् → ं]; परो [-अः before a voiced sound → ओ]; वेति [वा + इति → वेति, guṇa]; उदारचरितानां

(§4). The words are generous; the speaker is not.

## 20. Revision

पुनरावृत्तिः — one screen to remember.

> **अन्वयः, prose order, in seven steps:** (1) split (पदच्छेदः); (2) find the verbs — supply
> अस्ति / भवति or the verb before; (3) find each verb's subject by agreement — supply नरः if
> none; (4) attach the other cases — accusative, ablative "from, through", genitive "of / who
> has"; (5) attach adjectives by gender, number and case; (6) relative before correlative;
> (7) read out: subject — other cases — object — verb. **Two rules:** never change a form;
> supplied words in square brackets. **The three verses:** विद्या ददाति विनयम् — Hitopadeśa,
> introductory verses, 6; अयं निजः परो वेति … वसुधैव कुटुम्बकम् — Hitopadeśa 1.71, a jackal
> (and the Pañcatantra, Book 5, the third brahmin); यस्य बुद्धिर्बलं तस्य — the Pañcatantra, Book
> 1, introducing the lion Bhāsuraka and the hare (the Hitopadeśa's version, 2.122: a crow).
> **The śloka:** four quarters of eight; one breath per half-verse. **The ledger:** विनयः, याति,
> आप्नोति, वसुधा, मदः — 508, Volume 3's last new words; धनम् upgraded; cells drilled 6.

**The third verse's two readings.** The Pañcatantra reading यस्य बुद्धिर्बलं तस्य is also the one
Apte's dictionary quotes from the Pañcatantra (s.v. तद्) [REF:apte1890 s.v. तद्]; the
Hitopadeśa (2.122, GRETIL numbering) has बुद्धिर्यस्य बलं तस्य. Verse numbers differ between
Pañcatantra editions, so the course cites it by book and story. In the Pañcatantra the e-text
names the speaker only as *sa* (*so 'bravīt*, l. 1280, before the crow story the verse closes;
*sa āha*, after Karaṭaka's question, l. 1378), Damanaka by the frame story: that is an
inference, so the lesson does not ask for the Pañcatantra speaker. In the Hitopadeśa the crow
speaks it, answering his wife (*vāyaso brūte*, `hitop_u.htm` l. 2027).

**What is not printed.** The Mahā Upaniṣad as a locus of वसुधैव कुटुम्बकम्: unverified (no
edition opened, sites disagree on the number), so it does not print and no key is added. No
modern institutional use of the line, and no anthology loci. No claim about which book has
either verse first. If the room brings the slogan, answer with the callout: the line's book,
place and speaker.

**अन्वयः as tradition.** The callout attributes the practice to "Sanskrit readers and
teachers" only: no commentary was opened for this lesson, so none is named. A named
commentary, and the anvaya as a commentator's method in depth, are v5.c01's. School
textbooks in India set अन्वय as an exercise; the lesson's §7 says only "you may have met it at
school", because no textbook page was opened.

**Gloss ratios** (method: the verse's words counted in pause form, a compound as one word;
"glossed" = a word neither owned nor read whole under a label taught before the text): the
first verse 0 of 13; the second 2 of 12 (गणना, लघुचेतसाम्); the third 2 of 13 (the उन्मत्तः of
मदोन्मत्तः, निपातितः). The thread verse: nothing ◐, every form ✓ or read whole.

**What not to teach.** The metre's long–short rules (v5.c04): count syllables, nothing more.
The formation of -ता / -त्व [v4.c04], the causative behind निपातितः [v4.c05], the s-stem
चेतस् and its compounds (v4.c05). आप्नुवन्ति is ◐: say it if asked, never ask for it. The
anvaya is a procedure, not a translation contest: mark the steps, not the elegance.

**Grammar terms.** अन्वयः, श्लोकः and पदच्छेदः are terms, not vocabulary rows; in Hindi
text they are अन्वय, श्लोक, पदच्छेद. The ablative read as "through" is a reading note on the
case of v3.c01.l02, which deferred "the ablative of cause": nothing is taught as a new use.

**Differentiation.** Hindi-medium learners often know वसुधैव कुटुम्बकम् already and translate it
before reading it: send them to the speaker first (§12 mistake 6). English-medium learners
translate in verse order: hold them to step 2 — no translation before the verbs are found.
Fast finishers: the anvaya of the thread verse (exercise 6) in class.

**What students find hard:** supplying a subject that is not printed (the doer of याति);
leaving every form as it is while moving it; the verbless second verse; and hearing a quarter
as eight syllables when a consonant cluster splits across two of them.
