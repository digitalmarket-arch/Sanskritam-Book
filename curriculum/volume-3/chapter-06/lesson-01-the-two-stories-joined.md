## 1. Hook

Write two lines on the board, one above the other. Top: एकः काकः अस्ति। सः पिपासितः अस्ति। —
the crow story as Volume 2 printed it, every word in its pause form. Bottom: एकः काकोऽस्ति। स
पिपासितोऽस्ति। — the same two sentences as a book prints them. Same words; and said at speed,
the same sounds. Today you read both Volume 2 stories the book's way, straight through, with no
stop at a seam. Then you read a crow you have not met: the 1.x book's crow, told in the past,
with the harder words Volume 2 put aside — and every one of them is now yours.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — no new history today: the course's own history, and pointers to where the
two stories come from.

This lesson makes no new HISTORY claim, so it prints no callout and nothing in §19 tests
history. What it has is course history, told plainly.

The 1.x course printed two graded readers, a crow and a lion and mouse (1.x book, pp. 64–65).
Volume 2 retold both in pause form, in v2.c06.l01 and v2.c06.l02, and changed the 1.x wording
only where it needed a form not yet taught — or, in the lion, where a 1.x choice was not
idiomatic. The crow that Volume 2 printed is today's Text A1; the lion is Text A2. Both are
reprinted unchanged, only joined.

Text B1, in §17, goes back to the 1.x crow. It puts back the 1.x details that Volume 2 set
aside — तृष्णार्तः for "thirsty", the stones in the plural, and the moral that compares
intelligence with strength — and tells the story in the past, in the imperfect you learned in
v3.c03.l03. It is our telling, not a source text.

Where the stories themselves come from is v2.c06.l01–l02 §4, and nothing is added here: the
crow is an Aesopic fable of which no version is known in the Pañcatantra, the Hitopadeśa or the
Jātakas; the lion and the mouse is an Aesopic fable whose cousins are told in India's fable
books. Those lessons carry the citations. The benediction's label stays v1.c06.l04's:
traditional, its original source unrecorded.

The authentic Sanskrit begins in the next lesson. Lesson 2 reads eight sentences of the
Hitopadeśa itself, lesson 3 six of the Pañcatantra. Today is the bridge: the course's own
stories, printed the way those books are printed.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why a reader of Sanskrit must read joined print, and why this lesson
re-reads two stories you already know.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Sanskrit literature is handed down joined. Whitney: "in the form of the
> language as handed down to us by its literature, the words composing a sentence or paragraph
> are adapted to and combined with one another by nearly the same rules which govern the making
> of compounds; so that it is impossible to take apart and understand a Sanskrit sentence
> without knowing those rules." [REF:whitney1889 §101]

Chapter 4 gave you those rules and the method that takes a joined line apart. Taking a line
apart is not yet reading it. A reader who stops at every seam to split it reads the way a
child spells: correctly and slowly, with the sense lost between the pieces. The three speeds of
§9 are the course's way of moving the split from the eye to the ear. At speed 1 you read the
pause form, word by word. At speed 2 you read the joined line and hear the seams go by. At
speed 3 you read at the pace of speech, and the seam is simply how the words sound together.
No speed norm is cited here: the targets in §9 are the course's own, the crow's taken from the
sandhi exam's booking (v3.c04.l05 §20).

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Formulaic chunks are taught whole and analysed later. [REF:nation2013]

This lesson is the "later" for two whole stories. In Volume 2 you read the crow and the lion
as chunks of meaning, word by word, with some forms glossed whole. Today you read the same
texts with every form taught and every seam named, and you read them faster. The texts do not
change; your reading does. Station 0 and the two bookings of §20 put the new skill back in
front of you after one day and after one week, because spaced review helps retention
[REF:cepeda2006].

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the lesson's idea in three lines you own, each printed joined, its pause
form beneath, then the gloss:

> अद्य द्वे कथे संहितया पठामः। (adya dve kathe saṃhitayā paṭhāmaḥ.)
> अद्य द्वे कथे संहितया पठामः। (adya dve kathe saṃhitayā paṭhāmaḥ.)
> "Today we read the two stories joined." — आज हम दोनों कहानियाँ जुड़े रूप में पढ़ते हैं।

> पदपाठः शनैः, संहिता शीघ्रम्। (padapāṭhaḥ śanaiḥ, saṃhitā śīghram.)
> पदपाठः शनैः, संहिता शीघ्रम्। (padapāṭhaḥ śanaiḥ, saṃhitā śīghram.)
> "The pause form slowly, the joined text fast." — पदपाठ धीरे, संहिता जल्दी।

> सन्धिर्नावसानम्। (sandhir nāvasānam.)
> सन्धिः न अवसानम्। (sandhiḥ na avasānam.)
> "A join is not a pause." — सन्धि विराम नहीं है।

The first line has no seam: every word ends in a vowel before a consonant, so joined and pause
form are one. द्वे कथे is the feminine dual of कथा, and the dual -ए never joins. संहितया, the
instrumental of संहिता, says *how* we read: "in joined form". The second line has one meeting
that changes nothing, पदपाठः before श — the visarga stays before a sibilant —, and a comma that
stops sandhi: शनैः keeps its ः. The third line carries two seams — सन्धिः + न → सन्धिर्न [the
-इः column, v3.c04.l02] and न + अ → ना [सवर्णदीर्घः, v3.c04.l01]. संहिता, सन्धिः and अवसानम्
are v3.c04.l04's headwords; पदपाठः is the grammar term of that lesson; शनैः and शीघ्रम् are
owned (v3.c04.l02, v3.c03.l04). And the third line is the whole lesson: where words meet, the
voice does not stop.

शब्द आपका है: तृष्णा "प्यास" और आर्तः "पीड़ित" — तृष्णया आर्तः, "प्यास से पीड़ित", तृतीया
तत्पुरुष, बिल्कुल पाशबद्धाः = पाशैः बद्धाः की तरह। 1.x के कंकड़ बहुवचन में थे — पाषाणान्; और उसकी
सीख थी बुद्धिः बलात् श्रेष्ठा, "बुद्धि बल से श्रेष्ठ है", जिसमें बलात् पञ्चमी है और "से" (तुलना)
का काम करती है। इनमें से कोई भी ग़लत नहीं था; खण्ड 2 ने इन्हें केवल इसलिए हटाया कि ये रूप तब
तक पढ़ाए नहीं गए थे।

अब एक नया पत्ता, केवल पहचान के लिए — पूर्वकालिक कृदन्त।
अगले पाठों में हितोपदेश और पञ्चतन्त्र के वाक्यों में ऐसे रूप मिलेंगे: उक्त्वा "कहकर", गत्वा
"जाकर", आगत्य "आकर"। हिन्दी बोलने वाले के लिए यह बात जानी-पहचानी है:

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** हिन्दी में -कर / -के / -करके वाला रूप वही काम करता है: मुख्य क्रिया का
> कर्ता पहले कोई काम करता है — राम खाना खाकर सोया। देखकर, खाकर; और करना से करके। [REF:kachru2006]

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** हिन्दी की स्कूली व्याकरण-परम्परा इस -कर वाले रूप को पूर्वकालिक
> क्रिया कहती है। [REF:guru1952]

पर तीन अन्तर ध्यान से देखिए। पहला: हिन्दी में एक ही प्रत्यय हर क्रिया पर लगता है — -कर,
-के, -करके; संस्कृत में दो हैं — सादी क्रिया पर -त्वा, उपसर्ग वाली क्रिया पर -य: उक्त्वा, पर आगत्य।
दूसरा: हिन्दी का रूप उसी धातु-रूप पर बनता है जो आप बोलते हैं — देख से देखकर; संस्कृत में धातु
का रूप बदल सकता है — पश्यति से दृष्ट्वा, गच्छति से गत्वा। उक्त्वा "कहकर" का परिवार उवाच और
उक्तम् है, वदति नहीं। इसीलिए यह पुस्तक इन्हें अभी पूरे शब्द की तरह पढ़ती है; इन्हें बनाना चौथे
खण्ड के पाठ v4.c04 में आएगा। तीसरा: आप इन्हें पढ़ेंगे, लिखेंगे नहीं — कोई अभ्यास, कोई प्रश्न इनका रूप नहीं
माँगता, और कोई अंक इन पर नहीं।

अन्त में मंगल-श्लोक। सर्वे भवन्तु सुखिनः — पहले खण्ड से आप इसे बोलते आए हैं। आज इसका हर शब्द
खुल गया: कुछ पढ़ाए गए, कुछ किसी पत्ते से पहचाने गए, और दो टुकड़ों — आमयः और -भाक् — का अर्थ
दिया गया।

## 8. English Explanation

One reading skill, one restored text, one recognition card, one closed thread. The work: read
joined narrative straight through, as the books you will read next are printed.

### The three speeds on joined print

Volume 2 gave you a fluency protocol in three speeds — भागशः, slow; वाक्यशः, natural; द्रुतपाठः,
timed (v2.c06.l01 §9) — and you ran it on pause-form text. Today the same three speeds run on
joined print:

1. **Speed 1 — the pause form, slowly, word by word.** Read the second line of each pair. Every
   word stands in its own shape; every final is sounded as it is before a pause.
2. **Speed 2 — the joined line at a steady pace.** Read the first line of each pair. No stop
   inside a breath-group; a breath at each daṇḍa, "!" and dash. You hear each seam pass.
3. **Speed 3 — the joined line at natural pace, timed.** The whole story, one run, the clock
   on. The targets: the crow under one minute (the figure Volume 3's crow bookings have used
   since the sandhi exam), the lion under 75 seconds. A target is not a pass mark.

**Before each speed**, run one check over the line: look at the end of each word and the start
of the next — the left word first, as Chapter 4's method does (v3.c04.l04 §8, steps 1–3). A
space after a vowel, an anusvāra or a visarga; a word written into the next; a stop. You are
not splitting now. You are seeing where the seams are, so that your voice does not stop at
them.

### The seam hunt — every seam named

Each seam in Texts A1 and A2 is one you were taught in Chapter 4. Before speed 2, find them and
name them; the full list stands under each text in §13. Three that need care:

- **-अ, a space, a vowel** — मूषक आगच्छति. The -अ hides -अः [or -ए]; the sense chooses मूषकः.
  Do not take it for a vocative.
- **ओऽ** — काकोऽस्ति, पिपासितोऽस्ति. The avagraha marks an अ that is gone: say *kāko 'sti* with no
  sound for ऽ — and do not sound the lost अ.
- **The dash and the "!"** — मूषको वदति — मां मुञ्च! No seam crosses them, so वदति and मुञ्च stand
  as they are.

### Text B1 — the 1.x crow, in the past

§17 prints Text B1: our telling of the crow, in nine lines, in the imperfect, with the 1.x
book's details back in place. Read it as you read A1 and A2 — pause form first, then joined,
then at speed. Three things in it are new to this text, and all three are yours:

- **तृष्णार्तः** "tormented by thirst" — a compound of today's तृष्णा "thirst" and आर्तः
  "afflicted": तृष्णया आर्तः, an instrumental tatpuruṣa, built as पाशबद्धाः = पाशैः बद्धाः was
  in v3.c05.l04. Its inner seam is आ + आ → आ [सवर्णदीर्घः]. पिपासितः, Volume 2's word, means the
  same.
- **पाषाणान्** "stones" — the accusative plural (v3.c02.l01). The 1.x crow threw stones; Volume
  2's crow threw one stone, again and again, because the plural was not yet taught.
- **बुद्धिर्बलाच्छ्रेष्ठा** "intelligence is better than strength" — the 1.x moral. बलात् is the
  ablative (v3.c01.l02) in its "than" use beside श्रेष्ठः, and श्रेष्ठा is the feminine of
  श्रेष्ठः, agreeing with बुद्धिः — both taught in v3.c02.l03 with this very sentence. Its seam त् + श → च्छ is a ◐ row of Chapter 4's consonant map: you read it and
  split it; you are never asked to make it, and बलाच्श्रेष्ठा, without the change of श to छ, is
  also correct [REF:whitney1889 §203]. The moral is printed joined here; the 1.x book printed it
  word by word.

**Which 1.x details were never wrong.** v2.c06.l01 §4 already said it of तृष्णार्तः: "not wrong
either". Today you can check the other two yourself. The plural पाषाणान् is a regular a-stem
cell, and the moral is grammatical Sanskrit: श्रेष्ठः takes the ablative for "than", and v3.c02.l03 already taught
the moral in pause form. Volume 2 changed these three only because they needed
forms you did not yet own. The one thing in the 1.x crow that was not a form of Sanskrit at all
is its spacing, which §22 shows.

### The R5 card — read it for the texts of the next lessons

§15 prints the absolutive card: a verb form in -त्वा or -य, "having …-ed". No line of today's
texts uses one; the Hitopadeśa sentences of lesson 2, the Pañcatantra sentences of lesson 3
and the exam passage do. It is a recognition card, like R1–R4 and R6: you read it, you find its
doer, you never build it. Its owned relatives, checked in the lessons named: तेन उक्तम् "said
by him" (v3.c02.l05 §13, the R2 frame), गत "gone" (v1.c03.l01), the chunk उवाच "said"
(v3.c03.l03), आगच्छति [v2.c05.l01] and पश्यति [v2.c04.l01].

### The benediction — closed

§3 parsed the benediction word by word. That closes the thread Volume 1 opened: "Volume 3
teaches the system" (v1.c06.l04 §15). Every word now has its status — taught, recognised under
a card, or glossed — and the lesson that gave it.

closes at the teeth, and the back of the tongue releases the [k]. No [ɐ] slips in between: not
\*kaścitakāka-.

**Narrow note — every अ sounded.** However long the block, every short अ keeps its beat:
जलमुपर्यागच्छति has [ɐ] in जल-, in -म-, in -प-, in -ग-, in -च्छ-: never \*jalmupryāgacchti.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — four checks for joined reading, mirror in hand.

1. **ओऽ — no ghost vowel.** In काकोऽस्ति the lips round for [oː] and go straight to [s]. Watch
   the mirror: the lips do not open again for an [ɐ] between them.
2. **स्तृ — teeth, then the tap.** In काकस्तृष्णार्त the tongue tip hisses [s] behind the
   teeth, closes on the teeth for [t̪], then taps once for the syllabic [r̩]. Three moves of
   the tongue tip, no vowel between.
3. **ष्ण — curled, and held curled.** In तृष्णा the tongue tip curls back for [ʂ] and stays
   curled for [ɳ]. Do not straighten it between them.
4. **तृष्णार्त आसीत् — a hiatus.** After the final [ɐ] the mouth opens further for [ɑː] with no
   glide and no catch. The two vowels are heard one after the other.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real reading errors on joined text, each with its fix. These feed the quiz
distractors.

1. **Pausing at every seam.** काको … अस्ति read as two breaths. *Fix:* a seam is not a pause:
   read to the daṇḍa in one breath-group (§9 drill 2).
2. **Sounding the lost अ.** काकोऽस्ति read as \*kāko asti. *Fix:* the avagraha marks an अ that
   is gone: *kāko 'sti* (§11 check 1).
3. **Splitting जलायेतस्ततः as \*जलायः इत….** *Fix:* the ए is अ + इ [गुणः]: जलाय इतस्ततः. जलाय is
   the dative "for water", and there is no word \*जलायः.
4. **Taking काक उपायम् for a vocative "O crow".** *Fix:* -अ, a space, a vowel hides -अः [or
   -ए]: the reverse table of v3.c04.l04 §8. काकः is the doer of अचिन्तयत्.
5. **Reading a -त्वा form as a tense.** उक्त्वा read as "he said". *Fix:* the R5 card (§15):
   "having said"; the tense is the main verb's.
6. **The Hindi-speaker habit: dropping the plural ending.** पाषाणान् written as \*पाषाण, because
   Hindi says कंकड़ डाले with the noun unchanged in the direct case. *Fix:* Sanskrit marks the
   accusative plural: -आन्.

*our telling (Volume 2) — v2.c06.l01 §17, joined; the joined lines are the sandhi exam's task C

1. एकः काकोऽस्ति। (ekaḥ kāko 'sti.)
   एकः काकः अस्ति। (ekaḥ kākaḥ asti.)
   "There is a crow." — एक कौआ है।
2. स पिपासितोऽस्ति। (sa pipāsito 'sti.)
   सः पिपासितः अस्ति। (saḥ pipāsitaḥ asti.)
   "He is thirsty." — वह प्यासा है।
3. स जलमिच्छति। (sa jalam icchati.)
   सः जलम् इच्छति। (saḥ jalam icchati.)
   "He wants water." — वह पानी चाहता है।
4. स एकं घटं पश्यति। (sa ekaṃ ghaṭaṃ paśyati.)
   सः एकम् घटम् पश्यति। (saḥ ekam ghaṭam paśyati.)
   "He sees a pot." — वह एक घड़ा देखता है।
5. घटेऽल्पं जलमस्ति। (ghaṭe 'lpaṃ jalam asti.)
   घटे अल्पम् जलम् अस्ति। (ghaṭe alpam jalam asti.)
   "In the pot there is a little water." — घड़े में थोड़ा पानी है।
6. स एकं पाषाणं घटे क्षिपति। (sa ekaṃ pāṣāṇaṃ ghaṭe kṣipati.)
   सः एकम् पाषाणम् घटे क्षिपति। (saḥ ekam pāṣāṇam ghaṭe kṣipati.)
   "He throws a stone into the pot." — वह एक कंकड़ घड़े में डालता है।
7. स पुनः पुनः क्षिपति। (sa punaḥ punaḥ kṣipati.)
   सः पुनः पुनः क्षिपति। (saḥ punaḥ punaḥ kṣipati.)
   "Again and again he throws." — वह बार-बार डालता है।
8. जलमुपर्यागच्छति। (jalam upary āgacchati.)
   जलम् उपरि आगच्छति। (jalam upari āgacchati.)
   "The water comes up." — पानी ऊपर आता है।
9. स जलं पिबति। (sa jalaṃ pibati.)
   सः जलम् पिबति। (saḥ jalam pibati.)
   "He drinks the water." — वह पानी पीता है।
10. स सुखी भवति। (sa sukhī bhavati.)
    सः सुखी भवति। (saḥ sukhī bhavati.)
    "He becomes happy." — वह सुखी हो जाता है।
11. बुद्धिरेव बलम्। (buddhir eva balam.)
    बुद्धिः एव बलम्। (buddhiḥ eva balam.)
    "Intelligence itself is strength." — बुद्धि ही बल है।

**The seams, by lesson:** -अः + अ → ओऽ [काकोऽस्ति, पिपासितोऽस्ति; v3.c04.l02] · सः before a
consonant → स [स पिपासितः, स जलम्; v3.c04.l02] · सः before ए → स by the ordinary -अः + vowel rule
(स एकम्; v3.c04.l02) · -म् + a vowel written together, -म् + a consonant → ं [जलमिच्छति, एकं घटं;
v3.c04.l03] · -ए + अ → एऽ, पूर्वरूपम् [घटेऽल्पम्; v3.c04.l01] · इ + आ → या, यण् [उपर्यागच्छति;
v3.c04.l01] · -इः + a vowel → -इर् [बुद्धिरेव; v3.c04.l02] · -अः before क and प stays (एकः काकः,
पुनः पुनः; v3.c04.l02). **Untaught: none.** Words: 38.

### (2) The R5 card — the absolutive, "having …-ed" (recognition only)

*For the texts of the next lessons. You will meet it in the Hitopadeśa passage of lesson 2,
the Pañcatantra passage of lesson 3 and the exam passage of lesson 5. Formation is Volume 4's
(v4.c04).* Card image: `planned:assets/images/volume-3/v3c06l01-r5-card.svg`.

> A verb form in **-त्वा** (after a simple verb) or **-य** (after a prefix) means "**having
> …-ed**". It never changes. Its doer is the doer of the main verb: स पाषाणं दृष्ट्वा …
> "having seen a stone, he …". In an "X-ed by Y" sentence (R2), its doer is the instrumental Y:
> एवमुक्त्वा तेन … बन्धनानि छिन्नानि "having said so, by him the bonds were cut" (H8).

The card's two Sanskrit fragments, with their IAST: स पाषाणं दृष्ट्वा (sa pāṣāṇaṃ dṛṣṭvā) ·
एवमुक्त्वा तेन (evam uktvā tena). "H8" names the eighth Hitopadeśa sentence of lesson 2.

**Card examples, each with the owned word beside it:**

| Absolutive | Read it as | Beside it, owned |
|---|---|---|
| उक्त्वा | "having said" | the R2 card's तेन उक्तम् [v3.c02.l05] and the chunk उवाच [v3.c03.l03] |
| दृष्ट्वा | "having seen" | the absolutive belonging to पश्यति — display |

| निष्क्रम्य | "having come out" | निस् + क्रम्य; the verb is not owned — glossed |

IAST of the five: उक्त्वा (uktvā) · दृष्ट्वा (dṛṣṭvā) · गत्वा (gatvā) · आगत्य (āgatya) ·
निष्क्रम्य (niṣkramya).

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Whitney calls this form the gerund. It is "made in the later language by
> one of the two suffixes त्वा *tvā* and य *ya*, the former being used with a simple root, the
> latter with one that is compounded with a prepositional prefix" [REF:whitney1889 §990]. "In
> general, the gerund is an adjunct to the subject of a sentence"; and "when a passive form is
> given to the sentence, the gerund qualifies the agent in the instrumental case"
> [REF:whitney1889 §994].

One line of caution, from the same section: -त्वा after a prefix and -य without one are met
too (Whitney §990a); read them the same way.

**Three things the card does not ask.** You never build one: no exercise, no quiz item, no
homework task and no Station 0 key asks for an absolutive, and nothing scores one. You never
analyse its root: उक्त्वा belongs with उवाच and उक्तम्, not with वदति; दृष्ट्वा with पश्यति;
गत्वा with गच्छति — read each whole. And you never use one in a sentence of your own: Text B1
and your retelling in exercise 4 use the imperfect, as the 1.x crow does. The course's name is
"absolutive"; Hindi पूर्वकालिक कृदन्त. The tradition's names stay in §22. Practice: exercise 5,
marked unscored.

   "He wandered here and there for water." — वह पानी के लिए इधर-उधर भटका।
3. तदा स एकं घटमपश्यत्। (tadā sa ekaṃ ghaṭam apaśyat.)
   तदा सः एकम् घटम् अपश्यत्। (tadā saḥ ekam ghaṭam apaśyat.)
   "Then he saw a pot." — तब उसने एक घड़ा देखा।

   "There was a little water in the pot." — घड़े में थोड़ा पानी था।
5. काक उपायमचिन्तयत्। (kāka upāyam acintayat.)
   काकः उपायम् अचिन्तयत्। (kākaḥ upāyam acintayat.)
   "The crow thought of a way." — कौए ने एक उपाय सोचा।
6. स पाषाणान्घटेऽक्षिपत्। (sa pāṣāṇān ghaṭe 'kṣipat.)

   "He threw stones into the pot." — उसने कंकड़ घड़े में डाले।

   "Intelligence is better than strength." — बुद्धि बल से श्रेष्ठ है।
   *— the 1.x moral, printed joined; its seam is ◐: read, never required.*

**Walk it once.** Lines 1–2 are the problem: the 1.x word तृष्णार्तः, and today's इतस्ततः — the
crow does not just want water, as in A1; he goes looking for it, जलाय, "for water", the dative of
purpose you met in v3.c01.l03. Lines 3–4 are the pot and its little water: line 4 is A1's line 5
with आसीत् for अस्ति. Line 5 is the plan, उपायम्, the line A1 never had. Lines 6–7 are the stones
— the 1.x plural पाषाणान् — and the water rising शनैः शनैः, little by little. Line 8 is the payoff,
two verbs joined by च. Line 9 is the 1.x moral, with its ablative "than": a sentence with no verb,
like A1's moral, and अस्ति understood.

**The seams, line by line** (Chapter 4's maps): (1) कश्चित् + क: त् before a voiceless stop
unchanged, written together · काकः + त → काकस्त · तृष्णार्तः + आ → तृष्णार्त + gap; inside the
compound तृष्णा-आर्तः → तृष्णार्तः [सवर्णदीर्घः]. (2) सः + ज → स · जलाय + इतः → जलायेतः [गुणः] ·
इतस्ततः + अ → इतस्ततोऽ [-अः + अ → ओऽ]. (3) तदा + स: no seam · सः + ए → स · एकम्, + a consonant → ं ·
घटम् + अ → घटमपश्यत्. (4) as A1 line 5, with आसीत्. (5) काकः + उ → काक + gap · उपायम् + अ. (6) सः + प →
स · पाषाणान् + घ: न् before a consonant unchanged, written together · घटे + अ → घटेऽ. (7) शनैः + श: ः
stays · शनैः + ज → शनैर् · जलम् + उ · उपरि + आ → उपर्या [यण्]. (8) काकः + ज → काको · जलम् + अ · the
comma, a stop · सुखी + च: none · च + अ → चा [सवर्णदीर्घः]. (9) बुद्धिः + ब → बुद्धिर् · बलात् + श्रेष्ठा →
बलाच्छ्रेष्ठा — ◐, read and split, never required; बलाच्श्रेष्ठा is also correct.

**What it adds up to.** Thirty-eight words. Every form is taught: कश्चित् and तदा [Chapter 5],

**The ◐ seam बलाच्छ्रेष्ठा.** त् + श → च्छ is a ◐ row of v3.c04.l03's map. Accept बलाच्श्रेष्ठा
everywhere; never ask for either; never put either in a learner's join task. Whitney §203: the
following श "may be" converted to छ, and "some authorities regard the conversion … as everywhere
obligatory, others as only optional" [REF:whitney1889 §203].

as in v3.c04.l02. None is printed in learner text in this chapter.

**The R5 card, for the teacher.** The tradition's names are क्त्वा (ktvā) for -त्वा and ल्यप् (lyap)
for -य. Absolutives are indeclinables: 1.1.40 क्त्वातोसुन्कसुनः [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.40]. -त्वा
is used where two actions share one doer and the absolutive's action is the earlier: 3.4.21
समानकर्तृकयोः पूर्वकाले [REF:ashtadhyayi 3.4.21]. In a compound, not one with नञ् as first member,
-त्वा is replaced by -य: 7.1.37 समासेऽनञ्पूर्वे क्त्वो ल्यप् [REF:ashtadhyayi 7.1.37]. Whitney §989:
the gerund (his word) is "used generally as adjunct to the logical subject of a clause, denoting
an accompanying or (more often) a preceding action" [REF:whitney1889 §989]; §994c: "if the
logical subject, the real agent, is put by the construction of the sentence in a dependent case,
it is still qualified by the gerund" [REF:whitney1889 §994]. None of this is learner text: the
card's one sentence is. GLOSSARY row to add: **absolutive** — क्त्वा (ktvā) / ल्यप् (lyap) "the
-त्वा / -य form, 'having …-ed'; recognition card R5: v3.c06.l01; formation v4.c04" — पूर्वकालिक
कृदन्त [Hindi school grammar: पूर्वकालिक क्रिया].
