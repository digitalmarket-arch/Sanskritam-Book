## 1. Hook

A child at bedtime says "tell me a story", and the storyteller knows how to begin: once,
somewhere, there was someone. Sanskrit story books can begin the same way, and when they do, they
use a fixed set of pieces. On the board today stand four first sentences from two books, the
Hitopadeśa and the Pañcatantra — and every one of them begins with the same word, अस्ति, "there
is". Then a puzzle. Two of them say प्रतिवसति स्म. प्रतिवसति is a present tense, "he lives near" —
and yet the story means "he lived". By the end of the lesson you will know why, you will have
read the first six sentences of the Pañcatantra's fourth book, and you will begin a story of
your own the way those books do.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — today's text comes from a book that, according to the Encyclopaedia
Iranica, was translated into Middle Persian, Syriac and Arabic; and a Pali collection also tells
of a monkey and a crocodile.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** According to the Encyclopaedia Iranica, a physician named Borzūya, of the
> time of the Sasanian king Khosrow I (reigned 531–579 CE), translated the Pañcatantra from Sanskrit
> into Middle Persian, and that version was soon translated into Syriac and later into Arabic [REF:iranica s.v.
> Borzūya]; [REF:iranica s.v. Kalila wa Demna]. So the Sanskrit work is older than that; by how much
> is not known.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** A Pali Jātaka, the Suṃsumāra-jātaka (no. 208), tells of a crocodile whose
> wife craves a monkey's heart; the monkey, carried out from the bank, says that his heart hangs on a
> fig tree, and so gets back to land [REF:cowell1895 vol. II, no. 208].

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** The Pañcatantra names its own author: its opening verses say that
> Viṣṇuśarman, having surveyed the essence of all the treatises on worldly affairs, made this charming
> treatise in five books (tantras) [REF:panchatantra kathāmukha, opening verses].

Read the three callouts for what they say and for what they leave out. The first gives a point by
which the Pañcatantra already existed — according to the Encyclopaedia Iranica, a translation
made in the time of Khosrow I (reigned 531–579 CE) — and no date for the Sanskrit book itself: the encyclopaedia's statement fixes the translation,
not the original. The second places a story with the same pair of animals in a Pali collection;
it says what that story tells and nothing about which telling came first or whether one borrowed
from the other. The third is the book's own word about itself, and it is printed as the book's
word.

The name Pañcatantra means "five books", and the third callout gives the reason. Today you open
the fourth. The book cites itself by its own divisions, and so does this course: the Pañcatantra
is cited by book and story, and every Sanskrit line of it you read today carries the source line
"text of the GRETIL e-text".

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why a story told in the past can use a present tense, and why a fixed opening
is learned as a whole.

English can do the same: "So this monkey lives by the sea, and one day a crocodile turns
up…" — the events are past, the tense is present, and the listener is not confused. Sanskrit grammar describes the same use for its own present.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Whitney: "The present has, besides its strictly present use, the same
> subsidiary uses which belong in general to the tense: namely, the expression of habitual action,
> of future action, and of past action in lively narration." [REF:whitney1889 §777] With स्म, he
> adds, the present "has rather more definitely the value of a past tense", and the later examples
> are "hardly to be distinguished from the present of lively narration — of which the whole
> construction is doubtless a form". [REF:whitney1889 §778]

So the construction of today's lesson is not a strange exception. It is the present of lively
narration, tied to a small word, स्म, that tells the reader for certain that the story is past.
The imperfect, which you have used since Chapter 3, stays what it was: Whitney calls it "the tense
of narration", expressing "simple past time, without any other implication" [REF:whitney1889 §779].

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Formulaic chunks are taught whole and analysed later. [REF:nation2013]

The story opening is such a chunk. कस्मिंश्चित्, "in a certain …", is learned today as a word of
the formula, before its seam न् + च is yours to build anywhere else. Station 0 and the bookings of
§20 bring the formula back after one day and after one week, because spaced review helps
retention [REF:cepeda2006].

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the lesson's idea in three lines you own, each printed joined, its pause form
beneath, then the gloss:

> "निवसति स्म" इत्यस्य अर्थो "न्यवसत्"। ("nivasati sma" ity asya artho "nyavasat".)
> "निवसति स्म" इति अस्य अर्थः "न्यवसत्"। ("nivasati sma" iti asya arthaḥ "nyavasat".)
> "'निवसति स्म' means 'न्यवसत्' — he lived." — "निवसति स्म" का अर्थ है "न्यवसत्" — वह रहता था।

> वानरो जम्बूपादपे निवसति स्म। (vānaro jambūpādape nivasati sma.)
> वानरः जम्बूपादपे निवसति स्म। (vānaraḥ jambūpādape nivasati sma.)
> "The monkey lived in a rose-apple tree." — बंदर जामुन के पेड़ पर रहता था।

> मकरः सलिले निवसति स्म। (makaraḥ salile nivasati sma.)
> मकरः सलिले निवसति स्म। (makaraḥ salile nivasati sma.)
> "The crocodile lived in the water." — मगरमच्छ पानी में रहता था।

In the first line the closing quotation mark stops sandhi before इति — स्म stands as it is — and
beyond इति sandhi resumes: इति + अस्य gives इत्यस्य. An opening quotation mark is not a stop, so
अर्थः before "न्यवसत्" joins as अर्थो. In the second line जम्बूपादपे is a compound whose last
member, पादप-, is an a-stem, so its locative is yours. The third line has no seam at all: मकरः
before स keeps its ः, and every other word ends in a vowel before a consonant.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी व्याख्या

हिन्दी में कहानी कैसे शुरू होती है? "किसी गाँव में एक राजा रहता था।" इस एक वाक्य में तीन
टुकड़े हैं: एक जगह, "किसी" के साथ — किसी गाँव में; एक कोई — एक राजा; और बीता हुआ समय — रहता था। [REF:kachru2006]
संस्कृत की कोई-कोई कथा भी ठीक इन्हीं तीन टुकड़ों से खुलती है: अस्ति कस्मिंश्चिद्ग्रामे … निवसति स्म —
"किसी गाँव में … रहता था।" आज के पाठ का विषय है आरम्भ-सूत्र, यानी कथा शुरू करने का बँधा हुआ
ढाँचा।

फ़र्क़ कहाँ है? हिन्दी बीता हुआ समय क्रिया में ही रखती है — "रहता था", यह अभ्यस्त भूतकाल है।
संस्कृत यहाँ वर्तमान काल की क्रिया लेती है — निवसति, "रहता है" — और उसके बाद एक छोटा-सा शब्द
रखती है: स्म। स्म लगते ही वाक्य भूतकाल का हो जाता है: निवसति स्म = रहता था। इसलिए निवसति स्म का
अनुवाद "रहता है" करना ग़लत है, और "रहता था था" जैसा दोहरा भूतकाल भी नहीं बनता — स्म अकेला ही
पूरा भूतकाल ले आता है। पाणिनि ने इसके लिए एक सूत्र दिया है; वह §8 में एक बार छपा है।

दूसरा मेल "किसी" में है। हिन्दी किसी, कोई का तिर्यक रूप है — "किसी गाँव में"। संस्कृत कस्मिंश्चित्,
कश्चित् का सप्तमी रूप है — "किसी … में"। पिछले अध्याय में आपने कश्चित् सीखा था, और कस्मिंश्चित्
केवल दिखाया गया था। आज यह आरम्भ-सूत्र के एक पूरे शब्द की तरह आपका है। घोष ध्वनि से पहले यह
कस्मिंश्चिद् बनता है: कस्मिंश्चिद्वने, कस्मिंश्चिद्ग्रामे।

तीसरा टुकड़ा नाम का है। "रक्तमुख नाम का एक बंदर" — संस्कृत में रक्तमुखो नाम वानरः। पहले नाम,
फिर नाम शब्द, फिर वह कौन है। यहाँ नाम का अर्थ "नाम है" नहीं, "नाम से" है: X नाम Y = "X नाम का
एक Y"।

आज का मूल पाठ पञ्चतन्त्र के चौथे तन्त्र का आरम्भ है: समुद्र के किनारे एक जामुन का पेड़, उस पर
रक्तमुख नाम का बंदर, और एक दिन पानी से निकलकर आया करालमुख नाम का मगरमच्छ, जिसे बंदर अतिथि
मानकर जामुन खिलाता है। इसके छह वाक्य हैं, बीच में अतिथि-सत्कार के चार श्लोक छोड़े गए हैं — उनकी जगह … छपा
है। कुछ शब्दों के अर्थ हाशिये में दिए हैं; उन्हें बनाना आपसे नहीं माँगा जाता। ददौ
"उसने दिया" लिट् लकार, यानी परोक्ष भूत, का रूप है — यह चौथे खण्ड में आएगा। निष्क्रम्य और उक्त्वा
पाठ 1 के पूर्वकालिक कृदन्त वाले पत्र से पढ़िए: "निकलकर", "कहकर" — बिल्कुल हिन्दी के -कर की तरह।

अपनी कथा लिखते समय आप लङ् लकार भी ले सकते हैं — न्यवसत् — और स्म-सहित लट् भी — निवसति स्म।
दोनों भूतकाल हैं। आज का नया हुनर दूसरा वाला है।

## 8. English Explanation

One reading skill, one sūtra, one authentic text. The skill: read a story's first sentence and
find its pieces — the place, the someone, the name, the past — in a fixed order.

### The story-opening formula, in five steps

You already own every part of it. अस्ति has stood in your sentences since Volume 1
(its headword row, v1.c05.l02); the locative of place since v3.c01.l02; नाम since v1.c06.l02's मम नाम …; the
opening कश्चित् … आसीत् since v3.c05.l02; the present and the imperfect of every owned verb since
v3.c03.l01 and l03. What is new is how they fit together, and one fact about स्म. Run these steps
on any first sentence:

1. **Head word.** Is the first word अस्ति, "there is"? Then the sentence is setting a scene, not
   reporting an event. अस्ति at the head means "there is".
2. **The place.** Find the locative: समुद्रोपकण्ठे "near the sea-shore", गोदावरीतीरे "on the
   Godāvarī's bank". If कस्मिंश्चित् stands before it, read "in a certain …": कस्मिंश्चित् is the
   locative of कश्चित्, and it agrees with the place noun.
3. **The subject.** Find the nominative that names who or what "there is": जम्बूपादपः,
   शाल्मलीतरुः, नगरम्. Adjectives beside it agree with it: महान्, विशालः.
4. **The name.** Look for नाम. The word before it is the name; the word after it is what the
   bearer is: रक्तमुखो नाम वानरः — "a monkey called Raktamukha". नाम here is "by name", the owned
   word used as an adverb [REF:whitney1889 §1111].
5. **The verb and its time.** Find the verb. If a present tense is followed by स्म, read it as
   past: प्रतिवसति स्म "lived". If there is no verb but अस्ति, the sentence only sets the scene.
   Then say which pieces are there and which are missing.

Not every opening has every piece. Some have अस्ति, a place and a subject and stop there; some
leave out अस्ति and begin with the place. Step 5's last instruction is part of the skill: a
missing piece is a finding, not a failure.

### Worked example — sentence P2 of today's text

> तत्र च रक्तमुखो नाम वानरः प्रतिवसति स्म। (tatra ca raktamukho nāma vānaraḥ prativasati sma.)
> तत्र च रक्तमुखः नाम वानरः प्रतिवसति स्म। (tatra ca raktamukhaḥ nāma vānaraḥ prativasati sma.)
> "And there lived a monkey named Raktamukha ('Red-face')." — और वहाँ रक्तमुख नाम का एक बंदर रहता था।

1. **Head word.** तत्र, not अस्ति: this is the story's second sentence, and the scene is already
   set.
2. **The place.** तत्र, "there" — the place of sentence P1, the tree by the sea.
3. **The subject.** वानरः, nominative singular.
4. **The name.** रक्तमुखो नाम वानरः: a monkey called Raktamukha. रक्तमुखो is रक्तमुखः before a
   voiced sound (Chapter 4, lesson 2).
5. **The verb and its time.** प्रतिवसति स्म — present + स्म: "lived". प्रतिवसति is प्रति + वसति,
   "lives near" or "dwells"; प्रति is on the upasarga card's list, and प्रतिवसति is one of its display verbs
   (v3.c05.l03). Pieces present: the place (as तत्र), the name, the स्म-past; अस्ति is missing.

### The sūtra behind स्म

The puzzle of the hook has an answer in Pāṇini's grammar. Pāṇini states the rule in two words.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** According to Pāṇini, **3.2.118 लट् स्मे** (*laṭ sme*) — "the present
> (लट्), when स्म stands with it": for something past, the present tense is used with स्म.
> प्रतिवसति स्म "he lived". [REF:ashtadhyayi 3.2.118]; [REF:kashika 3.2.118]

Whitney's description of the same fact, from the side of the language rather than its rules:

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "In connection with certain particles, the present has rather more
> definitely the value of a past tense … With the asseverative particle *sma*." [REF:whitney1889
> §778]

That is the whole new grammar of the lesson. From today, every present tense you own can tell a
past with स्म — निवसति स्म, भक्षयति स्म, वदति स्म — in the 3rd person singular and plural:

> वानरा जम्बूफलानि भक्षयन्ति स्म। (vānarā jambūphalāni bhakṣayanti sma.)
> वानराः जम्बूफलानि भक्षयन्ति स्म। (vānarāḥ jambūphalāni bhakṣayanti sma.)
> "The monkeys ate rose-apples." — बंदर जामुन खाते थे।

### Imperfect or स्म?

Both are past. The course's own narration uses the imperfect (v3.c03.l03), and it stays right:
न्यवसत् "he lived" is as good a past as निवसति स्म. The स्म-present belongs to the
story's opening and to what someone "used to" do. In exercise 4, in your own stories and in the
Volume 3 exam's composition, you may use either. What you may not do is put स्म after an
imperfect: स्म goes with the present, and the present + स्म is already past.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — four checks, mirror in hand.

1. **स्म — no vowel between.** The tongue tip makes the [s] behind the upper teeth while the lips
   are already closing for [m]. There is no [ɐ] between them: स्म, not \*सम.
2. **ण्ठ — curl and hold.** For [ɳ] the tongue tip curls back to the roof of the mouth; it holds
   there for [ʈʰ], released with a puff of breath. A hand before the mouth feels the puff.
3. **कस्मिंश्चित् — no closure for the anusvāra.** Before श the anusvāra closes nothing: the
   vowel [i] is nasalised, air through the nose, and the tongue moves straight to the palatal
   [ɕ]. Then [t͡ɕ] — the tongue body against the hard palate.
4. **तरोरधः — the tap.** The र् of तरोः before अधः is a single tap of the tongue tip, [ɾ], run
   straight into the vowel: /t̪ɐɾoːɾɐd̪ʱɐh/. No pause before it, no trill.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real reading and writing errors, each with its fix. These feed the quiz
distractors.

1. **Translating प्रतिवसति स्म "he lives".** *Fix:* with स्म the present tells the past: "he
   lived" (§8, the sūtra 3.2.118).
2. **Writing स्म after an imperfect** — \*न्यवसत् स्म. *Fix:* स्म goes with the present:
   निवसति स्म. The imperfect is past already; it takes no स्म.
3. **Reading नाम as "the name is"** and the next word as its predicate — \*"the name of Raktamukha
   is monkey". *Fix:* X नाम Y is "a Y called X": the name before नाम, the kind after it.
4. **Splitting समुद्रोपकण्ठे as समुद्रः उपकण्ठे.** *Fix:* it is a compound, one word in the
   pause form; its first member is a stem, समुद्र-, and अ + उ → ओ is its inner seam.
5. **Taking महान् for a plural or a verb.** *Fix:* it is the masculine nominative singular of
   महत्, "great", agreeing with जम्बूपादपः.
6. **The Hindi-speaker habit** — glossing word by word, निवसति "रहता है" + स्म, and then adding a
   था: \*रहता है था or \*रहता था था. *Fix:* स्म makes the whole verb past by itself: निवसति स्म =
   रहता था.

## 15. Grammar

### The formula card

|---|---|---|---|

| **X नाम Y** | "a Y called X" (नाम "by name") | नाम [v1.c06.l02] | P2, P3, D3, D4 |

The card is on the wall (`planned:assets/images/volume-3/v3c06l03-formula-card.svg`); copy it from
memory tonight (§21).

**कस्मिंश्चित् in your own writing.** Before a voiced sound it is कस्मिंश्चिद्: कस्मिंश्चिद्वने,
कस्मिंश्चिद्ग्रामे. Before a voiceless consonant it is written into the next word unchanged:
कस्मिंश्चित्समुद्रतीरे. Choose a place word that begins with a consonant other than न or म: before
a nasal the seam would be a recognition seam, never asked of you. And कस्मिंश्चित् is masculine
and neuter: it goes with ग्रामे, वने, नगरे, समुद्रतीरे. With a feminine place word (नद्याम्, सभायाम्)
its feminine form would be needed, which is not yours: leave कस्मिंश्चित् out there.

### Two labels

- **तत् "so".** In P5, तद्भक्षयतु: तत् is not "that" pointing at a thing; it is the neuter of सः
  used as an adverb, "so, therefore" — the label lesson 2 gave it in तन्मे.
- **नाम "by name".** The owned नाम, used as an adverb after a name: रक्तमुखो नाम वानरः.

### The long compound — read whole

सुकोमलबालुकासनाथे is shown with its structure — [सु-कोमल]-[बालुका]-[सनाथ] — and read whole: "having
very soft sand (as its lord)", that is, "furnished with very soft sand", a locative agreeing with
तीरोपान्ते. You own कोमल "soft" and the prefix सु "very"; बालुका "sand" and सनाथ "furnished with" are
glossed. Nothing in it is yours to build. Long compounds belong to some styles of Sanskrit prose;
you will read more of them in later volumes.

## 16. Conversation

सम्भाषणम् — a child and a mother at bedtime. Ten turns; each printed joined, its pause form
beneath, then the gloss. Audio: 🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-3/v3c06l03-conversation.mp3`.

— बालकः: मातः, कथां कथय! (mātaḥ, kathāṃ kathaya!)
  मातः, कथाम् कथय! (mātaḥ, kathām kathaya!)
  "Mother, tell a story!" — माँ, कहानी सुनाओ!

— माता: शृणु। अस्ति कस्मिंश्चित्समुद्रतीरे महान्वृक्षः। (śṛṇu. asti kasmiṃścit samudratīre mahān vṛkṣaḥ.)
  शृणु। अस्ति कस्मिंश्चित् समुद्रतीरे महान् वृक्षः। (śṛṇu. asti kasmiṃścit samudratīre mahān vṛkṣaḥ.)
  "Listen. On a certain sea-shore there is a great tree." — सुनो। किसी समुद्र के किनारे एक बड़ा पेड़ है।

— बालकः: तत्र को निवसति स्म? (tatra ko nivasati sma?)
  तत्र कः निवसति स्म? (tatra kaḥ nivasati sma?)
  "Who lived there?" — वहाँ कौन रहता था?

— माता: तत्र रक्तमुखो नाम वानरो निवसति स्म। (tatra raktamukho nāma vānaro nivasati sma.)
  तत्र रक्तमुखः नाम वानरः निवसति स्म। (tatra raktamukhaḥ nāma vānaraḥ nivasati sma.)
  "There lived a monkey named Raktamukha." — वहाँ रक्तमुख नाम का एक बंदर रहता था।

— बालकः: स किं भक्षयति स्म? (sa kiṃ bhakṣayati sma?)
  सः किम् भक्षयति स्म? (saḥ kim bhakṣayati sma?)
  "What did he eat?" — वह क्या खाता था?

— माता: स जम्बूफलानि भक्षयति स्म। (sa jambūphalāni bhakṣayati sma.)
  सः जम्बूफलानि भक्षयति स्म। (saḥ jambūphalāni bhakṣayati sma.)
  "He ate rose-apples." — वह जामुन खाता था।

— बालकः: ततः किमभवत्? (tataḥ kim abhavat?)
  ततः किम् अभवत्? (tataḥ kim abhavat?)
  "Then what happened?" — फिर क्या हुआ?

— माता: एकदा करालमुखो नाम मकरः सलिलादागच्छत्। (ekadā karālamukho nāma makaraḥ salilād āgacchat.)
  एकदा करालमुखः नाम मकरः सलिलात् आगच्छत्। (ekadā karālamukhaḥ nāma makaraḥ salilāt āgacchat.)
  "One day a crocodile named Karālamukha came from the water." — एक दिन करालमुख नाम का एक
  मगरमच्छ पानी से आया।

— बालकः: वानरः किमकरोत्? (vānaraḥ kim akarot?)
  वानरः किम् अकरोत्? (vānaraḥ kim akarot?)
  "What did the monkey do?" — बंदर ने क्या किया?

— माता: वानरोऽवदत् — "भोः! भवानतिथिः।" ततः स तस्मै जम्बूफलानि प्रायच्छत्। (vānaro 'vadat — "bhoḥ! bhavān atithiḥ." tataḥ sa tasmai jambūphalāni prāyacchat.)
  वानरः अवदत् — "भोः! भवान् अतिथिः।" ततः सः तस्मै जम्बूफलानि प्रायच्छत्। (vānaraḥ avadat — "bhoḥ! bhavān atithiḥ." tataḥ saḥ tasmai jambūphalāni prāyacchat.)
  "The monkey said: 'Sir! You are a guest.' Then he gave him rose-apples." — बंदर बोला — "हे
  महाशय! आप अतिथि हैं।" फिर उसने उसे जामुन दिए।

Housekeeping. मातः is the vocative of माता [v3.c02.l04], standing in its pause form before the
comma; कथय is the imperative of कथयति [v3.c03.l04's table]; शृणु is the whole-word imperative of
v3.c03.l04. कस्मिंश्चित् is written into समुद्रतीरे unchanged before स, and महान् into वृक्षः before
व — a consonant-final word written together with the next. कः before न becomes को; सः before a
consonant becomes स; सलिलात् before आ becomes सलिलाद्; वानरः before अ becomes वानरोऽ; भवान् before अ
stays single — the न् after a long vowel is not doubled. The dash and the closing quotation mark
are stops. प्रायच्छत् is प्र + अयच्छत्, the augment after the prefix (v3.c03.l04). The mother tells
the story's past with the present + स्म and its events with the imperfect: both are past. The
names are gloss-line items.

[REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.40]; [REF:ashtadhyayi 3.4.21]; [REF:ashtadhyayi 7.1.37]. Never make an
absolutive a required answer.

**The Jātaka.** §4 cites no. 208 only. Nos. 57 and 224 are not cited (no. 224 is two verses only).
The callout says only what both texts show; do not add "cousin", "halfway across the river", or any
word about which telling is older or about borrowing. The Jātaka story was read in a transcription

bibliographic data are "search-confirmed; page not opened" in the verification log. No date range
for the Pañcatantra is given, no translator's name for the Syriac, no year for any version.

**The exam guard.** The Volume 3 exam's task C is an unseen passage that continues this story. Do
not read on beyond P6 in class, do not tell what happens next, and do not set a comparison of the
two tellings' later events: the optional comparison box of the chapter's research is not printed
here for that reason.

**Hindi.** §7's contrast (किसी गाँव में एक राजा रहता था against अस्ति कस्मिंश्चिद्ग्रामे … निवसति स्म)
is limited to the three pieces, the place of the past (Hindi in the verb, Sanskrit in the present +
स्म), and किसी as the oblique of कोई [REF:kachru2006].

**Differentiation.** Hindi-medium learners read the formula at once — it is their own story
opening — but translate निवसति स्म "रहता है"; give them §12 mistake 6 and §6's first line twice.
English-medium learners take नाम for "the name is"; give them P2's worked example (§8) and three
more names to place before नाम.

**What students find hard:** the stretch sentence P3 (read it in the four breath-groups of §9);
the long compound (read it whole, never build it); keeping स्म off an imperfect; and leaving a
missing piece missing instead of inventing one.
