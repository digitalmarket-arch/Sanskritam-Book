## 1. Hook

Volume 3 opened on one hunter and one dove, and every word stood alone on the page in its pause
form. Since then the words have taken their endings, their persons and
their tenses, they have learned to join, to stand for each other, to fuse into compounds, and in
the last four lessons they have become books: the Hitopadeśa's doves, the Pañcatantra's monkey,
three verses with their speakers. Today a page of the Pañcatantra that you have never seen is
put in front of you, joined, with five words glossed in the margin, and you read it. That is
what Volume 3 was for. Two sittings, six tasks, 508 words. Pass, and Volume 4, पाणिनीयम्,
opens.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — the volume in one paragraph, then the three source lines task F tests,
restated here as they were taught.

Volume 3 began where Volume 2's stories left the endings unexplained, and it explained them.
Chapters 1 and 2 gave the nouns their eight cases and three numbers; Chapter 3 gave the verb its
persons, its second family of endings and four lakāras; Chapter 4 joined the words as books join
them; Chapter 5 gave the pronouns, the words that never change and the compounds; Chapter 6 read
the books. In the course of it, 148 words were added to the ledger (360 → 508), eight tables were
built, and twenty-eight sūtras of Pāṇini were quoted to you, each in its own lesson. The 1.x
book's errors that touched this material were corrected in the open, lesson by lesson. Lesson 2's
Hitopadeśa passage was printed as a mosaic: eight of the book's sentences, each with its place in
the book, the gaps marked "…" and bridged in our own words. Today's exam tests only what the
volume delivered, in the form it delivered it.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** In the Hitopadeśa (Book 1, Mitralābha, verse 71 in the GRETIL numbering) the
> verse अयं निजः परो वेति … वसुधैव कुटुम्बकम् is spoken by a jackal who has made friends with a deer in
> order to eat him, answering a crow who distrusts him [REF:hitopadesha mitralābha 71].

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** In the Pañcatantra (Book 1) the verse यस्य बुद्धिर्बलं तस्य introduces the story
> of the lion Bhāsuraka and the hare [REF:panchatantra tantra 1].

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** The line सत्यमेव जयते is from the Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad, 3.1.6 [REF:mundaka 3.1.6];
> its जयते is the middle form, where classical prose says जयति [v3.c03.l02].

Each callout says who speaks, or where a line stands, and nothing more. The first is lesson 4's
callout in brief: the generous words are a jackal's argument. The second places a verse at the
door of a story. The third is v3.c03.l02's, and it carries a grammatical fact with it. The other
cards of task F are facts of grammar and of the course's own printed pages, and need no callout.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why the exam is built the way it is.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Retrieval practice beats re-reading for long-term retention.
> [REF:roediger2006]

Every task makes you retrieve: a dialogue understood at the speed it is spoken, a sentence
written as it was heard, a page read that you have never seen, a form produced with no table in
front of you, a story told in your own Sanskrit, a claim judged by its source. None of them can
be answered by recognising a page you have re-read.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Formulaic chunks are taught whole and analysed later. [REF:nation2013]

The exam marks where that "later" has arrived. उवाच and आह are still chunks, and the absolutive
is still a card you read: the exam asks you to read them, never to make them. What Volume 3
analysed — the cells of the eight tables, the joins of the three maps, the four compound kinds,
the speech frame, the formula, the anvaya — the exam asks you to produce.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spaced practice produces more durable learning than the same practice
> massed together. [REF:cepeda2006]

That is why the Station 0 items arrive after one day and one week, why the exam is taken in two
sittings with a break between them, and why §20 books two more items into Volume 4.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the day in three lines, each printed joined, its pause form beneath, then the
gloss.

> अद्य परीक्षास्ति। (adya parīkṣāsti.)
> अद्य परीक्षा अस्ति। (adya parīkṣā asti.)
> "Today there is an exam." — आज परीक्षा है।

> वयं कथाः पठामो लिखामश्च। (vayaṃ kathāḥ paṭhāmo likhāmaś ca.)
> वयम् कथाः पठामः लिखामः च। (vayam kathāḥ paṭhāmaḥ likhāmaḥ ca.)
> "We read stories and write them." — हम कहानियाँ पढ़ते हैं और लिखते हैं।

> यत्र सन्धिस्तत्रावसानं नास्ति। (yatra sandhis tatrāvasānaṃ nāsti.)
> यत्र सन्धिः तत्र अवसानम् न अस्ति। (yatra sandhiḥ tatra avasānam na asti.)
> "Where there is a seam, there is no pause." — जहाँ सन्धि है, वहाँ विराम नहीं।

Nine seams, all Chapter 4's: परीक्षा + अस्ति fuse to आ [सवर्णदीर्घः]; वयम् before क becomes
वयं; कथाः before प keeps its ः; पठामः before ल becomes पठामो; लिखामः + च gives श्च; सन्धिः
before त gives स्त; तत्र + अवसानम् fuse to आ, अवसानम् before न becomes अवसानं, and न + अस्ति is
नास्ति. The third line is the relative pair यत्र … तत्र of v3.c05.l03. परीक्षा "exam" is the
title word you have read whole since Volume 2; सन्धिः and अवसानम् are v3.c04.l04's.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — one worked model of each kind of task. None of them is an item of the exam; each
shows how the kind is done and scored. Every course-written line is printed joined, its pause
form beneath.

### Task A's kind — questions on a heard text

The text heard is Text A1, the Volume 2 crow (lesson 1 §13). Two questions in the booklet's shape,
and one spiral question:

1\. स किमिच्छति? (sa kim icchati?)
   सः किम् इच्छति? (saḥ kim icchati?)
   "What does he want?" — वह क्या चाहता है? → **जलम्** — किम् asks for the thing, so the answer
   stands in the accusative, the case the verb gives it.

2\. घटे किमस्ति? (ghaṭe kim asti?)
   घटे किम् अस्ति? (ghaṭe kim asti?)
   "What is in the pot?" — घड़े में क्या है? → **अल्पम् जलम्** — a phrase is a full answer.

3\. (spiral) In स एकं पाषाणं घटे क्षिपति, the word पाषाणम् — which case, and which job? →
   **द्वितीया; कर्म** — the thing thrown.

### Task B's kind — a sentence heard, written, then split

Heard twice at natural pace, once slowly (Text B1, line 8, lesson 1 §17):

काको जलमपिबत्, सुखी चाभवत्। (kāko jalam apibat, sukhī cābhavat.)
काकः जलम् अपिबत्, सुखी च अभवत्। (kākaḥ jalam apibat, sukhī ca abhavat.)
"The crow drank the water and was happy." — कौए ने पानी पिया और सुखी हुआ।

What the ear catches: [kɑːkoː] — an -ओ before a voiced sound, so -अः; [d͡ʑɐlɐmɐpibɐt̪] — म्
running into a vowel, written together; a breath at the comma, so अपिबत् stands in its pause
form; [t͡ɕɑːbʱɐʋɐt̪] — च + अ fused. Scoring: the joined line 1, the pause form 1.

### Task C's kind — a glossed passage, read, translated, asked

The model is lesson 2's sentence H2, which you have read (Hitopadeśa 1, prose after verse 38 —
text of the GRETIL e-text [REF:hitopadesha mitralābha]):

> तन्मे मित्रं हिरण्यको नाम मूषिकराजो गण्डकीतीरे चित्रवने निवसति। (tan me mitraṃ hiraṇyako nāma mūṣika-rājo gaṇḍakī-tīre citra-vane nivasati |)
> — margin: मूषिकराजः "king of the mice"

A translation unit is scored for its sense (1) and for who does what (1): "So: my friend, the
mouse-king named Hiraṇyaka, lives in the Bright Forest on the Gaṇḍakī's bank" earns both; "my
friend lives in a forest" earns the roles but loses the sense of the name and the place. A
question in Sanskrit:

हिरण्यकः कुत्र निवसति? (hiraṇyakaḥ kutra nivasati?)
हिरण्यकः कुत्र निवसति? (hiraṇyakaḥ kutra nivasati?)
"Where does Hiraṇyaka live?" — हिरण्यक कहाँ रहता है? → **गण्डकीतीरे चित्रवने** — either word
alone also scores: कुत्र asks for a place, and the text gives it in the locative.

1\. यदा काकस्तृष्णार्तोऽभवत्, तदा स जलायेतस्ततोऽभ्रमत्। (yadā kākas tṛṣṇārto 'bhavat, tadā sa jalāyetastato 'bhramat.)
   यदा काकः तृष्णार्तः अभवत्, तदा सः जलाय इतस्ततः अभ्रमत्। (yadā kākaḥ tṛṣṇārtaḥ abhavat, tadā saḥ jalāya itastataḥ abhramat.)
   "When the crow was tormented by thirst, he wandered here and there for water." — जब कौआ
   प्यास से व्याकुल हुआ, तब वह पानी के लिए इधर-उधर भटका।

2\. "जलं कुत्रास्ति?" इति काकोऽचिन्तयत्। ("jalaṃ kutrāsti?" iti kāko 'cintayat.)
   "जलम् कुत्र अस्ति?" इति काकः अचिन्तयत्। ("jalam kutra asti?" iti kākaḥ acintayat.)

3\. स घटमपश्यत्, घटेऽल्पं जलमासीत्। (sa ghaṭam apaśyat, ghaṭe 'lpaṃ jalam āsīt.)

join running again after इति; every verb of the narration an imperfect (the अस्ति inside the
thought is the crow's own present).

— सीता: अर्जुन, सर्वान्पाठानपठः? (arjuna, sarvān pāṭhān apaṭhaḥ?)

  अहम् तु कथाम् उच्चैः अपठम्। (aham tu kathām uccaiḥ apaṭham.)
  "And I read a story aloud." — और मैंने एक कहानी ज़ोर से पढ़ी।

  काम् कथाम्? (kām kathām?)
  "Which story?" — कौन-सी कहानी?

— सीता: वानरस्य मकरस्य च। (vānarasya makarasya ca.)
  वानरस्य मकरस्य च। (vānarasya makarasya ca.)
  "The monkey's and the crocodile's." — बंदर और मगरमच्छ की।

— अर्जुनः: गुरुरागच्छति। (gurur āgacchati.)
  गुरुः आगच्छति। (guruḥ āgacchati.)
  "The teacher is coming." — गुरुजी आ रहे हैं।

— गुरुः: नमो वः। उपविशत, लिखत च। (namo vaḥ. upaviśata, likhata ca.)
  नमः वः। उपविशत, लिखत च। (namaḥ vaḥ. upaviśata, likhata ca.)
  "Hello, all of you. Sit down, and write." — सबको नमस्ते। बैठो, और लिखो।

— अर्जुनः सीता च: नमस्ते, गुरो! (namaste, guro!)
  नमः ते, गुरो! (namaḥ te, guro!)
  "Hello, teacher!" — नमस्ते, गुरुजी!

Housekeeping. अर्जुन is the vocative, in its pause form before the comma. सर्वान् is written into
पाठान्, a consonant-final word before a consonant; पाठान् + अ is written together, the न् after a
long vowel not doubled; अपठः is the 2nd person singular imperfect of पठति. ह्यः keeps its ः before
श; श्लोकान् + अ as before; श्लोकः is the GLOSSARY's name for the verse form. अहम् before त becomes
अहं; तु stands second (v3.c05.l03); कथाम् + उ is written together; उच्चैः + अ gives उच्चैर्. काम् before
क becomes कां — the feminine accusative of किम्. गुरुः + आ gives गुरुर्. नमः before व becomes नमो,
and वः, the short "to you all", leans on the word before it (v3.c05.l01); उपविशत and लिखत are
plural imperatives (v3.c03.l04), the comma a stop. नमस्ते is नमः + ते, the course's first word,
proved in three lines in v3.c05.l01; गुरो is the vocative of गुरुः [v3.c02.l03]. The names are the
course's own.
