## 1. Hook

Look at a family photograph and say who is in it, in Sanskrit you already own: एषः मम पिता।
एषा मम माता। Now try to say "my parents". Hindi has a word for the pair, माता-पिता; Sanskrit
has something stranger. It takes the word for father and puts it in a number you have never
used — a number for exactly two — and says पितरौ, "the two fathers", meaning father and
mother. Pāṇini recorded that in a sūtra of two words. By the end of the lesson you can say
पितरौ, call out हे मातः! the way Volume 1 promised, and count your own hands, ears and eyes
the way Sanskrit counts them: in twos.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — the three words of Pāṇini behind the title, and the king of the doves in
the text our serial retells.

The title, पितरौ, is the dual of पिता: "the two fathers". You will build the form yourself in
§8. Why it can mean "father and mother" is not something the endings tell you. The tradition
says it in one sūtra.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** According to Pāṇini (1.2.70 पिता मात्रा, 'father, with
> mother'), the word for father, used together with mother, can stand for both: पितरौ, 'the
> parents'. [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.2.70]

The sūtra is pitā mātrā: पिता in the nominative and मात्रा in the instrumental, "father,
with mother". You can now read both words, and §15 shows where each comes from. Read it as a
statement about usage: पितरौ *can* mean the parents. It does not always do so. It can mean
"two fathers", and the plural पितरः also means "the ancestors".

The story in §17 is part 4 of the serial's second episode, and it comes from an old text.
Our retelling says so on every page.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** The serial is our retelling after the frame story of the first
> book of the Hitopadeśa, the *Mitralābha*, a collection of fables in prose and verse
> attributed to Nārāyaṇa and dated, on the usual scholarly estimate, to roughly the
> 9th–12th centuries CE. In that frame the doves have a king. Flying in the sky with his
> flock, he sees the rice grains the hunter has scattered, and he himself warns that grains
> in a lonely forest are suspicious. The flock comes down all the same, and then, the text
> says, all of them were bound in the net. [REF:hitopadesha mitralābha]

Our retelling keeps the king and the net, and changes what the text does not say. In the
text the king warns and is overruled; in ours the warning came from the free dove in the
tree (lessons 1 and 3). And the text says only that *all* the doves were bound; our king is
caught last, his two feet in the snare, and a young dove calls to its parents. §22 sets the
departures out in one line for the teacher.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — a third grammatical number, and one word with two stems.

English and Hindi have two numbers, one and more than one. Sanskrit has three.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Sanskrit has three grammatical numbers: singular, dual and plural.
> The dual is used, with very rare exceptions, wherever two things are meant — whether a
> natural pair (हस्तौ "the two hands") or two individuals taken together. So the plural of
> Sanskrit begins at three. [REF:whitney1889 §264] [REF:whitney1889 §265]

Hindi does not have a number for two, and neither does English: there, a pair is just a plural ("two hands", दो हाथ). Sanskrit puts a separate
ending on the noun, on its adjective and on its verb when exactly two are meant. For the
noun, the work is lighter than it sounds. The dual has three forms in every word, not
eight (§15 Step 3): one for the nominative and accusative, one for the instrumental, dative
and ablative, and one for the genitive and locative.

The second fact is about the stem. Look at पिता, पितरम्, पित्रा, पितुः: one word, and the
part before the ending changes shape.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Many Sanskrit nouns have a stronger and a weaker stem. In the
> nominative and accusative singular and dual and in the nominative plural — the five
> "strong cases" — the stem is fuller than in the rest: पितरम्, पितरौ, पितरः against
> पित्रा, पितृभिः. In ṛ-stems the strong stem ends in -अर्- for most kinship nouns and in
> -आर्- for agent nouns and for स्वसा: पितरम्, but दातारम्, स्वसारम्. [REF:whitney1889 §311]
> [REF:whitney1889 §370]

You will meet the strong cases again in other kinds of noun; the ṛ-stems are your first
meeting with them. Lesson 3's strengthened vowel (गुरवे, गुरोः) was a change
in one sound; here the stem gains a whole syllable.

Last, a map between Hindi and Sanskrit, stated as a contrast, not a ranking:

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Hindi दो is followed by a plural noun (दो हाथ, दो लड़के); Sanskrit
> uses its dual (द्वौ हस्तौ, द्वौ बालकौ). Hindi's tatsama kinship and agent words माता, पिता,
> दाता, कर्ता, and the literary भ्राता, have the shape of the Sanskrit nominative singular;
> the dictionary stems are मातृ, पितृ, दातृ, कर्तृ. [REF:kachru2006] [REF:whitney1889 §373]

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the pattern, said in Sanskrit you own, pause form:

> **एषः मम पिता। एषा मम माता।**
> (eṣaḥ mama pitā. eṣā mama mātā.) "This is my father. This is my mother." — ये मेरे पिता
> हैं। ये मेरी माता हैं।

> **एकः पिता। द्वौ पितरौ। बहवः पितरः।**
> (ekaḥ pitā. dvau pitarau. bahavaḥ pitaraḥ.) "One father. Two fathers. Many fathers." — एक
> पिता। दो पिता। बहुत-से पिता।

> **पिता — ऋकारान्तः। पितरौ — द्विवचनम्।**
> (pitā — ṛkārāntaḥ. pitarau — dvivacanam.) "पिता: a word ending in ṛ. पितरौ: the dual." —
> पिता — ऋकारान्त शब्द। पितरौ — द्विवचन।

> **पिता मात्रा — पितरौ। माता च पिता च।**
> (pitā mātrā — pitarau. mātā ca pitā ca.) "Father, with mother: पितरौ. Mother and father."
> — पिता माता के साथ: पितरौ — माता और पिता।

> **मम द्वौ हस्तौ। मम द्वे नेत्रे।**
> (mama dvau hastau. mama dve netre.) "My two hands. My two eyes." — मेरे दो हाथ। मेरी दो
> आँखें।

The first line is pure ownership. The second counts with three numbers: एकः, द्वौ, and the
owned बहवः of lesson 1. The third names what you see with two grammar terms, ऋकारान्तः
"ending in ṛ" and द्विवचनम् "the dual" — terms you use, not ledger words. The fourth says the
sūtra of §4 in its own words, and the last puts the dual where it lives most naturally: on the
body's pairs.

भी। ये संस्कृत से आए
तत्सम शब्द हैं, और मज़े की बात यह है कि ये संस्कृत के प्रथमा एकवचन वाले रूप हैं — यानी
वाक्य का कर्ता बनकर जो रूप आता है, वही। संस्कृत के शब्दकोश में ये शब्द ऐसे नहीं मिलते: वहाँ
मातृ, पितृ, दातृ, कर्तृ लिखे होते हैं, जिनके अन्त में ऋ है। इसलिए इन्हें ऋकारान्त शब्द
कहते हैं (ऋकारान्त = जिसके अन्त में ऋ हो)। पहले खण्ड में आपने पढ़ा था कि मातृ "कोश वाला"
रूप है और माता "वाक्य वाला" रूप — आज वह बात पूरी तालिका बनकर खुलती है।

इन शब्दों की एक ख़ास बात है: इनका मूल रूप कुछ जगहों पर फैल जाता है। पितरम् (पिता को),
पितरौ (दो पिता), पितरः (कई पिता) — यहाँ पित- के बाद -अर्- आता है। पर पित्रा (पिता से),
पितुः (पिता का) में मूल छोटा है। ऐसी पाँच जगहें हैं — प्रथमा और द्वितीया का एकवचन, प्रथमा
और द्वितीया का द्विवचन, और प्रथमा बहुवचन — जहाँ मूल मज़बूत होता है। रिश्ते वाले ज़्यादातर
शब्दों में यह -अर्- है (पितरम्, मातरम्, भ्रातरम्), और "करने वाला, देने वाला" जैसे शब्दों
में -आर्- (दातारम्, कर्तारम्)। स्वसा (बहन) रिश्ते का शब्द होकर भी मज़बूत रूपों में दाता की तरह चलती है:
स्वसारम्। बुलाने का रूप (सम्बोधन) है हे पितः!, हे मातः! — पहले खण्ड में जिस मातः का वादा
किया गया था, वह यही है।

अब दूसरी नई बात — द्विवचन। हिन्दी में दो वचन हैं: एकवचन और बहुवचन। "दो हाथ", "दो लड़के" —
दो के साथ भी हिन्दी बहुवचन ही लगाती है। संस्कृत में तीसरा वचन है, ठीक दो के लिए: द्विवचन
(दो के लिए अलग रूप)। एकः हस्तः — एक हाथ; द्वौ हस्तौ — दो हाथ; और तीन से बहुवचन शुरू होता
है: त्रयः हस्ताः। इसलिए "दो हाथ" का अनुवाद द्वौ हस्ताः नहीं, द्वौ हस्तौ है। अच्छी ख़बर यह है
कि द्विवचन में हर शब्द के सिर्फ़ तीन रूप होते हैं, आठ नहीं: एक रूप प्रथमा-द्वितीया का, एक
तृतीया-चतुर्थी-पञ्चमी का (-भ्याम्), और एक षष्ठी-सप्तमी का (-ओः)। आज आप पहला रूप हर
शब्द के लिए बनाना सीखेंगे — रामौ, फले, बालिके, नद्यौ, अग्नी, गुरू, पितरौ — और बाकी दो
को पहचानना।

दो के लिए संख्या-शब्द भी द्विवचन में ही है: पुल्लिंग में द्वौ, स्त्रीलिंग और नपुंसकलिंग में
द्वे। द्वौ हस्तौ, द्वे नेत्रे, द्वे बालिके। "दोनों" के लिए उभौ / उभे: उभौ पादौ "दोनों पैर"।
एक सावधानी: फले जैसा -े वाला नपुंसक द्विवचन सप्तमी एकवचन जैसा दिखता है (फले = "फल पर" भी), और
बालिके जैसा स्त्रीलिंग द्विवचन सम्बोधन एकवचन जैसा (हे बालिके!)। वाक्य से तय कीजिए: द्वे फले में फले
द्विवचन है।

और अब शीर्षक का रहस्य। हिन्दी में "माता-पिता" कहते हैं। संस्कृत में पिता का द्विवचन पितरौ
भी "माता-पिता" का अर्थ दे सकता है। पाणिनि का सूत्र 1.2.70 पिता मात्रा यही कहता है: माता के
साथ आने पर पिता शब्द दोनों का अर्थ दे सकता है। पर ध्यान रखिए — पितरौ का अर्थ "दो पिता" भी
हो सकता है; हमेशा "माता-पिता" ही नहीं।

आख़िर में प्रणाम की बात। अहम् मातरम् नमामि — "मैं माता को प्रणाम करता हूँ" — यहाँ क्रिया
नमामि के साथ द्वितीया (मातरम्) आती है। पर नमः शब्द के साथ सिर्फ़ चतुर्थी आती है: मात्रे नमः,
"माता को नमस्कार"। हिन्दी दोनों में "को" लगाती है, संस्कृत दो अलग विभक्तियाँ। हाँ, नमामि के
साथ चतुर्थी (मात्रे नमामि) भी शब्दकोश में मिलती है, इसलिए वह भी ग़लत नहीं; यह पाठ द्वितीया
का अभ्यास कराता है। और एक बात: हिन्दी में आदर के लिए एक व्यक्ति के साथ भी बहुवचन बोलते हैं —
"पिताजी आते हैं"। इस पाठ्यक्रम की संस्कृत एक व्यक्ति के लिए एकवचन ही रखती है: पिता
आगच्छति। सब कुछ आज भी विराम-रूप में है — हर शब्द अलग, बिना जोड़े।

## 8. English Explanation

No new sound and no new sign: ॠ, which Volume 1 taught "for completeness", now turns up in
real words. The lesson's load is one table (T5, the ṛ-stems) and one card (the dual, three
endings in every word). The recipe is the volume's — a scene, the question, the forms you
already own, the pattern named, your table, production.

### Step 1 — the scene

A family photograph: a father, a mother, two children. Three sentences, all readable with what
you own:

> **एषः मम पिता। एषा मम माता।**
> (eṣaḥ mama pitā. eṣā mama mātā.) "This is my father. This is my mother." — ये मेरे पिता
> हैं। ये मेरी माता हैं।

> **मम पितरौ गृहे स्तः।**
> (mama pitarau gṛhe staḥ.) "My parents are at home." — मेरे माता-पिता घर पर हैं।

One label, printed here once for the whole lesson. **स्तः** (staḥ) "the two are", and every
verb in **-तः** you will meet today (गच्छतः, निवसतः, पश्यतः), is **the verb's dual, taught
fully in c03.l01** — a preview (○). You read it and copy it; you are not asked to build it.
v2.c02.l04 showed स्तः and promised it; this is the promise kept halfway.

You know पिता, माता, मम, गृहे. What is left over is पितरौ.

### Step 2 — the question

Two questions. What is पितरौ — which word is it a form of, and which cell? And why is there
an ending for *two*, when Hindi and English manage with "more than one"?

### Step 3 — the evidence you already own

Before any rule, look at what is already yours. Cover the right-hand column and say what each
form shows; then uncover and compare.

### Step 4 — the pattern, named

**ऋकारान्तः (ṛkārāntaḥ) "ending in ṛ".** पिता, माता, भ्राता, स्वसा, दुहिता and today's दाता
"giver" and कर्ता "doer" are stems in -ऋ: पितृ, मातृ, दातृ. Their nominative singular ends in
-आ (पिता), their vocative in -अः (हे पितः), their ablative and genitive singular in -उः
(पितुः).

**The strong stem.** In five cells — the nominative and accusative singular, the nominative
and accusative dual, the nominative plural — the stem grows a syllable: **-अर्-** in most
kinship words (पितरम्, पितरौ, पितरः), **-आर्-** in agent words and in स्वसा (दातारम्,
दातारौ, दातारः; स्वसारम्). The nominative singular shows it only as its long -आ. In the
other cells the ऋ stays short before a consonant ending (पितृभिः), becomes र् before a vowel
(पित्रा, पित्रे) and -अर्- before the -इ of the locative (पितरि); the ablative-genitive has
-उः (पितुः); and two plural cells lengthen it (next paragraph).

**ॠ, a real vowel.** In the accusative plural and the genitive plural the ऋ is lengthened:
पितॄन्, मातॄः, पितॄणाम्. Volume 1 gave you ॠ for completeness; here it is, in three words.

**द्विवचनम् (dvivacanam) "the dual" — for exactly two.** One form for the nominative and
accusative (and the vocative), and two more forms for all the other cases. For the
ṛ-stems, the strong stem + -औ: पितरौ, मातरौ, दातारौ. That is the title, and it is the shape
of अयोगवाहौ: रामौ, the two Rāmas, ends the same way.

And the meaning "parents": that is Pāṇini's sūtra in §4, पिता मात्रा. Hold it as TRADITION,
attributed: the endings make पितरौ "two fathers"; the sūtra records that the word for father,
used with mother, can stand for both.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — no new sounds; the work is a short and a long syllabic r told apart, the strong
stem heard, and the two dual endings -औ and -ओः kept apart. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-3/v3c02l04-endings-drill.mp3` (drills 1–4, the listening pairs
and the dictation).

**Drill 1 — ऋ against ॠ.** Say पितृभिः then पितॄन्, slowly, five times. The ऋ of पितृभिः is one
beat: a syllabic r, the tongue tip tapping lightly at the ridge behind the upper teeth, with
voice on it and no vowel after it. The ॠ of पितॄन् is the same sound held for two beats. Never say
"pitrī": no i-vowel follows. Then मातृषु — मातॄणाम्, same contrast.

**Drill 2 — the strong stem.** Tap one beat per syllable: पि-ता (2) · पि-त-रम् (3) · पि-त-रौ
(3) · पि-त-रः (3) against पि-त्रा (2) · पि-तुः (2). Then the agent words, with the long आ
held: दा-ता-रम्, दा-ता-रौ. The strong cells have one syllable more; the ear can hear the
table.

**Drill 3 — -औ against -ओः.** पितरौ — पित्रोः · रामौ — रामयोः. -औ is a diphthong, the jaw
opening for /ɐ/ and closing toward /u/; -ओः is a pure long /oː/ followed by a breathed /h/.
Five pairs, then the audio calls one and you hold up one finger for -औ, two for -ओः.

**Drill 4 — the calling forms.** हे पितः! हे मातः! — the final ः breathed, the vowel before it
short. Not "he pitā!": that is the nominative. Then in a row, as if calling across a room:
हे राम! · हे बालिके! · हे गुरो! · हे पितः! · हे मातः!

**Listening pairs (8).** Each pair is played once in the order printed, then one word of each
pair alone; write which one you heard. पितृभिः / पितॄन् · मातृषु / मातॄणाम् · पिता / पितः ·
पितरौ / पितरः · पितरम् / दातारम् · रामौ / रामयोः · पितरौ / पित्रोः · फले (dual) / फले
(locative). The last pair sounds the same; the track plays it inside a short phrase — द्वे फले,
"two fruits", or फले जलम्, "water on the fruit" — and you name the job. The answers are in §19's key.

**Dictation** is §18 exercise 7: six words and two sentences, read from the audio.

**Narrow note — /r̩/ and /r̩ː/:** both are a syllabic r: the r itself carries the syllable,
with no vowel before or after it. /r̩ː/ is the same sound held about twice as long, as /ɑː/
is to /ɐ/. Neither is [ɾi] or [ɾiː]: the "ri" of modern Hindi speech is a regional habit,
not the target (STYLE_GUIDE §2.1).

**Narrow note — -औ and -ओः:** /ɐu̯/ starts at the /ɐ/ of अ and glides toward /u/; /oː/ is one
pure, long vowel, never English "go" [oʊ]. The ः after it is a voiceless /h/.

**Narrow note — पितः:** at the pause the vocative ends in a visarga, /pit̪ɐh/. The form
behind it is पितर्; the final र् is written ः when a pause follows. Joined forms are Chapter
4's.

1. **The syllabic r (ऋ).** Mirror: say पितृभिः slowly. For ृ the tongue tip rises toward the
   ridge behind the upper teeth and taps or nearly touches it, with voice on, and the lips
   do not round or spread. If you see the lips spread for an "i", you said "ri".
2. **The long ॠ.** Same tongue, same lips, held for two beats: पि-तॄॄ-न्. Count with a
   finger: one tap for ऋ, two for ॠ.
3. **The lips of -औ.** Mirror: say हस्तौ, कर्णौ, पादौ. The jaw opens for /ɐ/, then the lips
   round as it closes toward /u/: a visible movement. For -ओः (पित्रोः) the lips are rounded
   from the start and do not move until the breath of ः.
4. **The ओ of ओष्ठः.** Touch your lips and say ओष्ठौ: the word for "lips" starts with a
   rounded, long /oː/, and its ष्ठ curls the tongue back for /ʂ/ and stops it there for
   /ʈʰ/. The tongue leaves the roof of the mouth with a puff of air.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; these feed the quiz distractors.

1. **a-stem endings on ṛ-stems: \*पितास्य, \*पिताम्.** पिता ends in -आ, so the a-stem or
   ā-stem endings seem to fit. *Fix:* पिता is पितृ: the genitive is पितुः, the accusative
   पितरम्. The -आ of the nominative is not the stem.
2. **The strong stem swapped: \*पितारम्, \*दातरम्.** *Fix:* kinship words take -अर-
   (पितरम्, मातरम्, भ्रातरम्), agent words take -आर- (दातारम्, कर्तारम्) — and स्वसा goes
   with दाता: स्वसारम्.
3. **The masculine accusative plural on a feminine: \*मातॄन्.** *Fix:* मातॄः. The feminine
   ṛ-stem ends in -ॄः, as मतीः did in lesson 3 [REF:whitney1889 §373].
4. **The Hindi दो reflex: \*द्वौ हस्ताः.** Hindi says दो हाथ with a plural. *Fix:* द्वौ हस्तौ.
   Two takes the dual; the plural starts at three.
5. **The -े that is not a locative.** Reading द्वे फले as "two on the fruit". *Fix:* with द्वे,
   फले is the dual "two fruits"; in वृक्षे फलम् अस्ति, वृक्षे is the locative. The sentence
   decides.
6. **नमः with the accusative (the Hindi को reflex): \*मातरम् नमः.** *Fix:* मात्रे नमः — the
   noun नमः takes only the dative. The verb takes the accusative: अहम् मातरम् नमामि (Apte also
   allows मात्रे नमामि: that is not an error).

उदाहरणानि — the family at work: the plan's six model sentences, then five more.

### The six model sentences

> **मम पिता माता च गृहे स्तः।**
> (mama pitā mātā ca gṛhe staḥ.) "My father and mother are at home." — मेरे पिता और माता घर
> पर हैं।

> **पितरौ वनम् गच्छतः।**
> (pitarau vanam gacchataḥ.) "The parents go to the forest." — माता-पिता वन को जाते हैं।

> **अहम् मातरम् नमामि।**
> (aham mātaram namāmi.) "I bow to my mother." — मैं माता को प्रणाम करता हूँ।

> **हे मातः! हे पितः!**
> (he mātaḥ! he pitaḥ!) "Mother! Father!" — माँ! पिताजी!

> **एतत् मम भ्रातुः पुस्तकम्।**
> (etat mama bhrātuḥ pustakam.) "This is my brother's book." — यह मेरे भाई की पुस्तक है।

> **बालकस्य द्वौ कर्णौ स्तः।**
> (bālakasya dvau karṇau staḥ.) "The boy has two ears." — लड़के के दो कान हैं।

Three notes. In sentence 1 two singular doers joined by च, पिता and माता, take स्तः, "the two
are": two people, the dual verb (previewed, ○). In sentence 3 the one bowed to is the object of
नमामि, so मातरम् is accusative; beside it, the noun नमः takes the dative: मात्रे नमः
"homage to mother" (§12 mistake 6). In sentence 6 the Sanskrit "has" is a genitive and a
verb "are": "of the boy, two ears are", as in v3.c01.l04's genitive.

### Five more

> **व्याधः जालस्य कर्ता।**
> (vyādhaḥ jālasya kartā.) "The hunter is the maker of the net." — शिकारी जाल का बनाने वाला
> है।

> **कर्ता प्रथमायाम्, कर्म द्वितीयायाम्।**
> (kartā prathamāyām, karma dvitīyāyām.) "The doer (is) in the first case, the done-to in
> the second." — कर्ता प्रथमा में, कर्म द्वितीया में।

> **मम द्वे नयने, उभौ ओष्ठौ च।**
> (mama dve nayane, ubhau oṣṭhau ca.) "My two eyes, and both lips." — मेरी दो आँखें, और
> दोनों होंठ।

> **माता पुत्र्यै फलम् ददाति।**
> (mātā putryai phalam dadāti.) "The mother gives the daughter a fruit." — माँ बेटी को फल
> देती है।

> **भ्रातरः ग्रामम् गच्छन्ति।**
> (bhrātaraḥ grāmam gacchanti.) "The brothers go to the village." — भाई गाँव जाते हैं।

The second line is the role names of Chapter 1 now inflected: कर्ता is today's headword in
the nominative, and प्रथमायाम्, द्वितीयायाम् are the locatives of the ā-stem case names, as

plural, a strong cell: भ्रातरः with -अर-, and the plural partner गच्छन्ति of lesson 1.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** (1) ṛ-stems (पितृ, मातृ, दातृ, स्वसृ) have a strong stem in the five
> strong cases (nominative and accusative singular and dual, nominative plural): -अर्- in

> nominative-accusative-vocative; instrumental-dative-ablative in -भ्याम्; genitive-locative

## 16. Conversation

सम्भाषणम् — one scene: Arjuna visits Rāma and shows him a photograph of his family. Turns 1–7
are about the photograph; then Rāma's mother comes in. Twelve turns, pause form; audio records
into the Chapter 2 dialogue pack (§27).

— **अर्जुनः** — पश्य! एषः मम पिता। एषा मम माता।
(paśya! eṣaḥ mama pitā. eṣā mama mātā.) "Look! This is my father. This is my mother." —
देखो! ये मेरे पिता हैं। ये मेरी माता हैं।

— **रामः** — तव पितरौ कुत्र निवसतः?
(tava pitarau kutra nivasataḥ?) "Where do your parents live?" — तुम्हारे माता-पिता कहाँ
रहते हैं?

— **अर्जुनः** — ग्रामे। मम पिता गुरुः।
(grāme. mama pitā guruḥ.) "In the village. My father is a teacher." — गाँव में। मेरे पिता
अध्यापक हैं।

— **रामः** — तव भ्राता अपि अस्ति?
(tava bhrātā api asti?) "Do you have a brother too?" — क्या तुम्हारा भाई भी है?

— **अर्जुनः** — आम्। मम द्वौ भ्रातरौ स्तः। एका स्वसा अपि अस्ति।
(ām. mama dvau bhrātarau staḥ. ekā svasā api asti.) "Yes. I have two brothers. There is one
sister too." — हाँ। मेरे दो भाई हैं। एक बहन भी है।

— **रामः** — तव स्वसुः नाम किम्?
(tava svasuḥ nāma kim?) "What is your sister's name?" — तुम्हारी बहन का नाम क्या है?

— **अर्जुनः** — तस्याः नाम सीता।
(tasyāḥ nāma sītā.) "Her name is Sītā." — उसका नाम सीता है।

— **रामः** — हे मातः! एषः अर्जुनः, मम मित्रम्।
(he mātaḥ! eṣaḥ arjunaḥ, mama mitram.) "Mother! This is Arjuna, my friend." — माँ! यह अर्जुन

(svāgatam arjuna!) "Welcome, Arjuna!" — स्वागत है, अर्जुन!

— **अर्जुनः** — मातः, नमस्ते!
(mātaḥ, namaste!) "Mother, namaste!" — माँ जी, नमस्ते!

(tava gṛhe sarvam kuśalam?) "Is all well at your home?" — तुम्हारे घर में सब कुशल है?

(ām, sarvam kuśalam. dhanyavādaḥ.) "Yes, all is well. Thank you." — हाँ, सब कुशल है।
धन्यवाद।

   (mama pitarau api jāle staḥ!) "'My parents are in the net too!'" — मेरे माता-पिता भी जाल
   में हैं!

   (vyādhaḥ vṛkṣasya samīpe hasati.) "The hunter laughs near the tree." — शिकारी पेड़ के पास
   हँसता है।
8. व्याधः तण्डुलानाम् दाता न, कपोतानाम् शत्रुः।
   (vyādhaḥ taṇḍulānām dātā na, kapotānām śatruḥ.) "The hunter is no giver of grains — he is
   the doves' enemy." — शिकारी चावलों का दाता नहीं, कबूतरों का शत्रु है।
9. तरौ मुक्तः कपोतः अस्ति। तस्य नयने नृपम् पश्यतः।

10. नृपः कपोतान् पश्यति। सः किम् वदति?

The quiet craft notes. Lines 1 and 4 put the owned adjective बद्धः "bound" after its noun as a
predicate, agreeing in number: बद्धाः with many doves, बद्धौ with two feet — the dual of an
a-adjective is -औ, as रामौ. Line 3 counts the feet with द्वौ, line 4 says "both" with उभौ:
the two words of today side by side. Line 5 is the calling form twice, line 6 the title word
in its sentence: the young dove's पितरौ are its father and mother (§4). Line 8 is lesson 3's
irony turned round: in lesson 3 the doves called the hunter good, a giver of grains to guests;
here दाता, today's headword, is denied him. In line 9 the eyes are नयने, the dual of
today's नयनम्, and they are the doer of पश्यतः, "the two see" (○). Line 10 hands the king's
speech to Chapter 3, where the verb gets its commands.

## 22. Teacher Notes

अध्यापकटिप्पणी

**Open with a photograph.** Bring, or draw, a family photograph: a father, a mother, two
children. Ask the class for the two owned sentences, एषः मम पिता। एषा मम माता।, then for "my
parents". Write पितरौ under the two parents and wait. Someone will say "but that's पिता"; you
have your lesson.

**Timing plan (45 min):** Station 0, two items, scores logged (8) → steps 1–5, the scene, the
question and the evidence table built from the class's memory of मातृ, मातः, अयोगवाहौ, स्तः
(6), the pattern named and the sūtra read once (4), T5 assembled in pairs and checked (4),
the dual card row by row (3) — 17 in all → drills: §9 drills 1–3 (4), exercise 2 on the
body pairs, said aloud (5), exercise 1 a–f (4) — 13 → the story read twice, the class reading
the second time (7). The bookings are read aloud as the story ends. Exercise 1 g–j, exercise 2
written out in full, and exercises 3, 4, 6 and 7 go home as §21 item 1; exercise 5 is §21
item 2.

**Board work:** T5 with the five strong cells in a second colour and the -अर्- / -आर्- split
boxed; the bold मातॄः. To the right, the dual card with its two right-hand columns shaded:
"the same in every row". Under both, the Hindi row: दो हाथ → द्वौ हस्तौ.

**The sūtra, for you.** 1.2.70 पिता मात्रा stands in the एकशेष ("one remains") group headed
by 1.2.64 सरूपाणामेकशेष एकविभक्तौ, *sarūpāṇām ekaśeṣa ekavibhaktau* — of words of the same
form in the same case, one remains [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.2.64] [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.2.70]. Do not
present the group; the learner gets one sūtra, attributed. The compound मातापितरौ, with its
first member माता- by 6.3.25 आनङ् ऋतो द्वन्द्वे [REF:ashtadhyayi 6.3.25], is v3.c05.l04's:
do not write it on the board today, even if a student knows it. If a student offers it, praise
it and park it.

**Strong and weak.** Whitney's term is "the strong cases" [REF:whitney1889 §311]; the course
says "strong cells" and does not name Whitney's further split of the weak cases into
"weakest" and "middle"; today the ear test of §9 drill 2 is enough.

**बद्धौ is an adjective.** The story's बद्धाः and बद्धौ are the owned predicate adjective
बद्धः of v2.c06.l02, agreeing in number. The -त participle used as a verb with its doer in
the instrumental is next lesson's recognition card; do not open it here.

**पितरः "the ancestors".** One line if asked: the plural पितरः can mean "the fathers" or "the
ancestors". No ritual content.

**The serial — departure.** The Hitopadeśa says only that all the doves were bound in the net
(*te sarve jāla-nibaddhā babhūvuḥ*); the king falling last, his two feet in the snare, and the
young dove's parents are ours [REF:hitopadesha mitralābha]. In the text the king is the one
who warned; in ours the free dove of episode 1 warned (lessons 1 and 3). The departures are on
the record for v3.c06.l02, which reads the text itself.

**Pronunciation, not a §12 row.** The commonest slip is ॠ said "rī", and ऋ "ri". Correct it
by ear (§9 drill 1, §11 checks 1–2), never by mocking a regional habit.

**Differentiation.** Hindi-medium learners know माता, पिता, दाता, कर्ता already: use that, and
then show them that their Hindi word is a Sanskrit nominative, not the dictionary form. Their
hard point is दो + plural (exercise 4). English speakers find the dual itself strange and the
ṛ-stems easy once the strong stem is heard; give them exercise 2 twice. Script-side learners
build the tables first and say them second.

**What students find hard:** -अर्- against -आर्- (they want पितारम्); मातॄः against पितॄन्;
the -े of the dual taken for a locative; and नमः with मातरम्. For the last, write both frames
one above the other: अहम् मातरम् नमामि। · मात्रे नमः। — and never mark मात्रे नमामि wrong.

**What not to teach:** the dual verb as a pattern (c03.l01); the oblique duals as production;
मातापितरौ; the neuter ṛ-stems, नृ and नप्तृ (v4.c05); the एकशेष group; the plural of respect.
One line each where the lesson meets them; nothing more.

**Hindi.** The tatsamas माता, पिता, दाता, कर्ता: Hindi uses them as everyday or formal words;
भ्राता is literary (everyday भाई). Hindi school grammar's use of कर्ता as a कारक name is the
same word [REF:guru1952].
