## 1. Hook

Since Volume 2 you have bowed with गुरवे नमः "homage to the teacher" — and v2.c01.l01 warned
you not to build "X-वे नमः" for every noun you own. रामाय नमः, yes; \*रामवे, no. So what is
गुरवे? If गुरु took the -आय of रामाय, it would be \*गुर्वाय. It doesn't. Something happens to
the last vowel of गुरु, and the same thing happens to अग्नि "fire": अग्नये, "to the fire".
Today that something gets a table — and with it the night (रात्रौ), the guest at the door
(अतिथिः), and the enemy in the serial (शत्रुः).

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — where the serial's birds come from, and the one word of today's card
that the source itself uses.

The story in §17 is part 3 of the serial's second episode. Its source frame is old, and our
retelling says so on every page.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** The serial is our retelling after the frame story of the first
> book of the Hitopadeśa, the *Mitralābha* ("the gaining of friends"), a collection of
> fables in prose and verse attributed to Nārāyaṇa and dated, on the usual scholarly
> estimate, to roughly the 9th–12th centuries CE. The frame describes a great tree on a
> river bank, and says of it, in the joined spelling the text prints, *rātrau pakṣiṇo
> nivasanti*: "at night birds dwell (there)", having come from many lands.
> [REF:hitopadesha mitralābha]

Three of today's words stand in that one clause. *rātrau* is रात्रौ "at night", the
locative of the रात्रिः you own — today's table makes it. *nivasanti* is the plural of the
owned निवसति, lesson 1's -अन्ति. And *pakṣiṇo* is पक्षिणः "birds" in its joined spelling:
the word that looks like an i-stem and is not one, today's recognition card (§15 Step 7).
The text's tree is also called a *taruḥ*, तरुः, one of today's headwords.

Our retelling takes that clause and moves it: in §17 the free dove of episode 1 says it as
a warning to the flock. In the Hitopadeśa it is the narrator's description of the tree;
§22 records the change.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — one vowel, two grades, and why a table of i- and u-stems is shorter
to learn than it looks.

Lay three cells of गुरुः side by side: गुरुम्, गुरवे, गुरोः. The stem is गुरु. In the first
cell the उ stands as it is. In the second, the same position holds अव्; in the third, ओ.
That is not three different stems: it is one stem whose last vowel has a plain grade and a
strengthened grade, and the endings choose between them.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** In the masculine i- and u-stems the final vowel of the stem appears in
> a strengthened form before certain endings: इ as ए and उ as ओ before the -ः of the
> ablative-genitive singular (अग्नेः, गुरोः) and in the vocative singular (अग्ने, गुरो); and
> as अय्, अव् before the vowel of the dative singular (अग्नये, गुरवे) and of the nominative
> plural (अग्नयः, गुरवः). Before the other endings the stem vowel stays plain (अग्निम्,
> गुरुभिः), or is lengthened (अग्नीन्, गुरून्). The locative singular ends in -औ for both.
> [REF:whitney1889 §336] [REF:whitney1889 §338] [REF:whitney1889 §339] [REF:whitney1889 §341]

Why अय् and अव्, not ए and ओ, before a vowel? Before a vowel ending the strengthened vowel
is written with its glide: ओ + -ए gives अव् + ए, गुरवे; ए + -ए gives अय् + ए, अग्नये. Chapter
4 shows the same change at the seams between words. Grammar has a name for this
strengthening, गुणः (guṇaḥ); the course takes it up in v3.c04.l01.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The ablative and genitive singular of the i- and u-stems are one form:
> अग्नेः is "from the fire" and "of the fire", गुरोः "from the teacher" and "of the
> teacher". Only the sentence decides. The a-stem keeps them apart (रामात्, रामस्य).
> [REF:whitney1889 §336]

So the load is smaller than sixteen cells per noun suggests. Learn the plain stem, the
strengthened stem, the -औ, and the lengthened plural cells, and the rest are endings you
already own: -म्, -भिः, -भ्यः, -सु (here -षु).

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spacing the retrieval of a thing over days helps it last: today's
> Station 0 is two earlier bookings landing on schedule, and this lesson books two of its
> own (§20). [REF:cepeda2006]

And the course principle that today pays off, stated with its one source: formulaic
chunks are taught whole and analysed later [REF:nation2013]. गुरवे नमः was a chunk in
Volume 2; today it is analysed.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the pattern, said in Sanskrit you own, pause form:

> **कस्मै नमः? — गुरवे।**
> (kasmai namaḥ? — gurave.) "Homage to whom? — To the teacher." — किसको नमस्कार? — गुरु
> को।

> **कपिः कस्मात् फलानि गृह्णाति? — तरोः।**
> (kapiḥ kasmāt phalāni gṛhṇāti? — taroḥ.) "From what does the monkey take fruits? — From
> the tree." — बन्दर किससे फल लेता है? — पेड़ से।

> **कस्य पुस्तकम्? — गुरोः।**
> (kasya pustakam? — guroḥ.) "Whose book? — The teacher's." — किसकी पुस्तक? — गुरु की।

> **अग्निः इकारान्तः। गुरुः उकारान्तः।**
> (agniḥ ikārāntaḥ. guruḥ ukārāntaḥ.) "अग्निः ends in i. गुरुः ends in u." — अग्नि
> इकारान्त है। गुरु उकारान्त है।

The first three lines are the c01 askers, कस्मै, कस्मात्, कस्य, answered with today's
cells. Look at the last two answers: तरोः and गुरोः have one ending, and कस्मात् and कस्य
ask two different questions — one form, two jobs. The last line gives the tradition's
names (संज्ञा) for the two stem classes: इकारान्तः "ending in इ", उकारान्तः "ending in उ".
They are grammar terms, not ledger words.

हिन्दी में समझिए

हिन्दी में एकवचन "गुरु" परसर्ग से पहले नहीं बदलता (बहुवचन में परसर्ग से पहले वह गुरुओं
बनता है): गुरु को, गुरु से, गुरु का, गुरु के साथ — शब्द वही रहता है,
काम बदलता है तो बस परसर्ग (को, से, का) बदलता है। संस्कृत में काम बदलने पर शब्द का अपना
अन्त बदलता है। अब तक आपने यह अकारान्त शब्दों में देखा — रामः, रामम्, रामेण, रामाय। आज
दो नए वर्ग आते हैं: इकारान्त शब्द (जिनका मूल रूप इ पर समाप्त होता है, जैसे अग्नि, कवि, कपि)
और उकारान्त शब्द (जिनका मूल रूप उ पर समाप्त होता है, जैसे गुरु, शत्रु, वायु)।

सबसे बड़ी बात एक ही है: कुछ रूपों से पहले अन्तिम स्वर "बलवान्" हो जाता है। इ बदलकर ए
बनता है और उ बदलकर ओ। गुरोः — "गुरु का" या "गुरु से"; अग्नेः — "आग का" या "आग से"।
सम्बोधन में भी यही: हे गुरो! हे अग्ने! और जब आगे स्वर से शुरू होने वाला अन्त जुड़ता है,
तो ए का अय् और ओ का अव् हो जाता है: गुरवे — "गुरु को", अग्नये — "आग को"; प्रथमा
बहुवचन में गुरवः — "गुरु लोग", अग्नयः। यही गुरवे नमः का रहस्य है जो आप दूसरे खण्ड से
बोलते आ रहे हैं। इस बलवान् होने का शास्त्रीय नाम गुण है; उसे चौथे अध्याय में नाम से पढ़ेंगे।

सप्तमी (स्थान बताने वाली विभक्ति) इन दोनों वर्गों में -औ पर समाप्त होती है: अग्नौ — "आग
में", गुरौ — "गुरु में", और रात्रौ — "रात में, रात को"। दूसरे खण्ड में "रात को" कहने का
रूप नहीं था; आज मिल गया।

तृतीया में -ना जुड़ता है: अग्निना, अतिथिना। पर गुरुणा, शत्रुणा, तरुणा में ण है — वही
देखी हुई वर्तनी जो रामेण में थी: शब्द में पहले र, ऋ या ष हो और बीच में कोई रोकने वाला
व्यञ्जन न हो, तो न का ण। बहुवचन में द्वितीया का स्वर लम्बा हो जाता है: गुरून्, अग्नीन्।

स्त्रीलिंग शब्द (मति "बुद्धि, विचार", धेनु "गाय") लगभग पुल्लिंग की तरह चलते हैं। केवल
दो जगह अन्तर याद रखिए: तृतीया एकवचन में -ना नहीं — मत्या, धेन्वा; और द्वितीया बहुवचन
में -न् नहीं, विसर्ग — मतीः, धेनूः। तीन रूप ऐसे हैं जिनके दो-दो रूप बराबर सही हैं: मतये
या मत्यै, मतेः या मत्याः, मतौ या मत्याम्। परीक्षा में दोनों में से कोई भी लिखें, दोनों सही हैं।

बहु ("बहुत") भी उकारान्त है और अपनी संज्ञा से मेल खाता है: बहु जलम् "बहुत पानी", बहूनि
फलानि "बहुत-से फल", बहवः वृक्षाः "बहुत-से पेड़"। और "से बढ़कर" के लिए संस्कृत पञ्चमी
रखती है: बुद्धिः बलात् श्रेष्ठा — "बुद्धि बल से श्रेष्ठ है"। यहाँ कोई कहीं से अलग नहीं होता;
पञ्चमी तुलना का काम करती है।

अन्त में एक धोखेबाज़ शब्द: पक्षी "चिड़िया"। प्रथमा में यह सुखी जैसा दिखता है, विसर्ग
के बिना — पर इसका मूल रूप पक्षिन् है, इकारान्त नहीं। इसलिए पक्षिणः "चिड़ियाँ" या "चिड़िया
का", पक्षिणा "चिड़िया से"। आज इसके सात रूप केवल पहचानने हैं, बनाने नहीं; पूरा वर्ग चौथे
खण्ड में आएगा। सब कुछ आज भी विराम-रूप (शब्द अलग-अलग, बिना जोड़े) में है।

## 8. English Explanation

No new sound, no new sign: the lesson's load is one table (T4) for two stem classes, a
feminine Δ card, one adjective that agrees, one new use of the ablative, and one look-alike
to recognise. The recipe is the volume's — a scene, the question, the forms you already
own, the pattern named, your table, production.

### Step 1 — the scene

A teacher, a guest, a fire at night. Two sentences, both readable now:

> **गुरवे नमः।**
> (gurave namaḥ.) "Homage to the teacher." — गुरु को नमस्कार।

> **रात्रौ अग्निः अस्ति।**
> (rātrau agniḥ asti.) "At night there is a fire." — रात को आग है।

You know every word in them. You do not yet know two of the shapes: the -वे of गुरवे and
the -औ of रात्रौ.

### Step 2 — the question

गुरुः is a noun in -ः, as रामः is. रामः gives रामाय "to Rāma". So गुरु + -आय would give
\*गुर्वाय. Why गुरवे? And रामः gives रामे "in Rāma", वृक्षः gives वृक्षे "in the tree". So
"in the night" should end in -े. Where is the -े of रात्रौ?

### Step 3 — the evidence you already own

Before any rule, look at what is already yours. Cover the right-hand column and say what
each form tells you; then uncover and compare.

Read the right-hand column down. Two cells look like रामः's (अग्निः, अग्निम्). Three show
the same move: the last vowel of the stem turns into a vowel and a glide — गुरवे from गुरु
and बहवः from बहु (उ → अव्), विभक्तयः from विभक्ति (इ → अय्). That is the answer to the first
half of Step 2. The last row is a warning: सुखिनः looks like a plural of an i-word, and it is a
plural — but of a different class, as §15 Step 7 shows.

### Step 4 — the pattern, named

**Two stem classes, one table.** A noun whose stem ends in इ is इकारान्तः (ikārāntaḥ),
"i-final": अग्नि, कवि, कपि, अतिथि. A noun whose stem ends in उ is उकारान्तः (ukārāntaḥ),
"u-final": गुरु, शत्रु, तरु, साधु. They decline so closely alike that one table, T4, holds
both.

**The strengthening.** In the dative, the ablative-genitive and the vocative singular, and
in the nominative (= vocative) plural, the stem vowel is strengthened: इ → ए (written अय्
before a vowel), उ → ओ (written अव् before a vowel).

| cell | i-side | u-side | the stem vowel |
|---|---|---|---|
| dative sg. | अग्नये | गुरवे | अय् · अव् + -ए |
| ablative = genitive sg. | अग्नेः | गुरोः | ए · ओ + -ः |
| vocative sg. | हे अग्ने | हे गुरो | ए · ओ, no ending |
| nominative (= vocative) pl. | अग्नयः | गुरवः | अय् · अव् + -अः |

The tradition calls this strengthening गुणः (guṇaḥ); the course takes up that name in
v3.c04.l01, where it joins words as well as endings. For now it is enough to say "the
vowel strengthens".

**The locative.** The locative singular is **-औ**, and it replaces the stem vowel: अग्नौ,
गुरौ, रात्रौ. That answers the second half of Step 2: there is no -े in रात्रौ because the
i-stems do not use it.

**Three more marks.** The instrumental singular puts -न- before -आ: अग्निना, and, with the
ṇ you know from रामेण, गुरुणा (§15 Step 3). The accusative plural lengthens the vowel and
adds -न्: अग्नीन्, गुरून्. The genitive plural lengthens it and adds -नाम्: अग्नीनाम्,
गुरूणाम्. Everything else is endings you own from T2: -भिः, -भ्यः, -षु.

So §3's prompt 2 is answered: "with the teacher" is **गुरुणा सह**.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — no new sounds; the work is the strengthened vowels heard clearly, the
diphthong -औ kept apart from the long ओ, and the glides said as glides. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-3/v3c02l03-endings-drill.mp3` (drills 1–4, the listening
pairs and the dictation).

**Drill 1 — the strengthened vowels.** Say the singular of गुरुः slowly, one cell per
breath, and listen to the vowel after र: गुरुः — गुरुम् — गुरुणा — गुरवे — गुरोः — गुरौ —
गुरो. Short उ, short उ, short उ, then अ + व, then long ओ, then the diphthong औ, then long
ओ again. Then the i-side: अग्निः — अग्निम् — अग्निना — अग्नये — अग्नेः — अग्नौ — अग्ने.

**Drill 2 — गुरौ against गुरो.** This is the pair that costs marks. गुरो (the vocative) ends
in ओ, one pure long vowel, the lips rounded and still from start to end — never English
"go". गुरौ (the locative) ends in औ, a glide: it starts on the open अ of /ɐ/ and closes
toward उ. Say them as a pair five times: गुरो — गुरौ. Then the i-side: अग्ने — अग्नौ (ए is
pure; औ glides).

**Drill 3 — the listening pairs.** Eight pairs from the track, each played twice. For
each pair, played in the order printed, write the case and number of the second word (key in
§19): गुरवे / गुरोः · गुरौ / गुरो · अग्नये / अग्नेः ·
अग्नौ / अग्ने · गुरवः / गुरून् · अग्नीन् / अग्निभिः · मत्या / मतीः · पक्षी / पक्षिणः. The
traps: in अग्नीन् the ई is long and the word ends on a dental nasal; in अग्निभिः the इ is
short and the word ends in a breath. मत्या is two syllables, मत्-या, with no इ in the
middle.

**Drill 4 — dictation.** Six words and two sentences, read twice at speaking speed, pause
form: गुरुणा · अग्नये · तरोः · शत्रूणाम् · अतिथौ · धेनूः; शिष्यः गुरुणा सह पठति। · रात्रौ
आकाशे ताराः सन्ति। Check the ण of गुरुणा and शत्रूणाम्, the long ऊ of शत्रूणाम् and धेनूः,
and the औ of अतिथौ and रात्रौ.

/pɐkʂiɳɐh/ · सुखिनः /sukʰin̪ɐh/.

**Narrow note — ओ against औ:** /oː/ is one long rounded mid-back vowel, held steady; /ɐu̯/
starts at the near-open central /ɐ/ and glides toward /u/. In गुरो the jaw does not move
during the vowel; in गुरौ it closes.

**Narrow note — the glides:** in गुरवे the व is the labiodental approximant /ʋ/, a full
consonant that opens the syllable /ʋeː/; in अग्नये the य is /j/, opening /jeː/. Neither is
a vowel: गु-र-वे, three syllables; अग्-न-ये, three syllables.

**Narrow note — length decides:** अग्नीन् has /iː/, अग्निभिः has /i/; गुरून् has /uː/,
गुरुभिः has /u/. Long is about twice the duration of short.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — four checks, one mirror. The strengthening ladder
(`planned:assets/images/volume-3/v3c02l03-strengthening-ladder.svg`) prints a small mouth
icon beside each rung: rounded and still for ओ, opening-then-closing for औ.

1. **The still-lips check (गुरो).** Mirror: say ओ and hold it for two beats. The rounded
   lips must not move. If they close further toward the end, you said English "go".
2. **The glide check (गुरौ).** Mirror: start with the jaw half-open for /ɐ/, then let it
   close and the lips round toward उ, in one movement. You should see the jaw rise.
3. **The lip check (गुरवे).** For व the lower lip rises close to the upper teeth but does
   not press; air flows quietly, no buzz. Then the tongue moves to a pure ए.
4. **The curl check (गुरुणा).** Say र and keep the tongue tip curled back through the उ;
   the nasal comes out retroflex, /ɳ/, as in रामेण. In अग्निना nothing curls the tongue:
   the tip touches the upper teeth for /n̪/.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; these feed the quiz distractors.

1. **a-stem endings on i- and u-stems: \*गुरुस्य, \*अग्निस्य, \*गुर्वाय.** The T1 habit
   carried over. *Fix:* the genitive is गुरोः, अग्नेः; the dative is गुरवे. Only the
   a-stem has -स्य and -आय.
2. **The ṇ wrong: \*गुरुना, \*अग्निणा.** *Fix:* look for र or ष earlier in the word, with
   nothing blocking: गुरुणा (र before). No trigger: न — अग्निना.
3. **The feminine accusative plural on a masculine, or the reverse: \*गुरूः, \*मतीन्.**
   *Fix:* masculine -ईन्/-ऊन् (गुरून्, अग्नीन्); feminine -ईः/-ऊः (मतीः, धेनूः).
4. **Strengthening forgotten: \*गुरुए, or गुरुः for "of the teacher".** *Fix:* the dative
   is गुरवे, the genitive गुरोः — the vowel strengthens in these cells.
5. **गुरोः read as a plural, because it ends in ः.** *Fix:* the nominative plural is
   गुरवः; गुरोः is "of the teacher" or "from the teacher", one teacher.
6. **The Hindi को reflex with नमः: "गुरु को नमस्कार" → \*गुरुम् नमः.** *Fix:* नमः takes the
   dative, गुरवे — the rule of Pāṇini's you met in v3.c01.l03.

### Step 7 — the R1 card: the -इन् look-alike पक्षी

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** पक्षी "bird" is not an i-stem. Its stem is पक्षिन्, a stem in -इन्.
> The nominative singular पक्षी has a long ई and no ः, and before vowel endings the -इन्-
> shows up (पक्षिणम्, पक्षिणा, पक्षिणः), with ṇ because ष comes earlier and only the vowel इ
> stands between. [REF:whitney1889 §440]

Recognition only: read and parse these seven cells when you meet them; do not produce them.

| cell | form | reads as |
|---|---|---|
| nom. sg. | पक्षी pakṣī | "a bird" (doer) |
| acc. sg. | पक्षिणम् pakṣiṇam | "a bird" (done-to) |
| instr. sg. | पक्षिणा pakṣiṇā | "with a bird" |
| abl./gen. sg. **and** nom./acc. pl. | पक्षिणः pakṣiṇaḥ | "from / of a bird" or "birds" — the sentence decides |
| loc. sg. | पक्षिणि pakṣiṇi | "on a bird" |
| instr. pl. | पक्षिभिः pakṣibhiḥ | "with birds" |
| gen. pl. | पक्षिणाम् pakṣiṇām | "of birds" |

The owned सुखी "happy" is the same kind of word: सर्वे भवन्तु सुखिनः "may all be happy" —
सुखिनः is its nominative plural, with न (no र or ष in सुखी). It looks like an i-stem in the
nominative singular; the -इन् class as a class is v4.c05's.

## 16. Conversation

सम्भाषणम् — a student and his teacher; a guest at the door. Twelve turns, pause form.
Audio: 🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-3/v3c02l03-guest-roleplay.mp3`.

— **शिष्यः** — हे गुरो! गृहे एकः अतिथिः अस्ति।
(he guro! gṛhe ekaḥ atithiḥ asti.) "Teacher! There is a guest in the house." — गुरुजी! घर
में एक अतिथि है।

— **गुरुः** — कः अतिथिः?
(kaḥ atithiḥ?) "Which guest?" — कौन-सा अतिथि?

— **शिष्यः** — एकः साधुः। सः ग्रामात् आगच्छति।
(ekaḥ sādhuḥ. saḥ grāmāt āgacchati.) "A holy man. He comes from the village." — एक साधु।
वह गाँव से आ रहा है।

— **गुरुः** — भवान् अतिथये जलम् भोजनम् च ददातु।
(bhavān atithaye jalam bhojanam ca dadātu.) "Please give the guest water and food." —
कृपया अतिथि को पानी और भोजन दीजिए।

— **शिष्यः** — अस्तु। अतिथिः क्षीरम् अपि इच्छति?
(astu. atithiḥ kṣīram api icchati?) "All right. Does the guest want milk too?" — ठीक है।
क्या अतिथि दूध भी चाहता है?

— **गुरुः** — आम्। गृहे धेनुः अस्ति। धेनुः बहु क्षीरम् ददाति।
(ām. gṛhe dhenuḥ asti. dhenuḥ bahu kṣīram dadāti.) "Yes. There is a cow at home. The cow
gives a lot of milk." — हाँ। घर में गाय है। गाय बहुत दूध देती है।

— **शिष्यः** — हे गुरो! अतिथिः रात्रौ कुत्र निवसति?
(he guro! atithiḥ rātrau kutra nivasati?) "Teacher! Where will the guest stay at night?" —
गुरुजी! अतिथि रात को कहाँ ठहरेगा?

— **गुरुः** — अत्र, अग्नेः समीपे।
(atra, agneḥ samīpe.) "Here, near the fire." — यहीं, आग के पास।

— **शिष्यः** — रात्रौ अग्निः साधुः।
(rātrau agniḥ sādhuḥ.) "At night a fire is good." — रात को आग अच्छी होती है।

— **गुरुः** — आम्। अतिथिः अपि गुरुः।
(ām. atithiḥ api guruḥ.) "Yes. A guest, too, is a teacher." — हाँ। अतिथि भी गुरु है।

— **शिष्यः** — शत्रुः अपि?
(śatruḥ api?) "An enemy too?" — शत्रु भी?

— **गुरुः** — गृहे शत्रुः अपि अतिथिः।
(gṛhe śatruḥ api atithiḥ.) "At home even an enemy is a guest." — घर में शत्रु भी अतिथि
है।

Housekeeping: ददातु, अस्तु, आम्, अत्र and समीपे are owned (v2.c05.l04, v2.c05.l02,
v1.c05.l01, v1.c05.l03, v3.c01.l04); ददातु is the polite request chunk, with भवान् as
v2.c05.l04 taught it (never with त्वम्). Turn 8's अग्नेः is
the genitive that समीपे asks for; turn 9 uses साधुः as an adjective agreeing with अग्निः.
Turns 10 and 12 are course sentences, written for this dialogue — no source is claimed
for either. The present निवसति in turn 7 carries the "will stay" of the English, as the
present often does in Sanskrit. Pause form between and within turns.

   doves laugh. They say: 'The hunter is good! He gives grains to guests.'" — कबूतर हँसते

   फँस जाते हैं।

   (te śatroḥ jāle santi.) "They are in the enemy's net." — वे शत्रु के जाल में हैं।
10. तरौ मुक्तः कपोतः तिष्ठति।
    (tarau muktaḥ kapotaḥ tiṣṭhati.) "In the tree the free dove stays." — पेड़ पर मुक्त
    कबूतर ठहरा रहता है।

hunter a good host who feeds his guests (अतिथिभ्यः, today's dative plural), and line 7
answers them. Line 8's बद्धाः agrees with कपोताः, lesson 1's agreement in number. The
tree is तरुः throughout, the Hitopadeśa's word (§4, §14).

## 22. Teacher Notes

अध्यापकटिप्पणी

**Open with the bow.** Write गुरवे नमः on the board and under it रामाय नमः. Ask the class
to make the guru-line the way the Rāma-line is made: someone will offer \*गुर्वाय. Write it
with a star, and ask what the Volume 2 bow actually says. The gap between \*गुर्वाय and
गुरवे is the lesson.

**Timing plan (45 min):** Station 0, two items, scores logged (8) → steps 1–5: the scene,
the question, the evidence table from the class's memory, the pattern named on the ladder,
the table assembled in pairs and checked against §15, then the feminine card (17) →
drills: §9 drills 1–3, exercise 1 aloud, a short round of exercise 3 and of exercise 6
(13) → the story read twice, the craft notes (7). If the drills overrun, exercise 4 (R1)
moves to homework.

**Board work:** the strengthening ladder (§15 Step 2) on the left, the stem at the bottom,
the four strengthened cells in a second colour and the -औ cell boxed. On the right, T4 as
the class fills it, the ṇ cells of गुरुः boxed. Under both, the feminine Δ in one line:
मत्या · मतीः · (मतये/मत्यै · मतेः/मत्याः · मतौ/मत्याम्).

**The two-way cells.** Whitney's wording is "at will": "The later grammar allows the dat.,
abl.-gen., and loc. fem. to be formed at will with the fuller fem. terminations"
[REF:whitney1889 §336]. His गति and धेनु columns print both forms in each of the three
cells [REF:whitney1889 §339] [REF:whitney1889 §341]. So: never mark मत्यै, मत्याः,
मत्याम्, रात्र्याम् wrong, and never mark मतये, मतेः, मतौ wrong. Make no claim about which
is more frequent; the course drills the masculine-like form first only because it is the
one the learner already has from अग्निः.

**The strengthening has a name.** It is guṇa (गुणः); the course names it in v3.c04.l01,
where it returns at the seams between words, and gives no sūtra for it here. Say
"strengthened" today.

**"Than" and the genitive.** Monier-Williams gives श्रेष्ठ "better than (abl. or gen.)"
[REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. śreṣṭha]; Apte has "Best, most excellent, pre-eminent (with gen. or
loc.)" [REF:apte1890 s.v. श्रेष्ठ]. The course drills the ablative only; if a learner writes
बलस्य श्रेष्ठा, do not mark it wrong, and say that the course's frame is बलात्. Quiz Q9 does
not offer the genitive for that reason. The ablative of comparison is in Whitney's list of
special ablatives [REF:whitney1889 §292].

**The R1 card is a watch item.** It stays in this lesson by the volume plan's decision;
if the class is overloaded, exercise 4 moves to homework and §17 line 2's पक्षिणः stays a
labelled gloss. The likely R1 slip is \*पक्षिनः: the ष of पक्ष् is a trigger and only the
vowel इ stands between it and the nasal, so the spelling is पक्षिणः. Do not take this into
§12; it is a recognition card.

**The sūtras behind §12 and §13.** Mistake 6 rests on the sūtra quoted in v3.c01.l03, 2.3.16
नमःस्वस्तिस्वाहास्वधालम्वषड्योगाच्च [REF:ashtadhyayi 2.3.16]; in learner text this chapter
says "the rule of Pāṇini's" without the number. शिष्यः गुरुणा सह पठति is the company frame
of 2.3.19 [REF:ashtadhyayi 2.3.19]. सह after its partner is this course's practice order,
not a rule of Sanskrit. The ṇ columns rest on 8.4.1 and 8.4.2, kept for v4.c02
[REF:ashtadhyayi 8.4.1] [REF:ashtadhyayi 8.4.2].

**The feminine of u-adjectives.** बह्वी is one line in §15 Step 5. §15 also carries one
learner line with a back-link to v2.c06.l02: the she-form of लघुः, लघुः or लघ्वी, is
re-addressed to v4.c05 [REF:whitney1889 §344]. Do not drill either.

**What not to teach:** the neuter i- and u-nouns (मधु, वारि: v4.c05); पतिः and सखा, which are
irregular (सखा whole in v3.c05.l01; the rest v4.c05); the dual (अग्नी, गुरू: lesson 4); the
-इन् class as a class (v4.c05); the name गुणः as a rule (v3.c04.l01). One line each where
the lesson meets them; nothing more.

**The Ṛgveda.** अग्निम् is the Ṛgveda's first word as v2.c02.l03 printed it (§4 of that
lesson). The volume plan's अग्निमीळे is not printed: the Vedic ळ and the verb are not
owned. The accusative point stands without them.

**Differentiation.** Hindi-medium learners know the words गुरु, अतिथि, शत्रु, साधु, धेनु as
tatsamas that never change in the singular; give them exercise 5's contrast line first,
then the ladder. English speakers find the ladder easy and the गुरो/गुरौ pair hard: give
them §9 drill 2 and §11 checks 1–2 twice. Script-side learners fill the §8 Step 5 frame
before they say anything.

**What students find hard:** reading गुरोः as a plural (it ends in ः); the feminine
accusative plural (they write \*मतीन्); the two-way cells (they ask "which one is right?" —
the answer is "both"); and the ablative "than", where nothing moves.

**The serial.** In the Hitopadeśa the line *rātrau pakṣiṇo nivasanti* ("at night birds
roost") is the narrator's description of the tree, not a dove's warning, and the text's
doves are not warned from the tree [REF:hitopadesha mitralābha]. In the text the king of
the doves himself warns the flock and is overruled; our free dove of episode 1 warns
instead. The grains are तण्डुलाः, the text's *taṇḍula-kaṇān* "rice grains" without the
compound. These departures are on the record for v3.c06.l02, which reads the text itself.
