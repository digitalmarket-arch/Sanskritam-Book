## 1. Hook

Two Sanskrit sentences you can read both say "गुरु को" in Hindi: सः गुरवे पुस्तकम् ददाति
"he gives the teacher a book", and गुरवे नमः "homage to the teacher". The case is the same,
the dative. But in the first the teacher *gets* something; in the second nothing is handed
over at all. The dictionary's "to" cannot tell them apart, and neither can its "with":
व्याधः जालेन कपोतान् गृह्णाति, "with the net", is the hunter's tool, while शिष्यः गुरुणा सह
पठति, "with the teacher", is a companion. Today you draw the map that tells them apart —
six jobs, their usual cases, and the three places where case and job part company. The
title, **कारकाणि** (kārakāṇi) "the roles", is the GLOSSARY term कारकम्, in the plural: a
neuter a-stem in -आणि, and you can read it now. 411 words. Pass, and Chapter 2 is complete.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — where the six jobs come from, what modern linguists have set beside
them, and where the three thread lines come from.

Chapter 1 named the six jobs one pair at a time, as each case arrived, and said that the
tradition defines them in its own way. Here is that way, named once and left for Volume 4.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** Pāṇini defines the six kārakas in a run of sūtras
> (1.4.24–1.4.55) — one general definition for each, with further sūtras for special cases
> — and assigns their cases in another (2.3.1 ff.). Volume 4 reads them (v4.c05).
> [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.4.23]; [REF:cardona1997]

A kāraka is a job in an action, defined first; a case ending is a form, assigned
afterwards. So the two can come apart — the map of §15 — and the picture has a modern
neighbour.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** Linguists have set Pāṇini's kārakas beside the semantic roles of
> modern grammar — for example the 'deep cases' that Charles Fillmore proposed in 'The Case
> for Case' (1968) [REF:fillmore1968]. Paul Kiparsky and J. F. Staal (1969) describe
> Pāṇini's grammar as working through levels, with the kārakas standing between the meaning
> of a sentence and its case endings [REF:kiparskystaal1969]. The likeness is a parallel
> between two analyses; it is not evidence that one drew on the other.

Hold the last sentence as it stands: a parallel, not a derivation (§19 asks it). Three
lines you have carried since earlier volumes are parsed in §17; task D is the first.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** उद्यमेन हि सिध्यन्ति कार्याणि न मनोरथैः… is the Hitopadeśa,
> introduction (prastāvikā), verse 36; the collection is dated, on the scholarly estimate,
> to c. 9th–12th c. CE. अहिंसा परमः धर्मः is printed in the course from the Mahābhārata's
> critical edition, 1.11.12 (v2.c06.l03). [REF:hitopadesha prastāvikā 36];
> [REF:mahabharata 1.11.12]

The third line, सर्वे भवन्तु सुखिनः…, has no recorded source; v1.c06.l04 says so and labels
it *traditional*, and so does this lesson.

One line of course history. The 1.x book printed the 8×3 table of राम — eight cases, three
numbers — on its page 125. The 2.0 audit verified it, and this chapter restores it in §15
with the ṇ and ṣ cells marked and देवेन, देवानाम् beside रामेण, रामाणाम्.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — two levels, a form and a job, and why the exam tests both.

No new fact about Sanskrit forms enters today; every ending on the page is one the four
lessons taught. The new thing is a distinction you have been using without a name.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** A case is a form: the ending a noun wears (गुरवे, गुरुणा, मया). A role
> is a function: the job the noun does in the action (the receiver, the means, the doer).
> The two usually go together — the dative names a receiver, the instrumental a means — but
> not always. In गुरवे नमः the dative goes with the word नमः and names no receiver; in
> शिष्यः गुरुणा सह पठति the instrumental with सह names a companion, not a means; in मया
> कृतम् "done by me" the doer stands in the instrumental, because the -त form of a
> transitive verb agrees with the thing done. [REF:whitney1889 §282]; [REF:cardona1997]

So every noun has two answers, and the exam asks for both: its case, read off the ending,
and its job, read off the sentence. A table of endings gives the first; only the map of
§15 gives the second.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Retrieval practice beats re-reading for long-term retention.
> [REF:roediger2006]

Each task retrieves the chapter by a different route: A from form to form, B from noun to
adjective, C from sentence to job, D from verse to case and number, E from your own family
to whole sentences.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spaced practice, with gaps between sessions, is retained longer than
> the same practice massed together. [REF:cepeda2006]

That is why the exam opens with Station 0, which runs lesson 4's +1-day and lesson 3's
+1-week, and why §20 books two more items for Chapter 3. And task E is scored on
communication first and endings second (§18): a clear sentence with one wrong ending
scores higher than a silence.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the map in Sanskrit you own, pause form throughout:

> **कर्ता प्रथमायाम्, कर्म द्वितीयायाम्।** (kartā prathamāyām, karma dvitīyāyām.) "The doer
> (is) in the first case, the done-to in the second." — कर्ता प्रथमा में, कर्म द्वितीया में।
>
> **जालेन — तृतीया, करणम्।** (jālena — tṛtīyā, karaṇam.) "'With the net': the third case,
> the means." — जालेन — तृतीया, करण।
>
> **गुरुणा सह — तृतीया, न करणम्।** (guruṇā saha — tṛtīyā, na karaṇam.) "'With the
> teacher': the third case, not the means." — गुरुणा सह — तृतीया, पर करण नहीं।
>
> **गुरवे नमः। गुरवे — चतुर्थी, न सम्प्रदानम्।** (gurave namaḥ. gurave — caturthī, na
> sampradānam.) "Homage to the teacher. 'To the teacher': the fourth case, not the
> receiver." — गुरु को नमस्कार। गुरवे — चतुर्थी, पर सम्प्रदान नहीं।
>
> **मया कृतम्। मया — तृतीया, कर्ता।** (mayā kṛtam. mayā — tṛtīyā, kartā.) "Done by me.
> 'By me': the third case, the doer." — मेरे द्वारा किया गया। मया — तृतीया, कर्ता।
>
> **रामः रामौ रामाः — एकवचनम्, द्विवचनम्, बहुवचनम्।** (rāmaḥ rāmau rāmāḥ — ekavacanam,
> dvivacanam, bahuvacanam.) "One Rāma, two, many: singular, dual, plural." — एकवचन,
> द्विवचन, बहुवचन।

Six lines. The first is lesson 4's own line (l04 §13). The next four are the map's heart,
each said as "case, then job": one where the job matches, three where it does not (the
first three are §8 Step 1's sentences; मया कृतम् is §8 Step 4's R2 card). The last is the 8×3 table's first row, named with the three
number terms — terms you use, not ledger words.

की क्रिया में संज्ञा का काम, उसकी भूमिका) दूसरी। ज़्यादातर दोनों साथ चलते हैं: प्रथमा में
कर्ता, द्वितीया में कर्म, तृतीया में करण (साधन), चतुर्थी में सम्प्रदान (पाने वाला), पञ्चमी
में अपादान (जिससे अलग हुआ जाए), सप्तमी में अधिकरण (जगह या समय)। पर हमेशा नहीं। इस अध्याय
का आख़िरी सबक़ यही है कि विभक्ति और काम कहाँ-कहाँ अलग हो जाते हैं।

तीन जगहें याद रखिए। पहली: गुरवे नमः — "गुरु को नमस्कार"। गुरवे चतुर्थी है, पर यहाँ गुरु को
कुछ दिया नहीं जा रहा; चतुर्थी नमः शब्द के कारण आई है। इसलिए गुरवे सम्प्रदान नहीं। दूसरी:
शिष्यः गुरुणा सह पठति — "शिष्य गुरु के साथ पढ़ता है"। गुरुणा तृतीया है, पर गुरु पढ़ने का
साधन नहीं, साथी है। तीसरी: मया कृतम् — "मेरे द्वारा किया गया"। मया तृतीया है, फिर भी काम
करने वाला, यानी कर्ता, मैं ही हूँ। यहाँ कृतम् उस चीज़ से मेल खाता है जो की गई, और करने वाला
तृतीया में आता है। यह ढाँचा आज सिर्फ़ पहचानने के लिए है, बनाने के लिए नहीं।

हिन्दी यहाँ भी मदद करती है और धोखा भी देती है। "को" दोनों वाक्यों में है — "गुरु को
पुस्तक देता है" और "गुरु को नमस्कार" — पर संस्कृत में पहले वाक्य का गुरु सम्प्रदान है और
दूसरे का नहीं। "से" भी दो काम करता है — "जाल से पकड़ता है" (साधन) और "पेड़ से गिरता है"
(अलग होना)। और "के साथ" (सह के साथ तृतीया) साधन नहीं, साथ है। इसलिए हर संज्ञा के लिए दो
सवाल पूछिए: इसका रूप क्या है? और यह वाक्य में क्या कर रही है?

पाणिनि के अनुसार कारक छह ही हैं। षष्ठी (सम्बन्ध, "का, के, की") और सम्बोधन ("हे राम!")
कारक नहीं, क्योंकि ये क्रिया में किसी की भूमिका नहीं बताते। हिन्दी का स्कूली व्याकरण
सम्बन्ध और सम्बोधन को भी कारक गिनता है; यह उसकी अपनी परम्परा है। परीक्षा में ऐसी बात हमेशा
"किसके अनुसार" के साथ कहिए: "पाणिनि के अनुसार…"।

आज की परीक्षा के पाँच कार्य: A — रूप बदलना (एकवचन, द्विवचन, बहुवचन के बीच, बारह बार);
B — एक विशेषण, विशाल, को दस संज्ञाओं से मिलाना, और 3 व 4 को सही लिंग में रखना; C —
आठ वाक्यों की हर संज्ञा का रूप और काम; D — उद्यमेन हि सिध्यन्ति… श्लोक का शब्द-शब्द
विश्लेषण; E — अपने परिवार और घर के बारे में कम-से-कम छह वाक्य, रिकॉर्ड करके। कार्य E में
अंक पहले समझ के मिलते हैं, फिर शुद्धता के। छोटे, साफ़ वाक्य बोलिए; एक अन्त ग़लत हो जाए तो
रुकिए मत। सब कुछ आज भी विराम-रूप में है — हर शब्द अलग, बिना जोड़े।

### Step 4 — the pattern named

**कारकम्** (kārakam), plural **कारकाणि** (kārakāṇi), is the tradition's name for a job in
the action. There are six: **कर्ता** the doer, **कर्म** the done-to, **करणम्** the means,
**सम्प्रदानम्** the receiver, **अपादानम्** the fixed point of separation, **अधिकरणम्** the
location. Each has a usual case. Two cases name no job at all: according to Pāṇini the
genitive (सम्बन्धः, relation) and the vocative (सम्बोधनम्, address) are not kārakas. And
in three owned sentences the case and the job part company: (1) नमः takes the dative with
no receiver (गुरवे नमः); (2) सह takes the instrumental of company, not of means (गुरुणा
सह); (3) with a transitive verb's -त form the doer is in the instrumental (मया कृतम्).

**R2, the "X-ed by Y" frame — a recognition card.** बद्धः "bound", मुक्तः "freed"
(v2.c06.l02) and the Volume 1 stem कृत "done" are -त forms of verbs that take an object.
Used as the verb, such a form agrees with the thing done, and the doer goes into the
instrumental: **मया कृतम्** (mayā kṛtam) "(it is) done by me". The -त forms of a verb of
motion (गत) and of verbs with no object (क्रुद्धः, पिपासितः, सुप्त) agree with the doer itself
and are not this frame (§15 (6)), though गच्छति takes a कर्म. You read R2; you do not build
it (v4.c04).

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the exam circuit, one check per task.

- **Task A (pen):** read the instruction first (plural? dual? singular?), then the case of
  the form given, and keep that case. Before you write -आणाम्, look along the stem for र,

- **Task B (pen):** look at the noun's last letters for its gender, number and case, then

**One example for each row of the map** (the bold noun is the row's job):

> **व्याधः मृगम् पश्यति।** (vyādhaḥ mṛgam paśyati.) "The hunter sees the deer." — शिकारी
> हिरन को देखता है। *व्याधः — प्रथमा · कर्ता; मृगम् — द्वितीया · कर्म.*

> **व्याधः जालेन कपोतान् गृह्णाति।** (vyādhaḥ jālena kapotān gṛhṇāti.) "The hunter

> · करणम्, the tool.*

> **माता बालकाय फलम् ददाति।** (mātā bālakāya phalam dadāti.) "The mother gives the boy a

> **फलम् वृक्षात् पतति।** (phalam vṛkṣāt patati.) "The fruit falls from the tree." —
> फल पेड़ से गिरता है। *वृक्षात् — पञ्चमी · अपादानम्, the fixed point.*

> **कपोताः वृक्षे निवसन्ति।** (kapotāḥ vṛkṣe nivasanti.) "The doves live in the tree." —
> कबूतर पेड़ पर रहते हैं। *वृक्षे — सप्तमी · अधिकरणम्.*

> **रामस्य पुत्रः।** (rāmasya putraḥ.) "Rāma's son." — राम का बेटा। *रामस्य — षष्ठी ·
> सम्बन्धः; according to Pāṇini not a kāraka.*

> **हे राम!** (he rāma!) "Rāma!" — हे राम! *सम्बोधनम्: a call, not a job in the action.*

**The three lines where case and job part company:**

> **स्वस्ति तुभ्यम्।** (svasti tubhyam.) "Blessings to you." — तुम्हारा कल्याण हो। *तुभ्यम् —
> चतुर्थी, but no receiver: the dative goes with the word स्वस्ति.*

> **बालकः मित्रेण सह गच्छति।** (bālakaḥ mitreṇa saha gacchati.) "The boy goes with his
> friend." — लड़का दोस्त के साथ जाता है। *मित्रेण — तृतीया, but company, not करणम्.*

> **तेन उक्तम्।** (tena uktam.) "Said by him." — उसके द्वारा कहा गया। *तेन — तृतीया, and
> still the कर्ता: the R2 frame (§15 (6)); उक्तम् is display.*

**Worked aloud** (lesson 3's model sentence सः अतिथये भोजनम् ददाति "he gives food to the
guest"): सः — प्रथमा · कर्ता; अतिथये — चतुर्थी, and with ददाति something is handed over,
so सम्प्रदानम्, case and job together; भोजनम् — द्वितीया · कर्म. Compare स्वस्ति तुभ्यम्: the same
case, and no giving. Two steps for every noun — the ending, then the sentence.

### (1) The roles map

### (4) The recurring endings

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Whitney: "The endings which have almost or quite unbroken range,
> through stems of all classes, are *bhyām* and *os* of the dual, and *bhis*, *bhyas*,
> *ām*, and *su* of the plural." In Devanagari: -भ्याम्, -ओः; -भिः, -भ्यः, -आम्, -सु.
> [REF:whitney1889 §310a]

"Almost or quite": the card has exceptions, and you own them. (a) The a-stem has -ऐः, not
-भिः, in the instrumental plural: देवैः, रामैः. (b) Vowel stems put -न्- before the -आम्
of the genitive plural: देवानाम् · रामाणाम्, बालिकानाम्, नदीनाम्, अग्नीनाम्, गुरूणाम्,
पितॄणाम्. (c) The a-stem shows -ए- before -भ्यः and -सु: रामेभ्यः, रामेषु. (d) -सु is
written -षु after a vowel other than अ or आ (रामेषु, गुरुषु, नदीषु), and stays -सु after आ
(बालिकासु). Never say "six endings shared by every noun"; say "almost".

### (5) The free facts

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** (1) A neuter's nominative and accusative are always one form: फलम्,
> फले, फलानि. (2) The vocative dual and plural equal the nominative: हे रामौ, हे रामाः;
> only the singular has a vocative of its own. (3) In nouns and third-person pronouns the
> dative and ablative plural are one form: रामेभ्यः, बालिकाभ्यः, गुरुभ्यः, तेभ्यः. (4) The
> ablative and genitive singular are one form in every stem but the a-stem: अग्नेः, गुरोः,
> बालिकायाः, नद्याः, पितुः — against रामात्, रामस्य. [REF:whitney1889 §§328–330, 337–338,
> 364, 373]; [REF:whitney1889 §491]

Free fact 3 stops at nouns and third-person pronouns. अहम् and त्वम् keep the two cells
apart — अस्मभ्यम् "to us" against अस्मत् "from us", युष्मभ्यम् against युष्मत् — shown here
as display only (○, v3.c05.l01). Every free fact is a cell you do not have to learn twice.

### (6) The R2 card — "X-ed by Y" (recognition only)

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** A -त form made from a verb that takes an object (बद्धः "bound",
> मुक्तः "freed", कृत "done", उक्त "said") describes what has undergone the action. Used as
> the verb of its sentence, it agrees with the thing done, and the doer stands in the
> instrumental: मया कृतम् "(it is) done by me"; तेन उक्तम् "(it is) said by him". A -त form
> made from a verb of motion (गत "gone") or from a verb with no object (सुप्त "asleep",
> क्रुद्धः "angry", पिपासितः "thirsty") is used here in an active sense: it agrees with the
> doer itself, and it is not this frame — even though, as §15 (1) shows, गच्छति takes a
> कर्म: ग्रामम् गच्छति. [REF:whitney1889 §952]; [REF:whitney1889 §282]

| -त form | from a verb | frame | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| बद्धः · मुक्तः (owned, v2.c06.l02) · कृत (owned, Volume 1) | with an object | R2: agrees with the thing done; the doer in the instrumental | **मया कृतम्** (mayā kṛtam) "done by me" — मया owned since v3.c01.l03 |

## 16. Conversation

सम्भाषणम् — the family conversation, task E's model; read it before the sitting, unscored.
Ten turns, pause form. A teacher asks, a student answers.

— **गुरुः** — तव परिवारे कति जनाः सन्ति?
(tava parivāre kati janāḥ santi?) "How many people are in your family?" — तुम्हारे परिवार
में कितने लोग हैं?

— **छात्रा** — पञ्च जनाः। मम पितरौ, द्वौ भ्रातरौ, अहम् च।
(pañca janāḥ. mama pitarau, dvau bhrātarau, aham ca.) "Five people. My parents, two
brothers, and I." — पाँच लोग। मेरे माता-पिता, दो भाई, और मैं।

— **गुरुः** — तव पिता किम् करोति?
(tava pitā kim karoti?) "What does your father do?" — तुम्हारे पिता क्या करते हैं?

— **छात्रा** — मम पिता गुरुः। सः विद्यालयम् गच्छति।
(mama pitā guruḥ. saḥ vidyālayam gacchati.) "My father is a teacher. He goes to the
school." — मेरे पिता अध्यापक हैं। वे विद्यालय जाते हैं।

— **गुरुः** — तव माता?
(tava mātā?) "And your mother?" — और तुम्हारी माता?

— **छात्रा** — मम माता आपणे अस्ति। सा मधुराणि फलानि ददाति।
(mama mātā āpaṇe asti. sā madhurāṇi phalāni dadāti.) "My mother is at the market. She gives
(sells) sweet fruits." — मेरी माता बाज़ार में हैं। वे मीठे फल देती (बेचती) हैं।

— **गुरुः** — तव गृहम् कुत्र अस्ति?
(tava gṛham kutra asti?) "Where is your house?" — तुम्हारा घर कहाँ है?

— **छात्रा** — नद्याः तीरे। गृहस्य समीपे विशालः वृक्षः अस्ति।
(nadyāḥ tīre. gṛhasya samīpe viśālaḥ vṛkṣaḥ asti.) "On the river's bank. Near the house
there is a big tree." — नदी के किनारे पर। घर के पास एक बड़ा पेड़ है।

— **गुरुः** — वृक्षे पक्षिणः निवसन्ति?
(vṛkṣe pakṣiṇaḥ nivasanti?) "Do birds live in the tree?" — क्या पेड़ पर पक्षी रहते हैं?

— **छात्रा** — आम्। रात्रौ बहवः कपोताः तत्र निवसन्ति।
(ām. rātrau bahavaḥ kapotāḥ tatra nivasanti.) "Yes. At night many doves live there." —
हाँ। रात को बहुत-से कबूतर वहाँ रहते हैं।

Housekeeping. जनः and परिवारः are owned (v1.c03.l04, v2.c02.l01), as are करोति, आपणः, आम्
and तत्र. The scene walks the chapter: सन्ति beside a plural doer (turn 1); the duals पितरौ
and द्वौ भ्रातरौ (turn 2); पिता (turns 3–4); मधुराणि फलानि (turn 6); नद्याः and an agreeing

plural for a parent (वे … जाते हैं), as Hindi does; the Sanskrit keeps the singular for one

**The R2 card, for you.** The card is built on transitive verbs (बद्धः, मुक्तः, कृत, उक्त).
गत comes from a verb of motion, whose goal is still a कर्म (ग्रामम् गच्छति), and सुप्त, the
owned क्रुद्धः and पिपासितः from verbs with no object. In the course's sentences all four agree
with their doer. 3.4.72 lets the -त form name the doer after both kinds; with a verb of
motion the R2 frame is also possible (तेन ग्रामः गतः), but no learner-facing text of the course uses it: सुप्तस्य सिंहस्य is "of the
sleeping lion", with no hidden instrumental. बद्धः in सिंहः जाले बद्धः भवति is a transitive
-त form used as a predicate adjective (v2.c06.l02): it agrees with the one bound, and the
binder is not named. Do not teach the formation (v4.c04) or voice (v4.c05).
