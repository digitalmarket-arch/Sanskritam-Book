## 1. Hook

Since Volume 2 you have said फलम् मह्यम् रोचते "the fruit pleases me" — and that lesson
warned you that रोचते "wears the other ending family", and left it there. Yesterday you
built गच्छति in nine cells, and its he-form ends in -ति, its they-form in -न्ति.
रोचते ends in -ते. So does the verb of the motto you have owned since Volume 1,
सत्यम् एव जयते. And क्षमस्व "forgive me" ends in something stranger still. These are not
three irregular words. They are the visible corner of a second family of endings, with
its own nine cells — and today you get the table.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — two names from the tradition, one finding from modern scholarship,
and where the motto's verb comes from.

The two families of endings have names in the grammatical tradition, beside the course's
plain "first family" and "second family". They are worth having because the dictionary
uses them, in abbreviation, after the root of a verb.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** The Indian grammarians call an active form परस्मैपदम्
> (parasmaipadam) "a word for another" and a middle form आत्मनेपदम् (ātmanepadam) "a word for
> oneself". [REF:whitney1889 §529]

The names describe a difference of meaning: an action done for someone else, and an action
done for oneself. Whether that difference still decides anything is a question for the
evidence, and Whitney answers it.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Whitney calls this distinction "doubtless the original foundation of
> the difference of active and middle forms", and adds that in the recorded language the
> contrast of meaning "is in no small measure blurred, or even altogether effaced". So the
> learner does not choose the family by meaning: the dictionary's label decides — Apte
> marks लभ् "A." (ātmanepada), as he marks गम् "P." (parasmaipada). [REF:whitney1889 §529]
> [REF:apte1890 s.v. लभ्]

That is why today's lesson takes each verb with its family attached, the way a noun comes
with its gender. लभते "gets" is a second-family verb because the dictionary says so, not
because getting is something you do for yourself.

The motto you have owned since Volume 1 has a locus, and its verb is today's family.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** सत्यम् एव जयते is a line of the Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad, 3.1.6. The
> text prints it joined, with its second half: सत्यमेव जयते नानृतम् (satyameva jayate
> nānṛtam) "truth alone prevails, not untruth". [REF:mundaka 3.1.6]

The joined line is shown once, as display: नानृतम् is न "not" + अनृतम् "untruth", which
you do not need to own; the joining is Chapter 4's. The course keeps printing the part it
owns, in pause form: सत्यम् एव जयते. §15 Step 7 explains why its verb is जयते and not the
जयति a classical dictionary gives.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — one grid, two sets of endings, and why the family is learned with the
word.

Lay yesterday's table beside today's. The grid is the same: three persons, three numbers,
nine cells. What changes is only the ending in each cell.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Sanskrit has two families of personal endings that carry the same
> persons and numbers: गच्छति, गच्छसि, गच्छामि in the first; लभते, लभसे, लभे in the
> second. Some verbs are conjugated in both families, others in one only, and with some the
> family changes with a prefix [REF:whitney1889 §530]. The course teaches each verb in one
> family only, the one its §14 row gives, and you learn that family with the word, as you
> learn a noun's gender.

So the load today is one row of new endings, not a new system. The stem does not change
(लभ-), the stretch of the stem vowel before म and व is the one you met in lesson 1
(लभामहे, like गच्छामः), and the 3rd-person plural loses the stem's अ exactly as गच्छन्ति
does (लभ + अन्ते → लभन्ते).

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The passive in -यते is a different thing. It is a present stem made
> from a root with the sign -य-, inflected with the second family's endings, and it turns the
> one done-to into the subject: उच्यते "is said", दृश्यते "is seen". Whitney: it "is used only
> in a passive sense, and is formed from all roots for which there is occasion to make a
> passive conjugation". [REF:whitney1889 §768]

You read three such words today (the R3 card, §15 Step 6) and do not build them. Two of
today's headwords, मन्यते and जायते, also end in -यते and are *not* passives; the word list
decides, as §12 warns.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Hindi has no second family of verb endings [REF:kachru2006]. What Hindi
> shares with Sanskrit here is the liking frame: in मुझे फल अच्छे लगते हैं the liker stands in a
> को-form and the verb agrees with the liked thing, as रोचन्ते agrees with फलानि in फलानि
> मह्यम् रोचन्ते. [REF:masica1991]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spacing the retrieval of a thing over days helps it last: today's
> Station 0 is two earlier bookings landing on schedule, and this lesson books two of its
> own (§20). [REF:cepeda2006]

And the course principle that today pays off, stated with its one source: formulaic
chunks are taught whole and analysed later [REF:nation2013]. रोचते was a chunk in Volume 2;
today it is analysed.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the pattern, said in Sanskrit you own, pause form:

> **कः पुस्तकम् लभते? — छात्रः लभते।**
> (kaḥ pustakam labhate? — chātraḥ labhate.) "Who gets the book? — The student gets it." —
> किताब किसे मिलती है? — छात्र को मिलती है।

> **अहम् गच्छामि। अहम् पुस्तकम् लभे।**
> (aham gacchāmi. aham pustakam labhe.) "I go. I get a book." — मैं जाता हूँ। मुझे किताब
> मिलती है।

> **किम् तुभ्यम् रोचते? — मह्यम् फलानि रोचन्ते।**
> (kim tubhyam rocate? — mahyam phalāni rocante.) "What do you like? — I like fruits." —
> तुम्हें क्या अच्छा लगता है? — मुझे फल अच्छे लगते हैं।

> **"गच्छति" इति परस्मैपदम्। "लभते" इति आत्मनेपदम्।**
> ("gacchati" iti parasmaipadam. "labhate" iti ātmanepadam.) "'gacchati' is the first
> family; 'labhate' is the second." — "गच्छति" परस्मैपद है; "लभते" आत्मनेपद है।

The first line answers the asker कः with a second-family verb in the 3rd person; the
second puts the two families side by side in the 1st person: गच्छामि, but लभे. The third
is the liking frame of Volume 2 with a plural liked thing, so the verb is plural too:
रोचन्ते. The last line gives the tradition's names (संज्ञा) for the two families,
परस्मैपदम् and आत्मनेपदम्, with the इति that closes a quotation (lesson 1). They are
grammar terms, not ledger words.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

कल आपने गच्छति के नौ रूप बनाए: गच्छामि, गच्छसि, गच्छति, गच्छन्ति और बाकी। उन सबके
अन्त -मि, -सि, -ति, -न्ति जैसे थे। अन्त-चिह्नों के इस समूह को परम्परा परस्मैपद कहती है —
क्रिया के अन्त-चिह्नों का पहला परिवार। आज दूसरा परिवार आता है: आत्मनेपद। लभते "पाता है"
इसी परिवार की क्रिया है।

यह परिवार आपके लिए बिल्कुल नया भी नहीं है। दूसरे खण्ड से आप फलम् मह्यम् रोचते — "मुझे
फल अच्छा लगता है" — बोलते आ रहे हैं। उसके अन्त में -ति नहीं, -ते है। पहले खण्ड का
सत्यम् एव जयते भी -ते पर समाप्त होता है। और क्षमस्व का -स्व भी इसी परिवार का है; तालिका में
उसकी अपनी जगह चौथे पाठ में आएगी।

अब तालिका। पुरुष और वचन वही हैं — उत्तम, मध्यम, प्रथम पुरुष; एकवचन, द्विवचन, बहुवचन —
केवल अन्त बदलते हैं। प्रथम पुरुष में -ति की जगह -ते आता है — लभते "वह पाता है"; -न्ति की
जगह -न्ते — लभन्ते "वे पाते हैं"। मध्यम पुरुष में -सि की जगह -से — लभसे "तुम पाते हो"। उत्तम
पुरुष में -मि की जगह केवल -ए — लभे "मैं पाता हूँ"; और बहुवचन में -महे — लभामहे "हम पाते
हैं"। तीन रूप नए आकार के हैं: प्रथम पुरुष द्विवचन लभेते "वे दोनों पाते हैं", मध्यम पुरुष
द्विवचन लभेथे और मध्यम पुरुष बहुवचन लभध्वे "तुम सब पाते हो"। लभावहे "हम दोनों पाते हैं" और
लभेथे "तुम दोनों पाते हो" आज केवल पहचानने हैं, बनाने नहीं।

सबसे ज़रूरी नियम: परिवार क्रिया अपने साथ लाती है, जैसे संज्ञा अपना लिंग साथ लाती है। आप
किसी क्रिया को एक परिवार से दूसरे में नहीं ले जा सकते: लभति गलत है, गच्छते भी गलत है।
कौन-सी क्रिया किस परिवार की है, यह शब्दकोश बताता है — आप्टे के कोश में P. का अर्थ परस्मैपद
और A. का अर्थ आत्मनेपद है। इसलिए हर नई क्रिया उसके प्रथम पुरुष एकवचन रूप के साथ याद
कीजिए: गच्छति, पर लभते।

"अच्छा लगना" में हिन्दी और संस्कृत एक ही ढंग से चलती हैं। "मुझे फल अच्छे लगते हैं" — यहाँ
पसंद करने वाला "मुझे" यानी को-रूप में है, और क्रिया "लगते हैं" फलों से मेल खाती है।
संस्कृत में भी क्रिया पसंद की चीज़ से मेल खाती है: फलानि मह्यम् रोचन्ते — फलानि बहुवचन है,
इसलिए रोचन्ते; संस्कृत में फलानि ही कर्ता है, प्रथमा में। पर "मिलना" में दोनों भाषाएँ उलटी चलती हैं।
हिन्दी कहती है "मुझे किताब मिलती है"; संस्कृत कहती है अहम् पुस्तकम् लभे — पाने वाला
कर्ता है, प्रथमा में। "मुझे किताब मिलती है" के लिए "मह्यम् पुस्तकम् लभते" लिखना हिन्दी की
आदत है; संस्कृत में इसका अर्थ होगा "(वह) मेरे लिए किताब पाता है"।

आज केवल पहचानने के लिए एक पत्र भी है, जिसे पाठ R3 कहता है। उच्यते "कहा जाता है", दृश्यते "दिखता है, देखा जाता है",
श्रूयते "सुना जाता है" — ये कर्मवाच्य के रूप हैं: इनमें जिस पर काम होता है (कर्म), वही
प्रथमा में आता है और क्रिया उसी से मेल खाती है। इन्हें आज केवल पढ़ना है। सावधान: आज की दो क्रियाएँ, मन्यते "मानता है, सोचता है"
और जायते "पैदा होता है", भी -यते पर समाप्त होती हैं, पर ये कर्मवाच्य नहीं हैं। अन्त नहीं,
शब्द-सूची फ़ैसला करती है।

और एक ईमानदार बात। शास्त्रीय संस्कृत का कोश "जीतता है" के लिए जयति देता है — पहला
परिवार। मुण्डक उपनिषद् का जयते दूसरे परिवार का रूप है, जिसे व्याकरण केवल वि और परा उपसर्गों
के बाद बताता है। दोनों संस्कृत हैं; आदर्श-वाक्य उपनिषद् का रूप रखता है। सब कुछ आज भी
विराम-रूप में है — शब्द अलग-अलग, बिना जोड़े।

## 8. English Explanation

No new sound, no new case: the lesson's load is one new row of endings on the grid you
built yesterday, the rule that each verb brings its family with it, the liking frame in the
plural, and one recognition card. The recipe is the volume's — a scene, the question, the
forms you already own, the pattern named, your table, production.

### Step 1 — the scene

A teacher and two students under trees by the water. Three sentences, all readable now
except for two verbs you will meet as headwords today:

> **वृक्षाः वर्धन्ते। पुष्पाणि शोभन्ते। मह्यम् पुष्पाणि रोचन्ते।**
> (vṛkṣāḥ vardhante. puṣpāṇi śobhante. mahyam puṣpāṇi rocante.) "The trees grow. The
> flowers look lovely. I like the flowers." — पेड़ बढ़ते हैं। फूल सुन्दर लगते हैं। मुझे फूल
> अच्छे लगते हैं।

The doers are plural (वृक्षाः, पुष्पाणि), and the verbs are plural. But they do not end in
the -न्ति of yesterday's गच्छन्ति. They end in -न्ते.

### Step 2 — the question

Why do some verbs end in -ते where गच्छति ends in -ति? And if रोचते has a plural रोचन्ते,
what are its other seven cells?

### Step 3 — the evidence you already own

Before any rule, look at what is already yours. Cover the right-hand column and say what
each form tells you; then uncover and compare.

| owned form | where owned | what it shows |
|---|---|---|
| फलम् मह्यम् रोचते "the fruit pleases me" | v2.c05.l03 (§14 row: "-ते, the other ending family — middle voice, taught whole") | a 3sg in -ते |
| क्षमस्व "forgive (me)" | v2.c01.l04 | a request in -स्व; its cell: l04 |
| सत्यम् एव जयते | v1.c06.l04 | -ते in a motto |
| "the second ending family is Volume 3–4" | v2.c05.l03 §8 (again in §15 Step 4) | the promise |
| गच्छति · गच्छन्ति · गच्छसि · गच्छामि | v3.c03.l01 | the first family, to set beside |

Read the right-hand column down. Two owned verbs end in -ते in the 3rd person singular,
where every verb of yesterday's table ends in -ति. A third owned form, क्षमस्व, ends in a
shape no first-family cell has. And Volume 2 told you, in so many words, that a second
family existed and would come. The last row is the yardstick: today's endings are set
beside yesterday's, cell by cell.

### Step 4 — the pattern, named

**Two families of endings.** The first family, yesterday's, is परस्मैपदम् (parasmaipadam),
"a word for another"; the second is आत्मनेपदम् (ātmanepadam), "a word for oneself" (the
names and their history are in §4). Both carry the same nine cells. A verb belongs to one
family, and the dictionary says which: Apte prints **P.** for the first family and **A.**
for the second after the root.

| root | Apte's label | 3sg | family |
|---|---|---|---|
| गम् | P. | गच्छति | first (परस्मैपदम्) |
| लभ् | A. | लभते | second (आत्मनेपदम्) |
| रुच् | A. | रोचते | second (आत्मनेपदम्) |

So the answer to Step 2 is not a rule of meaning or of sound. रोचते ends in -ते because रुच्
is a second-family verb; गच्छति ends in -ति because गम् is a first-family verb. You never
move a verb from one family to the other: \*लभति and \*गच्छते are not Sanskrit.

### Step 5 — the table, assembled by you

Now build it. Lesson 1's table is printed on the left; fill the second family on the right
from these moves, then check against §15 Step 1 only when you have finished:

- **-ति → -ते** (3sg), **-न्ति → -न्ते** (3pl), **-सि → -से** (2sg);
- **-मि → -ए** (1sg: the ending is only -ए, and it replaces the stem's -अ: लभ + ए → लभे);
- 1st-person dual and plural: **-वहे**, **-महे**, with the stretch before व and म that you
  met in lesson 1 (लभावहे, लभामहे);
- three new shapes: 3rd-person dual **-एते**, 2nd-person dual **-एथे**, 2nd-person plural
  **-ध्वे**.

| पुरुषः | first family, गच्छति | second family, लभते |
|---|---|---|
| उत्तमः एकवचनम् | गच्छामि | ___ |
| उत्तमः द्विवचनम् | गच्छावः ◐ | ___ ◐ |
| उत्तमः बहुवचनम् | गच्छामः | ___ |
| मध्यमः एकवचनम् | गच्छसि | ___ |
| मध्यमः द्विवचनम् | गच्छथः ◐ | ___ ◐ |
| मध्यमः बहुवचनम् | गच्छथ | ___ |
| प्रथमः एकवचनम् | गच्छति | लभते |
| प्रथमः द्विवचनम् | गच्छतः | ___ |
| प्रथमः बहुवचनम् | गच्छन्ति | ___ |

The answers: लभे · लभावहे · लभामहे · लभसे · लभेथे · लभध्वे · लभते · लभेते · लभन्ते. The
two cells marked ◐, लभावहे and लभेथे, you parse when you meet them; you are not asked to
produce them. The other seven are today's productive cells, and they are the same for every
second-family verb you own: रोचते, and today's लभते, भाषते, वर्धते, शोभते, मन्यते, जायते.

So §3's prompt 1 is answered: रोचते is the 3rd person singular of a second-family verb, and
its plural is रोचन्ते.

### Step 6 — production

§18 runs it in three ways: the family sort (twelve verbs, each with its dictionary label,
put into the 3rd person singular and plural), liking aloud (मह्यम् … रोचते / रोचन्ते,
singular and plural things), and describing the scene by the water in six sentences of
your own. Pause form throughout. The pronouns वयम्, यूयम्, आवाम्, युवाम् are lesson 1's
labelled whole words; they work with this family exactly as with the first (वयम् लभामहे,
यूयम् भाषध्वे).

उच्चारणम् — no new sounds; the work is the final ए heard clearly, the -ते kept apart from
the -ति, and one new cluster. Audio: 🔊

**Drill 1 — the final ए.** Every second-family 3rd-person form ends in ए, and the ए is
long and pure, /eː/, held steady — never English "-tay". Say slowly, one form per breath:
लभते — लभेते — लभन्ते; then लभे — लभसे — लभध्वे. Hold the last vowel for two beats each

2. लभे /lɐbʱeː/ · लभते
3. लभसे /lɐbʱɐseː/ · लभध्वे /lɐbʱɐd̪ʱʋeː/

6. मन्यते · मन्यसे

शोभन्ते /ɕoːbʱɐn̪t̪eː/ · रोचन्ते /ɾoːt͡ɕɐn̪t̪eː/ · पुष्पाणि /puʂpɑːɳi/. **The R3 card:**
उच्यते /ut͡ɕjɐt̪eː/ · दृश्यते /d̪r̩ɕjɐt̪eː/ · श्रूयते /ɕɾuːjɐt̪eː/. **The motto and the
dictionary:** जयते /d͡ʑɐjɐt̪eː/ · जयति /d͡ʑɐjɐt̪i/.

**Narrow note — the final ए:** /eː/ is one long front vowel, the tongue still from start to
end. English "day" glides to [ɪ]; the Sanskrit vowel does not. In लभते the last syllable is
/t̪eː/, the tongue tip on the upper teeth for the dental /t̪/.

**Narrow note — length inside:** लभेते /lɐbʱeːt̪eː/ has a long /eː/ after भ; लभते
/lɐbʱɐt̪eː/ has the short /ɐ/. Long is about twice the duration of short.

**Narrow note — the -न्ते cluster:** in लभन्ते the न is dental /n̪/, made where the /t̪/ is
made; the tongue does not move between them.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — three checks, one mirror. The two-families board
(`planned:assets/images/volume-3/v3c03l02-two-families.svg`) prints a small mouth icon
beside the -ति and the -ते columns.

1. **The still-tongue check (लभते).** Mirror: say ए and hold it for two beats. The jaw and
   the tongue must not move. If the jaw rises toward the end, you said English "-tay".
2. **The breath-into-व check (लभध्वे).** ध्व is the breathy ध /d̪ʱ/ straight into व /ʋ/,
   no vowel between. Tongue tip on the upper teeth for ध, release with a puff of breathy
   voice, and the lower lip is already rising toward the upper teeth for व. Say ल-भ-ध्वे in
   three syllables, never "labhadhave".
3. **The -ति / -ते check.** For -ति the tongue is high and front, the lips spread a little;
   for -ते the tongue is a step lower and the vowel is held twice as long. Alternate them in
   the mirror: गच्छति — लभते.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; these feed the quiz distractors.

1. **Converting a verb between families: \*लभति, \*गच्छते, \*रोचति.** *Fix:* the family is
   the dictionary's (Apte P. / A.); learn each verb with its 3sg — गच्छति, but लभते.
2. **First-family endings in the 1st person: \*लभामि, \*लभामः.** *Fix:* लभे, लभामहे. The
   second family's "I" is only -ए.
3. **The -ए look-alikes: लभते read as a locative; the dual फले or the locative वने read
   as verbs.** *Fix:* the sentence decides; a verb has a doer. In ते वने द्वे फले लभन्ते।
   only लभन्ते is a verb.
4. **मन्यते, जायते read as passives ("is thought", "is begotten").** *Fix:* R3 is only
   उच्यते, दृश्यते, श्रूयते; the headword list decides.
5. **The liker as subject (the English reflex): \*अहम् फलम् रोचे.** *Fix:* the liked thing
   is the subject, the liker मह्यम्, as in v2.c05.l03; and plural liked things take
   रोचन्ते: \*फलानि मह्यम् रोचते → फलानि मह्यम् रोचन्ते.
6. **The Hindi मिलना reflex with लभते: \*मह्यम् पुस्तकम् लभते, copied from "मुझे किताब मिलती है".**
   *Fix:* अहम् पुस्तकम् लभे — the getter is the subject; Hindi turns it round. (मह्यम्
   पुस्तकम् लभते can only mean "(he) gets a book for me".)

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — the plan's six model sentences, then seven more.

### The six model sentences

> **छात्रः पुस्तकम् लभते।**
> (chātraḥ pustakam labhate.) "The student gets a book." — छात्र पुस्तक पाता है।

> **अहम् संस्कृतम् भाषे।**
> (aham saṃskṛtam bhāṣe.) "I speak Sanskrit." — मैं संस्कृत बोलता/बोलती हूँ।

> **वृक्षाः वर्धन्ते।**
> (vṛkṣāḥ vardhante.) "The trees grow." — पेड़ बढ़ते हैं।

> **फलानि मह्यम् रोचन्ते।**
> (phalāni mahyam rocante.) "I like fruits." — मुझे फल अच्छे लगते हैं।

> **त्वम् किम् मन्यसे?**
> (tvam kim manyase?) "What do you think?" — तुम क्या सोचते हो?

> **सत्यम् एव जयते।**
> (satyam eva jayate.) "Truth alone prevails." — सत्य ही जीतता है।

("vṛkṣaḥ kapotānām gṛham" iti aham manye.) "I think a tree is the doves' home." — मैं
सोचता हूँ कि पेड़ कबूतरों का घर है।

— **गुरुः** — सत्यम्। वृक्षेषु पुष्पाणि जायन्ते, ततः फलानि।
(satyam. vṛkṣeṣu puṣpāṇi jāyante, tataḥ phalāni.) "True. On trees flowers arise, then
fruits." — सच। पेड़ों पर फूल आते हैं, फिर फल।

— **अर्जुनः** — गुरो, भवान् संस्कृतम् भाषते। वयम् अपि भाषामहे!
(guro, bhavān saṃskṛtam bhāṣate. vayam api bhāṣāmahe!) "Teacher, you speak Sanskrit. We
speak it too!" — गुरुजी, आप संस्कृत बोलते हैं। हम भी बोलते हैं!

— **गुरुः** — आम्! यूयम् संस्कृतम् भाषध्वे, अतः संस्कृतम् वर्धते।
(ām! yūyam saṃskṛtam bhāṣadhve, ataḥ saṃskṛtam vardhate.) "Yes! You all speak Sanskrit,
so Sanskrit grows." — हाँ! तुम सब संस्कृत बोलते हो, इसलिए संस्कृत बढ़ती है।

— **सीता** — मह्यम् एतत् वचनम् रोचते।
(mahyam etat vacanam rocate.) "I like this saying." — मुझे यह बात अच्छी लगती है।

— **अर्जुनः** — पश्य! अहम् एकम् फलम् लभे।
(paśya! aham ekam phalam labhe.) "Look! I get a fruit." — देखो! मुझे एक फल मिलता है।

— **गुरुः** — तत् फलम् मधुरम्। तत् तुभ्यम् रोचते? — **अर्जुनः** — आम्, मह्यम् रोचते!

you like it? — Yes, I like it!" — वह फल मीठा है। क्या वह तुम्हें अच्छा लगता है? — हाँ,
मुझे अच्छा लगता है!

second-family लभन्ते: one story, two families.

(5) → §9 pairs and dictation (4) → production: exercise 2, the sort, and exercise 3, liking,
aloud (6) → the story read once, the bookings, the homework (3). In class: exercises 2 and 3
aloud; at home (§21): exercises 1, 4 and 5, the written exercises 2 and 3, and exercise 6
recorded.

**Board work:** two columns, गच्छति on the left and लभते on the right, nine rows each, the
endings that change by one vowel in one colour and the three new shapes (-एते, -एथे, -ध्वे)
boxed. Under them a strip with Apte's two labels, P. and A., and three verbs under each.
In a corner, the -ए look-alikes: ते वने द्वे फले लभन्ते।, with the one verb underlined.

**The sūtras behind §4 and §15.** Pāṇini names the endings in two sūtras: 1.4.99 लः
परस्मैपदम् — "the substitutes of ल are parasmaipada" — and 1.4.100 तङानावात्मनेपदम् — "the
त-set and -आन are ātmanepada" — [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.4.99] [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.4.100]; which roots
take which family is governed by 1.3.12 अनुदात्तङित आत्मनेपदम् and the remainder rule 1.3.78
शेषात् कर्तरि परस्मैपदम् [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.3.12] [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.3.78]. The honesty
line rests on 1.3.19 विपराभ्यां जेः [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.3.19], with Apte s.v. जि "c1 P. (Atm.
when preceded by parā and vi)" [REF:apte1890 s.v. जि]. In learner text the lesson says only
"the grammarians"; no sūtra number is printed to the learner today.

**जायते.** Whitney: "from the Veda down, jā́yate *is born* etc. is found as altered passive
or original ya-formation by the side of √jan" [REF:whitney1889 §761]. The course files
जायते as the class-4 middle Apte gives (c4 A.) and makes no claim beyond that; do not call
it a passive in class, and do not argue the question.

**वर्धते.** Apte s.v. वृध्: "c1 A. (but Paras. also in the Second Future, Aorist and
Conditional, also in the Desiderative)" [REF:apte1890 s.v. वृध्]. Irrelevant to the present,
which is A. throughout; mention it only if a learner meets a first-family form of वृध् in a
text.

**Verbs in both families.** Apte's U. means both: such a root is उभयपदी (ubhayapadī), "of both families". करोति beside कुरुते is the course's first
example, and it is v4.c03's, where the 1.x कृ error is also fixed. One line, if asked. The
course teaches lesson 1's चिन्तयति and lesson 4's भक्षयति in the first family only, though
Apte marks both roots c10 U.; never mark a learner's first-family form of either wrong.

**The family is the word's.** The sentence to repeat is "learn each verb
with its 3sg". Insist that every verb in a learner's notebook carries -ति or -ते from the
day it is written down. The sort (exercise 2) is the drill for it, and lesson 3's Station 0
runs it again tomorrow.

**R3 is a watch item.** It stays a recognition card: no exercise asks for an R3 form, and a
learner who asks how to make "is eaten" is told "v4.c05". The likely confusion is मन्यते
read as "is thought" (§12, mistake 4); line 2 and line 4 of §17 are the cure, read side by
side. The Pañcatantra quotation is display; read it to the class once, joined, then in
pause form.

**Differentiation.** Hindi-medium learners know the liking frame already (मुझे … अच्छा
लगता है) and stumble on लभते, where Hindi's मिलना turns the sentence round: give them §12
mistake 6 and §17 line 1 first. English speakers stumble on the liking frame and find लभते
easy: give them §15 Step 4 and exercise 3 twice. Script-side learners fill the §8 Step 5
frame before they say anything.

**What students find hard:** the 1st person singular (they write \*लभामि); the final ए said
as a diphthong; the -ए look-alikes in a sentence; and the idea that the family is not a
matter of meaning — वदति and भाषते, one verb in each family, both "speaks", settle it.

**The serial.** In the Hitopadeśa the king counsels steadiness and gives the plan as an
impersonal command, *sarvair ekacittībhūya jālam ādāya uḍḍīyatām* "let all, of one mind,
take the net and fly up" (prose after Hit 1.34); the hunter runs after them and thinks a
verse (Hit 1.37) and turns back once they are out of sight; the dove's R3 line and "the net
looks fine" are ours [REF:hitopadesha mitralābha]. As in Chapter 2, the mouse's hole is near
the tree in our retelling, so the flight is short and no river is named. These departures are
on the record for v3.c06.l02, which reads the text itself.
