## 1. Hook

Since your first lesson you have carried words that were never quite single words. नास्ति
"there is no" — v2.c01.l04 said न and अस्ति had fused into it, and that the rule was Volume
3's. स्वागतम् "welcome", विद्यालयः "school", हिमालयः, परोपकारः: each one is two pieces
said without a break. Volume 1 promised that you would "watch *a* meet *i* and become ए in
live text". Today that promise is kept. Two vowels meet at a word's edge, and Sanskrit does
one of seven things — and each of the seven has a name and a sūtra.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — where the vowel rules sit in Pāṇini's grammar, in Hindi school grammar,
and in print.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** According to Pāṇini, the vowel-joining rules stand in the sixth
> book of the Aṣṭādhyāyī, under a heading that means "in joined text": they apply where
> sounds are said together without a pause, and not where a speaker stops.
> [REF:ashtadhyayi]

That heading is why this lesson keeps asking one question first: is there a pause between
the two words? A daṇḍa, a "?", a "!", a comma, the dash of a speech frame, or the closing
quotation mark before इति means a stop, and at a stop nothing joins. The heading's number
stays in the teacher's notes (§22); the seven sūtras you read today (§15) all stand under it.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** Hindi school grammar teaches the same joins in Hindi's
> tatsama words, under three heads — स्वर-सन्धि, व्यञ्जन-सन्धि, विसर्ग-सन्धि — and sorts the
> स्वर-सन्धि under five names: दीर्घ, गुण, वृद्धि, यण्, अयादि. These are the names the
> Pāṇinian tradition uses (सवर्णदीर्घः, गुणः, वृद्धिः, यण्, अयादि). [REF:guru1952]

If you went to school in Hindi, you have met five of today's seven names already. The two
the Hindi books do not need are पूर्वरूपम्, the अ that disappears after a final ए or ओ, and
प्रगृह्यम्, the dual that never joins (§15 Steps 6–7). The course prints the GLOSSARY
spelling व्यञ्जन-सन्धि; a Hindi schoolbook prints व्यंजन संधि — the same words, with the dot.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** The avagraha ऽ, the sign you will write today in तेऽपि, is
> "occasionally used in the manuscripts", sometimes as a hyphen or to mark a hiatus; "in
> printed texts, especially European, it is ordinarily applied" to one use only: to mark
> the elision of an initial अ after a final ए or ओ. [REF:whitney1889 §16]

So where you see ऽ, and where you do not, is a matter of print practice, not of the
language: the sound is the same either way, and the sign has no sound of its own
(v1.c05.l05). The course prints it, as printed Sanskrit books do.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why two vowels at a seam are a problem, and the three ways the language
solves it.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "The concurrence of two vowels, or of vowel and diphthong, without
> intervening consonant, is forbidden by the euphony of the later or classical language. It
> is avoided … either by fusion of the two concurrent sounds into one, by the reduction of
> one of them to a semivowel, or by development of a semivowel between them."
> [REF:whitney1889 §125]

The three solutions are today's map. **Fusion:** न अस्ति → नास्ति, two short अ become one
long आ; सदा एव → सदैव, आ and ए become ऐ. **A semivowel:** इति अवदत् → इत्यवदत्, the इ
becomes य्. **A semivowel developed between them:** a final ए, ऐ, ओ, औ splits into अ, आ +
a glide — and at a word end the glide of ए and ऐ is dropped, so a gap stays: ते आगच्छन्ति →
त आगच्छन्ति [REF:whitney1889 §132] [REF:whitney1889 §133]. That gap, the "permanent hiatus"
of Whitney's §125a, and the dual in -ई, -ऊ, -ए, which never joins [REF:whitney1889 §138],
are the two places on today's map where two vowels stand side by side at a seam.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Hindi writes its words apart and applies no sandhi between independent
> words in writing (वह घर जाता है). The joined shapes a Hindi speaker uses are tatsama words
> and compounds taken whole from Sanskrit: विद्यालय, हिमालय, सूर्योदय, परोपकार, सदैव,
> स्वागत, नमस्ते. [REF:kachru2006]

A Hindi speaker already says Sanskrit vowel sandhis — inside words. Today the same joins
happen between words.

The course principle behind the lesson, with its one source: formulaic chunks are taught
whole and analysed later [REF:nation2013]. नास्ति, स्वागतम् and विद्यालयः were given to you
whole, one or two volumes ago; today they are analysed. And the spacing of today's two
bookings (§20) follows the course's one claim for it: spaced review helps retention
[REF:cepeda2006].

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the pattern, said in Sanskrit you own. Each line is printed in pause form
and then **joined so far**: only today's vowel seams are joined.

> **अद्य सूर्य-उदये अहम् विद्यालयम् गच्छामि।** — pause form
>
> **अद्य सूर्योदयेऽहम् विद्यालयम् गच्छामि।** — joined so far
> (adya sūryodaye 'ham vidyālayam gacchāmi.) "Today at sunrise I go to school." — आज सूर्योदय
> पर मैं विद्यालय जाता हूँ।

> **नास्ति — सवर्णदीर्घः। सूर्योदये — गुणः। सदैव — वृद्धिः।**
> (nāsti — savarṇadīrghaḥ. sūryodaye — guṇaḥ. sadaiva — vṛddhiḥ.) "नास्ति: like vowels, one
> long. सूर्योदये: guṇa. सदैव: vṛddhi." — नास्ति: दीर्घ। सूर्योदये: गुण। सदैव: वृद्धि।

> **इत्यवदत् — यण्। त आगच्छन्ति — अयादि। तेऽपि — पूर्वरूपम्।**
> (ity avadat — yaṇ. ta āgacchanti — ayādi. te 'pi — pūrvarūpam.) "इत्यवदत्: yaṇ. त
> आगच्छन्ति: ayādi. तेऽपि: pūrvarūpa." — इत्यवदत्: यण्। त आगच्छन्ति: अयादि। तेऽपि: पूर्वरूप।

> **द्वे बालिके अत्र स्तः। बालिके — प्रगृह्यम्।**
> (dve bālike atra staḥ. bālike — pragṛhyam.) "The two girls are here. बालिके: pragṛhya,
> held apart." — दो लड़कियाँ यहाँ हैं। बालिके: प्रगृह्य, अलग रखा गया।

The first line is §8's scene. Its two seams fuse in two ways: सूर्य-उदये inside a compound
(guṇa, अ + उ → ओ), and सूर्योदये अहम् between words (पूर्वरूपम्, the अ of अहम् merges into
the ए). अहम् विद्यालयम् stays apart: a म् seam is lesson 3's. The next two lines give the
tradition's names (संज्ञा) for the six joins, each beside an owned word. The last line is
the one that never joins. The names are grammar terms, not ledger words.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

अब तक इस पाठ्यक्रम का हर संस्कृत वाक्य विराम-रूप में छपा है — हर शब्द अलग, वैसा ही जैसा
वह रुकने से पहले बोला जाता है। पर बोलते समय हम रुकते नहीं: शब्द एक के बाद एक आते हैं, और
जहाँ एक शब्द का अन्त और दूसरे का आरम्भ मिलते हैं, वहाँ ध्वनियाँ एक-दूसरे के अनुसार बदल जाती
हैं। इसे सन्धि कहते हैं — शब्दों का आपस में जुड़ना। आज का विषय स्वर-सन्धि है: जब पहला शब्द
स्वर पर ख़त्म हो और दूसरा स्वर से शुरू हो।

आप इसे पहले से जानते हैं। नास्ति = न + अस्ति: दो छोटे अ मिलकर एक लम्बा आ बन गए। विद्यालय
= विद्या + आलय, हिमालय = हिम + आलय, सूर्योदय = सूर्य + उदय, परोपकार = पर + उपकार, सदैव =
सदा + एव, स्वागत = सु + आगत। हिन्दी में ये शब्द पूरे के पूरे संस्कृत से लिए गए हैं — ये तत्सम
शब्द हैं — इसलिए इनमें संस्कृत की सन्धि पहले से बैठी है। फ़र्क़ बस इतना है कि हिन्दी अपने
अलग-अलग शब्दों को लिखते समय नहीं जोड़ती — "वह घर जाता है" — जबकि संस्कृत वाक्य के
शब्दों को भी जोड़ देती है।

हिन्दी व्याकरण की पुस्तकों में स्वर-सन्धि के पाँच नाम आते हैं, और वही पाँच पाणिनि की परम्परा
में भी हैं:

- **दीर्घ** — परम्परा में सवर्णदीर्घ: एक जैसे दो स्वर मिलकर एक लम्बा स्वर — अ + अ = आ,
  जैसे नास्ति; इ + इ = ई, जैसे इतीव।
- **गुण:** अ या आ के बाद इ/ई = ए, उ/ऊ = ओ, ऋ = अर् — न + इति = नेति, पर + उपकार = परोपकार।
- **वृद्धि:** अ या आ के बाद ए/ऐ = ऐ, ओ/औ = औ — सदा + एव = सदैव, मम + ओष्ठौ = ममौष्ठौ।
- **यण्:** इ, उ, ऋ के बाद कोई दूसरा स्वर आए, तो इ → य्, उ → व्, ऋ → र् — इति + अवदत् =
  इत्यवदत्, सु + आगतम् = स्वागतम्।
- **अयादि:** ए, ऐ, ओ, औ के बाद स्वर आए तो ये अय्, आय्, अव्, आव् बनते हैं। शब्द के अन्त में
  अय् और आय् का य् गिर जाता है और बीच में ख़ाली जगह रहती है: ते + आगच्छन्ति = त
  आगच्छन्ति — यहाँ बीच का रिक्त स्थान ज़रूरी है। पर -औ का आव् बना रहता है: पितरौ + अपि =
  पितरावपि।

दो नाम और हैं, जिनकी हिन्दी के शब्दों को ज़रूरत नहीं पड़ती:

- **पूर्वरूप:** शब्द के अन्त के ए या ओ के बाद छोटा अ आए, तो वह अ उसी में समा जाता है; उसकी
  जगह अवग्रह ऽ लिखा जाता है — ते + अपि = तेऽपि। अवग्रह की कोई ध्वनि नहीं: तेऽपि को "तेपि"
  ही बोलिए।
- **प्रगृह्य:** -ई, -ऊ, -ए पर ख़त्म होने वाला द्विवचन, यानी दो के लिए रूप, कभी नहीं जुड़ता — द्वे
  बालिके अत्र, जैसा है वैसा ही रहता है। पर ध्यान दीजिए: एकवचन बालिके, "हे लड़की", और फले,
  "फल में", जुड़ जाते हैं — बालिकेऽत्र, फलेऽपि। और -औ वाला द्विवचन भी जुड़ता है:
  कपोतौ + अपि = कपोतावपि।

तीन बातें याद रखिए। पहली: जोड़ने से पहले देखिए कि बीच में रुकावट तो नहीं — दण्ड, प्रश्नचिह्न,
विस्मयादिबोधक चिह्न, अल्पविराम, बोलने के ढाँचे का डैश, या इति से पहले का उद्धरण-चिह्न। वहाँ
कुछ नहीं जुड़ता। दूसरी: जोड़कर पढ़ते समय जोड़ पर रुकिए मत — नास्ति में एक ही लम्बा आ है, दो
अ नहीं; और सदैव को "सदैव्" मत बोलिए, अन्त का अ पूरा बोलिए। तीसरी: अलग करते समय हर ए को
तोड़िए मत। विद्यालये या वने अपने-आप में पूरे शब्द हैं; तोड़िए वहीं, जहाँ दोनों टुकड़े सचमुच
के शब्द बनें।

आज केवल स्वर-जोड़ जोड़े जाते हैं। विसर्ग वाले जोड़, जैसे रामः + अपि, दूसरे पाठ के हैं और
व्यंजन वाले, जैसे अहम् + अपि, तीसरे के; आज की जुड़ी पंक्तियों में वे विराम-रूप में ही रहते
हैं। इसीलिए आज की हर जुड़ी पंक्ति पर लिखा है: "joined so far" — अभी तक जितना सीखा, उतना
जुड़ा।

## 8. English Explanation

No new sound and no new sign: the avagraha you have known since v1.c05.l05. The lesson's
load is one map — what happens when a word-final vowel meets a word-initial vowel — read
first by splitting, then produced by joining. The same rules hold at the inner seam of a
compound and at the seam of a prefix and its verb. The recipe is the volume's: a scene, the
question, the forms you already own, the pattern named, your map, production.

> sunrise I go to school." — नमस्ते! स्वागत है! आज सूर्योदय पर मैं विद्यालय जाता हूँ।

### Step 4 — the pattern, named

**The fact.** Sanskrit joins words that are said together. The joining is called
**सन्धिः** (sandhiḥ). Until now every Sanskrit line in this course was printed in pause
form, each word as it sounds before a pause; this chapter shows the joins one kind at a
time. Today's kind is **स्वरसन्धिः** (svarasandhiḥ), "vowel joining": the left word, in
its pause form, ends in a vowel; the right word begins with one; nothing stands between
them.

**The names.** Six joins and one refusal, each with the tradition's name:

1. **सवर्णदीर्घः** (savarṇadīrghaḥ) — like vowels become one long vowel: नास्ति.
2. **गुणः** (guṇaḥ) — अ/आ + इ/ई, उ/ऊ, ऋ → ए, ओ, अर्: परोपकारः.
3. **वृद्धिः** (vṛddhiḥ) — अ/आ + ए/ऐ, ओ/औ → ऐ, औ: सदैव.
4. **यण्** (yaṇ) — इ/ई, उ/ऊ before an unlike vowel → य्, व्: स्वागतम्, इत्यवदत्.
5. **अयादि** (ayādi) — ए, ऐ, ओ, औ before a vowel → अय्, आय्, अव्, आव्; at a word end the य्
   drops and a gap stays: त आगच्छन्ति, but -औ keeps its व्: पितरावपि.
6. **पूर्वरूपम्** (pūrvarūpam) — after a word-final ए or ओ, a short अ disappears, and ऽ
   marks where it was: तेऽपि.
7. **प्रगृह्यम्** (pragṛhyam) — "held apart": a dual in -ई, -ऊ, -ए never joins: द्वे बालिके
   अत्र.

Beside them, the Hindi school names you may already know (§4, §7): दीर्घ, गुण, वृद्धि,
यण्, अयादि.

**The splitting heuristic.** Splitting comes first, because reading comes first. A joined
आ may hide अ+अ, अ+आ, आ+अ or आ+आ; a joined ए may hide अ+इ, अ+ई, आ+इ, आ+ई — or simply a
word in -ए; a joined ओ may hide अ/आ + उ/ऊ. Meaning decides, and your ledger or the
dictionary confirms (§15 Step 10).

वा /ʋɑː/ · सूर्यः /suːɾjɐh/.

**Narrow note — guṇa gives pure vowels.** The ए of नेति and the ओ of परोपकारः are the long
monophthongs /eː/ and /oː/ (STYLE_GUIDE §2.1), not English "ay" or "ow": अ + इ does not
sound like "a-ee" once it has joined. ऐ and औ, the vṛddhi results, are the diphthongs /ɐi̯/
and /ɐu̯/.

**Narrow note — the avagraha.** In तेऽपि the ऽ is silent: /t̪eːpi/, two syllables. The
vowel before it keeps its full length.

**Narrow note — the anusvāra in सिंहस्योपरि.** Before ह the anusvāra is a nasalized vowel,
/sĩɦɐ/, not /siŋ/ (STYLE_GUIDE §2.1).

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — three checks, one mirror. The split-and-join strip
(`planned:assets/images/volume-3/v3c04l01-split-and-join.svg`) shows each seam with a small
mouth icon: jaw open for आ, the tongue rising for य्, the lips rounding for औ.

1. **The no-reset check (नास्ति, इत्यवदत्).** Mirror: say न अस्ति, then नास्ति. In the pause
   form the jaw closes a little between the words; in नास्ति it does not move between न
   and स्ति. In इत्यवदत् the tongue glides from the इ position straight into य् with no
   break: watch that the jaw does not "reset" between the two words.
2. **The glide check (सदैव, ममौष्ठौ).** For ऐ start with the jaw half-open for /ɐ/ and let it
   rise towards /i/ in one movement; for औ, round the lips as the jaw rises. If nothing
   moves, you said ए or ओ — and that is guṇa, not vṛddhi (§12, mistake 3).
3. **The gap check (त आगच्छन्ति).** Keep the throat open between /ɐ/ and /ɑː/: no glottal
   catch, no य् sliding in. The jaw opens wider for the long आ, and that is all.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; these feed the quiz distractors.

1. **Joining a dual: \*द्वे बालिकेऽत्र.** The ए-before-अ rule applied to every ए. *Fix:* a
   dual in -ई, -ऊ, -ए is pragṛhya (1.1.11): द्वे बालिके अत्र. A vocative singular बालिके
   with no "!" after it (बालिके अत्र आगच्छ → बालिकेऽत्रागच्छ) or a locative singular (फले
   अपि → फलेऽपि) does join.
2. **Closing the gap: \*तागच्छन्ति for त आगच्छन्ति.** The अ left at a word end joined again
   with the next vowel. *Fix:* at a word end ए becomes अ and the gap stays (Whitney §133);
   the gap is not joined a second time.
3. **The wrong grade before ए/ऐ, ओ/औ: \*सदेव for सदैव, \*ममोष्ठौ for ममौष्ठौ.** Guṇa used
   where vṛddhi belongs. *Fix:* अ/आ + ए → ऐ, + ओ → औ — vṛddhi, 6.1.88 — not guṇa. सदेव is a
   real joined pair elsewhere, but never the join of सदा एव.
4. **Joining across a stop: \*उवाचाहम् for उवाच — "अहम् …", \*…दृश्यतेइति.** *Fix:* no
   sandhi across a daṇḍa, "?", "!", a dash, a comma or the closing quotation mark before
   इति; beyond इति the join resumes: "…न दृश्यते" इत्येकः कपोतः वदति।
5. **Over-splitting every ए: \*विद्यालय + इ for विद्यालये, \*वन + इ for वने.** *Fix:* a word
   in -ए — a locative, a dual, ते — is a whole word; split only where the halves are real
   words (the heuristic, §15 Step 10).
6. **Hindi-speaker schwa deletion in the joined word: सदैव said as \*sadaiv, इत्यवदत् as
   \*ityavdat.** The Hindi habit of dropping a final or medial short अ. *Fix:* every अ is
   sounded — /sɐd̪ɐi̯ʋɐ/, /it̪jɐʋɐd̪ɐt̪/ (STYLE_GUIDE §1.1).

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — one map, seven rows, each read first by splitting and then by joining, each
with the sūtra that states it. Work through it in order; §8 Step 5's frame is the page you
check here.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The vowel rules apply where the left word, in its pause form, ends in a
> vowel, the right word begins with a vowel, and nothing — a daṇḍa, "?", "!", a comma, the
> dash of a speech frame, the closing quotation mark before इति — stands between them. The
> checks run in a fixed order: (1) a dual in -ई, -ऊ, -ए? → no join; (2) like vowels → one
> long; (3) अ/आ + another vowel → guṇa or vṛddhi; (4) इ/ई, उ/ऊ, ऋ + an unlike vowel → the
> semivowel; (5) ए/ओ + अ → the अ merges; (6) ए, ऐ, ओ, औ + another vowel → अय्, आय्, अव्,
> आव्. [REF:whitney1889 §126] [REF:whitney1889 §127] [REF:whitney1889 §129]
> [REF:whitney1889 §131] [REF:whitney1889 §135] [REF:whitney1889 §138]

Each step's table has four columns: पदपाठः, the word-by-word text — the course's pause form —
and संहिता, the joined text, each with its IAST. Lesson 4 gives both names their story; today
they are column labels. A left word that ends in ः, म् or another consonant is never a vowel
seam: रामः अपि, अहम् अपि, हिमालयात् आगच्छामि are seams of lessons 2 and 3, and today they
stay as they are. The interjection हे never joins a following vowel (Whitney §138f); no line of this lesson puts
one before a vowel.

joins "rare and of sporadic character"). **-औ keeps its व्:** पितरावपि, written together,
because the first word now ends in a consonant. **-ए before अ is not here:** ते अपि goes
to Step 6. A word-final ओ that does not come from -अः, such as the vocative गुरो, "sometimes
retain[s] the v and sometimes lose[s] it" (Whitney §134a): the course never puts it before a
vowel.

### Step 6 — पूर्वरूपम्: ए/ओ + अ, and the avagraha

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "After final ए e or ओ o, an initial अ a disappears"; the avagraha ऽ
> marks the place. [REF:whitney1889 §135] [REF:whitney1889 §16]

| पदपाठः | IAST | संहिता | IAST |
|---|---|---|---|
| ते अपि | te api | तेऽपि | te 'pi |
| वने अस्ति | vane asti | वनेऽस्ति | vane 'sti |
| घटे अल्पम् | ghaṭe alpam | घटेऽल्पम् | ghaṭe 'lpam |
| समीपे अस्ति | samīpe asti | समीपेऽस्ति | samīpe 'sti |
| विद्यालये अद्य | vidyālaye adya | विद्यालयेऽद्य | vidyālaye 'dya |
| हिमालये अपि | himālaye api | हिमालयेऽपि | himālaye 'pi |

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** According to Pāṇini's sūtra 6.1.109 एङः पदान्तादति (eṅaḥ
> padāntād ati): "after a word-final ए or ओ, a short अ merges into it". [REF:ashtadhyayi
> 6.1.109] [REF:kashika 6.1.109]

The words are written together, and the romanization keeps them apart with an apostrophe
after a space: *te 'pi*. The ऽ is silent (§10). This is the avagraha v1.c05.l05 said you
would "*use* … when sandhi arrives in Volume 3". The ओऽ of सोऽहम् and कोऽपि comes from -अः +
अ: that is the visarga column of lesson 2.

### Step 7 — प्रगृह्यम्: the dual that never joins

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** "Certain final vowels … are uncombinable (pragṛhya), or maintain
> themselves unchanged before any following vowel": among them "the vowels ī, ū, and e as
> dual endings", and "the final, or only, vowel of an interjection". [REF:whitney1889 §138]

| पदपाठः | IAST | संहिता | IAST |
|---|---|---|---|
| द्वे बालिके अत्र | dve bālike atra | द्वे बालिके अत्र | dve bālike atra |
| द्वे फले अपि | dve phale api | द्वे फले अपि | dve phale api |
| अग्नी अत्र | agnī atra | अग्नी अत्र | agnī atra |
| गुरू अपि | gurū api | गुरू अपि | gurū api |
| नयने इव | nayane iva | नयने इव | nayane iva |

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** According to Pāṇini's sūtra 1.1.11
> ईदूदेद्द्विवचनं प्रगृह्यम् (īdūded dvivacanaṃ pragṛhyam): "a dual ending in ई, ऊ or ए is
> pragṛhya, 'held apart'". [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.11] [REF:kashika 1.1.11]

The look-alikes decide the marks. बालिके is a dual in द्वे बालिके, but the vocative singular
"O girl" in बालिके अत्र आगच्छ — printed with no "!", so that the seam is real: that one
joins, बालिकेऽत्रागच्छ. फले is a dual in द्वे फले, but a locative singular "in the fruit"
in फले अपि: that one joins, फलेऽपि. And the dual in -औ is **not** pragṛhya: कपोतौ अपि →
कपोतावपि, by Step 5. The words अग्नी "two fires", गुरू "two teachers" and नयने "two eyes" are
the dual card's cells (v3.c02.l04).

### Step 9 — inside a word: observed, named later

The same vowel grades appear inside words, between a stem and its ending or a prefix and its
verb. One line each — inside a word, named in v3.c04.l04, rules Volume 4:

- **The prefix seams** (v3.c03.l03): आ + अगच्छत् → आगच्छत्, अव + अगच्छत् → अवागच्छत्, प्र +
  अविशत् → प्राविशत्, उप + अविशत् → उपाविशत्, प्र + अयच्छत् → प्रायच्छत् are सवर्णदीर्घः; नि +

- **नयनम्** "eye" (v3.c02.l04) is ने + अनम्: the full अय् of 6.1.78 before a vowel, kept

### Step 10 — splitting: the heuristic

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** A joined shape can hide more than one pair. A joined आ may hide अ+अ,
> अ+आ, आ+अ or आ+आ; a joined ए may hide अ+इ, अ+ई, आ+इ, आ+ई — or simply a word in -ए; a joined
> ओ may hide अ/आ + उ/ऊ; य् or व् before a vowel may hide इ/ई or उ/ऊ. Meaning decides, and the
> ledger or the dictionary confirms. [REF:whitney1889 §126] [REF:whitney1889 §127]

So split in three moves: find the fused vowel; write every pair that could have made it;
keep the pair whose halves are both real words and fit the sentence. नास्ति: न + अस्ति — the
other candidates (ना + अस्ति, न + आस्ति) give no word you own. विद्यालये is not "विद्यालय +
इ": विद्यालये is a whole word, the locative, and nothing follows it. Lesson 4 turns this into
a fixed method for every kind of seam.

the gap after अयादि, which they close; guṇa against vṛddhi before ए and ओ; and splitting —
they break every ए into अ + इ. Answer the last with the heuristic: both halves must be real
words.
