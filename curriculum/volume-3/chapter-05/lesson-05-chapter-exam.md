## 1. Hook

Since Volume 1 you have said मम, तव, नमस्ते, कश्चित् and विद्यालयः as whole words, without
being told what is inside them. Four lessons opened them: two tables of pronouns and the polite
भवान्, the words that never change and the relative sentence they build, and the compound with
its vigraha. Today you show that the chapter is yours in one sitting: nouns turned into the
right pronoun, two sentences made one, ten compounds taken apart, a new page of the serial read
at sight, and a conversation with a guest written in your own joined Sanskrit. The title,
**परीक्षा** (parīkṣā) "exam", is one word, read whole. 488 words. Pass, and Chapter 5 is
complete.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — the chapter's honest lines in one place: where the serial comes from, the
tradition's names for what the chapter taught, and two 1.x lines put right.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** The doves and the mouse come from the frame story of the first book
> of the Hitopadeśa, attributed to Nārāyaṇa and dated by scholars to roughly the 9th–12th
> centuries CE; our five episodes are a retelling, not the text. [REF:hitopadesha mitralābha]

Task D is one more page of that retelling, written for today and labelled like every page of
the serial: *our retelling, after Hitopadeśa 1 (Mitralābha)*. Where it departs from the text,
the teacher's notes say so (§22); Chapter 6 reads the text's own sentences.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** Pāṇini names the classes this chapter taught, and four lessons
> quoted one or two sūtras each: 8.1.22 तेमयावेकवचनस्य for ते and मे; 1.1.27
> सर्वादीनि सर्वनामानि for the सर्वनाम class; 1.1.37 स्वरादिनिपातमव्ययम् for the अव्यय;
> 1.4.59 उपसर्गाः क्रियायोगे for the उपसर्ग; 2.2.29 चार्थे द्वन्द्वः and 2.2.24
> अनेकमन्यपदार्थे for two kinds of compound. [REF:ashtadhyayi 8.1.22] [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.27]
> [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.1.37] [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.4.59] [REF:ashtadhyayi 2.2.29]
> [REF:ashtadhyayi 2.2.24]

Each sūtra's own wording stands in its lesson (l01 §4, l02 §4, l03 §4, l04 §4); today they
return by number and name only, and §19 Q8 asks you to match each number to its gist, in the
form "According to Pāṇini".

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** Hindi school textbooks list six kinds of समास, counting कर्मधारय
> and द्विगु separately; the Sanskrit tradition counts four, with those two inside तत्पुरुष.

The exam asks for the tradition's four. A karmadhāraya named "तत्पुरुषः" is not wrong, since it
is a kind of tatpuruṣa; the keys accept it and give it the kind's ½ (§19).

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The demonstrative तद् has सः and सा in the nominative singular masculine
> and feminine, and forms from त- everywhere else: two stems in one table, not a sound change.
> [REF:whitney1889 §495]

That is the 1.x book's "स् → त् in the oblique cases", withdrawn in lesson 2 (l02 §15 Step 2).
The 1.x list of twenty-two upasargas, which printed अति and उप twice and left out नि and अपि, was
put right on lesson 3's card (l03 §15). Neither correction is asked today; both stand on the page
of §15.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — what the chapter's three systems have in common, and why the exam
retrieves them by five routes.

No new fact about Sanskrit enters today; every form on the page is one the four lessons taught.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Whitney describes the pronouns of the first and second persons as "made
> up of fragments coming from various roots and combinations of roots", and adds: "They have no
> distinction of gender." The demonstrative त "may be taken as model of a mode of declension
> usual in so many pronouns and pronominal adjectives that it is fairly to be called the general
> pronominal declension." [REF:whitney1889 §491] [REF:whitney1889 §495]

So the two halves of T8 are two kinds of table. अहम् and त्वम् are learned cell by cell, with no
gender; सः, सा, तत् are learned once and then lent to a family — एतद्, यद्, किम्, सर्व, अन्य, एक —
each changing only what its card says. Task A asks for cells of both kinds.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Sanskrit combines declinable stems "to form compounds which then are
> treated as if simple, in respect to accent, inflection, and construction". [REF:whitney1889
> §1246]

That is the compound's whole economy: one word, one ending, the vigraha outside. Task C runs it
backwards, from the word to the phrase; task E runs it forwards, from your own phrase to a word.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Retrieval practice beats re-reading for long-term retention.
> [REF:roediger2006]

Each task retrieves the chapter by a different route: A from a noun to its pronoun cell, B from
two sentences to one, C from a compound to its phrase, D from a page you have never seen to its
meaning, E from your own intention to joined Sanskrit.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Formulaic chunks are taught whole and analysed later. [REF:nation2013]

This chapter was that "later" for मम, तव, मह्यम्, नमस्ते, कश्चित् and विद्यालयः; §15 (5) lists
where each was opened.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spaced practice, with gaps between sessions, is retained longer than the
> same practice massed together. [REF:cepeda2006]

That is why the exam opens with Station 0, and why §20 books two items for Chapter 6.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the chapter in four Sanskrit lines you own, each printed joined, its pause
form beneath, then the gloss:

> अहं त्वं स सा तच्च। (ahaṃ tvaṃ sa sā tac ca.)
> अहम् त्वम् सः सा तत् च। (aham tvam saḥ sā tat ca.)
> "I, you, he, she and it." — मैं, तुम, वह [पुरुष], वह [स्त्री] और वह [वस्तु]।

> अत्र तत्र यत्र कुत्र सर्वत्र। (atra tatra yatra kutra sarvatra.)
> अत्र तत्र यत्र कुत्र सर्वत्र। (atra tatra yatra kutra sarvatra.)
> "Here, there, where, where?, everywhere." — यहाँ, वहाँ, जहाँ, कहाँ, सब जगह।

> वर्णमाला वर्णानां माला। (varṇamālā varṇānāṃ mālā.)
> वर्णमाला वर्णानाम् माला। (varṇamālā varṇānām mālā.)
> "The varṇamālā is the garland of sounds." — वर्णमाला वर्णों की माला है।

> अद्य वयं सर्वं लिखामः। (adya vayaṃ sarvaṃ likhāmaḥ.)
> अद्य वयम् सर्वम् लिखामः। (adya vayam sarvam likhāmaḥ.)
> "Today we write everything." — आज हम सब कुछ लिखते हैं।

Four lines, one for each lesson. The first is lessons 1 and 2 in a row: अहम् and त्वम् take ं
before a consonant (v3.c04.l03), सः drops its ः before a consonant (v3.c04.l02), and तत् + च →
तच्च. The second is lesson 3's place row, with no seam at all: every word ends in a vowel before
a consonant. The third is lesson 4's vigraha said as a sentence: the compound on the left, its
phrase on the right, the genitive plural वर्णानाम् with ं before म. The fourth is today: वयम्, a
✓ cell of lesson 1, and सर्वम्, the neuter that leaves सः for रामः [l02 §15 Step 3]. The title
word परीक्षा stands only in the heading: read it whole; it is not a ledger word.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी में समझिए

आज कोई नया शब्द नहीं, कोई नया नियम नहीं। आज पाँचवें अध्याय की परीक्षा है — एक बैठक,

वाक्य के आरम्भ में कभी नहीं आते। साथ में भवान् और भवती — आदर वाला "आप" — जिनके साथ क्रिया

पतति जैसे वाक्य में यम् द्वितीया है और सः प्रथमा। चौथी — समास: पहला पद मूल रूप में, विभक्ति

वस्तु है?

परीक्षा में आपसे केवल वही बनवाया जाएगा जो तालिकाओं में "✓" है। "◐" वाले रूप — अहम् और
त्वम् के द्विवचन [आवाम्, युवाम्], बहुवचन के चतुर्थी, पञ्चमी और सप्तमी रूप, इदम् और सखा के
बाकी रूप — केवल पहचानने हैं; कोई कार्य उन्हें नहीं माँगता और उन पर अंक नहीं। द्विगु, उपपद और
अव्ययीभाव भी केवल पहचान के लिए हैं। जहाँ दो उत्तर दोनों सही हैं, उत्तर-कुंजी दोनों को मानती
है: लघु रूप या पूरा रूप [युष्मान् या वः], स्पर्श-व्यञ्जन से पहले अनुस्वार या उसी वर्ग का
नासिक्य, श, ष, स से पहले विसर्ग या दुगना ऊष्म, और यदा के साथ तदा या ततः।

हिन्दी यहाँ मदद भी करती है और धोखा भी देती है। मदद इसलिए कि जो … वह वाला वाक्य हिन्दी में
भी पहले सम्बन्धवाचक उपवाक्य रखता है, और विद्यालय, हिमालय, माता-पिता जैसे समास आप रोज़ बोलते
हैं। धोखा तीन जगह: हिन्दी "मुझे" एक है, पर संस्कृत में "मुझको" माम् है और "मुझे देता है"
मह्यम् [या मे]; हिन्दी माता-पिता योजक-चिह्न से लिखती है, संस्कृत मातापितरौ एक शब्द है, द्विवचन
में; और हिन्दी "हम" एक व्यक्ति के लिए भी चलता है, जबकि यह पाठ्यक्रम वयम् केवल तीन या अधिक के
लिए लिखता है — कार्य A का सातवाँ वाक्य यही परखता है।

आज के पाँच कार्य: A — आठ वाक्यों में कोष्ठक में बताई गई संज्ञा की जगह सही सर्वनाम-रूप, और एक प्रश्न
विभक्ति का [आठ मिनट]; B — छह वाक्य-युग्मों को सम्बन्धवाचक और सहसम्बन्धी से एक वाक्य बनाना,
जुड़े रूप में [सात मिनट]; C — दस समासों का प्रकार और विग्रह, विभक्तियों सहित [नौ मिनट]; D —
कबूतरों और चूहे पर एक नया जुड़ा अनुच्छेद, देखते ही पढ़कर दस प्रश्नों के उत्तर [दस मिनट]; E —
अतिथि से कम-से-कम छह पंक्तियों का संवाद, जुड़े रूप में, नीचे विराम-रूप सहित [नौ मिनट]। सबसे
पहले दो मिनट निर्देश। §19 की प्रश्नोत्तरी बैठक के बाद घर पर होती है।

## 8. English Explanation

Five tasks, one sitting, the whole chapter. The recipe of the chapter's grammar lessons runs one
last time, because today's page — the pronoun tables, the grid and the four kinds — is assembled
from what the four lessons already gave you.

### Step 1 — the scene

A morning at school, in two lines of joined print, every word yours:

> यदा गुरुरागच्छति तदा सर्वे छात्रा उपविशन्ति। (yadā gurur āgacchati tadā sarve chātrā upaviśanti.)
> यदा गुरुः आगच्छति तदा सर्वे छात्राः उपविशन्ति। (yadā guruḥ āgacchati tadā sarve chātrāḥ upaviśanti.)
> "When the teacher comes, all the students sit down." — जब गुरुजी आते हैं, तब सब छात्र बैठ जाते हैं।

> तस्य मातापितरौ भवन्तं पश्यतः। (tasya mātāpitarau bhavantaṃ paśyataḥ.)
> तस्य मातापितरौ भवन्तम् पश्यतः। (tasya mātāpitarau bhavantam paśyataḥ.)

consonant → ं.

### Step 4 — the pattern named

**Three systems, one chapter.**

1. **The pronouns (T8).** अहम् and त्वम्: every singular cell, the plural nominative,
   accusative, instrumental and genitive, and the short forms मे, ते, नः, वः — never first.
   भवान् and भवती in their listed cells, always with a verb in the third person. तद् in three
   genders, and its family: एतद् = ए + तद्; यद् = य-; किम् = क-, but neuter किम्; सर्व and एक,
   but neuter सर्वम्, एकम्; अन्य like तद् throughout, अन्यत्. इदम् in its key cells.
2. **The words that never change.** The grid: -त्र "where", -तः "from where", -दा "when", -था
   "how", on the stems क-, इ-/अ-, त-, य-, सर्व-. The relative sentence: the relative clause
   first, the correlative after; each pronoun in the case of its own clause; the pair agreeing
   with its noun. च, वा, हि, तु never first; अधः, उपरि, समीपे with the genitive.
3. **Compounds.** Stem first, ending last, the seam inside, the vigraha outside. Four kinds:
   द्वन्द्वः "X and Y", तत्पुरुषः a case relation, कर्मधारयः adjective and noun as one thing,
   बहुव्रीहिः someone who has it — found by the context test.

Every ✓ cell and kind is yours to produce; every ◐ cell is yours to read when met, never to
produce. That is the line the exam keeps.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — connected speech, exam-day checks; no new sounds. A seam is spoken without a pause;
a space in joined print is a word boundary, not a breath. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-3/v3c05l05-task-d-passage.mp3` — task D's ten lines read joined at
natural pace, in the order 1 → 10, once, one voice; played only after the sitting, for §21
item 6. Nothing is played in the sitting.

**The leaning words.** मे, ते, नः and वः are unaccented and lean on the word before them: नमो वः
is one group, *namo-vaḥ*; गुरुर्मे पिता is two, *gurur-me | pitā*. Read §16 turn 7 with no stop
before वः.

**The compound in one breath.** A compound is one word: मातापितरौ, नीलकण्ठः, चित्रग्रीवः,
प्रियमित्रम् — one movement each, no catch at the inner seam, every short अ sounded:
चि-त्र-ग्री-वः, four syllables, never "chitragriv".

**The relative sentence in two breath-groups.** Read a relative sentence as two groups, the
relative clause and the main clause, with no breath inside either and none at a space: यः पठति |
स जानाति [§16 turn 4]. Where a comma closes the relative clause, the pause comes there and
nowhere else.

**Task D, read silently.** Task D is read at sight in the sitting, silently. Read each joined
line once through to its daṇḍa before you answer: the commas and daṇḍas tell you where the
clauses are. After the sitting you read it aloud and record it (§21 item 6), then play the track
once with the printed text in front of you and mark every seam where your recording paused or
misread.

## 13. Examples

### Task A's kind — the noun becomes a pronoun

Read the sentence, ask gender, number and case, then write the cell.

> बालिका गृहं गच्छति। (bālikā gṛhaṃ gacchati.)
> बालिका गृहम् गच्छति। (bālikā gṛham gacchati.)
> "The girl goes home." — लड़की घर जाती है। — बालिका, f. sg. nom. → **सा**.

> अहं सीतया सह गच्छामि। (ahaṃ sītayā saha gacchāmi.)
> अहम् सीतया सह गच्छामि। (aham sītayā saha gacchāmi.)
> "I go with Sītā." — मैं सीता के साथ जाता हूँ। — सीतया, f. sg. instr. → **तया**.

> गुरुः शिष्याय पुस्तकं ददाति। (guruḥ śiṣyāya pustakaṃ dadāti.)
> गुरुः शिष्याय पुस्तकम् ददाति। (guruḥ śiṣyāya pustakam dadāti.)
> "The teacher gives the student a book." — गुरुजी शिष्य को पुस्तक देते हैं। — शिष्याय, m. sg.
> dat. → **तस्मै**. And the spiral question of the kind: which case is शिष्याय, and why? —
> चतुर्थी, the dative: the student is the receiver (v3.c01.l03).

> गुरुर्वदति — "हे छात्राः! छात्राणां पुस्तकान्यत्र सन्ति।" (gurur vadati — "he chātrāḥ! chātrāṇāṃ pustakāny atra santi.")
> गुरुः वदति — "हे छात्राः! छात्राणाम् पुस्तकानि अत्र सन्ति।" (guruḥ vadati — "he chātrāḥ! chātrāṇām pustakāni atra santi.")
> "The teacher says: 'Students! The students' books are here.'" — गुरुजी कहते हैं — "छात्रो!
> छात्रों की किताबें यहाँ हैं।" — the second छात्राणाम्, said to them → **युष्माकम्**, "your
> (pl.)"; पुस्तकानि वः, with the short form after a word, is as right.

### Task B's kind — two sentences, one

> कपोतः आकाशे अस्ति। तम् व्याधः पश्यति। (kapotaḥ ākāśe asti. tam vyādhaḥ paśyati.) — the pair, in pause form
> यः कपोत आकाशेऽस्ति तं व्याधः पश्यति। (yaḥ kapota ākāśe 'sti taṃ vyādhaḥ paśyati.)
> यः कपोतः आकाशे अस्ति तम् व्याधः पश्यति। (yaḥ kapotaḥ ākāśe asti tam vyādhaḥ paśyati.)
> "The hunter sees the dove that is in the sky." — जो कबूतर आकाश में है, उसे शिकारी देखता है।

यः is the subject of its clause, तम् the object of पश्यति: each in its own case (l03 §15, rule
2). The seams: यः before क keeps its ः; कपोतः before आ → कपोत and a gap; आकाशे + अस्ति → आकाशेऽस्ति;
तम् before व → तं.

> मूषकः आगच्छति। तदा कपोताः हसन्ति। (mūṣakaḥ āgacchati. tadā kapotāḥ hasanti.) — the pair, in pause form
> यदा मूषक आगच्छति तदा कपोता हसन्ति। (yadā mūṣaka āgacchati tadā kapotā hasanti.)
> यदा मूषकः आगच्छति तदा कपोताः हसन्ति। (yadā mūṣakaḥ āgacchati tadā kapotāḥ hasanti.)
> "When the mouse comes, the doves laugh." — जब चूहा आता है, तब कबूतर हँसते हैं।

**The relative sentence — three rules** (l03 §15): (1) the relative clause first, the
correlative after; the other order is good Sanskrit too [REF:whitney1889 §512]; (2) each pronoun

agree in gender and number with the noun they point to — यो मूषकः … स, या बालिका … सा, यत्फलं …
तत्. The pairs: यः/या/यत् … सः/सा/तत्; यत्र … तत्र; यदा … तदा [ततः accepted]; यथा … तथा. Where a
relative clause ends in -न्, a comma closes it before the correlative.

**The particles and the placed words, ✓** (l03 §15): च, वा, हि, तु never first; एव, इव, अपि after
the word they mark; अधः, उपरि, समीपे with the genitive.
