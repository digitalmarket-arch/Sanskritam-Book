## 1. Hook

Since Volume 1 you have said विद्यालयः, हिमालयः, वर्णमाला and मातृभाषा as single words.
Each of them is two words wearing one ending. Today you take them apart and put them back
together: विद्यालयः is विद्यायाः आलयः "the abode of learning". And one old puzzle of the
serial gets its answer. The king of the doves has a name, चित्रग्रीवः — but he is not a
neck. He is someone *whose* neck is bright. That one difference, "the thing itself" or
"someone who has it", is the key to the whole lesson.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — the serial's source names its heroes with compounds, and the
grammarians named the kinds of compound with compounds.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** The serial is our retelling after the frame story of the first
> book of the Hitopadeśa, the *Mitralābha*, a collection of fables in prose and verse
> attributed to Nārāyaṇa and dated by scholarly estimate to roughly the 9th–12th centuries
> CE. At his first appearance its dove-king is introduced with a compound name:
> *citragrīva-nāmā kapota-rājaḥ* "a dove-king named Citragrīva" (prose after Hit 1.4). The
> mouse, asked to cut the snares, answers with another: *aham alpa-śaktiḥ* "I am of little
> strength" (prose after Hit 1.41). [REF:hitopadesha mitralābha]

The e-text prints the members of a compound with hyphens, as this course's tables do; the
words around the names (*nāmā*, *kapota-rājaḥ*) are not built today. What matters is the two
names: चित्रग्रीवः and अल्पशक्तिः each describe someone, and neither means what its last
word means.

Two more compounds are today's own: सिंहमूषकौ, "the lion and the mouse" of the Volume 2
story, and नीलकण्ठः, "the blue-throated one". The first is "this *and* that"; the second
points away from itself, to someone who has a blue throat. The tradition defines exactly
these two kinds in one line each.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** Pāṇini defines the dvandva in two words — 2.2.29 चार्थे द्वन्द्वः,
> 'in the sense of च, a dvandva' — and the bahuvrīhi in two: 2.2.24 अनेकमन्यपदार्थे, 'two or
> more words, pointing to something else'. Three of the four names are examples of
> themselves: तत्पुरुषः 'his man', बहुव्रीहिः '(one who has) much rice', द्विगुः '(worth) two
> cows'; the fourth, अव्ययीभावः, is not one of its own kind: it means 'becoming an
> indeclinable'. [REF:ashtadhyayi 2.2.29] [REF:ashtadhyayi 2.2.24] [REF:whitney1889 §1263]
> [REF:whitney1889 §1293] [REF:whitney1889 §1312] [REF:whitney1889 §1313]

Read the two sūtras with the words you own: च "and" is in the first, so a dvandva is a
"च-compound"; पदार्थे is "in the sense of a word", and अन्य "other" tells you whose — a
bahuvrīhi points to some *other* word, outside it. The other sūtra numbers behind today's
kinds stay in the teacher's notes (§22); in this lesson the rest is "according to Pāṇini",
without a number.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** Hindi school textbooks list six kinds of समास, counting कर्मधारय
> and द्विगु separately; the Sanskrit tradition counts four, with those two inside तत्पुरुष.

§7 sorts eight Hindi words both ways.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — what a compound is, in a modern grammar's terms, and the same idea in
English and German.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** In Sanskrit, declinable stems combine "to form compounds which then are
> treated as if simple, in respect to accent, inflection, and construction". The members
> meet "according to the general rules for external combination", and a stem with strong and
> weak forms enters a compound as a prior member in its weak form. [REF:whitney1889 §1246]
> [REF:whitney1889 §1249]

That sentence holds three of today's four rules: one word (*treated as if simple*), one
ending (*inflection*: the compound declines as one word), and the inner seam (*external
combination*: the sandhi of Chapter 4, between members as between words).

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Whitney sorts Sanskrit compounds into three principal classes:
> copulative compounds, whose members would be joined by "and"; determinative compounds,
> whose prior member depends on the last, either in a case relation (dependent) or as a
> describing adjective (descriptive); and secondary adjective compounds, chief among them the
> possessives, which add the idea of *having*. The tradition's names map onto them: dvandva
> is the copulative, tatpuruṣa the determinative, with karmadhāraya its descriptive
> division, and bahuvrīhi the possessive. [REF:whitney1889 §1247] [REF:whitney1889 §1263]
> [REF:whitney1889 §1293]

| Whitney's class | the tradition's name | today's example |
|---|---|---|
| copulative | द्वन्द्वः | सिंहमूषकौ "the lion and the mouse" |
| determinative, dependent | तत्पुरुषः — the case-tatpuruṣa | विद्यालयः "the abode of learning" |
| determinative, descriptive | कर्मधारयः, a kind of तत्पुरुषः | नीलकमलम् "a blue lotus" |
| possessive | बहुव्रीहिः | नीलकण्ठः "one whose throat is blue" |

English and German build compounds too, and in both the last member is the head: a
*blackbird* is a kind of bird, and German *Fernseher*, "far-seer", is a television set. The
determinatives of Sanskrit work the same way: their part of speech is decided by the final
member (Whitney §1247), so a विद्यालयः is a kind of आलयः. English even has the possessive
kind: a *redhead* is not a head but someone who has red hair — exactly the step from
नीलकमलम् to नीलकण्ठः.

Compound accent — and the pair of readings that only accent tells apart — is
Volume 4's (v4.c04). No accent marks are printed in this course before Volume 5.

And the course principle that today redeems, with its one source: formulaic chunks are
taught whole and analysed later [REF:nation2013]. विद्यालयः has been a chunk since Volume 1;
today is its "later".

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the lesson in four Sanskrit lines you can read, each joined, its pause
form beneath:

> **द्वे पदे एकं पदं भवतः।** (dve pade ekaṃ padaṃ bhavataḥ.)
> **द्वे पदे एकम् पदम् भवतः।** (dve pade ekam padam bhavataḥ.)
> "Two words become one word." — दो पद एक पद बन जाते हैं।

> **विद्यालयो विद्याया आलयः।** (vidyālayo vidyāyā ālayaḥ.)
> **विद्यालयः विद्यायाः आलयः।** (vidyālayaḥ vidyāyāḥ ālayaḥ.)
> "A vidyālaya is the abode of learning." — विद्यालय विद्या का आलय है।

> **नीलकमलं कमलमेव। नीलकण्ठस्तु कण्ठो न।** (nīlakamalaṃ kamalam eva. nīlakaṇṭhas tu kaṇṭho na.)
> **नीलकमलम् कमलम् एव। नीलकण्ठः तु कण्ठः न।** (nīlakamalam kamalam eva. nīlakaṇṭhaḥ tu kaṇṭhaḥ na.)
> "A blue lotus is just a lotus. But Nīlakaṇṭha is not a throat." — नीलकमल कमल ही है। पर
> नीलकण्ठ गला नहीं है।

> **अल्पशक्तिः कः? यस्य शक्तिरल्पा सः।** (alpaśaktiḥ kaḥ? yasya śaktir alpā saḥ.)
> **अल्पशक्तिः कः? यस्य शक्तिः अल्पा सः।** (alpaśaktiḥ kaḥ? yasya śaktiḥ alpā saḥ.)
> "Who is alpaśakti? The one whose strength is little." — अल्पशक्ति कौन है? वह, जिसकी शक्ति
> थोड़ी है।

The first line is the definition, in the tradition's word पदम् "word" (v3.c04.l04). द्वे
पदे is a dual in -ए, so it never joins with the next vowel. The second line is a विग्रहः
(vigrahaḥ), "the taking apart": the compound on the left, its phrase with case endings on
the right. The third line is the lesson's test in two sentences — a blue lotus is a lotus,
but the blue-throated one is not a throat. The fourth is the relative frame of lesson 3,
यस्य … सः, doing a new job: every bahuvrīhi opens into it. The grammar terms समासः, विग्रहः
and the names of the kinds are GLOSSARY items, not ledger words.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

  चित्रग्रीवः है, \*चित्राग्रीवः नहीं।
- **विभक्ति केवल अन्तिम पद पर।** सिंहमूषकौ में औ [द्विवचन] अकेला अन्त में है।
- **भीतर भी सन्धि।** पर + उपकारः = परोपकारः, ठीक वैसे जैसे दो शब्दों के बीच।
- **समझ विग्रह से।** हर समास अपना विग्रह साथ लिए चलता है।

अब चार प्रकार। **द्वन्द्व** — "यह और वह": सिंहमूषकौ = सिंहः च मूषकः च [सिंह और चूहा]।
दो हों तो द्विवचन, अधिक हों तो बहुवचन, और लिंग अन्तिम पद का। **तत्पुरुष** — पहला पद
दूसरे से किसी विभक्ति के रिश्ते में: विद्यालयः = विद्यायाः आलयः [षष्ठी, "का"]। **कर्मधारय**
— विशेषण और विशेष्य, एक ही वस्तु: नीलकमलम् = नीलम् कमलम् [नीला कमल]। संस्कृत परम्परा
इसे तत्पुरुष का ही एक भेद मानती है। **बहुव्रीहि** — समास किसी और का वर्णन करता है, जो
समास के बाहर है: नीलकण्ठः = नीलः कण्ठः यस्य सः, "जिसका कण्ठ नीला है, वह" — शिव, या मोर।

पहचान की एक सीधी कसौटी है, प्रसंग-परीक्षा: समास वस्तु को ही बताता है, या किसी ऐसे को
जिसके पास वह वस्तु है? नीलकमल कमल ही है — कर्मधारय। नीलकण्ठ गला नहीं है, गले वाला है —
बहुव्रीहि। चित्रग्रीव भी गर्दन नहीं, कबूतरों का राजा है, जिसकी गर्दन रंगीन है।

अब हिन्दी से तुलना। हिन्दी में ये शब्द तत्सम हैं, और हिन्दी व्याकरण इन्हें अपनी परम्परा के
अनुसार छाँटता है [§4 में देखें — हिन्दी की स्कूली पाठ्यपुस्तकें छह भेद गिनती हैं, संस्कृत
परम्परा चार]:

| हिन्दी शब्द | हिन्दी व्याकरण का भेद | विग्रह [हिन्दी में] |
|---|---|---|
| राजपुत्र | तत्पुरुष | राजा का पुत्र |
| देशभक्ति | तत्पुरुष | देश की भक्ति |
| नीलकमल | कर्मधारय | नीला कमल |
| त्रिभुवन | द्विगु | तीन भुवनों का समाहार |
| पीताम्बर | बहुव्रीहि | पीला है अम्बर जिसका — कृष्ण |
| प्रतिदिन | अव्ययीभाव | हर दिन |
| यथाशक्ति | अव्ययीभाव | शक्ति के अनुसार |
| माता-पिता | द्वन्द्व | माता और पिता |

पीताम्बर को "पीला वस्त्र" पढ़ें तो वह कर्मधारय है; हिन्दी व्याकरण इसे दोहरे अर्थ का उदाहरण
बनाता है — यही हमारी प्रसंग-परीक्षा है। संस्कृत की परम्परा में कर्मधारय और द्विगु दोनों
तत्पुरुष के भीतर गिने जाते हैं, इसलिए वहाँ चार भेद हैं।

दो अन्तर ध्यान से देखिए। हिन्दी माता-पिता को योजक-चिह्न से लिखती है और उसमें द्विवचन
नहीं है [REF:kachru2006]; संस्कृत मातापितरौ एक शब्द है, द्विवचन में। और हिन्दी राजपुत्र में
राज- इसलिए है कि संस्कृत का राजन् समास में राज- बनकर आता है — पर राजन्, भक्ति, भुवन,
पीत, अम्बर अभी आपके संस्कृत शब्द नहीं हैं; ऊपर के आठों हिन्दी के शब्द हैं, जिन्हें आप
पहले से जानते हैं।

आज का अभ्यास संस्कृत में ही होगा: आप समास खोलेंगे [विग्रह], उसका प्रकार बताएँगे, और
अपने शब्दों से नये समास बनाएँगे — द्वन्द्व, तत्पुरुष, कर्मधारय और बहुव्रीहि। द्विगु,
उपपद और अव्ययीभाव आज केवल पहचान के लिए हैं; उन पर अंक नहीं।

## 8. English Explanation

One grammar point: a compound joins stems into one word, and is understood by its विग्रहः.
Four kinds, four rules, one test. No new table of endings: every ending you need today you
already own. The recipe is the volume's — a scene, the question, the forms you already own,
the pattern named, your table, production.

### Step 1 — the scene

The serial's names. Here is the line from today's episode (§17, line 2) that explains one of
them:

> **तस्य ग्रीवा चित्रा, अतः स चित्रग्रीवः।** (tasya grīvā citrā, ataḥ sa citragrīvaḥ.)
> **तस्य ग्रीवा चित्रा, अतः सः चित्रग्रीवः।** (tasya grīvā citrā, ataḥ saḥ citragrīvaḥ.)
> "His neck was bright: so he is Citragrīva." — उसकी गर्दन रंगीन थी, इसलिए वह चित्रग्रीव है।

Every word is yours or today's: ग्रीवा "neck" and चित्रः "bright" are new headwords; the
rest you own. Read the name in the light of the first half: चित्र + ग्रीवः.

### Step 2 — the question

What does a word made of two words mean, and how do you know which kind it is? Why is the
dove-king not called \*चित्राग्रीवा, when his neck is चित्रा ग्रीवा? Why does his name end in
-ः, like a masculine word, when ग्रीवा is feminine? And why is विद्यालयः "the abode *of*
learning", with an "of" that is nowhere in the word?

मातृ, not माता; रवि, not रविः. Third: where two members meet, the seam is a seam of Chapter 4:
हिम + आलयः → हिमालयः, पर + उपकारः → परोपकारः, मनस् + रथः → मनोरथः. Fourth: the meanings are
of different shapes. विद्यालयः is a kind of आलयः; पितरौ is two people; and the dove-king
चित्रग्रीवः is not a kind of neck.

### Step 4 — the pattern, named

**The four rules.**

1. **Stem first.** Every member but the last stands in its stem form, the dictionary's
   shape: मातृभाषा, not \*माताभाषा. An a-adjective goes in as its dictionary stem even where
   the vigraha shows its feminine: चित्रा ग्रीवा → चित्रग्रीवः, अल्पा शक्तिः → अल्पशक्तिः,
   शुभा रात्रिः → शुभरात्रिः.
2. **Ending last.** Only the last member is inflected: सिंहमूषकौ, not \*सिंहौमूषकौ.
3. **The seam inside.** Sandhi applies between members as between words: पर-उपकारः →
   परोपकारः [v3.c04.l01], मनस्-रथः → मनोरथः [v3.c04.l02]. In print a compound is one word;
   in this course's tables its members are shown with a hyphen.
4. **The vigraha outside.** A compound is understood through its विग्रहः, the phrase with
   case endings it compresses: विद्यालयः = विद्यायाः आलयः.

Two exceptions to rule 1 belong to this chapter, and you will see both: the dvandva of
माता and पिता begins माता- (मातापितरौ; according to Pāṇini, the first of two ṛ-stem kinship words in a
dvandva takes -आ), and महा- in महाभारतम् is a special compound form of the word for "great", not its
stem. Neither is a pattern to copy.

**The four kinds.** The tradition's names, each with its question:

- **द्वन्द्वः** "pair" — is it "X *and* Y"? सिंहमूषकौ = सिंहः च मूषकः च. The whole is dual
  for two, plural for more, in the gender of the last member.
- **तत्पुरुषः** "his man" — does the first member stand in a case relation to the second?
  विद्यालयः = विद्यायाः आलयः [genitive]. The whole names the second member.
- **कर्मधारयः** — is the first member an adjective describing the second, both one thing?
  नीलकमलम् = नीलम् कमलम्. The tradition files it as a kind of tatpuruṣa; its name is "of
  obscure application", as Whitney says, and no etymology is offered here.
- **बहुव्रीहिः** "(having) much rice" — does the compound describe someone or something
  else, named outside it? नीलकण्ठः = नीलः कण्ठः यस्य सः "he whose throat is blue".

**The context test.** Ask one question: does the compound name the thing itself, or someone
who has it? नीलकमलम् is a lotus: the thing itself — karmadhāraya. नीलकण्ठः is not a throat:
it is someone who has a blue throat — bahuvrīhi. So Step 2 is answered. चित्रग्रीवः is
someone whose neck is bright, and so it takes the gender of the one it describes, the
dove-king: masculine, चित्रग्रीवः. विद्यालयः is the thing itself, an आलयः; the "of" is the
genitive its vigraha restores.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — no new sounds; the work is the compound said as one word. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-3/v3c05l04-model-sentences.mp3` (the six model sentences, then
the drill words).

**Drill 1 — one breath.** A compound is one word: one breath, no pause at the inner seam.
Say विद्यालयः, मातापितरौ, नीलकण्ठः, चित्रग्रीवः, सिंहमूषकौ, each in one movement, as you say
a long simple word. The seam of परोपकारः is a vowel, ओ, not a gap: never \*पर-उपकारः with a
catch in the throat.

**Drill 2 — the Hindi pause.** Hindi says "माता-पिता" with a small break, and prints a hyphen.
Do not carry it over: मातापितरौ has no break, and its last sound is the glide /ɐu̯/ of the
dual. Say the pair: माता पिता [two Hindi words] — मातापितरौ [one Sanskrit word].

**Drill 3 — compound and vigraha.** Say each compound, then its vigraha, at speaking pace:
the compound in one breath, the vigraha as joined speech. विद्यालयः — विद्याया आलयः;
नीलकण्ठः — नीलः कण्ठो यस्य सः. The vigraha has seams between words; the compound has one
seam inside, and no pause at either.

**Drill 4 — the long words.** Clap the syllables: उ-दा-र-च-रि-तः [six], अ-ल्प-श-क्तिः — say
it al-pa-śak-tiḥ, four, the ल closing the first syllable and the प opening the second. Keep every short अ full; a Hindi
speaker's habit of dropping it would turn चित्रग्रीवः into "chitragriv".

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — three checks, one mirror.

1. **The retroflex pair (कण्ठः).** Curl the tongue tip back behind the ridge for ण, and
   release it from there for ठ with a puff of air. If the tip touches the teeth, you have
   said a dental "kanth".
2. **The two places of नीलकण्ठः.** Start with the tongue on the upper teeth for न, then move
   it back for ण्ठ. Watch in the mirror: the jaw barely moves; the tongue does the travel.
3. **The dual glide (मातापितरौ, सिंहमूषकौ).** Open the jaw for /ɐ/ and round the lips toward
   /u/ in one movement. If the lips do not move, you said a long ओ: \*मातापितरो is not a dual.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्याः भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix; these feed the quiz distractors.

1. **The first member inflected: \*माताभाषा, \*नीलःकमलम्, \*चित्राग्रीवः.** The phrase
   copied into the compound. *Fix:* rule 1, the stem first — मातृभाषा, नीलकमलम्,
   चित्रग्रीवः. This chapter has two exceptions, both read, not copied: the dvandva
   मातापितरौ, and the label महा- (§8 Step 4).
2. **The dvandva in the singular: \*सिंहमूषकः for the two of them.** *Fix:* a dvandva of two
   is dual, in the gender of its last member — सिंहमूषकौ.
3. **A bahuvrīhi read as a tatpuruṣa: नीलकण्ठः "a blue throat".** *Fix:* the context test.
   Said of Śiva or of the peacock, it is "one whose throat is blue" — नीलः कण्ठः यस्य सः.
4. **The inner seam left open: \*पर-उपकारः printed as the word.** *Fix:* rule 3 — परोपकारः.
   The hyphen belongs to the course's tables of members, not to the word.
5. **The vigraha without cases: \*विद्या आलयः.** Two stems laid side by side, nothing
   connecting them. *Fix:* give each member its case — विद्यायाः आलयः, the genitive.
6. **A bahuvrīhi given the gender of its last member: \*चित्रग्रीवा for the dove-king,
   because ग्रीवा is feminine.** *Fix:* the compound agrees with the one it describes:
   चित्रग्रीवः, masculine, for the king; नीलकण्ठः, masculine, for Śiva.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — four rules, four kinds, one test, and a recognition card. Work through it in
order; §8 Step 5's frame is the page you check here.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** A compound is one word: every member but the last stands in its stem
> form, only the last is inflected, and members meet by the rules that join separate words.
> Its sense is given by resolving it into the phrase it compresses, each member with its own
> case. [REF:whitney1889 §1246] [REF:whitney1889 §1247] [REF:whitney1889 §1249]

### Step 1 — the four rules

| rule | what it says | owned example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. stem first | every member but the last in its dictionary shape; an a-adjective as its dictionary stem | मातृभाषा, not \*माताभाषा · चित्रा ग्रीवा → चित्रग्रीवः |
| 2. ending last | only the last member takes the ending | सिंहमूषकौ, विद्यालयः, पाशबद्धाः |
| 3. the seam inside | Chapter 4's sandhi between members | हिम-आलयः → हिमालयः · पर-उपकारः → परोपकारः · मनस्-रथः → मनोरथः |
| 4. the vigraha outside | the compound is understood through its phrase | विद्यालयः = विद्यायाः आलयः |

The exceptions to rule 1 met in this chapter: माता- in the dvandva मातापितरौ, and the
special form महा- in महाभारतम्. Both are read whole.

### Step 2 — coining: from vigraha to compound

The four rules run backwards make a compound. Take a vigraha, strip the endings from every
member but the last, put the stems in order, join the inner seam, and give the last member
the ending the sentence needs:

| vigraha (pause form) | strip and order | join the seam | the compound |
|---|---|---|---|
| सिंहः च मूषकः च | सिंह · मूषक | — | सिंहमूषकौ, dual for two |
| जलस्य पात्रम् | जल · पात्र | — | जलपात्रम् |
| परस्य उपकारः | पर · उपकार | अ + उ → ओ | परोपकारः |
| चित्रम् वनम् | चित्र · वन | — | चित्रवनम् |
| अल्पा बुद्धिः यस्य सः | अल्प · बुद्धि | — | अल्पबुद्धिः, masculine, for "he" |

In the last row the adjective goes in as अल्प-, its dictionary stem, though the vigraha
shows अल्पा; and the compound ends in -ः because the one it describes is "he".

gender of its last member [REF:whitney1889 §1253]; a dvandva can also be a neuter singular

case-tatpuruṣa the whole names the second member, and the first can stand in any case
relation: the genitive in eleven of today's thirteen, the instrumental in पाशबद्धाः and
(or the genitive) in अमृततुल्यम् [REF:whitney1889 §1264]. A karmadhāraya's two members are one thing, so in its
vigraha both stand in the same case [REF:whitney1889 §1279]. A bahuvrīhi opens into the
relative frame of lesson 3, यस्य … सः, and in a sentence it takes the gender of the one it
describes [REF:whitney1889 §1293].

### Step 4 — the context test

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** A determinative compound names a kind of its last member; a possessive
> compound made of the same members adds the idea of *having*, and describes someone or
> something outside it. Whitney's example: *bṛhadratha* as a descriptive is "great chariot";
> as a possessive it is "having great chariots" [the same letters; accented texts tell the two
> apart by accent, Volume 4's topic]. [REF:whitney1889 §1293]

So ask: does the compound name the thing itself, or someone who has it?

| compound | the thing itself? | so it is |
|---|---|---|
| नीलकमलम् | yes — it is a lotus | कर्मधारयः |
| विद्यालयः | yes — it is an abode | तत्पुरुषः |
| नीलकण्ठः, said of Śiva | no — he has a blue throat | बहुव्रीहिः |
| चित्रग्रीवः, the dove-king | no — he has a bright neck | बहुव्रीहिः |
| अल्पशक्तिः, the mouse's word for himself | no — he has little strength | बहुव्रीहिः |
| सिंहमूषकौ | two things, "and" | द्वन्द्वः |

The test needs the context. Out of a sentence, a compound like अल्पशक्तिः could also be read
"little strength", a karmadhāraya; in the mouse's mouth, अहमल्पशक्तिः, it can only be "one
whose strength is little". Where a compound has two readings in earnest, the case is
Volume 4's (v4.c04).

### Step 6 — labels, not kinds

These words you own carry labels, so that rule 1 and the four kinds are not misapplied:

- **सुप्रभातम्** [v2.c01.l03] is a **प्रादि** compound: सु "well" + प्रभातम्. The tradition
  files it with the prefixes, not as a karmadhāraya. Read the label; the kind is Volume 4's.
- **अहिंसा** [v2.c06.l03] has the negative अ- first: "a tatpuruṣa whose first member is the

- **जगदीशः** and **सज्जनः**, read whole in v3.c04.l03, are a tatpuruṣa (जगतः ईशः, "lord of

## 20. Revision

पुनरावृत्तिः — one screen to remember.

> **A compound** (समासः) is one word: **stem first** (मातृभाषा), **ending last**
> (सिंहमूषकौ), **the seam inside** (परोपकारः, मनोरथः), **the vigraha outside** (विद्यालयः =
> विद्यायाः आलयः). **Four kinds:** द्वन्द्वः "X and Y", dual or plural — सिंहः च मूषकः च;
> तत्पुरुषः, a case relation — विद्यायाः आलयः; कर्मधारयः, adjective + noun, one thing —
> नीलम् कमलम्; बहुव्रीहिः, someone who has it — नीलः कण्ठः यस्य सः. **The context test:**
> the thing itself, or someone who has it? A bahuvrīhi takes the gender of the one it
> describes: चित्रग्रीवः. **Pāṇini:** 2.2.29 चार्थे द्वन्द्वः · 2.2.24 अनेकमन्यपदार्थे.
> **Read, not scored:** द्विगुः [सप्ताहः], उपपदम् [जलजम्, खगः, पादपः], अव्ययीभावः
> [यथाशक्ति]. **The ledger:** eight new — 488; cells drilled: 0; 24 compounds resolved.

**Whitney's classes.** §5 prints Whitney's three classes beside the tradition's names
[REF:whitney1889 §1247]: copulative = dvandva; determinative = tatpuruṣa, with its
descriptive division the karmadhāraya [REF:whitney1889 §1263]; possessive = bahuvrīhi
[REF:whitney1889 §1293]. Whitney classes the dvigu and the avyayībhāva as uses of other
classes, "reckoned and named as such by the Hindu grammarians" (§1247j); the lesson keeps
the tradition's names because the learner will meet them in the commentaries.

**Why सुप्रभातम् is a label, not a quiz item.** Whitney lists *súkṛta* "well done" among his
descriptive compounds (§1247e, §1279a), while the tradition files su- compounds under 2.2.18
as prādi. Both are defensible descriptions, so no item asks "karmadhāraya or not?" of
सुप्रभातम्; the lesson prints the tradition's label and leaves the kind to v4.c04.

**Compound accent (IR-026).** The 1.x book's इन्द्रशत्रु example — a tatpuruṣa "slayer of
Indra" against a bahuvrīhi "whose slayer is Indra", told apart by accent — is v4.c04's. §5
says so in one line, without the word; do not teach it now.

**The 1.x pages on compounds (pp. 42–43, 82–84), line by line.** (1) "राम + सीता = रामसीतौ": a
dvandva of two named things takes the gender of its last member [REF:whitney1889 §1253], and
Pāṇini says the same — 2.4.26 परवल्लिङ्गं द्वन्द्वतत्पुरुषयोः [REF:ashtadhyayi 2.4.26] — so राम +
सीता gives रामसीते. The lesson uses सीतारामौ in §18 exercise 3, in the order of its vigraha
सीता च रामः च. (2) "उप + नगर = उपनगर", glossed "Near city = suburb", as the avyayībhāva: an
avyayībhāva is an indeclinable, उपनगरम् "near the city" — compare Whitney's *upanadam* "near
the river" [REF:whitney1889 §1313]; "suburb", a noun, is not that kind. The lesson's
avyayībhāva is यथाशक्ति. (3) "राज + पुत्र": the stem is राजन्, which enters a compound as राज-,
as §7 says. (4) "दश + आनन = दशानन": as a word, दशाननः, a bahuvrīhi — दश आननानि यस्य सः.
(5) The 1.x accent example (p. 82) glosses both readings of इन्द्रशत्रु alike, "May Indra's
slayer prosper" and "May the slayer of Indra prosper"; the tatpuruṣa is "slayer of Indra", the
bahuvrīhi "whose slayer is Indra", as the note above has it. (6) The 1.x page's four kinds (pp. 42–43)
are not the lesson's: it lists the avyayībhāva as its fourth kind and has no karmadhāraya. The
lesson's four are the dvandva, the tatpuruṣa, the karmadhāraya and the bahuvrīhi, and the
avyayībhāva is read on the recognition card (§15 Step 5). (7) The 1.x lesson "6 Samāsa Types"
(pp. 83–84) is headed "All compound types Panini classifies" and drills six kinds in a fixed
order, although it calls the karmadhāraya an "appositional Tatpuruṣa" and the dvigu a
"numerical Karmadhāraya". Pāṇini names both as kinds of tatpuruṣa — 1.2.42 तत्पुरुषः
समानाधिकरणः कर्मधारयः [REF:ashtadhyayi 1.2.42]; 2.1.23 द्विगुश्च [REF:ashtadhyayi 2.1.23] — so
the tradition counts four — the avyayībhāva, the tatpuruṣa, the bahuvrīhi and the dvandva — as §4 says; six is the count of the Hindi school textbooks (§4,
§7). The same pages gloss उपनगरम् "near the city = suburb" again: see (2).

**जगदीशः, सज्जनः.** v3.c04.l03 read them whole and sent their analysis here (its §27). §15
Step 6 labels them; जगतः and सन् [the masculine nominative of सत्] are display forms, not drilled.

**The context test needs a context.** Out of a sentence, अल्पशक्तिः, नीलकण्ठः, शुभरात्रिः
and प्रियमित्रम् can each be read two ways. Every prompt in §18 and §19 therefore gives the
compound's use. When a learner offers the other reading of a bare compound, accept the
reasoning and ask for the context.

**The Hindi six-kind sorting.** §4 and §7 print the six kinds of the Hindi school textbooks
as attributed TRADITION, with no key. Pass C confirmed the six-kind count on the web in
current school material but could not confirm Guru's own count, so [REF:guru1952] is not
attached to it. The Hindi facts (the hyphen of माता-पिता, no dual) cite [REF:kachru2006]. Learners trained in Hindi grammar will call कर्मधारय and द्विगु kinds
of their own; that is right in Hindi grammar. In Sanskrit work, accept "कर्मधारयः" and
"तत्पुरुषः" for a karmadhāraya.

**The serial.** Episode 5 tells again, with names, the day the serial already passed in
v3.c03.l03–l04: "the same day, told again with names". Its departures from the Hitopadeśa
[REF:hitopadesha mitralābha], line by line in the GRETIL e-text: in the text the mouse,
recognising the voice, comes out and says *puṇyavān asmi priya-suhṛn me citragrīvaḥ
samāyātaḥ* (l. 474); ours says "स्वागतम्, प्रियमित्र!". The text has him see the doves
*pāśa-baddhāṃś* (l. 479); ours puts पाशबद्धाः in the king's mouth. *aham alpa-śaktiḥ |
dantāś ca me komalāḥ* (l. 492) is the text's, printed joined at the comma by the course's
stop rule. The text's *tathāpi yathā-śakti bandhanam eteṣāṃ khaṇḍaya* (l. 494) becomes
यथाशक्ति पाशान्कृन्त. The text frees the doves with an absolutive and a passive (*evam uktvā
tena sarveṣāṃ kapotānāṃ bandhanāni chinnāni*, l. 531); ours uses the active imperfect,
ततो हिरण्यकः … पाशानकृन्तत्. The closing dvandva चित्रग्रीवहिरण्यकौ प्रियमित्रे अभवताम् is
ours. The text calls the dove-king by name from his first appearance (*citragrīva-nāmā
kapota-rājaḥ*, l. 307); the serial named no one before this chapter. These departures are
on the record for v3.c06.l02, which reads the text itself.

**Differentiation.** Hindi-medium learners know these words already and want to stop at the
Hindi sorting: hold them to the Sanskrit vigraha with its case endings, and give them §12
mistake 5 first. English speakers grasp the bahuvrīhi through *redhead* at once but write
the first member inflected (\*नीलःकमलम्): give them §15 Step 2's coining table on the
worksheet (`planned:assets/worksheets/volume-3/v3c05l04-vigraha.pdf`) before exercise 3.

**What students find hard:** the bahuvrīhi's gender (\*चित्रग्रीवा); the adjective's stem
in the compound (\*चित्राग्रीवः); the vigraha's cases, left out (\*विद्या आलयः); and the
dvandva's number. Answer all four from §15 Steps 1–3, not from new rules.
