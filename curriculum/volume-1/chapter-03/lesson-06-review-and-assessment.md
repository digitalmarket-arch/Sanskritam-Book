## 1. Hook

Five lessons ago the 5×5 grid of stops was a preview on a map — the course's second lesson
showed it to you and promised it would one day be yours. Today it is: twenty-five letters,
five places, five manners, every cell earned letter by letter. The readable word list has
crossed ninety-five — मम to भोजन — and, for the first time in this course, you have read
complete Sanskrit sentences: बालकः चलति। कोकिलः कूजति। Nothing new enters this lesson.
Today you prove the grid.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — one paragraph, as in every review lesson.

The tradition that carried these twenty-five sounds across three millennia did it with
exactly this tool — scheduled, structured repetition, recited in varga order, tested from
memory — and memory science confirms the mechanism:

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Spaced practice produces far more durable learning than the same
> practice massed together. [REF:cepeda2006]

Chapter 3's material re-surfaces on schedule from here to the Volume 1 exam. Today is its
first interval; Chapter 2's second interval (Station 0) just ran inside it.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — this time, a word about *leverage*.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Retrieval practice strengthens memory more than re-reading — the
> testing effect. [REF:roediger2006]

There is a second reason today matters more than an ordinary review. The grid is not a
list of twenty-five facts; it is an engine with two axes, and both keep working after this
chapter. Chapter 4 has only eight letters left — the semivowels य र ल व and the sibilant
row श ष स plus ह — and every one of them will be taught by *location on the place-axis you
certify today*: श sits at the palatal station, ष at the retroflex, स at the dental.
Chapter 5's conjuncts are grid-pairs — two letters you already own, welded, like the स्त
inside नमस्ते. Certify place and manner now and both chapters become cheap. Let an error
survive today — a [z]-flavored ज, an alveolar त — and it gets rehearsed into every
syllable and every conjunct that follows, at ten times the repair cost.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — the complete stop grid one last time, as your scoring reference for task C.
Values are the course contract (STYLE_GUIDE §2). Columns, left to right, are the manner
row: voiceless · voiceless aspirated · voiced · voiced breathy · nasal — the same five
slots in every varga.

| वर्गः | स्थानम् · Place | Letters | IPA row |
|---|---|---|---|
| क-वर्ग | कण्ठ्य · velar | क ख ग घ ङ | /k kʰ g gʱ ŋ/ |
| च-वर्ग | तालव्य · palatal | च छ ज झ ञ | /t͡ɕ t͡ɕʰ d͡ʑ d͡ʑʱ ɲ/ |
| ट-वर्ग | मूर्धन्य · retroflex | ट ठ ड ढ ण | /ʈ ʈʰ ɖ ɖʱ ɳ/ |
| त-वर्ग | दन्त्य · dental | त थ द ध न | /t̪ t̪ʰ d̪ d̪ʱ n̪/ |
| प-वर्ग | ओष्ठ्य · labial | प फ ब भ म | /p pʰ b bʱ m/ |

Row-by-row self-diagnosis before you record:

- **क-वर्ग:** aspiration discipline — "sky"-k for क, never "kite"-k; ख must throw the
  tissue → l01 §9.
- **च-वर्ग:** kill the English lip-rounding; no [z] anywhere → l02 §9, §12.
- **ट-वर्ग:** the curl — tip back to the roof, crisp release → l03 §9.
- **त-वर्ग:** tip ON the teeth, not the ridge → l04 §9, §11.
- **प-वर्ग:** फ is a puffed stop, never [f]; भ hums and breathes at once → l05 §9.

Warm-up: one row top to bottom (क ख ग घ ङ), one voiceless column throat-to-lips
(क च ट त प), tissue up — then record.

## 10. IPA

No new values. Self-scoring reminders: each aspirate letter is ONE sound, not stop-plus-h
(ख /kʰ/, one beat); breathy voice must be transcribed and heard (मेघ /meːgʱɐ/ — drop the
ʱ and you have written a different letter); affricates keep the tie-bar (जल /d͡ʑɐlɐ/,
never [z]); retroflex and dental are different symbol sets (घट /gʱɐʈɐ/ vs कथा /kɐt̪ʰɑː/);
फल is /pʰɐlɐ/ — no [f] exists in the inventory; and the milestone sentences in pause form:
बालकः चलति /bɑːlɐkɐh t͡ɕɐlɐt̪i/, final visarga voiceless.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the four physical checks. These are the scoring rubric for task C: every
reading error should be caught by one of them.

- **Tissue (aspiration):** tissue before the lips. क barely stirs it; ख throws it. Same
  test separates त/थ, ट/ठ, च/छ, प/फ → l01 §9.
- **Throat-hand (voicing):** fingers flat on the larynx. ग hums from the first instant;
  क starts silent. घ hums AND moves the tissue — murmured breath on a voiced sound → l01 §11.
- **Nose-pinch (nasality):** pinch the nose shut. ङ ञ ण न म die instantly; the other
  twenty letters sail through → each varga lesson's §11.
- **Fingertip-on-teeth (place):** fingertip on the upper front teeth. त थ द ध न tap it;
  ट ठ ड ढ ण curl away to the roof. English t/d hit the ridge behind it — touching neither
  → l04 §11.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — two worked model answers before you start.

**Model dictation item (task B).** Audio says *bhojana*. You hear: an initial stop that
hums AND breathes — voiced breathy, labial row, fourth slot: भ. Then the ओ vowel — mātrā
ो. Then the affricate ज — never [z] — then dental न. Build it block-by-block: भ + ो = भो,
then ज, then न → **भोजन**. Common wrong answers: बोजन (breath dropped — §12 row 2), भोझन
(aspiration imagined on the ज — row 1's mirror image).

**Model register sort (task D).** Card: "खग means 'sky-goer' — ख as 'sky', ग as 'going'."
It *sounds* like poetry, so the reflex says SYMBOL — but check it: both elements are real
lexical items, the derivation is recorded in the dictionaries, the claim is checkable
[REF:apte1890 s.v. खग]. **FACT box.** Now the trap version: "ख is the sound of open space
itself, and every word containing it carries sky-energy." That is sound-symbolism stated
as a property of the sound — as worldview it could live in SYMBOL; as phonology it is
**rejected**. Same letter, two claims — telling them apart is the whole skill.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — the script laws restated, plus the new one this chapter earned.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** **Law 1 — inherent vowel:** an unmarked consonant reads with अ.
> **Law 2 — mātrā:** any other vowel after a consonant is written as a sign. **Law 3 —
> mātrā geography:** the sign's position is fixed by the script, regardless of when the
> vowel is spoken. **Law 4 — the leaning signs:** ं and ः attach to the whole akṣara
> (ayogavāha). **Law 5 — the grid:** every stop is fully named by place × manner — five
> places, five manners, twenty-five letters, no exceptions; and the homorganic-nasal rule
> of v1.c02.l06 now has all five nasal letters to point to. [REF:whitney1889 §§39–50]
> [REF:masica1991 ch.6]
