## 1. Hook

Six chapters ago you could not read one letter of this page. Today your stock is 237
words, the whole writing system answers to your pen, and yesterday you read a connected
passage about a boy who reads. One thing is missing, and it is the thing Sanskrit exists
for: **literature**. Today the course hands you three real texts — a line from a fable
book, a line a nation put on its emblem, and a verse the tradition has recited for
centuries — each labeled with exactly the honesty it deserves. Then the Volume Exam.
Bring everything; it all counts today.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतेन विवरणम् — the two lines of literature, fully readable:

> **विद्या ददाति विनयम्।**
> (vidyā dadāti vinayam.) — "Knowledge gives humility." — विद्या विनय देती है।

> **सत्यम् एव जयते।**
> (satyam eva jayate.) — "Truth alone prevails." — सत्य ही जीतता है।

Every word glossed whole, as always: ददाति "gives", एव "alone, indeed", जयते
"prevails". Pause form, daṇḍa honored — and notice that both lines run on words this
volume taught: विद्या and सत्य came home in Chapter 5; विनयम् wears lesson 1-of-5's
virāma; जयते rides Chapter 2's ए. Literature was the plan all along.

## 8. English Explanation

Three texts, three registers — then the exam architecture.

### Text one — a line from a fable book

**विद्या ददाति विनयम्** (vidyā dadāti vinayam) "knowledge gives humility."

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** This is the opening pāda of a celebrated verse in the *Hitopadeśa*
> — the fable collection's prologue (prastāvikā). The full verse chains onward (humility
> → worthiness → wealth → dharma → happiness), with joining changes between words; the
> chain awaits your sandhi (Volume 3). The first pāda, in pause form, has none: three
> words, zero joins. [REF:hitopadesha]

Your first line of real literature — and deliberately so: every word certified, no
sandhi, a complete thought. Read it cold in task C.

### Text two — the line on the emblem

**सत्यम् एव जयते** (satyam eva jayate) "truth alone prevails."

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** The line is from the Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad — satyam eva jayate,
> Muṇḍaka 3.1.6. In 1950 it was adopted as the national motto of India, inscribed
> beneath the State Emblem — which is why you have seen it on every coin and banknote.
> [REF:mundaka 3.1.6] [REF:stateemblem1950]

Two displays, one honesty note: in the source and on the emblem it is written joined —
**सत्यमेव जयते** — because म् + ए fuse in connected writing. You can already see the
seam: सत्यम् + एव. That joining is sandhi, Volume 3's subject; today read the pause
form, and *recognize* the joined one.

### Text three — the benediction with no address

**सर्वे भवन्तु सुखिनः सर्वे सन्तु निरामयाः।**
**सर्वे भद्राणि पश्यन्तु मा कश्चिद् दुःखभाग् भवेत्॥**

"May all be happy; may all be free of illness; may all see good things; may no one
partake of sorrow."

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** This maṅgala (benediction) verse is recited across India at
> the close of gatherings and recitation sessions. Its source text is **unrecorded**: it
> is often attributed to the Upaniṣads, but no principal Upaniṣad contains it. We label
> it *traditional* because that is what the evidence supports — and that honest label is
> itself today's register lesson.

Reading notes, honestly: the verse as recited carries two joining changes — कश्चिद् and
दुःखभाग् — whose pause forms are कश्चित् and दुःखभाक् (the द्/ग् you see are sandhi,
Volume 3). Every component is certified: सर्वे wears Chapter 5's repha, भवन्तु सन्तु
पश्यन्तु carry the न्त weld and श्य, भद्राणि the द्र, कश्चित् the श्च, दुःख its
mid-word visarga. Glosses stay whole-word ("भवन्तु — may they be"); the verb forms are
Volume 2-and-beyond machinery, used today, taught later.

### The śloka shape — count it yourself

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The verse above is a śloka — Sanskrit literature's workhorse verse
> form: **four pādas (quarters) of eight syllables each, thirty-two in all**. You do not
> need a reference for this: count them (§13 shows how; task E makes you). Meter theory
> proper — why some syllables sit where they sit — is Volume 5's subject.

The §19 quiz (10 questions) completes it: §18 proves you can *do*; §19 proves you know
*what is so*. Pass = every task at partial-or-better (rubric §22) and quiz 7+/10.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — verse delivery, honestly framed. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c06l04-shloka-recitation.mp3` (the maṅgala verse at
three speeds + the two literature lines), 🔊 reading-ladder and dictation tracks (§27).

**Read, don't sing — yet.** Traditional recitation carries melodic contours you have
heard all your life. Today's target is plainer and harder: *read* the verse in pause
form, every word complete, every syllable equal-weight. The melody belongs to
recitation practice (TRADITION register, and welcome at home); the exam scores the
reading.

**The pāda pause.** Read the śloka one pāda at a time — a hair of silence at each
quarter's end, a full daṇḍa-stop at each half: सर्वे भवन्तु सुखिनः ‖ सर्वे सन्तु
निरामयाः ॥ The 8-beat pulse will assert itself; let it — that pulse is the meter you
will study in Volume 5, arriving early through your own mouth.

**The seam discipline under pressure.** Verse tempo tempts old errors back: keep
भवन्तु's न्त welded, पश्यन्तु's श्य one onset, भद्राणि's द्र riding the round द, and
every final अ full — *bhavantu*, never "bhavant".

**The two literature lines.** विद्या ददाति विनयम् — three words, three beats, daṇḍa.
सत्यम् एव जयते — pause form first; then read the joined सत्यमेव जयते once, eyes open at
the seam.

**Warm-up before recording:** the milestone line (नमस्ते! अहम् अत्र अस्मि।), the trio
(धर्म कर्म अर्थ), one long word (संस्कृतम्), then pāda 1 slowly.

then the breath; and जयते ends on a steady long /eː/, no glide.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the volume's consolidated circuit; these checks score task C. Six
families, one check each — every reading error today should be caught by one of them.

- **Length (Ch 1–2):** मा vs म — hold long vowels two beats; निरामयाः has three long
  vowels in a row.
- **Station (Ch 3–4):** sibilants at their posts — पश्यन्तु's श palatal, सुखिनः's स
  dental; every ह voiced, every visarga voiceless.
- **Seams (Ch 5):** no vowel inside भवन्तु's न्त or विद्या's द्या; jaw still at each weld.
- **Repha timing (Ch 5):** सर्वे — tongue taps before the lips shape व, though the eye
  meets the hook late.
- **Finals (Ch 5):** भवेत् and विनयम् end closed — no echo vowel after त् or म्.
- **The pulse (today):** eight beats per pāda, a hair of silence between pādas — count
  on your fingers once, then trust the ear.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — two worked models before you start.

**Model syllable count (task E).** Take pāda 1: सर्वे भवन्तु सुखिनः. Count *spoken*
syllables, not written blocks: सर्-वे (2) भ-वन्-तु (3) सु-खि-नः (3) — **eight**. Now
pāda 4 in its recited form: मा (1) कश्-चिद् (2) दुःख-भाग् — careful: दुः-ख-भाग् (3) —
भ-वेत् (2) — **eight**. Four pādas, eight each, thirty-two: you have just verified the
śloka form with no authority but your own count. (Trap in row 4 of §12: a conjunct like
श्चि is one syllable — the eye sees a stack, the ear hears one beat.)

**Model register sort (task E card).** Card: "सर्वे भवन्तु सुखिनः is from the
Upaniṣads." It *sounds* right — the verse has the cadence and the company of Upaniṣadic
lines, and half the internet says so. But the claim names a source, so it must survive a
source-check, and it does not: no principal Upaniṣad contains the verse. **Reject** —
and replace, don't just delete: "a traditional maṅgala verse, source unrecorded" is the
honest full credit. Compare: "सत्यम् एव जयते is Muṇḍaka 3.1.6 and India's national
motto" — checkable, checked, twice true: **FACT/HISTORY**, with both references in
hand. Two verses, two verdicts — the four-register skill, now graduating with you.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The complete writing system in six laws: **1** inherent vowel ·

## 17. Story

कथा — समाप्तिः · The Completion. Six lines; each line: Devanagari, IAST, English,
Hindi. Every word self-read.

1. अहम् अत्र अस्मि। एतत् पुस्तकम् अस्ति।
   (aham atra asmi. etat pustakam asti.) "I am here. This is the book." — मैं यहाँ हूँ।
   यह पुस्तक है।
2. अहम् पठामि — विद्या ददाति विनयम्।
   (aham paṭhāmi — vidyā dadāti vinayam.) "I read: 'Knowledge gives humility.'" — मैं
   पढ़ता हूँ — विद्या ददाति विनयम्।
3. आचार्यः वदति — सत्यम् एव जयते।
   (ācāryaḥ vadati — satyam eva jayate.) "The teacher says: 'Truth alone prevails.'" —
   आचार्य कहते हैं — सत्यम् एव जयते।
4. बालिका हसति। छात्रः हसति। अहम् अपि हसामि।
   (bālikā hasati. chātraḥ hasati. aham api hasāmi.) "The girl laughs. The student
   laughs. I laugh too." — बालिका हँसती है। छात्र हँसता है। मैं भी हँसता हूँ।
5. ततः सर्वे वदति — सर्वे भवन्तु सुखिनः!
   (tataḥ sarve vadati — sarve bhavantu sukhinaḥ!) "Then all say together: 'May all be
   happy!'" — फिर सब मिलकर कहते हैं — सर्वे भवन्तु सुखिनः!
6. ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः।
   (oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ.) "Om — peace, peace, peace." — ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः।

Two honest footnotes and one bow. हसामि "I laugh" — the first-person shape you own from
वदामि and पठामि, glossed whole like all its family. Line 5 stretches one word — सर्वे
here plays "everyone", and its full grammar is Volume 2's; today it is the verse's own
word come alive. And line 6 is the śānti-pāṭha close promised in v1.c05.l02 §13 —
TRADITION register, read by you, every sign yours. The course's first story (v1.c01)
was six lines you could not read. So is its last. That is the volume.
