## 1. Hook

Every conversation this course has printed came with a script: रामः spoke, the boy laughed,
you watched from outside. Today's central question breaks the pattern. तव नाम किम्? — "what
is your name?" — is the first question in this book whose right answer appears nowhere in it.
Only you can supply it. And one debt comes due: since the very first lesson, a girl named
Maya has been waiting on a page she asked you a question from — a question the course could
print only in English. Today you answer her in Sanskrit.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिक सन्दर्भः — the oldest genre in the language.

Today's संज्ञा names the lesson: **संवादः** (saṃvādaḥ) "dialogue". Take it apart and you
find an old friend — the वद् of वदति "speaks" and वदामि "I speak", which you have been
reading since Chapter 3, now wearing सम् "together". A dialogue is, literally, a
speaking-together.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** संवादः is built from सम् "together" + वादः "speech, speaking":
> conversation as joint speech. The root is the same वद् that gives वदति and वदामि.
> [REF:apte1890 s.v. संवाद]

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** Dialogue is not a late luxury of Sanskrit literature — it is one
> of its oldest layers. The Ṛgveda, composed c. 1500–1200 BCE on the scholarly consensus
> dating, already contains hymns cast entirely as exchanges between two speakers, with no
> narrator at all. [REF:jamison2014 p.5]

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** The tradition's great teaching texts keep that form: the
> Upaniṣads stage instruction as question and answer between student and teacher, and the
> Bhagavad Gītā describes itself as धर्म्यं संवादम् (dharmyaṃ saṃvādam) — "a dialogue
> concerning dharma" — in its own closing chapter. [REF:bhagavadgita 18.70]

Your two small scenes today stand at the head of that long road: someone asks, someone
answers, and the language happens between them.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिक व्याख्या — why frames come before grammar.

मम नाम …। is what language pedagogy calls a **formulaic chunk**: a fixed frame learned as
a whole, with one open slot. You will use it correctly today without knowing what the -म
of मम does or why नाम looks the way it does — exactly as you have used वदामि and अस्मि
whole since Chapters 3 and 5.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Learning high-frequency conversational frames as unanalyzed wholes —
> chunks — is a standard, well-supported route into speaking: the frame becomes usable at
> once, fluency builds on known material, and the internal grammar is unpacked later
> without relearning. [REF:nation2013]

Two more design notes, honestly stated. First, everything in today's dialogue is built
from words you already read — that is the bootcamp principle: fluency grows by running
*known* material at speed, not by piling on new material [REF:nation2013]. Second, every
run through the scenes is retrieval practice — you produce the frames from memory, which
is the strongest rehearsal there is [REF:roediger2006]. And one delivery fact for §9: when
a sentence carries a question word (किम्, कः, का), the word itself does the asking; when
it carries none — त्वम् अपि छात्रः? — the rising tune alone turns statement into question,
as it has in this course since Chapter 3.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतव्याख्या — today's संज्ञा is **संवादः** (saṃvādaḥ) "dialogue" — सम् (sam)
"together" + वादः (vādaḥ) "speech". And the unlocked language, at once:

मम नाम माया।
(mama nāma māyā.) "My name is Māyā." — मेरा नाम माया है।

तव नाम किम्?
(tava nāma kim?) "What is your name?" — तुम्हारा नाम क्या है?

अहम् छात्रः अस्मि। त्वम् अपि छात्रः?
(aham chātraḥ asmi. tvam api chātraḥ?) "I am a student. Are you a student too?" — मैं
छात्र हूँ। क्या तुम भी छात्र हो?

One honest note: नाम (nāma) "name" is glossed as a chunk inside its frame, the same way
every verb has been glossed whole since Chapter 3. Why मम and तव end as they do, and what
work नाम's shape is doing — that machinery is Volume 3's. The frame is yours today.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी व्याख्या

आज आपके पास वह पूँजी है जो अंग्रेज़ी से आने वाले शिक्षार्थी के पास नहीं है — हिन्दी।
देखिए कैसे: "मेरा नाम राम है" और मम नाम रामः — चाल लगभग वही है, फ़र्क़ बस इतना कि संस्कृत
का यह साँचा "है" के बिना ही पूरा है। नामवाला वाक्य अध्याय 3 से बिना क्रिया के चलता आया है
(अहम् रामः) — आज वही साँचा नाम के साथ बोलना है। दूसरी पूँजी: तुम और आप। यही जोड़ी संस्कृत
में त्वम् और भवान् है — जिसे आप "तुम" कहते हैं, उसे त्वम्; जिसे "आप", उसे भवान्। और तीसरी:
"भी"। संस्कृत का अपि ठीक हिन्दी के भी की तरह अपने शब्द के *पीछे* चलता है — "मैं भी" =
अहम् अपि। "यह क्या है?" = एतत् किम्? — यहाँ भी "है" नहीं चाहिए।

अब तीन चेतावनियाँ। पहली और सबसे बड़ी: नाम। हिन्दी की आदत इसे एक ही झटके में "नाम्" जैसा
बोल देती है; संस्कृत में यह दो पूरे अक्षर हैं — ना-म — अन्तिम अ पूरा। मम नाम कहते समय
चारों अक्षर गिनकर बोलिए। दूसरी: छात्र रोज़ की हिन्दी का शब्द है, पर संस्कृत में वह विसर्ग
समेत खड़ा होता है — छात्रः — और शास्त्रीय ढंग में लड़की अपने लिए बालिका कहती है (आधुनिक
प्रयोग में छात्रा भी चलता है — उसका उल्लेख भर आज है, पाठ नहीं)। तीसरी: एतत् का ए सीधा,
लम्बा /eː/ है — यहाँ हिन्दी आपके साथ है, क्योंकि हिन्दी का ए भी यही है; अंग्रेज़ी की
"ay" वाली डगमगाहट से बचिए।

और आज का असली काम याद रखिए: यह पाठ पढ़ने का नहीं, *बोलने* का है। हर साँचा उसी समय सच्चा
होता है जब उसमें आपका अपना नाम बैठता है।

## 8. English Explanation

Two frames, three questions, one small word that walks behind — and then you talk.

### The two frames

The first frame you already own: **अहम् … अस्मि** — "I am …" — Chapter 5's identity frame,
verb glossed whole. Today adds its partner, the name frame:

**मम नाम …।** (mama nāma …) "My name is …" — say the frame, drop your name in the slot,
done. And notice what is *not* there: no "is". मम नाम सीता is a complete sentence. The
naming sentence has been verbless since Chapter 3 — अहम् रामः, मम पुत्रः — and today's
frame simply joins that family. नाम is glossed as a chunk; which ending does what inside
the frame is Volume 3's story, and we will not smuggle it in.

### The question kit, completed

Three question words, all old acquaintances, finally doing a full day's work together:

| Question | Ask it of | The answer frame |
|---|---|---|
| तव नाम किम्? "what is your name?" | anyone | मम नाम …। |
| त्वम् कः? "who are you?" | a boy or man, as a peer | अहम् रामः। / अहम् छात्रः अस्मि। |
| त्वम् का? "who are you?" | a girl or woman, as a peer | अहम् सीता। / अहम् बालिका। |
| भवान् कः? "who are you, sir?" | a man, respectfully | अहम् गुरुः। |

कः has waited since Chapter 2, का stood beside भाषा in Chapter 4's review as a fixed
chunk, किम् arrived with Chapter 5's virāma. One honesty note: the questions addressed to
*you* carry no verb — त्वम् अपि छात्रः? The you-form of अस्मि exists, and it opens
Volume 2; until then the verbless question is complete, correct, and yours.

### त्वम् or भवान् — one honest line

त्वम् (tvam) is the intimate you — friends, classmates, children; भवान् (bhavān) is the
respectful you said to a man, Chapter 5's polite chunk. Hindi speakers already own this
split as तुम/आप. The full polite family, feminine forms included, is Volume 2's business —
today, just choose the right one of the pair.

### अपि — the word that walks behind

**X अपि = "X too."** अहम् अपि "I too"; त्वम् अपि "you too". It leans on the word before
it, exactly like Hindi भी — it never walks in front. That placement is the whole of
today's rule; the rest of अपि's manners come later.

### एतत् — this, for pointing

**एतत्** (etat) "this" is the near partner of Chapter 5's तत् "that". Point and ask:
एतत् किम्? "what is this?" Answer with any thing-word you own: एतत् जलम्। एतत् हिमम्।
एतत् मित्रम्। — and note that मित्रम् stays a म्-word whoever the friend is, so you may
point at a person *through* it. The pointing words for persons themselves are Volume 2's.

### Seven words, one ledger

Yesterday's long words brought the readable ledger to about 226; today's seven — नाम,
त्वम्, तव, एतत्, अपि, छात्रः, बालिका — push it past 230. Nothing else is new. That is the
bootcamp bargain: a tiny toll, then the whole lesson is spent *using* what you own.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — no new sounds; the work is delivery. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c06l02-dialogue.mp3` (both scenes at three speeds, plus
the roleplay-gap track), 🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c06l02-word-list.mp3` (seven
words + dictation track).

**Drill 1 — the two question tunes.** Where a question word stands, the word itself asks:
तव नाम किम्? — keep the tune level and let किम् do the work. Where no question word
stands, the rise *is* the question: say मित्रम् अत्र अस्ति। flat, then मित्रम् अत्र
अस्ति? rising — then transfer the pair to त्वम् अपि छात्रः। / त्वम् अपि छात्रः? Three
rounds, alternating.

**Drill 2 — turn timing.** A dialogue adds a skill no word list can teach: the gap.
Pause-form reading between turns — finish your line, one full beat of silence, then the
answer. With the slow track: speak each turn along with the recording, holding the beat.

**Drill 3 — shadow both roles.** Play Scene 1; speak Rāma's lines in his gaps. Replay;
speak Sītā's. The roleplay-gap track leaves one side silent for you.

**Drill 4 — the name discipline.** नाम is two full beats: ना-म, final अ intact — never the
Hindi habit's clipped "nām". Then your own name in the frame, loud, five times:
मम नाम …। Every learner's hardest word today is their own name said with Sanskrit vowels.

## 10. IPA

No new symbols — new timing and tune. The frames in pause form:

**मम नाम माया** /mɐmɐ n̪ɑːmɐ mɑːjɑː/ · **तव नाम किम्** /t̪ɐʋɐ n̪ɑːmɐ kim/ ·
**अहम् छात्रः अस्मि** /ɐɦɐm t͡ɕʰɑːt̪ɾɐh ɐsmi/ · **एतत् किम्** /eːt̪ɐt̪ kim/.

**Word transcriptions:** नाम /n̪ɑːmɐ/ · त्वम् /t̪ʋɐm/ · तव /t̪ɐʋɐ/ · एतत् /eːt̪ɐt̪/ ·
अपि /ɐpi/ · छात्रः /t͡ɕʰɑːt̪ɾɐh/ · बालिका /bɑːlikɑː/.

**Narrow notes:** छात्रः opens on the aspirated palatal affricate [t͡ɕʰ] — the puff
belongs to छ and must survive; inside the same word sits the त्र join, [t̪ɾ] with no vowel
between. त्वम् is one onset, [t̪ʋ]: dental closure released straight into the labiodental
glide — an inserted vowel makes the non-word "tuvam". एतत् opens on a long steady [eː] —
never the English glide [eɪ]. And नाम is [n̪ɑː.mɐ], two syllables; the final [ɐ] is a full
beat, not an afterthought.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थानम् — nothing new to place; three checks to run.

1. **The त्व bridge.** Say त्वम् slowly: tongue tip on the teeth for त्, then the lower
   lip rises to the upper teeth for व — two stations, one gesture, no vowel between.
   Mirror check: the jaw must not bounce open inside त्व.
2. **The छ station.** छात्रः begins where च lives — tongue blade at the hard palate — with
   a real puff. Tissue before the lips: it barely stirs on च and *jumps* on छ.
3. **The steady ए.** Watch your mouth through the first beat of एतत्: lips and jaw frozen.
   If they glide, you said English "ay", not Sanskrit ए.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्य-भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix. These feed the quiz distractors.

1. **नाम clipped to "nām".** The Hindi habit deletes the final अ. *Fix:* two beats, ना-म,
   tap the desk on each; the frame is मम नाम — four full beats in a row.
2. **एतत् with an English glide.** "ay-tat" [eɪ] for /eː/. *Fix:* the Chapter 2 discipline
   — long, pure, jaw frozen; Hindi's ए is your anchor.
3. **A vowel smuggled into त्वम्.** "tuvam" for /t̪ʋɐm/. *Fix:* Chapter 5's timing drill —
   see, split, say; one onset, no bounce.
4. **अपि walking in front.** "अपि अहम्" for "I too". *Fix:* X अपि — it stands *behind*
   its word, exactly like Hindi भी: अहम् अपि, त्वम् अपि.
5. **छात्रः from a girl.** The ः-word is masculine; classically a girl says अहम् बालिका
   (or अहम् बाला). The modern feminine छात्रा exists and you will meet it in today's
   world — mentioned honestly here, taught in Volume 2 with the gender system.
6. **The flat question.** त्वम् अपि छात्रः said level is a statement — "you too are a
   student." *Fix:* no question word means the rise carries everything; run §9 drill 1
   until the pair feels automatic.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — three items with something inside them.

### नाम — a word older than the language

**नाम** (nāma) "name" — नाम · /n̪ɑːmɐ/

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Sanskrit नामन् (nāman), Latin nōmen, and English *name* descend from
> one shared Proto-Indo-European word — they are cognates, siblings from a common
> ancestor, not borrowings and not parent and child. Your first question today is built on
> a word your own language has carried all along. [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. नामन्]

### छात्रः — the student under the umbrella

**छात्रः** (chātraḥ) "student" — छात्र · /t͡ɕʰɑːt̪ɾɐh/

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The dictionaries connect छात्र to छत्र (chatra) "umbrella, parasol":
> the pupil as the one under the teacher's umbrella — shelter and instruction in a single
> image. [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. छात्र]

One reading note: the word carries Chapter 5 inside it — the छ puff, the ा mātrā, the त्र
join, the ः close. Four chapters of script in seven sounds.

### मम नाम … — the frame with no verb

**मम नाम माया।** (mama nāma māyā.) "My name is Māyā." — मेरा नाम माया है।

No "is" — and nothing missing. The naming sentence has been verbless since अहम् रामः in
Chapter 3; the name frame is the same sentence wearing नाम. Swap the name slot and the
frame answers for anyone: मम नाम सीता। मम नाम रामः। And turn it around with किम् in the
slot and तव in front, and it becomes the question: तव नाम किम्?

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — in this lesson the grammar *is* the frames: chunks used whole, boundaries
stated honestly. Chapter 6 adds nothing new to the script — that campaign closed with
Chapter 5.

### The frames are the grammar

Three frames, all verbless or whole-verb, all complete:

- **मम नाम …।** — name frame; nothing conjugates, nothing declines *for you yet*.
- **अहम् … अस्मि।** — identity frame; अस्मि glossed whole since v1.c05.l02.
- **त्वम् कः? / त्वम् का? / तव नाम किम्?** — the questions; no verb, none needed.

Two honest boundaries, so nothing is smuggled: the you-form of अस्मि exists and opens
Volume 2 — until then questions to *you* stay verbless. And the endings inside मम, तव,
नाम — why they look as they do — that is Volume 3's system, previewed by not one word here.

### The question kit

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The question word matches its answer: कः asks after masculine
> ः-words (कः? — रामः, छात्रः), का after feminine words in -आ (का? — सीता, बालिका), and
> किम् after neuter म्-words and things (किम्? — जलम्, नाम). Today they are fixed chunks;
> the system behind them is Volume 2's. [REF:apte1890 s.v. किम्]

### Three shapes in the answer slot

Look at what stands in the naming frames: रामः and छात्रः wear ः; जलम् and मित्रम् wear
म्; सीता, माया, बालिका stand unchanged. You have been *seeing* this split since Chapter 3
— मालाs beside बालकःs — and today you are using all three shapes on purpose. Why the
shapes exist is the gender-and-case story of Volume 2; today, match the shape you heard.

### अपि's one rule

अपि follows the word it adds to: अहम् अपि "I too", त्वम् अपि "you too", एतत् अपि "this
too". Behind, never in front. That is the entire rule taught today.

## 16. Conversation

सम्भाषणम् — the heart of the lesson: the course's first true conversation, in two scenes.
Every word is self-read. 🔊 audio: `planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c06l02-dialogue.mp3`
(slow pause-form, natural speed, and a roleplay-gap track with one side silent).

### Scene 1 — the name exchange

रामः meets सीता on the school path.

— नमस्ते! अहम् रामः। तव नाम किम्?
(namaste! aham rāmaḥ. tava nāma kim?) "Greetings! I am Rāma. What is your name?" —
नमस्ते! मैं राम हूँ। तुम्हारा नाम क्या है?

— मम नाम सीता। त्वम् कः?
(mama nāma sītā. tvam kaḥ?) "My name is Sītā. Who are you?" — मेरा नाम सीता। तुम कौन हो?

— अहम् छात्रः अस्मि। त्वम् का?
(aham chātraḥ asmi. tvam kā?) "I am a student. Who are you?" — मैं छात्र हूँ। तुम कौन हो?

— अहम् बालिका। अहम् सीता!
(aham bālikā. aham sītā!) "I am a girl. I am Sītā!" — मैं लड़की हूँ। मैं सीता!

Notice the pair of question words at work: Sītā asks the boy त्वम् कः?; Rāma asks the girl
त्वम् का?. To the गुरुः, both would say भवान् कः? — the respectful chunk from Chapter 5.
And notice Sītā's classical answer: the ः-word छात्रः is masculine, so she answers with
बालिका. (The modern feminine छात्रा exists — one honest mention, taught properly with
Volume 2's gender system.)

### Scene 2 — at the school door

रामः meets the बालकः of our stories — and the specced question of the day.

— नमस्ते! त्वम् कः?
(namaste! tvam kaḥ?) "Greetings! Who are you?" — नमस्ते! तुम कौन हो?

— अहम् रामः। अहम् छात्रः अस्मि। त्वम् अपि छात्रः?
(aham rāmaḥ. aham chātraḥ asmi. tvam api chātraḥ?) "I am Rāma. I am a student. Are you a
student too?" — मैं राम हूँ। मैं छात्र हूँ। क्या तुम भी छात्र हो?

— आम्! अहम् अपि छात्रः अस्मि!
(ām! aham api chātraḥ asmi!) "Yes! I too am a student!" — हाँ! मैं भी छात्र हूँ!

— एतत् किम्?
(etat kim?) "What is this?" — यह क्या है?

— एतत् जलम्।
(etat jalam.) "This is water." — यह जल है।

— एतत् किम्?
(etat kim?) "And this?" — और यह क्या है?

— एतत्? एतत् मित्रम्!
(etat? etat mitram!) "This? This is a friend!" — यह? यह मित्र है!

Delivery notes: त्वम् अपि छात्रः? has no question word — the rise carries everything (§9
drill 1). Between turns, pause form: one full beat. And the last joke is grammar wearing a
grin — मित्रम् stays a म्-word whoever the friend is, so pointing एतत् at a person works
exactly when it goes through मित्रम्. Swap roles and run both scenes with your own names —
the frames take any name you own.

## 18. Exercises

अभ्यासाः — six exercises; 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6 are production tasks. Do them in order.

**अभ्यासः 1 · नाम-लेखनम् (your name in the frames — production).** On paper, write both
frames with your own name in the slot: मम नाम ______। and अहम् ______ अस्मि। Then say
each aloud three times. If your name has sounds Devanagari does not carry, approximate
honestly — every learner of every language does this, and your approximation will improve
with the course. Girls: add अहम् बालिका; boys: अहम् छात्रः अस्मि.

**अभ्यासः 2 · प्रश्नमाला (the question chain).** Build the chain aloud, both parts, from
these cues: to a boy → त्वम् कः?; to a girl → त्वम् का?; to anyone → तव नाम किम्?; to a
man, respectfully → भवान् कः? Then answer all four as yourself. Self-check with §8's
table: question word matched to the person asked?

**अभ्यासः 3 · अभिनयः (roleplay — production).** Print the worksheet ✍
(`planned:assets/worksheets/volume-1/v1c06l02-roleplay.pdf`). With a partner, perform
Scene 1 and Scene 2 from the role cards — each partner sees only their own lines. Swap
roles and repeat. Solo mode: the roleplay-gap track 🔊 leaves one side silent for you.
Pause form between turns; rising tune where no question word stands.

**अभ्यासः 4 · परिचयः (record yourself — production).** Record your full self-introduction
in one take: नमस्ते! मम नाम ______। अहम् छात्रः अस्मि। (or अहम् बालिका।) Play it back
against §9's disciplines: नाम in two beats, त्वम्-style clusters one onset, every final अ
full. Keep the recording — lesson 4's Volume Exam will ask for this again, faster.

**अभ्यासः 5 · एतत्-क्रीडा (the pointing game — production).** Cut the worksheet's three
picture cards — जलम्, हिमम्, मित्रम्. Shuffle, point, ask aloud: एतत् किम्? Answer in the
frame: एतत् जलम्। Then play it in the room: point at real water, at a real friend. Three
rounds; the asking and the answering both count as production.

**अभ्यासः 6 · श्रुतलेखनम् (dictation — production).** Play the dictation track 🔊 (in the
word-list audio): six dialogue lines in random order, each read twice with a writing
pause. Write them in Devanagari, daṇḍas and question marks included. Self-check against
§16: conjuncts fused? every म् and ः in place? 5/6 is a pass; re-take tomorrow either way
(§20).

## 22. Teacher Notes

शिक्षक-टिप्पणी

**This is a speaking lesson — chairs in pairs from minute one.** The reading is light by
design (seven words); the win condition is every student asking and answering with their
real name, aloud, several times. Do not let the hour become silent reading about a
dialogue.

**Timing plan (45 min):** §3 retrieval, written (4) → hook: the unanswered question (2) →
the two frames on the board, names dropped in live (6) → question kit + the त्वम्/भवान्
line (6) → Scene 1: model, then pairs (7) → Scene 2 + अपि + the pointing game (7) → the
name round: every student introduces themselves to the room in sequence (6) → THE MAYA
MOMENT staged as below (4) → dictation start + homework briefing (3).

**Stage the Maya moment — do not narrate it.** Board or slide: the v1.c01.l01 dialogue
card exactly as first printed, English third line and all. Silence. Let a student read the
two Sanskrit lines; let the English line hang for ten unhurried seconds. Then write मम नाम
माया। under it and step aside — the room will finish the arc before you explain it. Then
have the whole class answer her question, one name at a time.

**The छात्रा question is coming — have the honest script ready.** Hindi-medium students
will ask why Sītā does not say छात्रा. Answer exactly what the course prints: छात्रा is
common modern usage and perfectly real; the classical default this course follows is अहम्
बालिका / अहम् बाला, and Volume 2's gender system will give छात्रा its proper seat. Never
"wrong" — "later, and here is the classical way today."

**Names resist Devanagari — normalize that early.** Approximations are correct behavior,
not errors; demonstrate with a hard name on the board. Shy speakers: run the name round
chorus-first (everyone repeats each introduction), then solo.

**Differentiation:** Hindi speakers own तुम/आप and भी — move them fast to delivery and
hold them to two disciplines: नाम in two beats and the rising tune said *confidently*.
English-habit learners rise on questions natively — their work is त्व as one onset and
dental त throughout. Watch both groups on अपि placement in free speech; it drifts
frontward under English influence ("also I am…").
