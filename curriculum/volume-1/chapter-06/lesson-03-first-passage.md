## 1. Hook

On the first day of this course — before you could read a single letter — lesson one played
you a sentence and asked you to take it on trust: अहं संस्कृतं पठामि, "I study Sanskrit." You
have chanted its verb in every review since, always by ear. Today the course hands you its
first real text: eight sentences that read as one story — a boy, his book, his school. Seven
sentences are about him. The eighth, read with your own eyes in your own voice, is that first
sentence come home — and it is about you.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिक सन्दर्भः — from turn-taking to text.

Chapter 6 climbs a ladder: long words at speed (lesson 1), a dialogue — two voices taking
turns (lesson 2) — and today a text that stands alone, the way most Sanskrit you will ever
meet stands, carrying its own thread: who is being talked about, and how sentence two knows
what sentence one said. One honest word about the title: अनुच्छेदः (anucchedaḥ) "passage,
paragraph" is the standard *modern* term, the same word Hindi uses for a paragraph; Sanskrit
as a living classroom language uses it daily.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Today's verb is a whole family. The root पठ् means "to read aloud, to
> recite" — in the tradition, reading *was* reading aloud. From it come पठति "he/she reads",
> पठनम् "reading" — this very chapter's title — and पाठः "a lesson, a recitation", the word
> that lives on as Hindi पाठ. [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. पठ्]

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** अर्जुन (arjuna) is an old Sanskrit word for "bright, white,
> silvery" and a personal name attested from the epics onward; its most famous bearer is the
> third Pāṇḍava, the hero of the Mahābhārata. [REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. अर्जुन]

Our अर्जुनः is simply a schoolboy who likes his book; the name's famous bearer belongs to
later volumes — today he only lends a very readable name.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिक व्याख्या — why you will read the *same* eight sentences many times, and why that is
the opposite of wasted time.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Fluency development is its own strand of language learning: timed
> re-reading of *known* material builds automatic recognition, and it is distinct from
> learning new material. A passage re-read at rising speeds is not "the same exercise
> repeated" — it is the drill that makes recognition free. [REF:nation2013]

That is this lesson's design: four new words in twenty-four, so that your eyes can practice
*flow* — whole known words at a glance instead of block-by-block assembly. And one more
lever, familiar from every §3 of this course: answer the comprehension questions *from
memory* before looking back at the text — retrieval strengthens what re-reading alone does
not [REF:roediger2006]. Read, close, answer, check: that loop is the lesson.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतव्याख्या — the lesson's idea in the simplest possible Sanskrit.

सः पठति।
(saḥ paṭhati.) "He reads." — वह पढ़ता है।

अहम् अपि पठामि।
(aham api paṭhāmi.) "I too read." — मैं भी पढ़ता/पढ़ती हूँ।

एतत् पुस्तकम्।
(etat pustakam.) "This is a book." — यह पुस्तक है।

The first pair is the whole passage in miniature — he reads; so do you. The third line
answers the dialogue lesson's एतत् किम्? with today's new noun — and it is a verbless nominal
sentence, complete and classical, exactly like धर्मः सत्यम् before it.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी व्याख्या

आज आप पहली बार वाक्य के बाद वाक्य पढ़ेंगे — आठ वाक्य, जो मिलकर एक छोटी-सी कहानी कहते हैं।
अब तक अक्षर थे, फिर शब्द, फिर अकेले वाक्य, पिछले पाठ में बातचीत — और आज पहला अनुच्छेद।
पढ़ने की दुनिया का असली दरवाज़ा यही है।

अच्छी ख़बर यह कि इस अनुच्छेद का लगभग हर शब्द आपकी हिन्दी का जाना-पहचाना मेहमान है। पुस्तक,
विद्यालय, छात्र, नाम — ये सब हिन्दी ने संस्कृत से ज्यों के त्यों लिए हैं। और "पढ़ना"? वह तो
पठति का ही सदियों में घिसा हुआ रूप है — ठीक वैसे, जैसे उलूक से उल्लू बना था। यानी आज की
क्रिया आपकी ज़बान पर पहले से है; नया केवल उसका संस्कृत चेहरा है: पठति — "पढ़ता है", और
पठामि — "मैं पढ़ता/पढ़ती हूँ"।

एक बात ईमानदारी से। अनुच्छेद में विद्यालयम् और पुस्तकम् — दोनों म् पर ख़त्म होते हैं, जबकि
इस अध्याय के पहले पाठ में आपने विद्यालयः पढ़ा था। क्यों? संस्कृत में शब्द की भूमिका बदलते ही
उसका अन्त बदल जाता है — कौन कर रहा है, किस पर हो रहा है, किसका है। यह पूरी व्यवस्था तीसरे
खण्ड की विषय-वस्तु है; आज हम पूरे शब्द का पूरा अर्थ साथ लेकर चलेंगे — विद्यालयम् = "विद्यालय
की ओर", पुस्तकम् = "पुस्तक (को)"। कोई नया नियम याद नहीं करना — बस खुली आँख से अन्तों को काम
करते देखिए।

और अन्त में वह क्षण, जिसके लिए यह पूरा अध्याय बना है। सात वाक्य अर्जुन के बारे में हैं; आठवाँ
वाक्य अचानक आईना बन जाता है: अहम् अपि संस्कृतम् पठामि — "मैं भी संस्कृत पढ़ता/पढ़ती हूँ।"
याद कीजिए — पहले ही पाठ में यही वाक्य आपको सुनाया गया था, जब आप एक भी अक्षर नहीं पढ़ सकते
थे। आज वही वाक्य आपकी अपनी आँखों से, अपनी आवाज़ में। इसे पढ़ना ही इसे सच कर देना है।

## 8. English Explanation

Nothing new in the script today, and almost nothing new in the mouth. What is new is the
*unit*: not the word, not the sentence — the text.

### What makes eight sentences one text

Read a word list and each entry stands alone. Read a passage and every sentence leans on the
one before it. The lean has a visible mechanism: **सः** (saḥ) "he". Sentence one introduces
बालकः, a boy; from then on the passage just says सः, and your mind keeps supplying *the boy
from sentence one*. That silent thread turns a pile of sentences into a story — and following
it is a reading skill in its own right. You have technically met सः once before, fused into
सोऽहम् in the last chapter's avagraha showcase; today it goes to work, five times in eight
sentences, and earns its own row in §14. One honest line beside it: the feminine partner of
सः is **सा** (sā) "she" — same job, female subject. You will put सा to work in §18.

### The cast of new words

- **पठति** (paṭhati) "reads, recites" — THE verb of this chapter: the title पठनम् "reading"
  is this verb's own noun. Whole-word gloss, as with every verb since Chapter 3. Beside it,
  its first-person twin **पठामि** (paṭhāmi) "I read" — the exact parallel of वदति/वदामि, and
  an old friend: you have chanted अहं पठामि in review mantras since Chapter 1.
- **पुस्तकम्** (pustakam) "book" — dictionary stem पुस्तक; its स्त weld is the one you have
  read daily in अस्ति since the conjunct lesson.
- **तस्य** (tasya) "his" — completing a family: मम "my" (Chapter 2), तव "your" (last
  lesson), तस्य "his" (today). The स्य is the Chapter 5 stem-loss mechanism at work.

### The endings, honestly

Lesson 1's विद्यालयः appears in this passage as विद्यालयम्, and पुस्तक wears म् too. Not a
typo — Sanskrit's ending system at work, and §15 says exactly as much about it as honesty
allows: a word's ending shifts with its role. We gloss such words whole — विद्यालयम् "to
school", पुस्तकम् "a book" — and keep walking.

### The text

Twenty-four words, eight sentences, every part certified. Read it once now, slowly — block
by block if you need to; slow is the honest first speed, and §9 gives you the other two. The
line-by-line walk happens in §17.

> बालकः अत्र अस्ति। सः छात्रः अस्ति। तस्य नाम अर्जुनः। सः विद्यालयम् चलति।
> सः पुस्तकम् पठति। सः संस्कृतम् पठति। छात्रः हसति। अहम् अपि संस्कृतम् पठामि।

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — the fluency protocol: one passage, three speeds, one target time. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c06l03-passage-three-speeds.mp3` (the passage at all three
speeds, plus the dictation track), `planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c06l03-words-and-dialogue.mp3`
(§14 words and the §16 dialogue).

**Drill 1 — the पठ् ladder.** The new verb's ठ is retroflex *and* aspirated: tongue-tip
curled back, real puff. Climb with the audio: पठ — पठति — पठामि — पठनम् (paṭha — paṭhati —
paṭhāmi — paṭhanam). If the puff fades on the longer words, start again.

**Speed 1 — भागशः (block by block, ≈ 90 seconds).** Every akṣara gets its beat: बा-ल-कः
अ-त्र अ-स्ति। Accuracy only. Once with the audio, once alone.

**Speed 2 — वाक्यशः (sentence pace, ≈ 60 seconds).** Whole words, whole sentences; breathe
at each daṇḍa. Listen for the thread: every सः should *sound* like it points at Arjuna.

**Speed 3 — द्रुतपाठः (reading pace, target under 45 seconds).** The bootcamp bar: the whole
passage under forty-five seconds with **zero clipped finals** — every final अ full, every
final म् a closed-lip landing, every visarga a clean breath. A fast read with "pustak" in it
scores nothing; re-time.

**Drill 2 — the rising question.** §16's questions carry their asking in tone alone, as every
question has since घटः अत्र अस्ति?: कः अत्र अस्ति? — pitch rising to the end. Ask each
question twice at the mirror; make the rise unmistakable.

**Narrow notes:** अर्जुनः is [ɐɾ.d͡ʑu.n̪ɐh] — the repha's tap closes the *first* syllable,

hum, no lip closure. And the ठ of पठति is /ʈʰ/ — retroflex place, strong aspiration; losing
either one makes it a different Sanskrit sound.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थानम् — the passage's four physical checkpoints, mirror at hand.

1. **The curl-and-puff (पठति, पठामि).** Tongue-tip curled back for ठ, then a real burst of
   breath. Paper-strip test: a strip at the lips should jump on ठ.
2. **The closed-lip landings (म्).** विद्यालयम्, पुस्तकम्, संस्कृतम्, अहम्, किम् — lips
   close fully and *stay* closed. Watch them in the mirror at speed 3; if they bounce open,
   an unwanted "-ma" is leaking.
3. **The clean breath (ः).** बालकः, छात्रः, अर्जुनः, सः — a short voiceless breath after the
   vowel, throat open, no new vowel added.
4. **The teeth-touch (त्, स्त, त्र).** तस्य, अस्ति, अत्र, पुस्तकम् — the त family lives on
   the teeth, not the ridge.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्य-भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix. These feed the quiz distractors.

1. **Speed strips the finals.** At reading pace the Hindi habit returns: "pustak",
   "vidyālay", "saṃskṛt". *Fix:* the target time only counts with every final vowel and
   final म् intact — a fast wrong read is a zero. Drop one speed, rebuild, re-time.
2. **पठति without the curl or the puff.** The familiar Hindi पढ़ slides in, or ठ flattens to
   an unaspirated ट. *Fix:* §9 drill 1 — retroflex curl plus a paper-moving puff.
3. **तस्य read "ta-sa-ya".** Three beats instead of two — the स्य weld ignored. *Fix:*
   Chapter 5's mechanism: तस्य = त + स् + य. Two blocks, two beats: ta-sya.
4. **सः flattened to "sa".** The visarga dropped, five times in one passage. *Fix:* the
   Chapter 2 breath drill on the passage's visarga words in a row: बालकः — छात्रः —
   अर्जुनः — सः.
5. **Eight islands instead of one text.** Every sentence read correctly, none connected —
   the reader cannot say who सः is. *Fix:* comprehension before speed: answer §16's
   questions once, then time yourself. If you know तस्य नाम अर्जुनः, the thread reads itself.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — three worked readings and one worked sentence, all four layers.

### अर्जुनः — the repha rides again

**अर्जुनः** (arjunaḥ) "Arjuna" — अर्जुन · /ɐɾd͡ʑun̪ɐh/

अ + र्जु + नः: the र vanishes from the line and perches as a hook on जु — written right,
read first: a-rju-naḥ. Last chapter's rule, first proper name.

### पुस्तकम् — the weld you already read daily

**पुस्तकम्** (pustakam) "book" — पुस्तक, किताब · /pust̪ɐkɐm/

पु + स्त + क + म्. The स्त weld is the one inside अस्ति — read in nearly every sentence
since Chapter 5. New word, zero new mechanics: this is what a bootcamp buys.

### तस्य — the mechanism, one more time

**तस्य** (tasya) "his" — उसका · /t̪ɐsjɐ/

त + स्य: स् + य by stem-loss. And a family completes: मम "my" — तव "your" — तस्य "his".
Three possessives, three chapters, one habit.

### तस्य नाम अर्जुनः। — the verbless sentence

(tasya nāma arjunaḥ.) "His name is Arjuna." — उसका नाम अर्जुन है।

No verb, nothing missing: a nominal sentence, complete classical usage — धर्मः सत्यम् taught
you the pattern; today it does narrative work inside a story.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — usually a script-rule section in Volume 1; today, for the first time, a
*reading*-rule section: what to notice, and what to postpone.

### The endings, exactly as much as honesty allows

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** A Sanskrit word's ending shifts with its role in the sentence — who
> acts, what is acted on, whose something is. That is why lesson 1's विद्यालयः stands in
> this passage as विद्यालयम्, and why पुस्तक wears म् as the thing being read. The full
> system is Volume 3's subject; today such words are glossed whole — विद्यालयम् "to school",
> पुस्तकम् "a book". [REF:whitney1889]

The same honest device the course has used since रामः वदति: whole-word glosses now, the
machinery at its proper time. Notice the endings; do not memorize a rule about them — there
is none on the table today.

### The thread: how सः holds a text together

सः "he" never introduces anyone — it *points back*. In this passage every सः points at
बालकः from sentence one. The same job, female subject, is done by सा "she" — one line, no
paradigm, put to work in §18. And तस्य "his" is the pointing family's possessive: तस्य नाम =
"the name *of him*" — of that same boy.

### Person lives in the verb's tail

पठति "he reads" — पठामि "I read": the -ति/-मि tails carry the person, exactly as वदति and
वदामि showed you; the verb system proper waits for later volumes. Note also what sentence
three does with no verb at all (§13) — and that the passage stays in **pause form**
throughout: every word spoken whole, the joined everyday forms (अहं for अहम् before a
consonant) being Volume 3's business, as previewed in the virāma lesson.

## 17. Story

कथा — अर्जुनः पठति · Arjuna Reads. The passage itself is today's story — eight lines; each
line: Devanagari, IAST, English, Hindi. Every word is self-read. Illustration:
`planned:assets/images/volume-1/v1c06l03-passage-card.svg`; audio 🔊 in the three-speeds track.

1. बालकः अत्र अस्ति।
   (bālakaḥ atra asti.) "A boy is here." — यहाँ एक बालक है।
2. सः छात्रः अस्ति।
   (saḥ chātraḥ asti.) "He is a student." — वह छात्र है।
3. तस्य नाम अर्जुनः।
   (tasya nāma arjunaḥ.) "His name is Arjuna." — उसका नाम अर्जुन है।
4. सः विद्यालयम् चलति।
   (saḥ vidyālayam calati.) "He walks to school." — वह विद्यालय की ओर चलता है।
5. सः पुस्तकम् पठति।
   (saḥ pustakam paṭhati.) "He reads a book." — वह पुस्तक पढ़ता है।
6. सः संस्कृतम् पठति।
   (saḥ saṃskṛtam paṭhati.) "He reads Sanskrit." — वह संस्कृत पढ़ता है।
7. छात्रः हसति।
   (chātraḥ hasati.) "The student laughs." — छात्र हँसता है।
8. अहम् अपि संस्कृतम् पठामि।
   (aham api saṃskṛtam paṭhāmi.) "I too read Sanskrit." — मैं भी संस्कृत पढ़ता/पढ़ती हूँ।

Walk it once with open eyes. Lines 1–3 build the thread: बालकः is introduced, becomes सः,
and line 3 names him with no verb at all. Lines 4–6 are where the endings quietly shift —
विद्यालयम् "to school", पुस्तकम् "a book": noticed, glossed whole, postponed (§15). Line 7
the passage does not explain — the student laughs, and the text trusts you to know why a boy
alone with a book might. And line 8 breaks the frame: for seven sentences you watched
Arjuna; the eighth is the reader, saying so in the reader's own person — पठामि. It is the
course's first sentence come home: lesson one played it as pure sound, अहं संस्कृतं पठामि,
and today its pause-form twin stands under your own eyes. The chapter is called पठनम् —
line 8 is पठनम् happening.
