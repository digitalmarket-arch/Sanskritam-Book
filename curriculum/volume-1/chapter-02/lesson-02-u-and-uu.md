## 1. Hook

Until today, your lips have been unemployed. Every vowel so far — अ, आ, इ, ई — left them
neutral while the tongue and jaw did all the work. Now round them into a small circle, the
shape you make to whistle or to blow out a candle, and two new vowels appear: उ and ऊ. For
the first time in this course you can *watch* yourself pronounce Sanskrit — the mirror stops
being a metaphor. And the stakes are already familiar: hold the rounded vowel one beat
longer and कुल (kula) "family" becomes कूल (kūla) "riverbank". One breath, and your whole
family is standing in the river. Two sounds, two letters, two signs that hang *below* the
line — and eight new words, one of them an owl with a literary future.

1. **The pair first:** इति (iti) means "thus". What does ईति (īti) mean — and which of the
   two would you never want visiting your village?
2. **Write from memory:** कि and की. Which sign stands on which side of क — and which of the
   two is the script's famous surprise?
3. State the left-hook rule in one sentence: where is ि *written*, and when is it *spoken*?
4. Which English vowel is the trap for इ — and why is the vowel of *bit* not the first vowel
   of इति?
5. **Read aloud:** तिल — मति — लीला. (All three are lesson 1 vocabulary.)

<details><summary>उत्तराणि · Answers</summary>

1. ईति (īti) is "plague, calamity". One beat of vowel separates "thus" from disaster — the
   unwanted visitor is ईति.
2. कि wears its sign on the **left** of क; की wears it on the right. The left-stander कि is
   the surprise.
3. ि is written to the left of its consonant but spoken *after* it: कि = k + i.
4. The lax /ɪ/ of *bit* — looser and lower than इ /i/; इति begins with a firm, close /i/.
5. tila — mati — līlā: short इ one beat, long ई two.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिक सन्दर्भः — today you complete the tradition's own opening line.

The Pāṇinian tradition begins its entire enterprise not with a rule but with a sound
catalogue: fourteen short sūtras that list every varṇa in a deliberate order. The very first
of them opens with three vowels — and after today, you own all three.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** The fourteen Māheśvara sūtras prefixed to the Aṣṭādhyāyī
> tradition open with अइउण् (a i u ṇ): its listed sounds are अ, इ, उ — the final ण् is a
> marker letter, a filing device explained in Volume 4. The tradition's own phonetics files
> उ and ऊ as ओष्ठ्य (oṣṭhya) "of the lips" — the articulation label you met in the sound map
> (v1.c01.l02). [REF:maheshvarasutras 1]

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** Pāṇini worked around the 4th century BCE on the scholarly
> estimate; the Aṣṭādhyāyī and its prefixed sound catalogue stand at the head of a grammar
> tradition that runs continuously from antiquity to the present. [REF:cardona1997]

So the sequence you are living through — अ, then इ, then उ — is not this course's
invention; it is the tradition's own first breath, and modern phonetics smiles at it: three
vowels, three corners of the mouth's vowel space (§5). One more appointment to book: lesson
5 brings ॐ, and the tradition's analysis reads it as a-u-m — today's उ is its middle sound.
That is a TRADITION-register statement, and it gets its full, carefully labeled treatment
when we arrive.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिक व्याख्या — what /u/ and /uː/ are, and why your lips finally matter.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** उ = /u/ — a close back rounded vowel: tongue body high and far back,
> lips actively rounded and pushed slightly forward. ऊ = /uː/ — the same vowel held roughly
> twice as long. [REF:ladefoged2015]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Vowels are described on three independent dials: tongue height, tongue
> backness, and lip rounding. अ आ इ ई used only the first two; उ/ऊ are the course's first
> rounded vowels — the third dial finally turns. [REF:ladefoged2015]

Three consequences for the learner:

**You have reached a corner of the vowel space.** /i/, /a/-type vowels and /u/ sit at the
extreme corners of the mouth's possibility space, and the great majority of the world's
vowel systems build on exactly these corners [REF:ladefoged2015]. With अ/आ (open), इ/ई
(close front) and now उ/ऊ (close back rounded), your Sanskrit vowel map has all three
corners planted.

**The English anchors, with their flaws stated.** The *oo* of *food*, said slowly and
carefully, is a fair approximation of ऊ — but in many modern English accents that vowel has
drifted forward toward [ʉː] or turned into a glide [uw], so trust the mirror and the audio
over your habit. The bigger danger is the false friend: the vowel of *put* and *book* is lax
/ʊ/ — shorter, looser, barely rounded — and it is **not** उ. उ is a genuinely close, firmly
rounded vowel: think "a short, crisp ऊ", never "the *put* vowel" (§12).

**Rounding is visible.** Height and backness happen behind closed doors; rounding happens on
your face. From today the mirror is a measuring instrument, not a pep talk: if you cannot
*see* a small, forward-pushed circle for the full duration of ऊ, the vowel is drifting. Use
it in every drill (§9, §11).

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतव्याख्या — the lesson's idea in the simplest possible Sanskrit.

उ इति ह्रस्वः स्वरः।
(u iti hrasvaḥ svaraḥ.) "*u* is a short vowel." — उ ह्रस्व स्वर है।

ऊ इति दीर्घः स्वरः।
(ū iti dīrghaḥ svaraḥ.) "*ū* is a long vowel." — ऊ दीर्घ स्वर है।

उलूकः अत्र अस्ति।
(ulūkaḥ atra asti.) "The owl is here." — उल्लू यहाँ है।

Two things to savor. First, इति — last lesson's vocabulary word is here doing its real job,
quoting the sound उ exactly as the tradition uses it. Second, उलूकः: the body of that word,
उलूक, is fully readable with today's letters. Its little tail ः is the visarga — met as a
category in the sound map, taught properly in lesson 6. Until then, read उलूक and let the
audio supply the final light breath. Returning terms today: ह्रस्व (hrasva) "short", दीर्घ
(dīrgha) "long", मात्रा (mātrā), and from the sound map ओष्ठ्य (oṣṭhya) "of the lips".

## 8. English Explanation

Two storylines again today: a new **pair of sounds** (rounded /u/ and /uː/) and a new
**mātrā position** (below the consonant). Both are one-step extensions of machinery you
already own — which is exactly why this lesson moves fast and reads far.

### Part one — the sounds: उ /u/ and ऊ /uː/

|  | उ (u) | ऊ (ū) |
|---|---|---|
| IPA | /u/ | /uː/ |
| Duration | 1 beat (hrasva) | 2 beats (dīrgha) — twice as long |
| Tongue | high, far back | same — held steady |
| Lips | rounded, pushed forward | same — rounding held to the very end |
| Jaw | nearly closed | nearly closed |
| English anchor | none exact — *put* is the trap, not the anchor (§5) | the *oo* of *food*, said carefully — see the §5 caution |
| Hindi anchor | the उ of सुन "listen" | the ऊ of फूल "flower" |

Unlike अ/आ, this pair does not change color as it lengthens: ऊ is उ with more time on the
clock, nothing else (§10). All the new work is in the lips — round them, push them slightly
forward, and *keep them there*. A long ऊ that starts rounded and relaxes halfway turns into
an English-style glide; the mirror catches it instantly.

### Part two — the mātrā compass gains a direction: below

You know the system: independent letter at the start of a word, dependent sign on a
consonant everywhere else, sign replaces the inherent अ. The only news is *where* this
pair's signs live.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The dependent signs of उ and ऊ attach **below** the consonant, hanging
> from the foot of its vertical stem: क + उ = कु (ku), क + ऊ = कू (kū). As always, the sign
> replaces the inherent अ. [REF:masica1991 ch.6]

The compass so far:

| Position | Signs | Example | Arrived |
|---|---|---|---|
| right | ा, ी | का, की | v1.c01.l04, v1.c02.l01 |
| left | ि | कि | v1.c02.l01 |
| **below** | **ु, ू** | **कु, कू** | **today** |
| above | े, ै | — | lesson 4 |

Lesson 3 will hang one more sign below (ृ), and lessons 4–5 put a roof on the house. By the
end of this chapter you will have walked the full compass.

### The payoff — reading, worked out

- **कुल** = कु (क + ु) + ल → ku-la "family"
- **मूल** = मू + ल → mū-la "root"
- **मुकुल** = मु + कु + ल → mu-ku-la "bud" — two hooks below, one word
- **उलूक** = उ + लू + क → u-lū-ka "owl" — word-initial u, so the **independent letter** उ
  opens the word; the sign ु never begins anything
- **तुला** = तु + ला → tu-lā "balance" — below-sign and right-sign cooperating in one word:
  the compass directions mix freely, one direction per vowel

Eight words today (§14), every one of them readable — and the running total of words you
read with your own eyes reaches twenty-eight.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — three drills, in order. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c02l02-u-drill.mp3` (drills 1–2) and
`planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c02l02-word-list.mp3` (drill 3).

**Drill 1 — smile to whistle (rounding warm-up).** In front of a mirror, say a long ई and
hold it — lips spread, a slight smile. Now, without stopping the voice, slowly round the
lips into a small circle *while sliding the tongue body back*: the sound should land on ऊ.
Watch the corners of your mouth travel. Warning flag: if the landing sound is thin and
Frenchish (a [y], like French *tu*), your lips moved but your tongue stayed forward — pull
the tongue back until the vowel turns deep and dark. Five slow slides, then five quick
इ → उ hops.

**Drill 2 — the length ladder.** Metronome at 60 (or tap the table). One breath: उ (one
beat) — ऊ (two beats) — उ — ऊ — उ — ऊ. Count silently "one-two" inside every ऊ, and hold
the lip-circle to the end of the second beat — rounding that decays mid-vowel is this
pair's version of sagging length. Three rounds, then reverse starting from ऊ.

**Drill 3 — the syllable grid, then words.** Read across, then down; short syllables one
beat, long two:

| कु /ku/ | कू /kuː/ |
|---|---|
| मु /mu/ | मू /muː/ |
| तु /t̪u/ | तू /t̪uː/ |
| लु /lu/ | लू /luː/ |

Then assemble real words from §14: कुल, कूल, मूल, मूक, तुला, मुकुल, उलूक, उत. Finish with
the pair rounds: कुल — कूल · कुल — कूल, keeping the *second* syllable identical — the
contrast lives only in the first vowel's clock. Precision first, speed never.

## 10. IPA

The new vowels: उ = **/u/** — close back rounded; ऊ = **/uː/** — the same symbol plus the
length mark you know from /ɑː/ and /iː/. The "long club" now has three members: आ /ɑː/,
ई /iː/, ऊ /uː/.

**Narrow note — a pure length pair.** Compare the course values: अ /ɐ/ vs आ /ɑː/ differ in
quality *and* quantity; उ /u/ vs ऊ /uː/ (like इ/ई) differ in quantity alone. Same tongue,
same lips, twice the time. That makes your ear the only referee for कुल vs कूल — there is
no color difference to lean on, which is exactly why the pair test (§18) exists.

**Names for the traps.** The lax vowel of English *put* has its own symbol, /ʊ/ — looser,
lower, barely rounded; it is a different vowel, not a "casual उ". The fronted *goose* vowel
of many English accents is transcribed [ʉː] — right length, wrong neighborhood. If a
recording of your ऊ sounds like either, revisit §11.

Phonemic slashes, as established in lesson 2 of Chapter 1: /u/ and /uː/ name the sound
categories that keep कुल and कूल apart — meaning-bearing units, exactly what the slashes
are for.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थानम् — walkthrough of the diagram (planned:
`assets/diagrams/volume-1/v1c02l02-mouth-position-u.svg` — sagittal view with the tongue
high-back, plus a front view of rounded lips: the course's first diagram with an *outside*
view, because for the first time the outside shows something).

To find उ /u/ and ऊ /uː/:

1. **Lips:** round them into a small circle and push them slightly forward — the whistle
   position, or blowing out a candle. This is the headline act; you must be able to *see* it.
2. **Tongue:** high and far back. You cannot see this one — feel it instead: start from ई
   (tongue high and *front*), keep the jaw still, and slide the tongue body backward while
   rounding. When the sound turns deep and hollow, you have arrived.
3. **Jaw:** nearly closed — about as closed as for इ/ई. If your jaw drops, you are drifting
   toward अ territory.
4. **Hold:** say ऊ for a slow two-count. The lip-circle must survive both beats — no
   relaxing, no spreading, no glide at the end.

**Mirror + candle check.** Mirror: alternate इ — उ — इ — उ; you should *see* the lips flip
from spread to circled on every hop while the jaw barely moves. Candle (or a fingertip an
inch from your lips): a properly forward-rounded ऊ sends a narrow stream of air you can feel
on the fingertip for two full beats — a lax /ʊ/ barely reaches it.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्य-भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix. These feed the quiz distractors.

1. **The *put* substitution (English speakers).** English offers /ʊ/ (*put*, *book*) as its
   everyday short u, so learners recycle it for उ — too lax, too open, barely rounded.
   *Fix:* build उ downward from ऊ, never upward from *put*: say a careful, fully rounded ऊ,
   then clip it to one beat without letting the mouth relax. उ is a short ऊ, not the *put*
   vowel.
2. **Fronting and gliding (English speakers).** The modern *goose* vowel has slid forward
   ([ʉː]) and often ends in a glide ([uw]); imported into Sanskrit it makes ऊ bright and
   unsteady. *Fix:* tongue back until the vowel sounds dark and hollow; in the mirror, the
   lip-circle must be identical at the start and end of the vowel — no motion, no glide.
3. **Length blur (Hindi and English speakers both).** Fast speech shrinks the 2:1 contrast
   until कुल and कूल merge; Hindi speakers survive this in Hindi because context rescues
   सुना/सूना — recitation and dictation offer no such rescue. *Fix:* count beats (§9 drill
   2); let the pair test (§18) be the judge, and retest after a night's sleep.
4. **The sign drawn beside, not below — or mirror-flipped (writing).** Learners park ु to
   the right of the consonant like a little footnote, or flip the hook's direction and turn
   कु into कू. *Fix:* the hook hangs from the **foot of the stem** — chant "under, not
   after" as you write; for direction, check every hook against the stroke card (§15) until
   the hand stops asking.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — the minimal pair, worked in all four layers, then two words with futures.

### कुल vs कूल — the pair bank grows

**कुल** (kula) "family, clan" — कुल, परिवार · /kulɐ/
**कूल** (kūla) "bank, shore" — तट, किनारा · /kuːlɐ/
[REF:apte1890 s.v. कुल, कूल]

Same consonants, same final vowel — only the first vowel's duration differs, and there is
no quality difference to help you (§10): this pair is a pure test of the clock. Your pair
bank now holds one entry per vowel family: कल/काल, इति/ईति, कुल/कूल. Every new vowel pair
is proving the same law — in Sanskrit, duration is meaning.

### The owl with a literary career

**उलूक** (ulūka) "owl" — उल्लू · /uluːkɐ/

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** The Pañcatantra devotes its entire third book, काकोलूकीय
> (kākolūkīya) "Of Crows and Owls", to a war between the crows and the owls — an owl king
> sits at the center of it. [REF:panchatantra tantra 3]

No spoilers — but remember this word: when your Sanskrit is ready to carry stories, this
owl will be waiting for you. And one gift for Hindi speakers: उल्लू is उलूक worn smooth by
centuries of inheritance — a direct descendant, not a borrowing.

### The root that will grow

**मूल** (mūla) "root, base" — जड़, मूल · /muːlɐ/

From this root the tradition grows मूल्य "value, price" — literally what a thing is *at
root* worth. You will read that word later this volume, when य arrives; today you plant मूल
and let it wait. Roots are patient.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — in Volume 1 this is a **script rule**: the below position, formally, and the
strokes that live there.

### The below rule

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** उ and ऊ follow the same two-form system as आ and इ/ई: the independent
> letters (उ, ऊ) open an akṣara that starts with the vowel — in practice, word-initially —
> and the dependent signs (ु, ू) attach to a consonant, replacing its inherent अ. These two
> signs attach **below** the consonant, at the foot of its vertical stem: क + उ = कु, म + ऊ
> = मू. A sign's position — right, left, below, above — is fixed by the script for each
> vowel; it is a fact of writing, not of sound. [REF:masica1991 ch.6]

The decision procedure is unchanged: *does the u-sound start the word?* → letter (उत,
उलूक). *Does it follow a consonant?* → sign, hung below (कुल, मूल). The two spellings never
compete for one position. Note what stays constant across the compass: ि stands left but
sounds after; ु hangs below but sounds after. **Position is graphics; order is speech.**

### Stroke order — उ, ऊ, ु, ू

One common convention, described here; teaching traditions vary in details, and your
teacher's consistent habit is fine. (Card: `planned:assets/images/volume-1/v1c02l02-stroke-order.svg`;
tracing: `planned:assets/worksheets/volume-1/v1c02l02-tracing.pdf`.)

- **उ** — one continuous stroke: a small upper curve opening leftward, flowing down into the
  large lower bowl that bulges right and closes back left at the base; headline last.
- **ऊ** — write a complete उ, then add the tail: a small curl leaving the lower bowl to the
  right and sweeping down. Like आ before it, the long letter is the short letter *plus
  ink* — the script drawing extra length as extra material.
- **ु** — after the consonant is complete: a small hook hanging from the foot of the stem,
  swinging down and curling up so its mouth opens to the upper **right**.
- **ू** — the mirror twin: the same hook flipped, its mouth opening to the upper **left**.
  Memory device: ह्रस्व ु looks *ahead* (rightward, the direction you are reading); दीर्घ ू
  has been going long enough to look back over its shoulder. Fonts vary slightly — the
  stroke card is the model, and "under, not after" (§12) is the chant.

The headline habit continues: letters hang from the clothesline; the below-signs hang from
the *letters*. Nothing about the line itself changes today.

## 20. Revision

पुनरावृत्तिः — one screen to remember.

> **The sounds:** उ /u/, ऊ /uː/ — close, back, **rounded**; same quality, different clock;
> dīrgha = 2 × hrasva. **The move:** round the lips and push them forward — the first vowel
> you can check in a mirror, and the rounding must survive both beats of ऊ. **The rule:**
> ु and ू hang **below** the consonant, from the foot of the stem — कु, कू; the independent
> letters उ, ऊ open words (उत, उलूक). Position is graphics; order is speech. **The proof:**
> कुल ≠ कूल — family vs riverbank; the pair bank is now कल/काल · इति/ईति · कुल/कूल.
> **The prize:** eight more readable words — twenty-eight in all.

  check every hook's direction against the stroke card.
- **In 1 week:** read the full §14 list aloud at speed with the mirror on; re-take the pair
  test (exercise 4); re-answer quiz Q1–2 and Q7 without looking.
- **From lesson 3:** the below position gains a third tenant — ऋ's sign ृ — and today's

  कि left, कु below, की right.

## 22. Teacher Notes

शिक्षक-टिप्पणी

**This is the mirror lesson.** For four sound-lessons the articulators have been invisible;
today one of them finally shows. Stage it: hand mirrors out (or phone front-cameras up)
*before* the first drill, do the smile-to-whistle slide together in unison, and let the
class see thirty mouths flip from ई to ऊ at once. Doing it as a group first defuses the
embarrassment adults feel about puckering at themselves — name it, laugh once, move on.

**Timing plan (45 min):** §3 retrieval, written (5) → hook: कुल/कूल spoken pair, class
votes on what they heard (4) → sound work: rounding, mirror, smile-to-whistle, length
ladder (9) → the below rule on the board + compass update (8) → syllable grid + word list
in pairs (8) → exercises 1 and 4 (8) → homework briefing incl. the 1-day re-dictation (3).

**Board work:** keep the mātrā compass permanently on the side board: क in the center,
ा and ी to its right, ि to its left, ु and ू hanging below, and a dotted slot above marked
"lesson 4". Every new word gets decomposed under the compass. Add ृ to the below family
next lesson in front of the class — the diagram growing week by week *is* the syllabus.

**The [y] trap is audible before it is visible.** When students round their lips without
retracting the tongue, the result sounds French or German (tu/über). Catch it by ear during
drill 1 and give the fix as a direction, not a correction: "lips stay — tongue goes back."
One demonstration of the wrong sound, deliberately, helps the class hear the difference.

**The classic writing errors:** the hook parked beside the consonant instead of below, and
the mirror-flipped hook (कु ↔ कू). Both are sequence-and-direction errors, so circulate
during exercise 2 and watch pens, not just finished worksheets. The chant "under, not
after" handles position; for direction, have students say "short looks ahead, long looks
back" as they write until it becomes silly, then unnecessary.

**Dictation logistics:** read each word twice at natural speed, 10-second writing gap; do
not over-round or over-lengthen as a hint. Collect error types, not just scores: beside-
not-below, flipped hook, and length errors each get different remediation (stroke card,
mnemonic, ladder drill respectively).

**Differentiation — Hindi-literate learners:** the writing is automatic for them; their
real work is length discipline and the full final vowel (कुल = *kula*, not "kul"). Hand
them the सुना/सूना bridge and make them the explainers: a Hindi speaker demonstrating that
pair to an English-speaking partner teaches both of them. In pair reading, give them the
recorder role — judging a partner's ऊ against the model sharpens their own — and hold them
to recitation-grade 2:1 in exercise 5, where fluency otherwise lets them coast.

**What students find hard:** sustaining rounding through the second beat of ऊ (the mirror
fixes what the ear forgives); hearing कुल/कूल at natural speed (normalize the 1-day retest —
scores jump after sleep); and believing that lips alone are not enough — the smile-to-
whistle slide, done slowly, converts the skeptics.

- **00:40** Watch my mouth. A long ī [iː] — lips spread, a little smile. Now, slowly: the
  smile becomes a circle, the tongue slides back… ū [uː]. That circle is the whole lesson.
- **01:05** Careful — if you round your lips but leave the tongue forward, you get a thin
  French vowel. Lips round AND tongue back. Deep and dark: ū [uː].
- **01:20** Now the clock. u [u] — one beat. ū [uː] — two. And here is the trap: the circle
  must last both beats. If the lips relax halfway, the vowel slides. Hold it: ū [uː].
- **01:40** And do not borrow the English *put* vowel — it is loose and lazy [ʊ]. Sanskrit
  u [u] is a short ū [uː]: small, firm, round.
- **01:55** Writing time. You know the game: the vowel shrinks into a sign. But this pair
  does something new — it hangs *below*. क [kɐ]… plus the hook under the stem… कु [ku].
- **02:20** The long twin flips its hook and hangs below too: कू [kuː]. Short looks ahead —
  long looks back. Under, not after.
- **02:40** Your mātrā compass now points three ways: ā to the right. i to the left. u and
  ū below. The roof comes in lesson four.
- **03:00** Eight new words — read with me, two beats on every long vowel. kula [kulɐ],
  family. kūla [kuːlɐ], riverbank. mūla [muːlɐ], root. mūka [muːkɐ], mute. tulā [t̪ulɑː],
  a balance. mukula [mukulɐ], a bud — two short u's, count them.
- **03:40** And two words that *begin* with u — so they begin with the full letter: uta
  [ut̪ɐ], "and, or". And ulūka [uluːkɐ]… the owl.
- **04:00** Remember this owl. One of the great Sanskrit story collections gives its owls
  and crows an entire war. When you can read stories, he will be waiting.
- **04:15** Now the riverbank. Kamalā — yes, the same Kamalā — is out at dusk with her
  mother. Listen, and read what you can: kamalā atra asti… paśya! ulūkaḥ [uluːkɐh]!
- **04:45** mama ulūkaḥ! mama mukulam! mama kūlam! My owl, my bud, my riverbank — some
  things about children need no translation.
- **05:05** Before you go: the mirror test. Say kula [kulɐ] — kūla [kuːlɐ]. Watch the
  circle. One beat, two beats. If you can *see* the difference, you can say it.
- **05:25** Dictation tomorrow, pair test after a night's sleep — the gap is the method.
- **05:40** Eight words down, twenty-eight in all. Next lesson: a vowel that sounds like an
  r. Yes, really. kūla [kuːlɐ]. Take the riverbank with you.
