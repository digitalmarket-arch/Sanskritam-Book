## 1. Hook

Say the English word *hey* — but in slow motion, stretched over three full seconds. Feel
that? Somewhere in the middle, your tongue quietly slides upward and the vowel changes under
you. English does this constantly and never tells you. That slide is exactly what the
Sanskrit vowel ए does **not** do: ए picks one position and holds it, frozen, to the last
millisecond. Today you learn two opposite arts with one mouth: in ए, holding a vowel
perfectly still — and in ऐ, gliding on purpose, because there the journey *is* the sound.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिक सन्दर्भः — why the tradition calls today's vowels "junction vowels".

The grammarians who catalogued the varṇamālā noticed that four vowels — ए ऐ ओ औ — do not
behave like the others. The simple vowels come in short/long pairs; these four are always
long, and in the give-and-take between words they keep decomposing into simpler vowels and
reassembling. So the tradition filed them under a name that is a tiny theory in itself:

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** The Pāṇinian tradition classes ए ऐ ओ औ as सन्ध्यक्षर
> (sandhyakṣara) "junction vowels" — vowels in which two simple vowels meet. The Māheśvara
> sūtras, the sound-catalogue prefixed to Pāṇini's grammar, seat them apart: अइउण् and
> ऋऌक् list the simple vowels; एओङ् and ऐऔच् hold the four junctions.
> [REF:maheshvarasutras 1–4]

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** The junction label records a real prehistory: in the development
> that led to Sanskrit, ए and ऐ arose as fusions of a + i, and ओ and औ as fusions of
> a + u. The traditional classification and the historical sound change match.
> [REF:whitney1889 §§27–29] [REF:burrow2001]

Two consequences you can use today. First, a vowel built from two vowels carries two
vowels' worth of duration — that is why ए and ऐ (and next lesson's ओ and औ) are **always
dīrgha**, with no short partners anywhere in the language. Second, a promise: in Volume 3,
when words start colliding and fusing (sandhi), you will watch *a* meet *i* and become ए in
live text. Today's lesson is the parts list for that machine — no sandhi drilling yet.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिक व्याख्या — a monophthong, a diphthong, and how to tell them apart with a phone.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** ए = /eː/ — a mid front unrounded vowel, always long: the front of the
> tongue raised to mid height, lips gently spread, and every articulator held still for the
> vowel's full two-mātrā duration. Sanskrit has no short e.
> [REF:whitney1889 §27] [REF:ladefoged2015]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** ऐ = /ɐi̯/ — a diphthong: a single syllable in which the tongue starts
> at /ɐ/ (exactly the अ position) and glides toward /i/. Its total duration is two mātrās,
> like any dīrgha vowel. [REF:whitney1889 §§27–29] [REF:ladefoged2015]

**Monophthong vs diphthong.** A monophthong holds one mouth position from first millisecond
to last; a diphthong is a controlled journey between two positions inside one syllable.
Both are ordinary equipment in the world's languages — the trouble is interference. In most
standard English accents the "long a" of *hey*, *day*, *same* is a diphthong [eɪ]: it
starts near ए and then drifts toward [ɪ]. English ears file that drift as "nothing"; in
Sanskrit the drift is the entire difference between ए and ऐ.

**The half-cut test.** You do not need a teacher to catch a glide — a phone will do.
Record yourself holding ए for two beats. In any free audio editor, cut the recording at
its midpoint and play the two halves separately. If the vowel is pure, the halves are
*identical twins*. If the second half sounds brighter, thinner, more i-like than the first —
you glided. This is this lesson's version of Chapter 1's waveform length-check: same phone,
new dimension. Purity is measurable.

**English anchors, with their imprecision stated.** For ए, the closest English sound is the
*ay* of *hey* — but only its very first instant, before the slide begins; take the starting
frame of that film and freeze it. For ऐ, the closest English sound is the *eye* of *eye* or
*aisle*, [aɪ] — genuinely a diphthong, so the *behavior* is right, but the starting point
differs: the English onset [a] is opener and fronter than /ɐ/, and English lets the
endpoint stretch. German *geht*, French *été*, Spanish or Italian *e* are honest pure-e
models: speakers of those languages do today's first task natively. English is, once
again, the odd one out — which is why §9 trains stillness as a skill, not an assumption.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतव्याख्या — the lesson's idea in the simplest possible Sanskrit.

ए इति दीर्घः स्वरः।
(e iti dīrghaḥ svaraḥ.) "*e* is a long vowel." — ए दीर्घ स्वर है।

ऐ इति दीर्घः स्वरः।
(ai iti dīrghaḥ svaraḥ.) "*ai* is a long vowel." — ऐ दीर्घ स्वर है।

एक तिल।
(eka tila.) "One sesame seed." — एक तिल।

Three notes. इति — lesson 1's quoting word — is earning its keep in every definition
sentence; you can read it yourself. एक is today's payoff word: the number one, readable as
ए + क. And listen to the ends of the first two sentences: that breathy ḥ (दीर्घः, स्वरः) is
the visarga — its sign arrives in lesson 6; your ear can start collecting it now. Today's
traditional term: सन्ध्यक्षर (sandhyakṣara) "junction vowel" — सन्धि-अक्षर, वह स्वर जिसके
भीतर दो स्वर मिलते हैं (§4).

## 8. English Explanation

Two sounds today — and they are opposites on purpose. Train them as a pair and each one
protects the other.

### Part one — ए: the discipline of stillness

|  | ए (e) | English "hey" vowel |
|---|---|---|
| IPA | /eː/ | [eɪ] |
| Movement | none — frozen start to finish | glides toward [ɪ] mid-vowel |
| Duration | always 2 beats (dīrgha) | floats with stress and mood |
| Status in Sanskrit | the target | the error (§12, mistake 1) |

ए is a mid front vowel: tongue-front raised halfway toward the palate, lips gently spread,
and then — nothing. No drift, no landing flare, no "y" at the end. The half-cut test (§5)
is the honest judge: both halves identical, every time.

And ए is **always long**. Not "long in some words": there is no short e anywhere in
Sanskrit for it to contrast with. Look at the vowel system you now own:

| ह्रस्व (1 beat) | दीर्घ (2 beats) |
|---|---|
| अ | आ |
| इ | ई |
| उ | ऊ |
| ऋ | ॠ |
| — | ए |
| — | ऐ |

The dash is the lesson. Every vowel until today came in a short/long pair; ए and ऐ have no
short partner, because each already carries two vowels' worth of material inside it (§4).
So एक is not "ek" — it is *eka*, with a first block that takes two full beats.

### Part two — ऐ: the engineered glide

ऐ is built from parts you already own. Say अ (one beat). Say इ (one beat). Now say them
faster, closer — अ-इ — and finally in a single breath, a single syllable, with the tongue
*moving smoothly the whole way*: ऐ /ɐi̯/. Not two syllables ("a-yi"), and no pit stop in
the middle: one syllable whose middle is motion. The onset is exactly your अ /ɐ/ — the
mouth's rest position — and the destination is इ territory, reached just as the two beats
run out.

Here is the elegant part: the tongue motion in ऐ is more or less the same motion that
*ruins* ए. Same gesture, opposite legality. In ए the glide is the error; in ऐ the glide is
the content. That is why this lesson teaches them together — each sound is the other's
warning label.

### Part three — writing above the headstroke

The mātrā rule from Chapter 1 generalizes to every vowel: independent letter word-initially,
dependent sign after a consonant. New today is only the *parking spot*: े and ै sit **above
the headstroke** — the first mātrās of the course to live on the roof.

| Consonant | + mātrā | = | Reads |
|---|---|---|---|
| क (ka) | े | के | ke /keː/ |
| क (ka) | ै | कै | kai /kɐi̯/ |

Worked readings, block by block:

- **एक** = ए + क → *eka* "one". The word begins with the vowel, so the independent letter
  ए opens it — exactly the आ-vs-ा logic you learned in Chapter 1, on a new vowel.
- **केतु** = के (क + े) + तु (त + ु) → *ketu* "banner, comet". One word, two mātrā
  positions: े above its consonant, ु below its own. The geography works as a system.
- **तैल** = तै (त + ै) + ल → *taila* "oil". Hear the journey in the first block: t,
  then अ→इ, then la.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — three drills, in order. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c02l04-e-drill.mp3` (drills 1–2) and
`planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c02l04-glide-contrast.mp3` (drill 2 contrast rounds).

**Drill 1 — the freeze-frame.** Sit before a mirror. Say ए and hold it for a slow
three-count — deliberately longer than its natural two beats, because a long hold exposes
drift. Rules: jaw does not creep, lips do not spread further, and above all the sound does
not brighten at the end. Watch the mirror the whole time: for ए, a mouth in motion is a
mouth in error. Three holds, then the recorded version: capture one, cut it in half (§5),
convict or acquit yourself. In real words ए takes its normal two beats — the three-count is
scaffolding, not the target.

**Drill 2 — building and boarding the glide.** First construct ऐ in slow motion: अ (one
beat) … इ (one beat), five times. Then compress: अ-इ inside one breath, five times. Then
fuse: ऐ — one syllable, two beats, continuous motion, five times. Now the contrast round
with the audio 🔊: it alternates ए — ऐ — ए — ऐ in random order; after each item, say its
partner. If your ए starts growing a tail, return to drill 1; if your ऐ loses its journey
and flattens, rebuild from अ … इ.

**Drill 3 — the syllable grid.** Read across each row, then down each column, steady beat,
two beats per syllable — pure on the left, gliding on the right:

| के /keː/ | कै /kɐi̯/ |
|---|---|
| मे /meː/ | मै /mɐi̯/ |
| ते /t̪eː/ | तै /t̪ɐi̯/ |
| ले /leː/ | लै /lɐi̯/ |

Then real words from §14: एक, एकता, केतु, केलि, एला, तैल — and the derivation pair तिल →
तैल said back to back. In recitation style, exaggerate the stillness of every े and the
travel of every ै. Precision first, speed never.

## 10. IPA

Two new transcriptions, one new mark.

**ए = /eː/** — mid front unrounded vowel plus the length mark ː you have used since आ. The
notation is a contract: one symbol before the ː, therefore one position, therefore nothing
moves. Compare the English vowel it is mistaken for: [eɪ] — *two* symbols, a start and a
destination. Read the transcriptions and the whole lesson is already in them: /eː/ is one
place held long; [eɪ] is a trip.

**ऐ = /ɐi̯/** — two symbols plus the new mark: the inverted breve below, ◌̯, which flags its
vowel as *non-syllabic* — a destination glided toward, not a second syllable. So /ɐi̯/
reads: start at /ɐ/, travel toward /i/, all within one syllable. Note the system's internal
honesty: the onset symbol is exactly अ's /ɐ/ — the diphthong begins at the mouth's rest
position, not at English [a].

Slashes vs brackets, as established in Chapter 1: Sanskrit phonemes stand in /slashes/;
English comparison sounds like [eɪ] and [aɪ], and the modern Hindi value [ɛː], stand in
[brackets] — they are realizations in *other* systems, never targets here.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थानम् — walkthrough of the diagram (planned:
`assets/diagrams/volume-1/v1c02l04-mouth-position-e.svg` — left panel: ए as a single frozen
tongue position, deliberately drawn with no arrows; right panel: ऐ with one motion arrow
from the अ position to the इ position).

To find ए /eː/:

1. **Tongue:** you own the two landmarks already — इ is your highest front vowel, अ is
   rest. ए sits between them: tongue-front raised toward the palate, but only halfway.
2. **Lips:** gently spread — a hint of a smile, less than इ demands.
3. **Jaw:** slightly more open than for इ, well short of अ.
4. **Then freeze.** Two full beats. The position is easy; the *stillness* is the skill.

To make ऐ /ɐi̯/: place the mouth at अ — jaw loose, tongue at rest — and over two beats
travel toward इ: jaw closing a little, tongue-front rising, one smooth motion arriving just
as the breath ends.

**Mirror check.** Alternate ए — ऐ — ए — ऐ ten times watching your jaw and lips: on every ए,
*nothing* should visibly move; on every ऐ you should *see* the jaw rise slightly as the
tongue climbs. Now the hand check: knuckles resting under your chin — ए holds steady
pressure; ऐ eases upward mid-vowel. If both feel identical, one of them is wrong — and it
is usually ए that has started traveling.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्य-भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix. These feed the quiz distractors.

1. **The "hey" habit (English speakers): gliding ए.** The English "long a" is a diphthong
   [eɪ], so *ketu* comes out "kay-too", *elā* becomes "ay-lah" — every ए grows an i-tail.
   *Fix:* the freeze-frame (§9 drill 1) plus the half-cut test (§5). Take only the first
   instant of *hey* and hold that frame. If the second half of your recording sounds
   brighter than the first, the tail is back.
2. **The Hindi value for ऐ (Hindi speakers): [ɛː] instead of the journey.** Modern Hindi ऐ
   is a pure vowel — the [ɛː] of मैं and है — correct and standard *in Hindi*, but not the
   Sanskrit sound. Reading तैल with the है-vowel erases the diphthong. *Fix:* rebuild from
   your own vowels — अ … इ, then अ-इ, then ऐ (§7, §9 drill 2). The value switches with the
   language; neither language is wrong in its own house.
3. **Shortening ए in closed syllables (English speakers).** English e before a consonant is
   short and lax (*met*, *bet*), so एक gets clipped to "ek" and केतु to "ket-". But ए has
   no short version — *there is nothing to shorten it to*. *Fix:* two beats, always, even
   before क: ए-क with the first block carrying two full counts. The §8 pair table is the
   proof — the hrasva column is empty beside ए.
4. **Parking the mātrā on the wrong consonant (everyone, in dictation).** Hearing *taila*,
   a learner writes तलै — the ै has migrated to the wrong block, and the page now reads
   *talai*, which is no word at all. *Fix:* say the word block by block before writing —
   तै-ल — and give each vowel to the consonant it follows *in speech*: the ै belongs to त,
   not to ल. New position, same old chant: letter, then vowel — each vowel on its own
   letter.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — one small word that is everywhere, and one word with a family history.

### एक — the busiest word you will learn this month

**एक** (eka) "one" — एक · /eːkɐ/
[REF:apte1890 s.v. एक]

Your first number word — and numbers are only its day job: Sanskrit uses एक for "one",
"a certain", "alone", "the same". It opens compounds across the entire literature. The
rest of the numbers arrive as their letters do; एक comes first because you can read every
stroke of it today: ए + क.

**एकता** (ekatā) "oneness, unity" — एकता · /eːkɐt̪ɑː/
[REF:monierwilliams1899 s.v. एकता]

Built from एक with the abstract suffix -tā you have already met in ममता "mine-ness"
(lesson 1 of this chapter): mama → mamatā, eka → ekatā. Two data points make a pattern —
Sanskrit builds "-ness" words with -tā, and you can now read both of them.

### तैल — oil remembers the sesame field

**तिल** (tila) "sesame" — तिल · /t̪ilɐ/ (lesson 1)
**तैल** (taila) "oil" — तेल · /t̪ɐi̯lɐ/
[REF:apte1890 s.v. तिल, तैल]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Sanskrit derives तैल "oil" from तिल "sesame": in building the new
> word, the first vowel i strengthens to ai — a regular grade-change the tradition calls
> वृद्धि (vṛddhi) "growth". Oil, in Sanskrit, is literally "the sesame-stuff"; the i → ai
> step is the a + i junction of §4 put to work inside the vocabulary.
> [REF:whitney1889 §§27–29] [REF:apte1890 s.v. तैल]

That is the honest one-paragraph preview — the full vṛddhi machinery is Volume 4's story.
For today, enjoy the receipt: sesame was *the* oil-plant of ancient India, so its name grew
into the word for oil itself. And Hindi तेल [t̪eːl] is this very word worn smooth by time,
the old glide settled into a pure long e — the bottle in your kitchen carries the whole
derivation.

### Two readable readings

**केतु** (ketu) "banner, comet" — पताका; धूमकेतु · /keːt̪u/ — a banner on a pole; a comet
is the sky's own banner, trailing its flag of light. **केलि** (keli) "play, amusement" —
खेल · /keːli/ — a synonym of lesson 1's लीला; the language keeps several words for play.
Read each one block by block, then whole: के-तु, के-लि. [REF:apte1890 s.v. केतु, केलि]

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — in Volume 1 this is a **script rule**: the fourth mātrā position, and the map
so far.

### The above-position rule, formally

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The dependent signs of ए and ऐ attach above the headstroke:
> क + ए = के, क + ऐ = कै. The independent letters ए, ऐ appear word-initially (एक, एला);
> the mātrās े, ै appear after consonants — the same distribution every vowel has followed
> since आ/ा. [REF:masica1991 ch.6]

### Mātrā geography — four of five positions unlocked

| Position | Mātrās | Example | Since |
|---|---|---|---|
| right | ा ी | का की | v1.c01.l04, v1.c02.l01 |
| left | ि | कि | v1.c02.l01 — the left-hook surprise |
| below | ु ू ृ | कु कू कृ | v1.c02.l02, v1.c02.l03 |
| above | े ै | के कै | **today** |
| composite (right + above) | ो ौ | को कौ | next lesson |

The position is fixed by the script, not by the sound: nothing about /eː/ is "up". Learn
the geography as geography — and notice next lesson's teaser: ो and ौ are literally built
from today's े ै plus the old ा.

### Stroke order — ए, ऐ, े, ै

One common convention, described here; teaching traditions vary in details, and your
teacher's consistent habit is fine too. (Card:
`planned:assets/images/volume-1/v1c02l04-stroke-order.svg`; tracing:
`planned:assets/worksheets/volume-1/v1c02l04-tracing.pdf`.)

- **ए** — the body first: the curved hook opening to the lower left, then the short
  diagonal stroke cutting back into its middle; headline last.
- **ऐ** — write a complete ए, then add one short slanted stroke *above* the headline at the
  left. ऐ is ए plus one extra stroke.
- **े** — after the consonant is complete, headline drawn: one short stroke slanting up
  from the headline, above the consonant's right shoulder.
- **ै** — the े stroke, then a second stroke beside and above it. ै is े plus one extra
  stroke.

Let the ink teach the phonology: in writing as in sound, **the ai-form is the e-form plus
one more move**. One stroke, one position (pure ए); two strokes, two positions (the journey
of ऐ). The script drew you a diagram and nobody told you.

## 16. Conversation

सम्भाषणम् — tiny, honest, and fully readable. 🔊 audio recorded into the Chapter 2 drill

— कति तिल?
(kati tila?) "How many sesame seeds?" — कितने तिल?

— एक तिल।
(eka tila.) "One sesame seed." — एक तिल।

— कति केतु?
(kati ketu?) "How many banners?" — कितने केतु?

— एक केतु।
(eka ketu.) "One banner." — एक केतु।

The honest note: this is **counting style** — bare dictionary-stems, the way a word list or

at those full forms — they run on the visarga ः, which arrives in lesson 6, and the grammar
behind the endings is Volume 3's work. Until then the course counts in stems and says so.
कति (kati) "how many" is lesson 1's question word — you have been able to read it for three
lessons; today it finally has something to count.

## 18. Exercises

अभ्यासाः — six exercises; 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 are production tasks. Do them in order.

**अभ्यासः 1 · मात्रा-परिवर्तनम् (mātrā transformation, writing).** In your notebook write
क म त ल in a column. Next to each, its e-form: के मे ते ले. Third column, the ai-form:
कै मै तै लै. Then cover the right columns and regenerate them from the bare consonants.
Read all three columns aloud — pure, pure, journey. Watch your own pen: one stroke above
the line for े, two for ै.

**अभ्यासः 2 · लेखनम् (tracing & writing).** Open the tracing worksheet ✍
(`planned:assets/worksheets/volume-1/v1c02l04-tracing.pdf`): trace ए and ऐ five times each
following the stroke arrows, then के कै मे मै ते तै ले लै — consonant complete first, mātrā
last, above the line. Final row: write एक, केतु and तैल unaided.

**अभ्यासः 3 · श्रुतलेखनम् (dictation, production).** A partner or teacher dictates eight
words — today's six (§14) plus तिल and कुल from earlier lessons — each read twice at
natural speed with a writing pause. (Self-learners: play the word-list audio 🔊 in shuffled
order.) Write each word in Devanagari. Self-check against §14: is every mātrā present, on
the correct consonant, in the correct position? The mixed set forces the real decisions:
ि vs े vs ै, left vs above. 7/8 is a pass; re-take tomorrow either way (§20).

**अभ्यासः 4 · श्रवणभेदः (glide-vs-pure discrimination, listening).** Play
`planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c02l04-glide-contrast.mp3` 🔊. Part A: ten isolated
vowels, each either pure [eː] or glided [eɪ] — mark each item शुद्ध (pure) or फिसलन
(glide). Part B: six syllables, each either के or कै — write which you heard. 8/10 and 5/6
pass; below that, redo §9 drill 2 and re-test after a night's sleep — discrimination scores
jump with rest.

**अभ्यासः 5 · शुद्धता-परीक्षा (the purity test, recording).** The lesson's signature
exercise. Record yourself holding ए for two beats, three separate takes. Cut each take at
its midpoint (§5) and play the halves: identical twins = pass; a brighter second half =
glide, take it again. Then record एक — and cut its first vowel in half too: the test works
inside real words. Log your three verdicts; bring the worst take to class or compare it
against the drill audio 🔊.

**अभ्यासः 6 · वाचनम् (read aloud & record).** Record yourself reading the full six-word
list (§14), then the pair तिल — तैल, then the review pair कुल — कूल. Play the model audio
🔊 and compare word by word. Checklist: did every ए hold still? Did तैल actually travel
अ→इ — or did a Hindi [ɛː] slip in? Did कूल keep its two beats? Re-record only the words
where you and the model differ.

1. (b) के — the e-mātrā above the headstroke. (Distractor (a) कै is the ai-form — one
   stroke too many; (c) parks the vowel on the left, ि's position.)

## 20. Revision

पुनरावृत्तिः — one screen to remember.

> **The sounds:** ए /eː/ — mid front, gently spread lips, **frozen** for two full beats;
> ऐ /ɐi̯/ — a deliberate two-beat journey from अ toward इ. Both always dīrgha: no short
> partners exist. **The test:** cut a held ए in half — identical twins or it glided.
> **The rule:** े and ै sit *above* the headstroke (के कै); independent ए ऐ open words
> (एक, एला). **The mnemonic:** one extra stroke = one extra position — ऐ is ए + 1, in ink
> and in sound. **The prize:** six new words — एक, एकता, केतु, केलि, एला, तैल — and the
> derivation तिल → तैल.

Spaced review (the schedule is the method [REF:cepeda2006]):

- **In 1 day:** re-take the dictation (exercise 3) cold; write the grid के कै मे मै ते तै
  ले लै from memory; run one half-cut test on ए.
- **In 1 week:** read the full §14 list at speed with the checklist of exercise 6;
  re-answer quiz Q3, Q5 and Q8 without looking.
- **Keep the spiral honest:** ऋ stays /r̩/ (not "ri"), and कुल/कूल keeps its two beats —
  both return in lesson 7's assessment alongside today's pure-vs-glide test.

## 22. Teacher Notes

शिक्षक-टिप्पणी

**The core event of this lesson is proprioceptive, not intellectual.** Students understand
"no glide" in seconds; *feeling* their own glide takes the mirror, the hand under the chin,
and above all the half-cut recording. Budget real time for the recording moment — one
volunteer's ए, cut live on a phone in front of the class, convinces the room faster than
any diagram. The lesson stands or falls on students believing their own ears.

**Timing plan (45 min):** §3 retrieval, written (5) → hook: *hey* in slow motion, class
votes on where the vowel changes (4) → ए work: mouth position, freeze-frame, live half-cut
demo (8) → ऐ work: build अ…इ → अ-इ → ऐ, contrast round (6) → mātrā above-line rule + board
work के कै + worked readings एक केतु तैल (8) → syllable grid + word list in pairs (7) →
exercises 1 and 4 (5) → homework briefing, incl. the 1-day re-test (2).

**Board work:** grow the mātrā-geography map on the side board — a large क with ा ी to the
right, ि to the left, ु ू ृ below, and today े ै landing on top; leave the composite slot
(next lesson) drawn but empty, as a visible cliffhanger. Under it keep the two-column strip
के मे ते ले / कै मै तै लै all lesson.

**The classic errors to police:** English speakers glide ए at natural speed even after a
perfect drill — re-run the half-cut test late in the lesson, when attention has moved on;
that is when the tail regrows. Hindi speakers produce a flawless pure ए (celebrate this —
have a Hindi speaker model मेला-quality purity for the class) but will read every ै as
[ɛː]; assign them the glide-referee role in pair work, judging partners' अ→इ journeys,
which trains their own. In dictation, watch for the migrating mātrā (तलै for तैल) — catch
it at the pen, not the page.

**The register moment:** when the Hindi-[ɛː] point comes up, say the sentence out loud to
the class, in so many words: "Hindi's ऐ is correct Hindi; Sanskrit's ऐ is correct Sanskrit;
we are switching systems, not fixing anyone's mother tongue." This framing costs ten
seconds and buys the trust of every Hindi speaker in the room.

**What students find hard:** hearing their own glide (self-recording is the only honest
mirror — insist on exercise 5 actually being done, not skimmed); keeping एक long ("ek" will
reappear for weeks — the empty-hrasva-column argument from §8 helps the analytical
students); and believing that stillness is a skill. The German/French/Spanish aside in §5
recruits any student who speaks those languages as a live model.
