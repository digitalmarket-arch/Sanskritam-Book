## 1. Hook

For four lessons the script has been perfectly obedient: signs march left to right in the
order you speak them. Today it plays its one famous trick. To write *ki*, Devanagari writes
the vowel **first**: कि — the hook you see on the left is the *i*, standing *before* the क
it is spoken *after*. The script writes time travel: your eye meets a vowel your ear has not
reached yet. Today your ear learns two new vowels, इ and ई; your hand learns to write the
trick without blinking — and the little word इति (iti), which you have been *saying* since
lesson 2, finally lands on the page as something you *read*.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिक सन्दर्भः — one small placement, no long history today.

You met the fourteen beats of Śiva's drum in lesson 2's story — and the fourteen sūtras the
tradition hears in them, the sound-catalogue prefixed to Pāṇini's grammar. Today's vowels
live in the catalogue's very first line.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī (composed around the 4th century BCE —
> estimates vary) is organized around a fourteen-sūtra catalogue of sounds that its rules
> reference throughout. [REF:cardona1997]

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** The first of those fourteen sūtras reads अइउण् (aiuṇ): the vowels
> a, i, u, closed by a marker sound. In the tradition's ordering, इ stands in the opening
> beat of the whole system — grouped with अ and उ before every other sound.
> [REF:maheshvarasutras 1]

The grouping is no accident of poetry: अ, इ and उ sketch the far corners of the mouth's vowel
space — low-central, high-front, high-back. How Pāṇini *uses* the ordering — naming whole
spans of sounds with two-letter abbreviations — gets its full treatment in Volume 4. For
today, enjoy the placement: by the tradition's own count, इ is the second vowel of the
language.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिक व्याख्या — what /i/ and /iː/ are, and why this pair has no safety net.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** इ = /i/ — a close front unrounded vowel: the tongue high and forward,
> close to the hard palate, lips unrounded. ई = /iː/ — the same vowel held roughly twice as
> long: dīrgha = 2 × hrasva in duration, with no change of quality. [REF:ladefoged2015]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The vowel of English *bit* is not इ: it is lax /ɪ/ — lower and more
> central than /i/. English *feet* comes close to ई in quality, though many accents glide it
> slightly. Hindi speakers transfer इ/ई largely correctly, but often blur the length contrast
> in fast speech. [REF:ladefoged2015], [REF:ohala1994]

Two consequences for the learner:

**The tongue finally travels.** अ is the mouth at rest; आ opens the jaw but leaves the tongue
lying low. इ is the first vowel of the course that sends the tongue on a real journey — up
and forward, its sides brushing the upper side teeth. From today, vowels are *places*: by
lesson 5 of this chapter you will have visited every corner of the vowel space.

**No safety net.** Lesson 4's pair अ/आ differed in length *and* quality — sloppy length still
left listeners a clue. इ and ई are identical in quality, so duration alone carries the
contrast: इति (iti) "thus" vs ईति (īti) "calamity" hangs on one beat of breath and nothing
else (§13). This is the purest length pair you have met, and the reason lesson 4's 2:1
counting discipline now graduates from good habit to sole lifeline.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतव्याख्या — the lesson's idea in the simplest possible Sanskrit, in frames you already
own from lesson 4.

इ इति ह्रस्वः स्वरः।
(i iti hrasvaḥ svaraḥ.) "*i* is a short vowel." — इ ह्रस्व स्वर है।

ई इति दीर्घः स्वरः।
(ī iti dīrghaḥ svaraḥ.) "*ī* is a long vowel." — ई दीर्घ स्वर है।

लीला अत्र अस्ति।
(līlā atra asti.) "Līlā is here." — लीला यहाँ है।

Now look at the little quoting word doing the work in the first two lines: इति (iti) "thus" —
it wraps whatever precedes it in spoken quotation marks. You have said it since lesson 2
(म इति वर्णः); from today you can *read* it: इ + ति. And the lesson's Sanskrit title, इकारः
ईकारश्च (ikāraḥ īkāraś ca) "the sound i and the sound ī": the tradition names a sound by
adding कार (kāra), and च (ca) means "and". The seam in ईकारश्च — where *ḥ* and *ca* fuse into
*ś ca* — is a joining rule (sandhi), treated properly in Volume 3.

## 8. English Explanation

Two things happen today: two new **sounds** (the front vowels /i/ and /iː/) and one new
**writing fact** — the script's only left-standing vowel sign, and with it the full logic of
where mātrās live.

### Part one — the sounds: इ /i/ and ई /iː/

|  | इ (i) | ई (ī) |
|---|---|---|
| IPA | /i/ | /iː/ |
| Duration | 1 beat (hrasva) | 2 beats (dīrgha) |
| Tongue | high and forward, near the palate | identical |
| Jaw | nearly closed | identical |
| Lips | unrounded, slightly spread | identical |
| English anchor | none exact — *bit* is lax /ɪ/, a different vowel | the *ee* of *feet* — kept pure, no glide |

Read the middle column against the right one: *identical, identical, identical*. Everything
about ई except its duration is इ. One mouth shape to learn, not two — and a pair harder to
hear, because duration alone must do the work (§5).

### Part two — the left-hook rule

How do you write *ki*? Lesson 4's habit says: finish क, add the vowel sign to the right.
Today the script breaks the habit exactly once:

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The short-i mātrā ि is written to the **left** of its consonant —
> earlier in the written line than the consonant it belongs to — although the vowel is
> spoken **after** that consonant: क + इ = कि (ki). The long-ī mātrā ी attaches to the
> right, like ा: क + ई = की (kī). [REF:masica1991 ch.6]

So in कि, writing order and speaking order part ways: the eye meets the hook first, the
mouth says it second. The rule to chant: **written before, spoken after.** A modern footnote
Hindi typists know: on a keyboard you *type* क first and ि second — logical order — and the
display swings the hook to the left for you.

### Part three — mātrā geography: the consonant is a house

Think of every consonant letter as a house. Mātrās are lodgers, and each has a fixed room:

| Room | Signs | Example | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| right | ा ी | का की | ा owned since l04; ी moves in today |
| left | ि | कि | today — the script's one left-dweller |
| below | ु ू ृ | कु | preview — lessons 2–3 of this chapter |
| above | े ै | के | preview — lesson 4 |
| right + above | ो ौ | को | preview — lesson 5 |

The room never depends on the sound — it is fixed by the script, sign by sign (§15). By the
end of this chapter the whole house is occupied.

### Part four — worked readings, block by block

- **तिल** (tila) "sesame": first block ति — hook seen first, but read consonant-then-vowel:
  *t* + *i* = *ti*; then ल = *la* → ti-la /t̪ilɐ/.
- **मति** (mati) "thought": म = *ma*; then the mid-word hook — it belongs **not** to the म
  on its left but to the त on its right: ति = *ti* → ma-ti /mɐt̪i/.
- **कील** (kīla) "pin": की = *k* + *ī* (bar right, canopy curling back left) = *kī*; ल =
  *la* → kī-la /kiːlɐ/.
- **लीला** (līlā) "play": ली + ला → lī-lā /liːlɑː/ — both vowels long, four beats total.
- **इति** (iti) "thus": the word *begins* with the vowel, so the independent letter इ opens
  it; then ति → i-ti /it̪i/.

Now the trap, laid bare: **मति** (mati) and **मित** (mita) are built from the same three
shapes — म, the hook, त. What differs is which consonant the hook stands before. The hook
always belongs to the consonant on its **right**. Misread that, and "thought" becomes
"measured" (§12, mistake 4).

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — three drills, in order. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c02l01-i-drill.mp3` (drills 1–2 plus the random-order
round) and `planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c02l01-word-list.mp3` (drill 3).

**Drill 1 — the length ladder, new floor.** Metronome at 60 (or tap the table). On one
breath: इ (one beat) — ई (two beats) — इ — ई — इ — ई. Keep the smile-tension constant: the
only thing allowed to change is duration. Then reverse, starting from ई. Three rounds.

**Drill 2 — the syllable grid.** Read across each row, then down each column, one beat left,
two beats right:

| कि /ki/ | की /kiː/ |
|---|---|
| मि /mi/ | मी /miː/ |
| ति /t̪i/ | ती /t̪iː/ |
| लि /li/ | ली /liː/ |

Then the random-order round from the audio: it says one syllable; you say its partner.

**Drill 3 — the no-net pair, then words.** Say इति — ईति three times, slowly, counting
beats: i-ti, ī-ti. Nothing but duration separates "thus" from "calamity" — make the
difference audible to a stranger. Then read the §14 list against the model audio: every
dīrgha two full beats, every final vowel alive.

Recitation note: traditional recitation counts mātrās in exactly this way — the drill is the
tradition's own discipline, borrowed whole. When in doubt, slow down and count.

## 10. IPA

The new values: इ = **/i/** — close front unrounded vowel; ई = **/iː/** — the same symbol
plus the length mark ː you met in lesson 4.

**A one-dimensional pair.** Compare the course's two length pairs so far:

1. अ /ɐ/ → आ /ɑː/ — the symbol *changes and* gains ː: quality and quantity both move.
2. इ /i/ → ई /iː/ — the symbol stays; only ː is added. Duration is the entire contrast.

**Narrow note — tense vs lax.** English *bit* is [ɪ]: tongue lower, more central, muscles
laxer. Sanskrit इ is /i/ — as tense and high as the *ee* of *feet* — merely short. "Short"
refers to time, never to a slackening of the mouth: a shortened *feet*-vowel is correct; a
Sanskrit-length *bit*-vowel is not.

Phonemic slashes, as always: /i/ and /iː/ name the categories that distinguish इति from ईति —
meaning-bearing units, which is exactly what the slashes claim.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थानम् — walkthrough of the diagram (planned:
`assets/diagrams/volume-1/v1c02l01-mouth-position-i.svg` — sagittal view of /i/ with an inset
of अ at rest for contrast).

To find इ /i/:

1. **Tongue:** raise the body of the tongue high and forward, toward the hard palate — sides
   pressing lightly against the upper side teeth, tip resting low behind the lower teeth.
   Compared with अ, this is the tongue's first real journey from rest.
2. **Jaw:** nearly closed — a pencil's width. (Lesson 4 asked for two fingers; today one.)
3. **Lips:** unrounded, corners slightly spread — a hint of a smile, not a grin.
4. **Hold it:** say ई for a slow two-count. The quality must not bend at the end — no glide,
   no drift toward *y*.

**Mirror + hand check.** Say अ — इ — अ — इ before a mirror: you should *see* the jaw close
and the mouth-corners spread for every इ. Then the back of the hand under the chin for इ —
ई: the chin barely moves, but the hold lasts twice as long on ई. Ten alternations.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्य-भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix. These feed the quiz distractors.

1. **The lax substitute (English speakers).** *Bit*, *sit*, *hit* train the mouth to relax
   into /ɪ/ whenever a vowel is short — so इ slumps into [ɪ] and stops matching ई in quality.
   *Fix:* build इ *from* ई: say a pure long ई, then cut its duration in half without moving
   anything. The smile-corners in the mirror are your gauge.
2. **Length blur in fast speech (Hindi speakers, and everyone at speed).** Careful इ/ई
   collapses toward a middle duration once the sentence speeds up — Hindi lets context absorb
   the damage; Sanskrit recitation and dictation do not. *Fix:* the metronome ladder (§9) at
   rising tempo — the ratio, not the speed, is the invariant. Remember the stakes: इति/ईति.
3. **The hook written on the wrong side.** A learner hears *ki*, finishes क, and — lesson-4
   habit firing — plants the sign on the right, producing की (kī). *Fix:* the chant
   **"written before, spoken after"**, plus a geometry check: each canopy curls *toward its
   own consonant* — ि's arc reaches right, ी's arc reaches back left. A canopy pointing away
   from its letter is on the wrong side.
4. **Reading the hook onto the wrong consonant.** Mid-word, the eye attaches ि to the letter
   it *follows* visually instead of the one it *precedes*: मति (mati) misread as मित (mita) —
   both real words, so nothing looks wrong. *Fix:* the hook belongs to the consonant on its
   **right**, always. Scan rule: see a hook → hold it → attach it to the *next* consonant.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — worked in all four layers: the no-net pair, the Chapter 1 callback, the moving
hook.

### The pair with no safety net

**इति** (iti) "thus; end of quote" — इस प्रकार · /it̪i/
**ईति** (īti) "plague, calamity" — विपत्ति · /iːt̪i/
[REF:apte1890 s.v. इति, ईति]

इति is Sanskrit's spoken quotation mark — it closes speech, thought, and entire books. You
have used it in every lesson since म इति वर्णः. Add one beat of vowel and you have ईति —
crop-destroying calamity. Unlike कल/काल, no quality change backs you up: one beat of breath
*is* the entire difference. (ईति is rare — meet it, hear it, let it stand guard over your
length discipline; it is not on your active list.)

### The Chapter 1 callback

**मत** (mata) "thought, opinion" — मत, विचार · /mɐt̪ɐ/
**मति** (mati) "thought, mind, intellect" — मति, बुद्धि · /mɐt̪i/
[REF:apte1890 s.v. मत, मति]

Chapter 1 gave you मत; swap its final vowel and you have today's मति — a close kin in
meaning, one hook apart in writing. Sanskrit families often cluster like this.

### The moving hook

**मति** (mati) "thought" · /mɐt̪i/ — **मित** (mita) "measured, moderate" · /mit̪ɐ/
[REF:apte1890 s.v. मति, मित]

Same three shapes on the page: म, hook, त. In मति the hook stands before त (ma-ti); in मित
it stands before म — mi-ta. One reading habit — *the hook belongs to the consonant on its
right* — is all that separates the two. This pair is the reading test of the whole lesson.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — in Volume 1 this is a **script rule**, and today's completes the map begun in
lesson 4.

### The left-hook rule, formally

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** In Devanagari, the dependent sign of इ — the mātrā ि — is positioned to
> the left of the consonant it modifies, preceding it in visual order while following it in
> pronunciation: क + इ = कि (ki), read *k* then *i*. The sign of ई — the mātrā ी — follows
> the consonant on the right: क + ई = की (kī). Word-initially the independent letters इ and
> ई are written, as in इति (iti). [REF:masica1991 ch.6]

Decision procedure, extended from lesson 4: *vowel starts the word?* → independent letter
(इ, ई). *Vowel follows a consonant?* → mātrā — ि standing left, ी standing right.

### Mātrā geography, formally

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** A mātrā's position is fixed by the script, sign by sign — not by any
> property of the vowel's sound: some signs stand right (ा ी), one stands left (ि), others
> below (ु ू ृ) or above (े ै), and two combine right with above (ो ौ).
> [REF:masica1991 ch.6]

The diagram `planned:assets/diagrams/volume-1/v1c02l01-matra-geography.svg` draws this as
the consonant-house of §8 with all five rooms labeled; this chapter fills them one lesson at
a time. Memorize position with the sign, as part of what the sign *is*.

### Stroke order — इ, ई, ि, ी

One common convention, described here; teaching traditions vary in details, and your
teacher's consistent habit is fine. (Card:
`planned:assets/images/volume-1/v1c02l01-stroke-order.svg`; tracing:
`planned:assets/worksheets/volume-1/v1c02l01-tracing.pdf`.)

- **इ** — the body's double curve first, drawn top to bottom in one flowing movement, then
  the headline. Keep the curves round — a boxy इ collapses into scribble.
- **ई** — write इ, then its extra closing curl on the right, tucked up against the headline;
  headline last. The long twin wears one more curl — let the shape say "more".
- **ि** — complete the consonant first. Then the vertical bar to its **left**, and the
  canopy: an arc from the bar's top, rightward over the headline, landing on the consonant.
- **ी** — complete the consonant. Then the vertical bar to its **right** — same bar as ा —
  and the canopy arcing back **leftward** over the top to touch the consonant.

The §12 check bears repeating while you write: each canopy curls toward its own consonant.
And the chant, updated for the left-dweller: *letter first — then the hook, on its own side.*

## 16. Conversation

सम्भाषणम् — tiny and honest; today's question word कति does the work. 🔊 recorded into the
Chapter 2 drill pack (§27). In the kitchen: लीला has spotted the sesame jar.

— लीला — **माता**, **तिल**? (mātā, tila?) "Mother — sesame?" — माता, तिल (मिलेंगे)?

— माता — आम्, **तिल**। (ām, tila.) "Yes — sesame." — हाँ, तिल।

— लीला — **कति**? (kati?) "How many?" — कितने?

— माता — *(a small handful)* **मित**, **मित**! (mita, mita!) "A measured few!" — बस थोड़े-से!

Everything bold you read yourself; the glue word आम् (ām) "yes" — हाँ 🔊 stays ear-only. Note
the honest gap: कति asks "how many?", but the number words that *answer* it begin arriving in
lesson 4 of this chapter (एक "one") — until then, कति is answered the way children answer it
everywhere: with fingers.

## 17. Story

कथा — तिलकथा · The Sesame Story. Five lines, almost entirely readable. Words you can read

1. **लीला** अत्र अस्ति।
   (līlā atra asti.) "Līlā is here." — लीला यहाँ है।
2. **माता** — **लीला**, पश्य! **तिल**!
   (mātā — līlā, paśya! tila!) "Mother: Līlā, look! Sesame!" — माता — लीला, देखो! तिल!
3. **लीला** — **कति**?
   (līlā — kati?) "Līlā: How many?" — लीला — कितने?
4. **माता** — **मित**, **मित**! पश्य — **कील**!
   (mātā — mita, mita! paśya — kīla!) "Mother: A measured few! And look out — a pin!" —
   माता — थोड़े-से! और देखो — कील! *(a stray pin lies beside the seeds; mother flicks it away)*
5. **माता** — **मम** **लीला**! **मम** **ममता**!
   (mātā — mama līlā! mama mamatā!) "Mother: My Līlā! My darling!" — माता — मेरी लीला! मेरी ममता!

grown into a noun: "my-ness", the possessive warmth of a mother. Read the story aloud twice:
once block by block, once at story speed.

## 19. Quiz

प्रश्नाः — answers in the key at the end; no peeking mid-quiz.

1. क + इ = ? (a) की (b) कि (c) इक (d) क
2. In कि, the vowel sign stands ______ the consonant and is spoken ______ it:

   only (d) the order the strokes were written in
5. The vowel of English *bit* differs from Sanskrit इ in: (a) duration only (b) quality —

1. (b) कि — the i-mātrā stands to the left; (a) की is *kī*, (c) इक reads *ika*. → §8, §15.
2. (b) before / after — written before, spoken after: the left-hook rule. → §8, §15.

7. (a) तिल = "sesame"; (b) लीला = "play"; (c) कति = "how many?". → §14.

## 20. Revision

पुनरावृत्तिः — one screen to remember.

> **The sounds:** इ /i/ · ई /iː/ — tongue high and forward, lips spread, one shape, and the
> *only* difference is duration: 2:1, no quality safety net. **The rule:** ि stands **left**
> of its consonant, spoken after it — written before, spoken after; ी stands right, like ा.
> **The geography:** the consonant is a house — right (ा ी), left (ि), below and above still
> to come. **The proof:** इति ≠ ईति, मति ≠ मित. **The prize:** 8 new words — इति, तिल, मति,
> कति, कील, लीला, ममता, मित.

- **In 1 day:** re-take the dictation (exercise 3) cold, and write कि की मि मी ति ती लि ली
  from memory — count corrected hooks.
- **In 1 week:** read the §14 list aloud at speed with full length discipline; re-run the
  इति/ईति discrimination; re-answer quiz Q1–2 and Q4 without looking.
- **Carried forward:** lesson 2 of this chapter (उ/ऊ) opens by re-summoning today's grid —

## 22. Teacher Notes

शिक्षक-टिप्पणी

**The reveal is the lesson.** Write क on the board, say "now watch me write *ki*" — and put
the hook down *to the left*, slowly, in silence. Let the room react before you explain.
Students who merely get told the rule re-derive it wrong within a week; students who *felt*
the surprise don't.

**Timing plan (45 min):** §3 retrieval, written (5) → hook reveal on the board (4) → sound
work: mirror, smile-check, length ladder (8) → left-hook rule + worked readings तिल मति कील
लीला इति, then the मति/मित trap (10) → syllable grid + word list in pairs (8) → exercises 1
and 6 (7) → homework briefing incl. the Chapter 1 +1-week re-run (3).

**Board work:** keep the consonant-house from §8 on the side board all lesson — one big क
with five labeled rooms; move sign-cards into rooms as they come up. Under it, a permanent
strip: क का कि की. Decompose every new word beneath it with akṣara ticks (म|ति vs मि|त).

**The two classic errors, and where to catch them:** (1) the hook planted right (की for
intended कि) — catch it during exercise 2 by watching pens, not worksheets: the error lives
in the writing sequence. The canopy check helps visual learners: "does your arc reach toward
its letter?" (2) mid-word hooks read onto the wrong consonant — catch it in dictation by
*deliberately* including both मति and मित in one pass, then having students swap slates and
hunt each other's hooks.

**Dictation logistics:** natural speed, no exaggerated lengths; collect error types
separately — wrong-side hook, missing curl on ी, length blur — each has a different fix
(§15, §15, §9 respectively).

**Differentiation — Hindi-literate learners:** the script mechanics are automatic; do not
let them idle. Their genuine work is recitation-grade 2:1 length (दिन/दीन shows they own the
contrast; fast speech is where they leak) and the known weak spot of careless कि/की swaps in
fast writing. Deputize them as hook-police for exercise 6 and recorders in pair reading;
pair them with script-beginners under the Chapter 1 rule: guide the hand with words, never
take the pen.

**What students find hard:** *believing* the reordering (the reveal handles this); mid-word
hooks at reading speed (मति/मित — normalize slow block-by-block reading for another week);
and, for English speakers, keeping short इ tense — the "cut ई in half" trick from §12 works
better than any tongue-position description.
