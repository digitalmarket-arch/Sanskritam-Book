## 1. Hook

Yesterday's mechanism built conjuncts you could see through: स्त is visibly स joined to त.
Today meet three signs it cannot explain: क्ष, त्र, ज्ञ. They stand on every Hindi shopfront
and in every Sanskrit text, and they look like letters the varṇamālā forgot — but the
alphabet closed in Chapter 4, and it is not reopening. These three broke the joining rules
so long ago that they hardened into new shapes entirely: combinations wearing costumes.
Behind each mask stand two friends you already know. Today you learn to see through cloth.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिक सन्दर्भः — where the costumes came from.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** Devanagari's conjuncts are the hardened handwriting of
> centuries. In hand-copied manuscripts the most frequent clusters were written countless
> times, and the scribes' fluent shortcuts slowly became the standard: strokes merged,
> parts shrank or rotated, and a few joins — क्ष, त्र and ज्ञ among them — fused so
> completely that their components stopped being visible at all. The shapes are
> conventions settled by long use, not designs. [REF:masica1991 ch.6]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Many schoolbook charts print क्ष त्र ज्ञ (often श्र too) at the end
> of the varṇamālā as if they were extra letters. That is a teaching convention with real
> utility — these signs are frequent and hard to sight-read, so charts make room for them
> — but it is not arithmetic: the inventory stays 13 + 33 + 2, and क्ष त्र ज्ञ are
> combinations of members, never new members. [REF:masica1991 ch.6]

Your Chapter 4 completion certificate is safe. Nothing new is entering the alphabet today
— you are only learning what its oldest handwriting habits look like.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिक व्याख्या — the third formation pattern, and one honest sound question.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Conjuncts form in three patterns: horizontal stem-loss (स्त, न्त —
> lesson 2), vertical stacking (ट्ट, ङ्क — met as they come), and a small irregular set of
> fused forms that must be learned as units: क्ष त्र ज्ञ श्र द्ध द्व द्य, plus the rarer
> ह्य and ह्म. Each is still, by composition, exactly two known consonants: क्+ष, त्+र,
> ज्+ञ, श्+र, द्+ध, द्+व, द्+य. [REF:masica1991 ch.6]

Why did exactly these fuse? Frequency did part of it (§4). Geometry did the rest: द and ह
are round, stemless letters — lesson 2's "drop the stem" instruction has nothing to grab —
so the script had to invent special joins for them; and the र hiding in त्र and श्र has
costume forms of its own, the next lesson's whole subject. [REF:masica1991 ch.6]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** In Unicode, every conjunct — however fused its printed shape — is
> stored as consonant + virāma + consonant: क्ष is the three code points क ् ष; ज्ञ is
> ज ् ञ. What print fuses, the encoding remembers. Type क, then ्, then ष on a phone and
> watch क्ष assemble itself. (Unicode Standard, Devanagari block.)

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** ज्ञ is written from ज् + ञ, and the classical value follows the
> spelling: /d͡ʑɲ/ — the palatal affricate released into the palatal nasal. Modern
> regional practice replaces it: [gj] across the Hindi belt (ज्ञान sounding like "gyāna"),
> [dn̪j]-type in Marathi-influenced recitation. These are regional habits carried into
> Sanskrit — real and respectable, and not the classical target. [REF:whitney1889]

This course has stood exactly here before: ऋ (v1.c02.l03), where the northern "ri" is a
habit and /r̩/ the target. Same posture today, same respect — train the target, name the
habits kindly, correct no one's mother tongue.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतव्याख्या — the lesson's idea in the simplest possible Sanskrit.

अत्र मित्रम् अस्ति।
(atra mitram asti.) "A friend is here." — यहाँ मित्र है।

ज्ञानम् बलम्।
(jñānam balam.) "Knowledge is strength." — ज्ञान ही बल है।

The second line has no word for *is* — the verbless nominal sentence, complete and
classical, exactly like इह रामः in Chapter 4. And a name for today's set: lesson 2 gave
you संयुक्ताक्षर (saṃyuktākṣara) "joined akṣara"; the fused ones are this lesson's title —
विशेषसंयोगाः (viśeṣasaṃyogāḥ) "the special joinings".

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी व्याख्या

आज के अक्षर आपके लिए नये नहीं हैं — हिन्दी पढ़ने वाली हर आँख इन्हें रोज़ देखती है: क्षमा
में क्ष, त्रिशूल में त्र, ज्ञान में ज्ञ, श्रीमान् में श्र, बुद्धि में द्ध, विद्या में द्य।
नया केवल यह भेद है कि ये अक्षर हैं ही नहीं — हर एक के भीतर दो पुराने परिचित जुड़े बैठे
हैं: क् + ष = क्ष, त् + र = त्र, ज् + ञ = ज्ञ, श् + र = श्र। स्कूल के चार्ट पर ये अकसर
वर्णमाला की पूँछ बनकर लटकते हैं — "क्ष त्र ज्ञ" — यह पढ़ाने की सुविधा है, गिनती नहीं।
गिनती वही रहेगी जो चौथे अध्याय में पूरी हुई: 13 स्वर, 33 व्यञ्जन, 2 अयोगवाह।

अब वह बात जिस पर हिन्दी बोलने वालों का ख़ास हक़ है: ज्ञ का उच्चारण। हिन्दी में ज्ञान
"ग्यान" बोला जाता है — रोज़ की भाषा के लिए यह बिलकुल ठीक है, कोई ग़लती नहीं। पर लिखावट
देखिए: ज्ञ में ज और ञ जुड़े हैं, ग और य नहीं। संस्कृत का शास्त्रीय लक्ष्य वही जुड़ाव है —
ज् की झंकार सीधे ञ की नासिका में खुलती है: /d͡ʑɲ/। ठीक वैसे ही जैसे ऋ के साथ हुआ था —
"रि" आदत है, /r̩/ लक्ष्य; यहाँ "ग्य" आदत है, ज्+ञ लक्ष्य। मराठी परम्परा इसी अक्षर को
"द्न्य" की ओर ले जाती है — आदतें अलग-अलग, अक्षर एक। हम लक्ष्य साधेंगे, और आदतों को
इज़्ज़त से उनका नाम देंगे।

तीसरी बात, पुरानी साथी: अन्तिम अ। मित्र "मित्र्" नहीं — मि-त्र; पुत्र पु-त्र; क्षेत्र
क्षे-त्र; शुद्ध शु-द्ध। हिन्दी की चाल में आख़िरी अ चुप रहता है; संस्कृत में वह हमेशा
बोलता है — त्र जैसे भारी अक्षर के बाद भी।

और आज का तोहफ़ा: अत्र, तत्र, यत्र — "यहाँ, वहाँ, जहाँ" — तीनों एक साथ पढ़ने में आ गये।
हिन्दी में भी जोड़ी बनाने की यही आदत है — जहाँ मित्र, वहाँ सुख; संस्कृत ठीक उसी ढंग से
कहती है: यत्र मित्रम् तत्र सुखम्। जो भाषा आप रोज़ बोलते हैं, वह इस पाठ को पहले से आधा
जानती है — आज बस पर्दा हटा है।

## 8. English Explanation

Seven cards today — not seven letters. Every card shows a unit, its seam, and a word.

### The cards — units with visible seams

- **क्ष (kṣa) = क् + ष** — a क grown a second pocket. One beat, no vowel inside: /kʂɐ/.
  Word: क्षमा (kṣamā) "patience".
- **त्र (tra) = त् + र** — त's curve riding a diagonal tail; the tail *is* the र. Word:
  मित्र (mitra) "friend".
- **ज्ञ (jña) = ज् + ञ** — the most disguised sign in the script: nothing in the modern
  shape shows its parts. Word: ज्ञान (jñāna) "knowledge".
- **श्र (śra) = श् + र** — the reduced श (loop kept, lower limb gone) on the same diagonal
  tail as त्र. Word: श्री (śrī) "radiance; the honorific".
- **द्ध (ddha) = द् + ध** — stemless द stacks instead: द on top, ध tucked beneath. Words:
  बुद्ध (buddha), शुद्ध (śuddha).
- **द्व (dva) = द् + व** — द over व. Word: द्वि (dvi) "two".
- **द्य (dya) = द् + य** — द over य. Word: विद्या (vidyā) "knowledge, learning".

Two rarer cousins, recognition only for now: ह्य (hya) and ह्म (hma) — round, stemless ह
inventing joins the same way द does. Name their components when you meet them; no words
today. And one free gift: the reduced श of श्र joins य identically — श्य (śya) — so
पश्य (paśya) "look!", flagged 🔊 since the course's first story, reads plain from today.

### अत्र retires — the location family completes

अत्र (atra) "here" has been carried by audio since Chapter 1 — the last story-glue word
still wearing 🔊. Its त्र was the lock; you now hold the key. With it come its siblings:

- **अत्र** (atra) "here" — यहाँ · **तत्र** (tatra) "there" — वहाँ · **यत्र** (yatra)
  "where" — जहाँ.
- यत्र is the *relative* where: it never asks, it points forward to a तत्र — यत्र मित्रम्
  तत्र सुखम्। "Where there is a friend, there is happiness." (Its asking cousin कुत्र
  (kutra) "where?" is readable today too, and will get its own turn on stage.)
- Beside Chapter 2's ततः अतः इतः "then / hence / from here" and कुतः "from where?", your
  connector toolkit is suddenly rich: place, motion and sequence, all in your own hand.

### Worked readings

- **ज्ञान** = ज्ञ + ा + न → jñā-na — /d͡ʑɲɑːn̪ɐ/. The mātrā rides the whole unit: ज्ञा.
- **क्षेत्र** = क्षे + त्र → kṣe-tra — /kʂeːt̪ɾɐ/ "field". Two ligatures in one word.
- **मित्र** = मि + त्र → mi-tra — /mit̪ɾɐ/; and पुत्र (putra) "son", नेत्र (netra) "eye",
  पात्र (pātra) "vessel" — the त्र family assembles itself.
- **विद्या** = वि + द्या → vi-dyā — /ʋid̪jɑː/; द्य + ा, the mātrā again on the unit.
- **द्वि** = द्वि → dvi — /d̪ʋi/ "two" — lesson one's cognate table, now readable (§13).

One rule covers all of them: **the vowel belongs to the whole unit.** क्षे, ज्ञा, त्रि,
श्री, द्वि — write the ligature, then dress it in any mātrā you own.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — four drills, in order. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c05l03-drill.mp3` (drills 1–3),
`planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c05l03-word-list.mp3` (drill 4 + dictation track).

**Drill 1 — the ज्ञ weld, slow motion.** Say ज and hold the closure: ज्. Keep the tongue
exactly where it is, drop the door to the nose, and hum: ञ. Now delete the pause between
them: ज्-ञ … ज्ञ. Then upward: ज्ञ — ज्ञा — ज्ञान. The whole event lives at one spot on
the palate; nothing moves but the nasal door.

**Drill 2 — the क्ष weld.** क् at the back, release straight into the curled hiss of ष:
क्-ष … क्ष. Then क्ष — क्षा — क्षमा — क्षेत्र. Guard the seam: no अ may leak in between
(that error has a name in §12).

**Drill 3 — the beat test.** Components spoken apart take two beats: क … ष. The unit takes
one: क्ष. Alternate and feel the difference: क-ष / क्ष, त-र / त्र, ज-ञ / ज्ञ. One shape,
one beat — the whole idea of the conjunct in your mouth.

**Drill 4 — words and the trio.** From the word-list audio, each word twice: the त्र
family first (मित्र पुत्र नेत्र पात्र — final अ full!), then ज्ञान क्षमा क्षेत्र, then
श्री श्रम, then बुद्ध शुद्ध — hold the double द a beat longer — then विद्या द्वि. Finish
with the location trio in rhythm: अत्र — तत्र — यत्र, and the sentence अत्र मित्रम्
अस्ति।

## 10. IPA

No new phonemes today — the chapter's promise holds. The ligatures are sequences of
Chapter 3–4 values written as units:

क्ष /kʂ/ · त्र /t̪ɾ/ · ज्ञ /d͡ʑɲ/ · श्र /ɕɾ/ · द्ध /d̪d̪ʱ/ · द्व /d̪ʋ/ · द्य /d̪j/ ·
ह्य /ɦj/ · ह्म /ɦm/

**Word transcriptions:** ज्ञान /d͡ʑɲɑːn̪ɐ/ · क्षेत्र /kʂeːt̪ɾɐ/ · मित्र /mit̪ɾɐ/ ·
श्री /ɕɾiː/ · बुद्ध /bud̪d̪ʱɐ/ · विद्या /ʋid̪jɑː/ · अत्र /ɐt̪ɾɐ/.

**Narrow notes:** the double closure in बुद्ध and शुद्ध is real duration — hold the
dental contact a beat before the breathy release. For ज्ञ, the regional realizations are
written in brackets because they are habits, not the phoneme sequence: [gj] (northern
"gyāna"), [dn̪j] (Marathi-side practice). Classical target: /d͡ʑɲ/ — same policy, same
notation, as ऋ's [ɾɪ] in v1.c02.l03.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थानम् — three seams to feel; the component cards
(`planned:assets/images/volume-1/v1c05l03-ligature-component-cards.svg`) mark each one.

1. **ज्ञ — one home, two exits.** Tongue-blade at the palate for ज्; it stays put while
   the velum opens and the sound walks out through the nose as ञ. If your tongue jumps to
   the ridge or the back of the mouth, you have wandered toward a habit (§12).
2. **क्ष — back, then curl.** The back of the tongue seals for क्; the release lands
   directly on ष's curled-back tip. Two articulators, zero vowel between them — palm on
   the jaw: it must not drop between क् and ष.
3. **त्र — teeth, then tap.** त् on the teeth releasing into a single ɾ tap. श्र is its
   sibling with the hiss of श in front: ɕ into tap.
4. **Mirror check:** run the beat test (§9 drill 3) watching the jaw — components bounce
   twice, the unit bounces once.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्य-भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix. These feed the quiz distractors.

1. **ज्ञ as [gj] when the classical target is wanted (mostly Hindi speakers).** In
   recitation or careful Sanskrit, ज्ञान arrives as "gyāna" — the mother-tongue habit
   doing its honest work in the wrong register. *Fix:* the slow-motion weld (§9 drill 1):
   ज् held, nasal door opened, nothing moving. Habit for Hindi, target for Sanskrit —
   both named, neither scolded.
2. **क्ष read as क + ष with a vowel inside** — "kaṣamā" for क्षमा. The invisible virāma
   was ignored. *Fix:* the beat test (§9 drill 3): क्ष is one beat; if your jaw bounced
   twice, an अ leaked into the seam.
3. **त्र confused with त्त.** Both hang below a त-like top in small print. *Fix:* read the
   tail — a straight diagonal stroke is र (त्र); a second full त-body is त्त (tta), the
   regular mechanism's product. Diagonal = त्र, always.
4. **श्र read as plain श.** The eye takes the loop and drops the tail, turning श्रम into
   शम. *Fix:* the tail check again — before saying any श, glance below the line; a
   diagonal tail means an र is waiting: /ɕɾ/, not /ɕ/.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — three families hiding in the word list.

### ज्ञान and the knowing family

**ज्ञान** (jñāna) "knowledge" — ज्ञान · /d͡ʑɲɑːn̪ɐ/

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** ज्ञान is the noun of the root √ज्ञा (jñā) "to know" — one line of
> preview: जानाति (jānāti) "he/she knows", a Volume 2 verb. And the family is older than
> Sanskrit: √ज्ञा, English *know*, and Greek *gnōsis* are cognates — parallel descendants
> of Proto-Indo-European \*ǵneh₃- "to know". (English later borrowed *gnosis* from Greek
> as a word; the deep kinship with *know* was always there.) [REF:mallory2006]

### मित्र — the friend with a long memory

**मित्र** (mitra) "friend" — मित्र, दोस्त · /mit̪ɾɐ/

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** As a dictionary word मित्र "friend" is neuter — in sentences it
> wears म्: मित्रम् अत्र अस्ति। [REF:apte1890 s.v. मित्र]

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** In the Ṛgveda, Mitra is a deity of pact and alliance — the
> god and the common noun "friend" are one and the same word. In this lesson मित्र does
> honest secular work; the deity keeps his hymns.

### द्वि and विद्या — the cognate shelf grows

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** द्वि (dvi) "two" ~ English *two*, Latin *duo*; and its neighbor
> त्रि (tri) "three" ~ *three*, Latin *trēs* — cognates from shared Proto-Indo-European
> ancestors, not borrowings in either direction. Both stood in lesson one's cognate table
> as promises; today both are readable. [REF:mallory2006]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** विद्या "knowledge, learning" belongs to √विद् (vid) "to know" —
> the root of वेद (veda) — and is cognate with Latin *vidēre* "to see" and English *wit*,
> all from Proto-Indo-European \*weid- "to see, to know". A genuine cognate, correctly
> framed: siblings from one ancestor, no loan, no "mother of languages". [REF:mallory2006]

And one small descent, in the गृह → घर tradition of Chapter 4: Sanskrit क्षेत्र (kṣetra)
"field" is the ancestor of Hindi खेत — today's word, worn smooth by everyday mouths on its
way to the farm. [REF:masica1991]

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — in Volume 1 this is a **script rule**: the learned-unit set, and its honest
boundary.

### The units and their components — learn both directions

| Unit | Components | IAST | IPA | Anchor word |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| क्ष | क् + ष | kṣa | /kʂɐ/ | क्षमा |
| त्र | त् + र | tra | /t̪ɾɐ/ | मित्र |
| ज्ञ | ज् + ञ | jña | /d͡ʑɲɐ/ | ज्ञान |
| श्र | श् + र | śra | /ɕɾɐ/ | श्री |
| द्ध | द् + ध | ddha | /d̪d̪ʱɐ/ | बुद्ध |
| द्व | द् + व | dva | /d̪ʋɐ/ | द्वि |
| द्य | द् + य | dya | /d̪jɐ/ | विद्या |
| ह्य | ह् + य | hya | /ɦjɐ/ | — recognition |
| ह्म | ह् + म | hma | /ɦmɐ/ | — recognition |

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** These forms are read and written as units, but they obey every law
> you know: the virāma sits invisibly inside each (§5), the final component carries the
> vowel, and mātrās dress the whole unit — क्षे, ज्ञा, श्री, द्वि. Lesson 5's exam asks
> for both directions: given components, write the unit; given the unit, name the
> components. [REF:masica1991 ch.6]

### Stroke order — the fused forms

Course convention as ever — body first, headline last; the stroke card
(`planned:assets/images/volume-1/v1c05l03-stroke-order.svg`) is the authority, and fonts
vary in details.

- **क्ष** — write क's left loop, add the second pocket below-right, then the stem;
  headline last.
- **त्र / श्र** — the top first (त's curve, or श's kept loop), then the diagonal tail
  down-left. The tail is a र in costume — its full wardrobe is the next lesson.
- **ज्ञ** — pure shape-learning: top arm, crossbar, hooked base, headline. Nothing
  visibly ज or ञ survives; the card, not etymology, guides your pen.
- **द्ध द्व द्य** — write the full द on top, tuck the partner beneath (ध, व, य),
  headline last. Stemless letters stack because they have no stem to drop.

### The honest boundary — and a milestone unlocked

Triple clusters exist: स्त्र (stra), न्त्र (ntra) — the same principles stack one layer
deeper, and the next lesson reads one inside a moonlit word. No memorizing the long tail:
a few dozen conjuncts cover the overwhelming majority of classical text, and the
mechanism plus today's units generate the rest on sight.

Two small sentence upgrades, both used above: neuter subjects wear म् where masculine
ones wear ः — मित्रम्, ज्ञानम् beside पुत्रः, श्रमः (whole-word habit since जलम् in
Chapter 3; the full case system is Volume 2's story). And the chapter's milestone line is
now fully readable: **नमस्ते! अहम् अत्र अस्मि।** (namaste! aham atra asmi.) "Namaste! I
am here." — अस्मि (asmi) "I am", glossed whole-word like every verb so far, is lesson 2's
mechanism applied to स् + मि. Lesson 5 will make it official; you could read it tonight.

## 16. Conversation

सम्भाषणम् — someone is looking for a friend. 🔊 audio recorded into the Chapter 5 drill
pack (§27).

— मित्रम् अत्र अस्ति?
(mitram atra asti?) "Is the friend here?" — क्या मित्र यहाँ है?

— आम्! मित्रम् अत्र अस्ति!
(ām! mitram atra asti!) "Yes — the friend is here!" — हाँ! मित्र यहाँ है!

— गुरुः तत्र अस्ति?
(guruḥ tatra asti?) "Is the teacher there?" — क्या गुरु वहाँ हैं?

— आम्, गुरुः तत्र अस्ति। पश्य!
(ām, guruḥ tatra asti. paśya!) "Yes, the teacher is there. Look!" — हाँ, गुरु वहाँ हैं।
देखो!

Housekeeping, honestly: nothing here is borrowed. आम् retired with lesson 1's virāma,
अस्ति with lesson 2's mechanism, अत्र and तत्र today, and पश्य through §8's श्य gift. The
question is carried by rising tone alone, as it has been since घटः अत्र अस्ति? in
Chapter 3.

## 17. Story

कथा — मित्रम् · The Friend. Six lines; each line: Devanagari, IAST, English, Hindi.

1. बालकः अत्र अस्ति।
   (bālakaḥ atra asti.) "The boy is here." — बालक यहाँ है।
2. मित्रम् तत्र अस्ति।
   (mitram tatra asti.) "His friend is there." — मित्र वहाँ है।
3. बालकः वदति — अत्र! अत्र!
   (bālakaḥ vadati — atra! atra!) "The boy calls: Here! Over here!" — बालक कहता है —
   यहाँ! यहाँ!
4. ततः मित्रम् चलति। मित्रम् वदति — नमस्ते! अहम् अत्र अस्मि!
   (tataḥ mitram calati. mitram vadati — namaste! aham atra asmi!) "Then the friend walks
   over. The friend says: Namaste! I am here!" — तब मित्र चला आता है। मित्र कहता है —
   नमस्ते! मैं यहाँ हूँ!
5. गुरुः हसति — मम पुत्रः! बालकः हसति — मम मित्रम्!
   (guruḥ hasati — mama putraḥ! bālakaḥ hasati — mama mitram!) "The teacher laughs: My
   son! The boy laughs: My friend!" — गुरु हँसते हैं — मेरा बेटा! बालक हँसता है — मेरा
   मित्र!
6. गुरुः वदति — यत्र मित्रम् तत्र सुखम्।
   (guruḥ vadati — yatra mitram tatra sukham.) "The teacher says: Where there is a
   friend, there is happiness." — गुरु कहते हैं — जहाँ मित्र, वहाँ सुख।

And the housekeeping that is really a milestone: for the first time, a story built on
full sentences — with its *is*, its *here*, its greeting — carries **zero 🔊 marks**.
Earlier stories went unglossed only by dodging the glue words; this one simply owns them.

Front: the unit, large (क्ष त्र ज्ञ श्र द्ध द्व द्य). Back: the components with the +

## 20. Revision

पुनरावृत्तिः — one screen to remember.

> **The set:** क्ष त्र ज्ञ श्र द्ध द्व द्य (+ ह्य ह्म to recognize) — units to the eye,
> two known consonants underneath: क्+ष, त्+र, ज्+ञ, श्+र, द्+ध, द्+व, द्+य. **The law:**
> the virāma sits invisibly inside; the vowel dresses the whole unit (क्षे, ज्ञा, श्री).
> **The sound:** ज्ञ = /d͡ʑɲ/ classical target; [gj] and [dn̪j] are regional habits,
> named with respect. **The traps:** no vowel in the seam; the tail is र (त्र ≠ त्त,
> श्र ≠ श). **The family:** अत्र तत्र यत्र — and अत्र, the last 🔊 glue word, retired.
> **The line:** नमस्ते! अहम् अत्र अस्मि। — readable tonight.

## 22. Teacher Notes

शिक्षक-टिप्पणी

**Stage the reveal.** Write क्ष large on the board and ask, deadpan: "New letter?" Let
the debate run two minutes — Hindi-literate students will say "it's just क्ष", others
will count Chapter 4's inventory on their fingers. Then x-ray it live: क + ् + ष,
ideally typed on a projected phone keyboard. The Unicode moment converts the room faster
than any diagram; the history (§4) can follow it, not precede it.

**Timing plan (45 min):** §3 retrieval, written (5) → hook + board reveal + Unicode
x-ray (6) → the seven cards, components chorus: teacher shows unit, class answers
components (8) → ज्ञ honesty station: habit vs target, both produced by everyone,
respect framing said aloud (6) → writing lab: stroke demos + tracing start (8) → word
list in pairs, beat test policing the seams (6) → location trio + correlative frame on
the board, exercise 5 launched (4) → homework briefing incl. re-dictation (2).

**The ज्ञ station needs the ऋ speech.** Some students will hear "your Hindi is wrong."
Repeat the v1.c02.l03 formula: the habit is a real, respectable product of a living
language; the target is what classical Sanskrit spells; educated speakers hold both and
choose by register. Hindi-literate learners then become your best allies — they already
sight-read every ligature and only need the sound relabeled.

**Differentiation:** English-habit learners struggle with /d͡ʑɲ/ and geminate द्ध — give
them the slow welds and the held closure. Hindi-literate learners get the discrimination
grid (त्र/त्त, श्र/श in small print) and referee roles in dictation. Fast finishers can
hunt स्त्र-type triples in any printed page and report whether the mechanism scaled.

**What students find hard:** believing क्ष is not a letter after a lifetime of chart
tails (the count 13+33+2, repeated cheerfully, settles it); keeping the classical ज्ञ
from collapsing mid-word (drill words, not syllables); the त्र/त्त tell at reading speed
(the tail chant: "diagonal is र").
