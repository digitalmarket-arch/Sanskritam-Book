## 1. Hook

Three words have trailed this course since its opening week — धर्म (dharma), कर्म (karma), अर्थ
(artha): the star of lesson 1's loan table, the word lesson 3 used to teach you the full final
vowel, and the ear-word beside it. You can say all three, gloss all three, defend their endings
against every "dharm" — but you have never read one of them. Between you and that trio stands a
single letter with two secret costumes. Today you learn both, and the oldest ear-words in your
notebook come home readable.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिक सन्दर्भः — the letter with its own proper name.

Chapter 5 has been one long study in combination: lesson 1 gave you the virāma, lesson 2 the
stem-loss mechanism, lesson 3 the fused ligatures learned as units. One letter has been quietly
excused from all of it: र. Today it pays its debt — with the tradition's own terminology in hand.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The tradition's name for the r-sound is रेफ (repha). Where other consonants
> are named with -कार — ककार (kakāra) "the k", मकार (makāra) "the m" — र bears a proper name of
> its own, and the grammarians use it constantly. [REF:apte1890 s.v. रेफ]

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** The hook itself is no printer's invention: the superscript r-form comes
> down through the manuscript tradition of Nāgarī and its sister scripts, which compacted r-initial
> clusters into a small sign above the line; print simply inherited the habit. [REF:masica1991 ch.6]

So the sound whose glyph is two strokes and a line (v1.c04.l01) turns out to be the script's most
theatrical letter: on-stage it is र; as first member of a cluster it flies above the headline; as
second member it shrinks to a diagonal. One sound, three costumes — and after today, no written
form of र anywhere in this volume will be a stranger.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिक व्याख्या — one phoneme, three spellings, and the script's second time-travel trick.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Written र has positional variants but spoken र does not: full letter र,
> superscript repha (धर्म), and subscript diagonal (प्र) all spell the same tap /ɾ/. Cluster
> position alone — first member or later member — decides which costume appears. [REF:masica1991 ch.6]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The unit of Devanagari is the akṣara, not the letter: within an akṣara,
> components sit where the script's rules put them, not in strict left-to-right sound order. The
> ि-hook stands *before* the consonant it is spoken *after* (v1.c02.l01); the repha stands at the
> *end* of the akṣara it is spoken *first* in. Two mirror-image instances of one principle: the eye
> takes in the block whole. [REF:masica1991 ch.6]

One consequence worth hearing before you see it: the repha's र closes the *previous* syllable.
धर्म divides dhar-ma — say it slowly and feel the r land at the end of the first beat, one door
earlier than the hook appears on paper. That mismatch — ear early, eye late — is the whole
difficulty of today, and it is exactly the difficulty you conquered when कि (ki) first taught your
eye to meet a vowel your mouth had not said.

चन्द्रः today.

## 8. English Explanation

No new sound today — र has been yours since Chapter 4. Today the *script* catches up: two faces,
one small irregular, and then the homecoming.

### Face one — र first: the repha

When र is the first member of a cluster, it vanishes from the writing line entirely and reappears
as a small hook above the headline — the **repha** — perched on the akṣara that *follows* it in
sound. Watch धर्म (dharma) being built. Say it slowly: dha — r — ma. Write ध. Now the र: no letter
appears. Instead it folds itself into a hook, flies over the next akṣara, and lands on म's
shoulder: र् + म = र्म, so ध + र्म = धर्म. The rule in four words: **written right, read left.**
The hook stands at the end of its akṣara but is read before all of it.

You have seen this trick before, in mirror image. In v1.c02.l01 the ि-hook was written *before*
the consonant and spoken *after* it — the script's first time-travel trick. The repha is the
second, running the other way: written after, spoken before. Same principle both times — the
akṣara is one block, and your eye must learn its scanning order: **top first.** Before reading any
akṣara aloud, glance at its upper right; if a hook rides there, an r leaves your mouth first.

Placement, precisely: the repha rides the following akṣara as a whole. In सूर्य (sūrya) the र
comes before य, so the hook sits over the य — सू + र्य, sū-rya. And when that akṣara wears a
rightward mātrā, the hook slides to the very end, onto the mātrā's post: watch वार्ता (vārtā)
"news" — a display word only — where the hook rides the ा of ता. Rightmost, always.

### Face two — र second: the diagonal

When र is a *later* member of a cluster, the preceding consonant stays whole and the र shrinks to
a short diagonal stroke tucked low into its stem: प्र (pra), क्र (kra), ग्र (gra). Round letters
take it on the body — द्र (dra) hangs it from द's tail. This face is the tame one: it sits where
it is read, after its consonant, inside the same block. One variant to recognize: the ट-varga
letters, which have no stem to lean on, take the r as a small caret beneath — ट्र (ṭra), as in the
display word राष्ट्र (rāṣṭra) "nation".

And the mechanism scales, exactly as lesson 3 promised: मन्त्र (mantra) ends in न्त्र — a
*three*-consonant cluster, न् + त् + र, spelled by the same two moves you already own (stem-loss
न् joins त, diagonal र tucks below). चन्द्र (candra) does the same with न्द्र. Nothing new to
learn — the old machine running one gear longer.

### The third little face — रु and रू

With उ and ऊ, र refuses the normal under-hooks and ties the vowel into its own body: **रु** (ru)
and **रू** (rū) — learned units, like the lesson-3 ligatures. Your word for them today is रूप
(rūpa) "form" — and Hindi speakers have written the pair since childhood: रुपया begins with it.

### The trio's homecoming

Now read these, slowly, with full final vowels:

**धर्म** (dharma) · **कर्म** (karma) · **अर्थ** (artha)

In v1.c01.l03 these were sounds and glosses only — that lesson drilled you: *final अ full —
artha, never "arth"* — and धर्म was the very word it used to name schwa deletion. For four
chapters you have carried them by ear. Today the eye joins: ध + र्म. क + र्म. अ + र्थ. Read them
again. Of the four ear-words that third lesson taught — अमृत, अग्नि, अर्थ, अहम् — अर्थ is the last
to come home. The trio is yours, in the original, forever.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — nothing new in the mouth today, so the drills move to the eye: reading fluency, both
faces. Audio: 🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c05l04-drill.mp3` (drills 1–3 + the कर्म/क्रम
ear test), `planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c05l04-word-list.mp3` (drill 4 + dictation track).

**Drill 1 — the hook ladder (र first).** Read across with the audio, r *first* in every beat, eyes
hitting the top of each block before anything else: र्क र्ग र्म र्य र्व र्ण (rka · rga · rma · rya
· rva · rṇa). Then the ladder inside words: धर् — धर्म; कर् — कर्म; सर् — सर्व.

**Drill 2 — the diagonal ladder (र second).** Read across, r *second*, riding out of each
consonant: प्र क्र ग्र द्र ट्र (pra · kra · gra · dra · ṭra). Then with mātrās aboard: प्रि (pri),
ग्रा (grā) — and notice प्रि: a diagonal r below, an i-hook in front — two of the script's
reordering tricks sharing one block, and your eye handles both.

**Drill 3 — the two-face switch.** Alternate slowly, naming the face before reading it: र्क — क्र,
र्ग — ग्र, र्त — त्र. Then the minimal pair of the day, three times: **कर्म — क्रम** (karma —
krama). Same three sounds; only the r's position differs. Finish with the ear test on the drill
audio: twelve items, each कर्म or क्रम in random order — write which you heard.

**Drill 4 — the words, then the trio.** The §14 list with the audio, block by block, then whole
(सू-र्य, सूर्य). Close with the homecoming, slow and full: धर्-म, कर्-म, अर्-थ — *dharma, karma,
artha* — every final अ complete. Then once at reading pace, timing yourself: the seventeen words
in under a minute is lesson 5's bar.

## 10. IPA

No new values today: every written guise of र — full letter, repha hook, diagonal — is the same
alveolar tap **/ɾ/** (recitation may trill [r]; the target taps).

**The triple that must not blur:** कृ (kṛ) /kr̩/ is the *vowel* ऋ riding क as a mātrā — as in
संस्कृतम् (saṃskṛtam); क्र (kra) /kɾɐ/ is the tap *after* क; र्क (rka) /ɾkɐ/ is the tap *before*
क. Three different objects, one family resemblance on paper.

**Narrow note — syllable division:** धर्म is [d̪ʱɐɾ.mɐ] — the repha's /ɾ/ closes the *first*
syllable: dhar-ma, not dha-rma. That is why the ear meets the r one door earlier than the eye
meets the hook (§5).

**Word transcriptions:** धर्म /d̪ʱɐɾmɐ/ · कर्म /kɐɾmɐ/ · अर्थ /ɐɾt̪ʰɐ/ · सूर्य /suːɾjɐ/ · प्रिय
/pɾijɐ/ · चन्द्र /t͡ɕɐn̪d̪ɾɐ/ · मन्त्र /mɐn̪t̪ɾɐ/ · रूप /ɾuːpɐ/.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थानम् — the articulators rest today; the work has moved to the eye and the pen. The geography
card (`planned:assets/images/volume-1/v1c05l04-repha-geography.svg`) is the map: one akṣara drawn
large, with the two r-territories marked — hook country above the headline, diagonal country low
on the stem.

1. **The eye check — scan the roof first.** Before reading any akṣara, glance above its headline.
   Hook present → an r leaves your mouth *before* the rest of the block. This one saccade is the
   lesson's entire motor skill.
2. **The floor check.** A short diagonal low on a stem (or a caret under a ट-varga letter) → r
   *after* that consonant, inside the same block. Where the ऋ-mātrā's curl ृ hangs below-center,
   the r-diagonal cuts across the stem — कृ and क्र live one storey apart; look twice.
3. **The pen check.** Writing order mirrors neither trap: akṣara first, hook last (§15). If your
   pen wants to draw the hook first, stop — you are writing the sound order, not the script's.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्य-भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix. These feed the quiz distractors.

1. **Reading the repha where it stands (too late).** धर्म comes out "dhamra" — the r attached to
   the following syllable instead of preceding it. *Fix:* the rule aloud — written right, read
   left; the r comes out *before* the akṣara it rides. Drill 1's ladder until the top-first scan
   is automatic.
2. **Writing the hook first, or on the wrong block.** The pen follows the ear, draws the hook
   before म exists — and it lands over ध, turning धर्म into a misread. *Fix:* the writing-order
   convention (§15): finish the akṣara, then perch the hook on it — the akṣara that *follows* the
   r in sound, rightmost element and all (सूर्य: over the य).
3. **र्क and क्र swapped.** Both look like "क with something extra", and under time pressure the
   faces trade places. *Fix:* geography, chanted — above = र first, below = र second — and the
   कर्म/क्रम minimal pair (§9 drill 3) until the switch feels physical.
4. **रु built from parts.** The regular under-hook produces a form that is simply not how रु is
   written. *Fix:* रु and रू are learned units, like क्ष and त्र — write them whole (रुपया is the
   anchor every Hindi writer owns), and keep कृ's vowel-curl out of their territory.

### चक्र and *wheel* — the marquee cognate

**चक्र** (cakra) "wheel" — पहिया · /t͡ɕɐkɾɐ/

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Sanskrit चक्र, English *wheel*, and Greek *kúklos* (source of English
> *cycle*) are parallel descendants of one Proto-Indo-European word for "wheel" — reconstructed
> \*kʷekʷlos, from the root \*kʷel- "turn". Cognates, not borrowings: nobody lent anybody a wheel.
> [REF:mallory2006]

Loan and cognate in one lesson, the lesson-1 tool sharpened: *karma* was handed over; *wheel* and
चक्र are cousins who stayed home.

### सूर्य and सर्व — worked readings

- **सूर्य** = सू + र्य → sū-rya — /suːɾjɐ/ "sun". The hook rides the य — the akṣara *after* the r
  in sound. Beside it, आचार्य (ācārya) "teacher, preceptor" ends the same way: आ-चा-र्य.
- **सर्व** = स + र्व → sa-rva — /sɐɾʋɐ/ "all". Hook over व; the व stays a light /ʋ/. पर्वत
  (parvata) "mountain" hides the same block mid-word: प-र्व-त.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — in Volume 1 this is a **script rule** section: no new letters, but the r-rules that
finish the conjunct system.

### The r-rules, formally

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** (1) र as *first* member of a cluster is written as the repha — a hook above
> the headline, placed on the following akṣara at its rightmost element (mātrā-post included) —
> and is read *before* that akṣara. (2) र as a *later* member is written as a short diagonal on
> the preceding consonant — low on the stem, a caret beneath stemless ट-varga letters — and is
> read in place, after it. (3) With उ and ऊ, र takes the irregular units रु and रू.
> [REF:masica1991 ch.6]

The encoding, meanwhile, never reorders anything: धर्म is typed ध + र + ् + म — sound order,
virāma inside, exactly as lesson 3's Unicode insight promised. The hook is the *renderer's*
choice; the machine remembers where the र truly lives. Type it on any phone and watch the letters
vanish into the hook.

### The writing-order convention

Write the akṣara first; perch the hook last. For धर्म: ध, then म, then the repha on म's shoulder,
headline closing as usual. Many Hindi writers do this automatically — today the habit becomes a
rule you can state: **the repha is written after the akṣara it is read before.** The pen and the
ear disagree on purpose; the rule referees.

### Stroke order — the two faces

Course convention as ever; the stroke card
(`planned:assets/images/volume-1/v1c05l04-stroke-order.svg`) is the authority, and fonts vary.

- **Repha** — one small sickle above the headline: start at the top, curve down-left to touch the
  line. Placed rightmost — over the stem, or over the mātrā's post if one extends the block (वार्ता).
- **Diagonal r** — one short stroke from the lower half of the host's stem, sweeping down-left:
  क्र, प्र, ग्र; on round द it springs from the tail: द्र; under ट it is a small caret: ट्र.
- **रु / रू** — written as whole units: र first, then the vowel tied into the body at the waist.
  Do not assemble them from र + ु parts (§12).

## 20. Revision

पुनरावृत्तिः — one screen to remember.

> **The two faces:** र first → repha, a hook above the *following* akṣara — written right, read
> left; र second → a diagonal on the *preceding* consonant (caret under ट-varga). **The twin:**
> the ि-hook plays the same time-travel in mirror. **The units:** रु रू — written whole, never
> assembled. **The triple:** कृ (vowel) · क्र (r second) · र्क (r first) — one storey apart. **The
> order:** akṣara first, hook last. **The pair:** कर्म ≠ क्रम. **The prize:** seventeen words —
> धर्म, कर्म, अर्थ home at last, चक्र carrying its wheel, मन्त्र scaling the machine.

## 22. Teacher Notes

शिक्षक-टिप्पणी

**Two summits, in order — and save the trio for the second.** First the machinery: both faces on
the board, drilled to comfort on syllables and *new* words (सूर्य, चक्र, ग्राम) that carry no
emotional freight. Then the homecoming, staged: write धर्म — कर्म — अर्थ on a clean board in
silence and let the room read them unprompted. Most classes need a beat to realize what just
happened; give the beat its space, then name it — these were the course's first ear-words, and
they just became text.

**Timing plan (45 min):** §3 retrieval, written (5) → hook + geography map: both faces on धर्म and
प्र (7) → repha lab: hook ladder, board building of धर्म/सूर्य, writing-order narration (8) →
diagonal lab: ladder, कर्म/क्रम switch, ट्र aside (7) → रु रू + रूप (3) → word list in pairs,
top-scan policing (6) → THE HOMECOMING: the trio read cold; then the loan-table beat — शर्करा on
the board (5) → exercises 1–2 launch + homework incl. the 1-day re-dictation (4).

**The कर्म/क्रम switch is the week's referee.** Run it as call-and-response with cards; students
hold up "hook" or "diagonal" before reading. The ear test sorts out who is reading the page and
who is guessing from vocabulary.

**Differentiation:** Hindi-literate learners read every form on sight — their work is *naming*:
put them on exercise 5's narration and on explaining writes-right-reads-left to script-newcomers;
the rule is new to them even when the habit is old. Script-newcomers need the top-first saccade
built deliberately — the spotting drill twice daily beats one long session. Watch their सूर्य: the
commonest slip is perching the hook on सू.

**What students find hard:** believing the hook is not decoration (the धर्म flight path converts
them); र्क vs क्र under time pressure (geography chant); keeping कृ out of the r-family (show
संस्कृतम् beside क्रम — one storey apart); and writing the hook last when the ear screams "r comes
first!" — the narration drill makes the discipline audible.
