## 1. Hook

Five days ago you could not read a single Devanagari letter. Today you will read twelve real
Sanskrit words aloud, write six letters from dictation, hear the difference between "sweet
sound" and "time" in a single vowel-beat — and prove all of it. Nothing new enters this
lesson. Everything you already own comes out and gets counted. That is not a lesser lesson;
it is the one that makes the other four permanent.

## 3. Previous Lesson Review

पुनरावृत्तिः — this section *is* the lesson today. Four stations, one per lesson. At each
station: answer from memory first [REF:roediger2006]; only then check the pointer.

### Station 1 — What Sanskrit is (lesson 1)

1. Give the two evidence-based dates: the Ṛgveda's composition range, Pāṇini's century.
2. *Yoga* and *mother* both connect English to Sanskrit — but differently. Explain the
   difference in one sentence each.
3. Name the four registers and give one example of each from anywhere in this chapter.
4. What does संस्कृतम् (saṃskṛtam) itself mean, and from what parts?

*Check against l01 §4, §5, §8, §13.*

### Station 2 — The sound map (lesson 2)

1. Recite the five places of articulation back-to-front, touching each with your tongue:
   क… च… ट… त… प.
2. What two axes organize the varṇamālā grid?
3. The honest counts: vowels? consonants? and the two अं/अः are called what — and why are
   they not vowels?
4. What do /slashes/ mean, and why do "as in English" anchors always lie a little?

*Check against l02 §5, §8, §10.*

### Station 3 — अ and the script's first law (lesson 3)

1. Describe /ɐ/ in three phrases: jaw, tongue, lips.
2. Why does क read *ka* and not *k*? Name the rule.
3. Write from memory, body before headstroke: अ क म.
4. Which §12 mistake do Hindi speakers make with धर्म — and is it carelessness?

*Check against l03 §5, §11, §15, §12.*

### Station 4 — आ, the mātrā, twelve words (lesson 4)

1. What changes between कल and काल — in the mouth, and in the meaning?
2. State the mātrā rule: when do you write आ, and when ा?
3. Write from memory: का मा ता ला — then the words माता and माला.
4. From the story: who is कमला, and what did she get?

*Check against l04 §13, §15, §14, §17.*

Score yourself: 13+ of 16 prompts answered cleanly → proceed to the assessment. Below that,
spend today re-studying the weakest station and take the assessment tomorrow — the spacing
will help, not hurt [REF:cepeda2006].

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिकः सन्दर्भः — one paragraph only, on why this lesson exists.

Review is not an afterthought in this course; it is load-bearing design. The tradition knew
this — the Vedas crossed three thousand years on structured repetition — and modern memory
research agrees on the mechanism:

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Practice that is *spaced over time* produces far more durable learning
> than the same practice massed together. [REF:cepeda2006]

Chapter 1's content will be re-summoned at set intervals from here to the Volume 1 exam.
Today is the first interval.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — why you are being tested instead of re-taught (worth two paragraphs of
your attention: learners who know why they are tested, test better).

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Retrieving knowledge from memory strengthens it more than re-reading
> it — the *testing effect*. In controlled studies, learners who practiced recall
> outperformed re-studiers a week later by large margins. [REF:roediger2006]

That is why §3 made you answer *before* checking, and why today's assessment is mostly
production — reading aloud, writing from dictation, sorting claims — rather than recognition.
Every act of pulling माला out of your own memory writes it deeper than ten glances at the
page. The discomfort of "almost remembering" is not failure; it is the feeling of the
strengthening happening. Budget for it.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — self-diagnosis version of the two checklists:

- **अ check:** knuckle under chin, say मम — two equal, small jaw-drops. Final syllable
  shrank? Lesson 3 §9.4 is your fix.
- **आ check:** say माता — the first syllable's jaw-drop visibly larger and *longer* than the
  second's? Good. Both the same? Lesson 4 §9's length ladder.
- **Mirror check:** twelve words, lips neutral throughout — any rounding or spreading means
  an English vowel got in.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — the chapter's two script laws, restated in six lines, plus the honest gap.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** **Law 1 — the inherent vowel.** An unmarked consonant letter is read
> with अ: क *ka*, म *ma*, मम *mama*. The independent letter अ appears only where the vowel
> starts a syllable by itself. [REF:masica1991 ch.6]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** **Law 2 — the mātrā.** After a consonant, a non-अ vowel is written as a
> sign on the consonant: क + आ = का. The independent letter आ appears word-initially.
> [REF:masica1991 ch.6]

The unit both laws build is the **akṣara** — one consonant-plus-vowel block (मा, ला), the
"syllable-letter" the whole script thinks in.

**The honest gap:** you cannot yet write a bare consonant — the म् of अहम् needs the
vowel-killer (virāma ्), and clusters like the स्क of संस्कृतम् need conjuncts. Both live in
Chapter 5. Until then, every consonant you write speaks its अ — and words like अहम् stay 🔊.

## 16. Conversation

सम्भाषणम् — the chapter's three exchanges, joined into one continuous scene for pair
practice. Roles: अ = the student (आप), ब = teacher/mother. Glue words stay 🔊 (आम्, तव, इति);
everything bold you read yourself.

— नमस्ते! (namaste!) — ब: नमस्ते!
— ब, pointing at a letter: एषः कः वर्णः? (eṣaḥ kaḥ varṇaḥ?)
— अ: **म** इति वर्णः। (ma iti varṇaḥ.)
— ब, pointing at a word: **माता**? — अ: आम्, **माता**। (ām, mātā.)
— अ: **मम** **माला**? (mama mālā?) — ब: आम्, तव **माला**। (ām, tava mālā.)

Swap roles and run it twice. Delivery notes: every final vowel alive (law of lesson 3), every
dīrgha two beats (law of lesson 4), नमस्ते ending on a pure long [eː].

## 19. Quiz

प्रश्नाः — ten questions across the chapter. Do them closed-book, after the §18 tasks.
