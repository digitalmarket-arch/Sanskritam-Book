## 1. Hook

Between the vowels and the consonants runs a border, and four sounds live right on it — too open to be
proper consonants, too shaped to be vowels. The tradition named them with perfect honesty: *the ones
standing between*. They are the friendliest letters left in the box — one is an old helper coming home —
and one of them unlocks the most famous name in Sanskrit. By tonight you will read राम, गुरु and देव with
your own eyes, and your sentences will learn to speak: रामः वदति। "Rāma speaks." From today, they do.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिक सन्दर्भः — a class named after its address.

On Chapter 1's sound map, after the five varga rows, came a slim row of four: य र ल व. The tradition calls
them अन्तःस्थ (antaḥstha) — "standing between" — because that is exactly where they live: between the
vowels, which flow with an open tract, and the stops, which block it. Four sounds made of *approach
without collision*, each the consonant twin of a vowel you already own.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The अन्तःस्थ class — य र ल व — sits between vowels and stops in both senses: in
> manner (approximants and liquids, more open than any stop) and in kinship (each pairs with a vowel:
> य~इ, र~ऋ, ल~ऌ, व~उ). [REF:whitney1889 §§51–58]

That kinship is not trivia: in connected speech इ before a vowel becomes य, and उ becomes व — the
vowel-twin machinery is one of sandhi's main engines. Full treatment in Volume 3; today you only need to
hear that the twins are real.

> **[HISTORY · इतिहासः]** As the modern Indo-Aryan languages grew out of Sanskrit's descendants, the
> inherited व wandered: several languages and regional varieties merged it partly or wholly with ब —
> Bengali almost completely, and the Hindi belt keeps doublets like विवाह beside ब्याह. Careful Sanskrit
> recitation keeps व and ब distinct, as the classical language did. [REF:masica1991], [REF:ohala1994]

And one homecoming, the last of its kind: ल, a helper since v1.c01.l04, joins its own class today. क, त
and म came home to their vargas in Chapter 3; with ल the helper era closes — from today, every letter you
use stands under its own family name.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिक व्याख्या — the physics of "in between".

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The four अन्तःस्थ sounds are made by approach without collision: the articulators
> come close enough to shape the airstream but never close enough to stop it or make it hiss. य /j/ and
> व /ʋ/ are approximants; ल /l/ is a lateral — the tip touches while air flows around the sides; र /ɾ/ is
> a tap — one contact so brief the stream barely notices. [REF:ladefoged2015]

Meet them through their vowel twins — the class's own logic:

- **य /j/** is ई in motion: glide out of ई quickly and the exit-sound — high tongue, no friction — is य.
  English *yes* has the same /j/; the small imprecision: some English habits harden an emphatic initial y
  toward [dʒ] ("jes") — Sanskrit य never hardens.
- **र /ɾ/** is ऋ set free. In v1.c02.l03 you held /r̩/ at the ridge, humming — the vowel. Today the same
  tip touches the same spot and leaves at once: the tap of American *butter*. Hold it and it is ऋ; flick
  it and it is र. In recitation a light trill [r] is traditional and acceptable; the target is the tap.
- **ल /l/** you have said since Chapter 1 — today it gets its papers. Tip to the teeth-ridge and *stay*;
  voice flows around the sides. The clear l of *leaf*, never the dark l of *full*; Hindi ल is exact.
- **व /ʋ/** is today's real work:

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** व is /ʋ/ — a labiodental approximant: the lower lip rises toward the upper teeth in
> a light touch, and air passes without friction. English [v] presses the same contact into a buzzing
> hiss; English [w] rounds the lips and abandons the teeth. व stands exactly between — contact without
> friction, teeth without buzz. [REF:ladefoged2015]

English speakers must resist choosing their nearest letter: *v* is too harsh, *w* too soft, and the
middle ground is a sound English simply does not keep. The drill that finds it is in §9.

## 6. Sanskrit Explanation

संस्कृतव्याख्या — the lesson's idea in the simplest possible Sanskrit.

य र ल व इति अन्तःस्थाः।
(ya ra la va iti antaḥsthāḥ.) "*ya, ra, la, va* are the in-between sounds." — य र ल व अन्तःस्थ वर्ण हैं।

रामः वदति।
(rāmaḥ vadati.) "Rāma speaks." — राम बोलते हैं।

गुरुः अत्र अस्ति।
(guruḥ atra asti.) "The teacher is here." — गुरु यहाँ हैं।

The first line is the familiar इति pattern naming today's class. The second you can read *entirely* —
subject, verb, daṇḍa. In the third, अत्र (atra) "here" and अस्ति (asti) "is" are still 🔊 ear-words from
the Chapter 1 story (अत्र's त्र-weld unlocks in Chapter 5); everything else is yours.

## 7. Hindi Explanation

हिन्दी व्याख्या

आज की चारों ध्वनियाँ — य र ल व — आपकी ज़बान पर बचपन से हैं: यह, राम, लाल, वन। चारों अक्षर भी जाने-पहचाने।
तो आज की असली कमाई क्या है? दो आदतों की चौकसी, एक पुरानी कसौटी, और एक पुराने मित्र का सम्मान।

पहली चौकसी — व बनाम ब। हिन्दी की कई बोलियों में व चुपके से ब बन जाता है: विवाह से ब्याह, वर्ष से बरस,
व्रत से बरत। यह बोलियों का अपना, सदियों पुराना रास्ता है — ग़लती नहीं। पर संस्कृत में व और ब दो अलग वर्ण
हैं, और दोनों अर्थ बदलते हैं। जाँच सीधी है: व बोलते समय दोनों होंठ आपस में मिलें ही नहीं — निचला होंठ ऊपर
के दाँतों को हल्का-सा छुए, बस। होंठ भिड़ गये तो ब निकल गया।

दूसरी चौकसी — व पर अंग्रेज़ी की परत मत चढ़ाइए। अच्छी ख़बर पहले: हिन्दी की अपनी व बिलकुल सही है — वही
हल्की, बिना रगड़ की ध्वनि जो "वन" में है। गड़बड़ तब होती है जब अंग्रेज़ी की "v" संस्कृत में घुस आती है —
दाँत निचले होंठ पर कसकर बैठते हैं और ध्वनि भिनभिनाने लगती है। रगड़ सुनाई दे तो समझिए अंग्रेज़ी बोल रही
है, संस्कृत नहीं।

कसौटी वही पुरानी — पूरा अन्तिम स्वर — पर आज सबसे प्यारे शब्द पर। हिन्दी "राम" कहकर रुक जाती है; संस्कृत
में यह नाम दो पूरे अक्षरों का है — रा-म, *rāma*। वैसे ही देव de-va है, "देव्" नहीं; जय ज-य है, अन्त का अ
पूरा। जिस नाम को आप सबसे अच्छी तरह जानते हैं, वही आज की सबसे बारीक परीक्षा है।

और सम्मान — ल का। पहले अध्याय से ल आपका सहायक वर्ण था: लता, माला, कमल — कितने शब्द उसी के कन्धे पर चढ़कर
पढ़े! आज वह सहायक नहीं रहा — अन्तःस्थ परिवार का पूरा सदस्य है। घर लौटने वाला यह अन्तिम सहायक है: क, त और
म पहले ही अपने वर्गों में जा बैठे थे। आज सहायक-युग सम्मान के साथ पूरा हुआ।

## 8. English Explanation

Four sounds, three of them nearly free — then the payoff aisle: राम, गुरु, देव, and two new verbs that
let your sentences finally *say* something.

### The four cards — the in-between family

- **य (ya) /j/ — the glide.** ई consonantized: high tongue, no friction, moving. Anchor: English *yes*;
  guard: never harden it toward "j".
- **र (ra) /ɾ/ — the tap.** One flick of the tip at the ridge — the *butter*-tap, now official. Its
  vowel-sibling ऋ /r̩/ is the same gesture *held*; recitation may roll a light trill, the target taps.
- **ल (la) /l/ — the homecoming.** Your helper since v1.c01.l04 — लता, माला, कमल all rode on it — now a
  full अन्तःस्थ. Nothing new to learn; one small celebration owed. The helper era ends today.
- **व (va) /ʋ/ — the middle ground.** Lower lip to upper teeth, touch without buzz. Not English v, not
  English w — the space between (§9 drill 2).

### Worked readings

- **राम** = रा + म → rā-ma — /ɾɑːmɐ/ "Rāma; pleasing". Two full beats — the second is the test.
- **गुरु** = गु + रु → gu-ru — /guɾu/ "teacher". The course word of v1.c01.l01, readable at last.
- **देव** = दे + व → de-va — /d̪eːʋɐ/ "god, deity". Pure long ए, light व, full final अ.
- **वायु** = वा + यु → vā-yu — /ʋɑːju/ "wind". Both headline sounds in one word.
- **कवि** = क + वि → ka-vi — /kɐʋi/ "poet". Short, sweet, and about to star in §17.

### The verbs grow — and the sentences with them

Two new verbs join चलति and कूजति: **भवति** (bhavati) "is, becomes" and **वदति** (vadati) "speaks". As
with चलति, learn each as a whole word — the -ति machinery, persons and numbers are Volume 2's story. One
honesty note: भवति usually wants a complement ("X *becomes* Y") whose endings are later machinery — so
today भवति is a word you read and say, while वदति goes straight to work:

**रामः वदति।**
(rāmaḥ vadati.) "Rāma speaks." — राम बोलते हैं।

**कविः वदति।**
(kaviḥ vadati.) "The poet speaks." — कवि बोलता है।

**वीरः चलति।**
(vīraḥ calati.) "The hero walks." — वीर चलता है।

Read them as always in pause form — each word complete, a small pause between (the convention of
v1.c03.l05); connected-speech sandhi remains Volume 3's story.

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — four drills, in order. Audio: 🔊 `planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c04l01-drill.mp3` (drills
1–2 + the व test track), `planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c04l01-word-list.mp3` (drill 3 + dictation
track), `planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c04l01-sentences.mp3` (drill 4).

**Drill 1 — the glide ladder.** The class teaches itself: each अन्तःस्थ is a vowel set in motion.

1. इ → य: say ई, then ई-अ, then faster — *iya, iya* — until the ई shrinks to a glide. That exit alone: य.
2. उ → व: say ऊ, then ऊ-अ, faster — and as it speeds up, drop the lip-rounding and let the lower lip
   drift up to touch the teeth *lightly*: व. (Keep the rounding and you have [w] — English's glide.)
3. ऋ → र: the brrr-ladder of v1.c02.l03, final rung. Hold ऋ for a beat — the vowel. Flick the same spot
   once on the way into अ — र. Alternate ऋ — र — ऋ — र until hold and flick feel different in the muscle.

**Drill 2 — the व laboratory (middle-ground reps).** (1) Say English *vine*, freeze the first sound:
teeth on lip, pressing, buzzing — too harsh. (2) Say *wine*, freeze: lips rounded, teeth nowhere — too
soft. (3) The middle: lip to teeth as for *vine*, but featherlight, **no buzz, no hiss**: व. (4)
Alternate at the mirror: [v] — व — [w] — व, watching lips, listening for friction. (5) Words: वन, वीर,
वायु, देव — every व a light touch.

**Drill 3 — syllables, then words.** One beat per short vowel, two per long: य या यु यो · र रा रि रु ·
ल ला ली लो · व वा वि वे. Then the §14 list from the audio, each word twice. Close with discrimination:
ब-व, बा-वा (lips-together vs lip-to-teeth); the hold/flick pair ऋ-र, ऋ-रा; the reversal pair वन — नव.

**Drill 4 — the sentence protocol.** With the sentences audio: **रामः वदति। कविः वदति। वीरः चलति।** Word
fully, tiny pause, next word, daṇḍa to close — block by block, word by word, then calm reading pace.

## 10. IPA

The new symbols: य **/j/** · र **/ɾ/** · ल **/l/** · व **/ʋ/**.

**Two bookkeeping notes.** First, the promise of v1.c02.l03 §10 is kept: ऋ /r̩/ and र /ɾ/ share an
articulator and split the work — the syllabicity stroke says "this one is the vowel"; bare /ɾ/ touches
and leaves. Second, व /ʋ/ sits in the IPA exactly where this lesson puts it in the mouth: [v] is a
labiodental *fricative*, [w] a rounded labio-velar glide, /ʋ/ the approximant between them. In recitation
र may surface as a trill [r]; the course transcribes /ɾ/ and treats [r] as a recitation variant.

**Narrow note — the sentences:** रामः वदति in careful pause form is [ɾɑːmɐh ʋɐd̪ɐt̪i] — visarga a clean
voiceless breath, the व a light touch, every final [ɐ] full: /ɾɑːmɐ/, never [ɾɑːm].

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थानम् — four positions, one diagram (`planned:assets/diagrams/volume-1/v1c04l01-va-articulation.svg`
— sagittal view of व: lower lip to upper teeth, light contact, no friction marks, lips unrounded).

1. **य:** tongue body arches high toward the palate — the ई position — tip low; the sound moves off into
   the following vowel. Nothing to see in the mirror; everything to feel.
2. **र:** tongue-tip flicks the ridge behind the upper teeth — one touch, gone. No contact at all means
   English r crept in; a rattle means you are reciting.
3. **ल:** tip touches the teeth-ridge and *stays* while voice flows around the sides. Hold लललल and feel
   air slipping past the tongue's edges — that side-channel is what "lateral" means.
4. **व — the day's mirror work:** lower lip rises to the upper front teeth, light as a feather, lips
   never rounding. Two checks: (rounding watch) say व five times watching only the lips — any circle
   means [w]; (buzz watch) a fingertip on the lower lip — strong vibration means [v]'s friction.

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** The Pāṇinīya Śikṣā tradition gives व the label दन्त्योष्ठ्य (dantyoṣṭhya) —
> "of the teeth and lips" — naming, two millennia early, the same articulators as "labiodental".

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्य-भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with a fix. These feed the quiz distractors.

1. **व as full [v] or full [w] (English speakers, both directions).** *Vine*-mouths press the teeth into
   a buzz; *wine*-mouths round the lips and lose the teeth. *Fix:* the व laboratory (§9 drill 2) —
   alternate [v]-व-[w]-व at the mirror until the light-touch middle is findable on demand.
2. **र over-trilled — or English-r'd.** A proud Spanish rrr is a performance, not a tap (save the light
   trill for recitation); the English bunched r of *red* never touches at all. *Fix:* the hold/flick
   ladder (§9 drill 1): one contact, felt on the ridge, then gone.
3. **य hardened to [dʒ] (some English habits).** An emphatic initial y slides toward "j": युग becomes
   "juga". *Fix:* glue य back to its twin — say ई first, then let it move; no closure, no [dʒ].
4. **The व/ब blur (Hindi regional habit).** Where the mother tongue says ब्याह for विवाह, संस्कृत व can
   drift lips-together into ब. *Fix:* the lip check — for व the lips never meet each other, only the
   teeth; drill ब-व, बा-वा at the mirror. (Keeping ब fully lips-together was l05's parting advice.)

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — four small stories the word list is hiding.

### राम — the most famous name, and an ordinary word

**राम** (rāma) "Rāma; pleasing" — राम · /ɾɑːmɐ/

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** As a common adjective राम means "pleasing, charming; dark-hued" — and as a proper
> name it belongs, above all, to the hero of the Rāmāyaṇa. Both beats full: rā-ma. [REF:apte1890 s.v. राम]

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** In the devotional traditions Rāma is revered as an avatāra of Viṣṇu, and his
> name is treasured as a mantra — repeated, sung, and written across India daily. Here it is a word you
> read; the reverence around it belongs to the tradition and is noted with respect.

### नव — the cognate table comes home

**नव** (nava) "new; nine" — नया; नौ · /n̪ɐʋɐ/

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Sanskrit नव, English *new* and Latin *novus* are parallel descendants of one
> Proto-Indo-European word — cognates, not borrowings. (The "nine" sense runs in parallel beside Latin
> *novem*.) [REF:mallory2006]

In v1.c01.l01 the cognate table was 🔊 listen-only — family resemblance taken on trust. नव is the first
cognate to walk in *already readable*: the Devanagari and the four-thousand-year-old likeness, same breath.

### युग — the yoke family, loan vs cognate one more time

**युग** (yuga) "age, era" — युग, ज़माना · /jugɐ/

Its first sense is "yoke; pair", grown into "age of the world" [REF:apte1890 s.v. युग]. It shares a PIE
family with English *yoke* — a cognate — while *yoga* is a member of the same family that English
**borrowed** outright from Sanskrit योग (yoga) [REF:mallory2006]. One root, both arrows from v1.c01.l01:
*yoke* = cognate, *yoga* = loan.

### गुरु — the course keeps its oldest promise

**गुरु** (guru) "teacher" — गुरु, शिक्षक · /guɾu/

Literally "heavy; weighty, venerable" — the teacher as the one who carries weight [REF:apte1890 s.v.
गुरु]. You have *said* it since the course's first hour (गुरुः — English borrowed it whole); today your
eyes catch up with your ears. Both u's short, both beats even: gu-ru.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — in Volume 1 this is a **script rule** section: three new letters, one promotion, and the
inventory's next-to-last milestone.

### Stroke order — य, र, व (and ल, at home)

Course convention as ever — body first, stem next, headline last; the stroke card
(`planned:assets/images/volume-1/v1c04l01-stroke-order.svg`) is the authority, and traditions vary.

- **य** — the body: begin at the left under the headline with a small open wave — a curl that does
  **not** close — sweep down into the open bowl and rise on the right into the stem-line; then stem;
  headline. **य vs थ:** थ ties a closed loop at its top-left where य keeps an open wave. Knot = थ,
  wave = य.
- **र** — the lightest consonant you will ever write: (1) one vertical drop from where the headline will
  sit; (2) from the stem's midpoint, one diagonal sweep down-left; (3) headline. Two strokes and a line —
  the busiest sound in the language wears the simplest clothes.
- **व** — draw ब's clean left loop — down, around, back up to join the stem-line without crossing — but
  leave the room *empty*: no tick inside. Then stem, headline. **व vs ब:** the inner tick is ब's alone.
  Tick = ब, empty loop = व — exactly the pair v1.c03.l05 promised was coming.
- **ल** — as you have written it since Chapter 1. Nothing changes today but its title — helper no more,
  अन्तःस्थ in full standing.

## 17. Story

कथा — कविः · The Poet in the Forest. The cast grows: a poet joins the family, and the cuckoo of Chapter 2
sings him in. Six lines; each line: Devanagari, IAST, English, Hindi — and, as since v1.c03.l05, no bold
and no 🔊: **every word is self-read.**

1. कविः चलति। ततः — वन!
   (kaviḥ calati. tataḥ — vana!) "The poet walks. Then — a forest!" — कवि चलता है। फिर — वन!
2. वायुः चलति। कोकिलः कूजति।
   (vāyuḥ calati. kokilaḥ kūjati.) "The wind moves. The cuckoo coos." — हवा चलती है। कोयल कूकती है।
3. कविः वदति — वन! वायु! रवि!
   (kaviḥ vadati — vana! vāyu! ravi!) "The poet speaks: 'Forest! Wind! Sun!'" — कवि बोलता है — वन! वायु!
   सूरज!
4. ततः रविः चलति।
   (tataḥ raviḥ calati.) "Then the sun moves on." — फिर सूरज ढल चलता है।
5. कविः वदति — जय!
   (kaviḥ vadati — jaya!) "The poet speaks: 'Victory!'" — कवि बोलता है — जय!
6. कोकिलः कूजति। कविः वदति।
   (kokilaḥ kūjati. kaviḥ vadati.) "The cuckoo coos; the poet speaks." — कोयल कूकती है। कवि बोलता है।

A poet's first poem is a list of names — he walks into the forest and *names* it. The shouts वन! वायु!
रवि! जय! are the naming form you know from l05's फल! — pointing with the voice; dressed for full
sentences these words would wear endings (Chapter 5 and Volume 3 machinery). The closing line is the
course's first duet: two subjects, two verbs, read entirely by you.

## 22. Teacher Notes

शिक्षक-टिप्पणी

**Two summits, in order.** First the व middle-ground (the one genuinely new motor skill), then the राम
moment. Do not reverse them: once the room has read राम, phonetic discipline is fighting devotional
electricity for attention.

**Timing plan (45 min):** §3 retrieval, written (5) → hook + glide ladders from इ, उ, ऋ (7) → the व
laboratory at mirrors, pair checks (8) → writing य र व: board demo, थ/य and ब/व insets, tracing start
(7) → word list in pairs (6) → THE राम MOMENT: build रा-म on the board in silence, let a student read it;
then रामः वदति। choral and solo (6) → exercises 2 and 6 briefing (3) → homework incl. the 1-day
re-dictation (3).

**Stage the राम moment with the register policy in hand.** Many students will feel something when they
first read the name; make room for that without converting a phonetics lesson into a devotional one. The
lexical senses and the name are FACT; the reverence is TRADITION, named respectfully (§13); and the
pronunciation target is non-negotiable — rā-ma, both beats, especially for the word everyone thinks they
already know.

**The व battle runs through two doors.** English-habit learners must *find* the middle; Hindi speakers
already own [ʋ] but may import English [v] from schooling or blur toward ब regionally. Mirrors and the
buzz-check for the first group; ब-व minimal drills for the second — and deputize confident Hindi speakers
as [ʋ] models. Being the reference accent is good for morale and better for accuracy.

**What students find hard:** believing व is neither v nor w (the mirror converts them); keeping र to one
flick under excitement; the थ/य and ब/व lookalikes in fast dictation (the tick/knot mnemonics catch most
of it); slowing down for pause form once sentences start answering questions — कः वदति? is fun; channel
it into exercise 5. And give ल's promotion its thirty seconds of ceremony: the scaffolding era ends today.
