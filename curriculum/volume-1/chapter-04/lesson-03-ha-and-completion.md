## 1. Hook

One letter left. Just one — and it is not an exotic one hiding in rare words: it is the
sound of breath itself, the softest thing a throat can do while still saying something.
And the moment it lands, something you have been building since the second lesson of
this course is finished: the sound map you first met as a stranger's table — 13 vowels,
33 consonants, 2 dependent signs — has no empty squares. Today you learn ह; today the
alphabet completes.

## 4. Historical Context

ऐतिहासिक सन्दर्भः — the last letter, and the class it keeps.

The course's letter-collection began with अ — the voice, mouth open, nothing in the way
(v1.c01.l03). It ends today with ह — the breath, throat open, voice still humming. The
tradition's recitation order agrees: the varṇamālā closes on the four ऊष्माणः
(ūṣmāṇaḥ) — श ष स ह — and ह is the final letter of the whole garland
[REF:whitney1889 §§19–75]. Last lesson gave you the three hissing sibilants; today's
letter sits in their traditional class while being, phonetically, a different creature.
Both halves of that sentence are true, and this course says both:

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** The traditional ūṣman class is श ष स ह. Phonetically, श ष स are
> sibilants — noisy friction shaped by the tongue. ह is not a sibilant: its friction
> happens at the glottis, in the open throat, with the tongue doing nothing. The class
> grouping is the tradition's; the phonetic distinction is real; neither cancels the
> other. [REF:whitney1889 §§59–65]

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** The grammatical tradition's own name for the class explains
> the grouping: ऊष्मन् (ūṣman) means "heat, hot breath, steam" — these four are the
> breath-sounds, the ones you could warm your hands on. By breath, ह belongs completely.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिक व्याख्या — the breath with the voice left on.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** ह is /ɦ/ — a voiced glottal fricative: strong airflow through the
> open glottis while the vocal folds keep vibrating in a slack, murmured mode. It is the
> breathy voice you built five times in Chapter 3 — the ʱ of घ झ ढ ध भ — standing alone,
> with no stop in front of it. [REF:ladefoged2015], [REF:whitney1889 §65]

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** विसर्ग ः /h/ and ह /ɦ/ are one gesture with the voice switched off
> and on: both push breath through an open throat. ः is voiceless — the hand on the
> throat feels nothing; ह is voiced — the hand feels a buzz the whole way through.
> [REF:whitney1889 §65]

Three consequences. **You already own the hardest part:** breathy voice cost real work
in Chapter 3; ह is that skill with the stop removed — for once the new letter is
*easier* than its ancestors. **The visarga gets its partner:** the ः/ह pair becomes a
self-checking instrument under your hand (§9). **Between vowels, keep it murmured:** in
मही and बाहु the voice never stops — vowel, breath, vowel, one unbroken hum. English
anchors both twins: the h of "a**h**ead" is usually voiced [ɦ]; the h of "**h**ouse" is
voiceless — pick the right one [REF:ladefoged2015].

## 8. English Explanation

One card today — and then the moment the whole volume has been walking toward.

### The card — ह, the voiced breath

- **ह (ha) /ɦ/ — voiced glottal fricative.** Open throat, strong breath, voice humming
  through it. English anchor: the h of "a**h**ead" — usually voiced between vowels — but
  *not* the h of "**h**ouse", which is voiceless (that one is your visarga). Hindi
  anchor: ह in हर and कहानी, spoken slowly — the same sound, minus the fast-speech habit
  of dropping it (§7, §12).

No tongue position, no lip work: ह is the one consonant whose mouth is entirely borrowed
— while the breath flows, your mouth is already shaped for the vowel that follows (§11).

### Worked readings

- **हिमालय** = हि + मा + ल + य → hi-mā-la-ya — /ɦimɑːlɐjɐ/ "the Himālaya". Four plain
  akṣaras, no conjuncts: the highest mountains on Earth, spelled with beginner letters —
  and the word is a picture: हिम "snow" + आलय "abode" (§13).
- **गृह** = गृ + ह → gṛ-ha — /gr̩ɦɐ/ "house". The ृ mātrā from v1.c02.l03 riding ग,
  then a full ह with a full final अ.
- **हसति** = ह + स + ति → ha-sa-ti — /ɦɐsɐt̪i/ "laughs" — verb four of the course,
  after चलति, भवति and वदति (कूजति stays with the cuckoo). A fifth, वहति "carries,
  flows", slips into today's list: वायुः वहति "the wind flows".

## 9. Pronunciation

उच्चारणम् — four drills, in order. Audio: 🔊
`planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c04l03-drill.mp3` (drills 1–2 + ह/ः pair test),
`planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c04l03-word-list.mp3` (drill 3 + dictation track),
`planned:assets/audio/volume-1/v1c04l03-full-varnamala.mp3` (drill 4 — the model
recital).

**Drill 1 — the throat switch.** Hand on the throat. अः: a clean voiceless puff —
nothing under the hand. Now हा: the same breath with the voice on — the hand buzzes
start to finish. Alternate slowly: अः — हा — अः — हा, feeling the switch flip. Then the
word pair: नमः (ends voiceless) — हरि (begins voiced).

**Drill 2 — the murmur bridge.** The Chapter 3 transfer: say घ, feel the breathy
release; now delete the ग-closure and keep only the release — that lingering murmured
breath *is* ह. Then between vowels, without breaking the hum: इह — मही — बाहु. The voice
never stops mid-word: vowel, breath, vowel, one thread of sound.

**Drill 3 — syllables, then words.** Read across, one beat per short vowel, two per
long: ह हा हि ही हु हू हे हो. Then the §14 list from the audio, each word twice. Finish
with the danger sets: मोह, देह, गृह (final vowels full) and मही, बाहु, इह (medial ह held
voiced).

**Drill 4 — THE FULL RECITAL.** The lesson's centerpiece. With the model audio, recite
the complete varṇamālā in traditional order, group by group, a breath between groups:

अ आ इ ई उ ऊ ऋ ॠ ऌ ए ऐ ओ औ — अं अः — क ख ग घ ङ — च छ ज झ ञ — ट ठ ड ढ ण — त थ द ध न —
प फ ब भ म — य र ल व — श ष स ह

Three rounds: reading from the §8 table; from the wall chart across the room; then eyes
closed. Accuracy first — every aspirate breathed, every retroflex curled. Speed is not
today's goal; the timed version is lesson 4's exam.

## 10. IPA

The formal value: ह /ɦ/ — voiced glottal fricative. Its voiceless partner: विसर्ग /h/.

**One phonation, two spellings:** the superscript ʱ (घ /gʱ/, भ /bʱ/) and the standalone
ɦ are the same murmured voice — ʱ rides a stop's release, ɦ stands alone. You have been
producing [ɦ] inside every breathy stop since Chapter 3.

**Narrow notes:** between vowels ह keeps full voicing — मही [mɐɦiː], बाहु [bɑːɦu] — the
murmur at its softest, and the sound easiest to lose (§12). In careful pause-form
reading गुरुः हसति is [guɾuh ɦɐsɐt̪i]: a voiceless breath closing the first word, a
voiced one opening the second — the whole ः/ɦ contrast in four syllables.

**Word transcriptions:** हिमालय /ɦimɑːlɐjɐ/ · गृह /gr̩ɦɐ/ (syllabic r̩ from ऋ's mātrā)
· हसति /ɦɐsɐt̪i/ · इह /iɦɐ/.

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थानम् — the letter card
(`planned:assets/images/volume-1/v1c04l03-ha-card.svg`) shows today's articulation — or
rather, the absence of one.

1. **Nothing to place.** Tongue at rest, lips relaxed: ह has no contact point in the
   mouth. The action is in the throat: glottis open enough for strong airflow, vocal
   folds slack enough to keep humming through it.
2. **The borrowed mouth.** Mirror up: say हा, ही, हू slowly. Watch the mouth take the
   *vowel's* shape already during the breath — wide for हा, spread for ही, rounded for
   हू. ह is a breath poured into the next vowel's mould.
3. **The instrument panel.** Throat-hand: buzzing throughout (silence means you made the
   visarga instead). Palm: steady warm airflow, no burst — there is no closure to
   release. Tissue: a gentle lean, never a jump.

## 12. Common Mistakes

सामान्य-भ्रान्तयः — real errors, each with its fix. These feed the quiz distractors.

1. **ह devoiced to English house-h everywhere (mostly English speakers).** Every ह comes
   out voiceless, and the murmur is lost. *Fix:* the throat switch (§9 drill 1) — the
   hand must buzz through every ह; if रामः and हरि feel the same under your hand, the
   switch is stuck.
2. **ह dropped between vowels (mostly Hindi speakers).** The कहना-habit: मही slides
   toward [mɐiː], बाहु toward [bɑːu]. Natural Hindi — but in Sanskrit the ह is
   meaning-bearing and stays. *Fix:* the murmur bridge (§9 drill 2), slow: म-ही, बा-हु,
   इ-ह — one unbroken hum with the breath audible inside it.
3. **The letter ह confused with the sign ः.** Both get called "the breath", and notes
   start showing रामह-style errors. *Fix:* two absolutes — sound: ह voiced, ः voiceless;
   writing: ह stands in the row and carries अ, ः only ever follows an akṣara.
4. **The recital raced.** Completion-day excitement turns the varṇamālā into a speed
   run, and the aspirates flatten first. *Fix:* accuracy has right of way — group by
   group, a breath between groups. The clock appears in lesson 4's exam, and even there
   errors cost more than seconds.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — three stories hiding in the word list.

### हिमालय — the word the world knows

**हिमालय** (himālaya) "the Himālaya" — हिमालय · /ɦimɑːlɐjɐ/

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** हिमालय is a transparent compound: हिम (hima) "snow" + आलय (ālaya)
> "abode, dwelling" — "the abode of snow". And English *Himalaya* is a genuine
> **loanword**: English borrowed the mountain range's Sanskrit name whole — like *yoga*
> and *karma*, a loan, not a cognate. [REF:apte1890 s.v. हिमालय]

Remember lesson 1's rule: a loan is handed over; cognates are siblings from one
ancestor. मातृ and *mother* are cognates; हिमालय and *Himalaya* are the same word, lent.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — in Volume 1 this is a **script rule**: one new letter, and the script's
closing law.

### Stroke order — ह

Course convention as ever — body first, headline last; the stroke card
(`planned:assets/images/volume-1/v1c04l03-stroke-order.svg`) is the authority, and
traditions vary in details.

- **ह** — one continuous body: start just below headline height with a small leftward
  hook, sweep down and around into the open bowl, and finish in the curl at the lower
  left with its short inner tick; headline last. Like द, ह is a round letter — **no
  right-hand vertical stem** — so mātrās attach to the body: हि wraps in front, ही hooks
  behind and over, हा stands its post to the right, हु tucks its hook close under the
  tail. Fonts vary in the details; the stroke card is the model.
- **ह vs ः — letter vs sign, on paper.** ह stands in the writing line as a full akṣara
  and carries the inherent अ; ः is two small dots that only ever follow a finished
  akṣara. An "h" opening a word must be ह; the breath trailing रामः must be ः.

### The closing law — the inventory is complete

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** With ह, the letter inventory is closed: 46 letters and 2 signs.
> Everything else you will ever read on a Sanskrit page is built from these by
> combination — the mātrā forms you already command, and the consonant joinings
> (conjuncts) that Chapter 5 opens. New combinations are coming; new letters are not.
> [REF:whitney1889 §§19–75]

(Plus a couple of editorial marks in due course: ॐ you own already; the avagraha ऽ
arrives with sandhi in Volume 3.)

### हरि and हर — the name-words, honestly labeled

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** As dictionary words: हरि means "tawny, bay-colored" among other
> senses, and is a proper name; हर means "taking away, removing, seizing", and is a
> proper name. [REF:apte1890 s.v. हरि]

> **[TRADITION · परम्परा]** In the devotional tradition हरि is a beloved name of Viṣṇu
> and हर of Śiva — paired to this day in the compound हरिहर (harihara). In this lesson
> both are vocabulary, doing honest secular work.

## 22. Teacher Notes

शिक्षक-टिप्पणी

**Two summits, in order — and stage the second deliberately.** First the sound: fast,
because breathy voice is Chapter 3 machinery and ह is that machinery simplified. Then
the completion. Keep the wall chart out of sight during the sound work. Stage it like
pa-varga staged its sentences: silence first; chart up, one cell empty; a student writes
ह into it; say the numbers aloud — thirteen, thirty-three, two — and start the recital
without further commentary. The room will understand before you name it. Then name it:
every square, theirs.

**Timing plan (45 min):** §3 retrieval, written (5) → hook + throat switch demo on
अः/हा (5) → ह sound lab: murmur bridge, intervocalic words, ह/ः pair test (8) → writing
ह: board demo, air-writing, tracing start (6) → word list in pairs, throat-hands on (6)
→ THE COMPLETION: wall chart, last cell, first full recital — teacher leads group by
group, class echoes (10) → exercise 5 quick-fire (3) → homework briefing incl. the
1-day re-dictation (2).

**The ह/ः confusion is the week's real enemy.** Both get called "the breath", so insist
on the two absolutes: voiced vs voiceless (hand on throat), letter vs sign (stands alone
vs only rides). The pair test converts doubters faster than explanation.

**Differentiation:** Hindi-literate learners know the glyph and the sound — load them
with the intervocalic discipline (मही, बाहु, the कहना-habit) and make them dictation
referees. English-habit learners need the voicing: *ahead*, not *house*, and the throat
hand until it is boring. Heritage chanters may already own the recital — give them the
accuracy checklist and the recorder role, and hold everyone to pause form.

**What students find hard:** keeping the voice on through a breath (whispered ह is just
ः — the throat-hand catches it); slowing the recital down on completion day; believing a
letter with "nothing to do" still needs drilling. The intervocalic words convince them:
say मही fast and the ह simply vanishes — the drill is what keeps it.
