## 1. Hook

Forty-eight symbols. Four chapters. In the course's second lesson the varṇamālā was a map of
sounds you could not yet read; today every cell is lit — thirteen vowels, two leaning signs,
thirty-three consonants, with ॐ riding above them as a bonus ligature. The word list has crossed
one hundred and fifty; five sentences run on five verbs. Nothing new enters this lesson. Today is
the Alphabet Exam — the volume's central certificate — and the whole sound-system is yours.

## 5. Scientific Explanation

वैज्ञानिकं विवरणम् — this time, a word about *combination*.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** Retrieval practice strengthens memory more than re-reading — the
> testing effect. [REF:roediger2006]

There is a second reason today matters. Chapter 5 — the virāma and the conjuncts — teaches
**combination, not new sounds**: every conjunct you will ever meet is letters you now own, welded
by a writing rule — the स्क inside संस्कृतम् is स् + क, the स्त inside नमस्ते is स् + त. Prove
the inventory now and Chapter 5 becomes mechanical rather than mysterious; leave a blurred श/ष or
a wobbling व in place, and the error gets welded into every cluster that contains it. This exam
is the cheapest moment you will ever have to fix these sounds.

   निकालिए: यही ष है। च की जगह से: यही श। कार्य A के पहले चार प्रश्न इसी की परीक्षा हैं।
2. **व और ब।** कई क्षेत्रों की बोलियों में व, ब में ढल जाता है — "वन" को "बन" कह देना। संस्कृत का

## 11. Mouth Position

मुखस्थितिः — the consolidated self-diagnosis circuit, one check per family. These are the scoring
rubric for task C: every reading error should be caught by one of them.

- **व — mirror check:** lower lip rises to the upper teeth and touches *lightly*. Full closure =
  ब; rounding with no teeth = [w]. → l01 §11.
- **र — tap count:** say राम slowly; the tongue tip strikes the ridge exactly once. An English r
  never strikes at all. → l01 §11.
- **श vs ष — the ट-tongue:** freeze the tongue at Chapter 3's ट station and breathe: ष. Slide
  forward to the च station: श. Two places, felt, not guessed. → l02 §11.
- **स — dental hiss:** tip near the teeth, thin clean hiss — English s nearly passes. → l02 §11.
- **ह vs ः — throat-hand:** fingers on the larynx. ह hums from the first instant; the visarga's
  puff is silent. → l03 §11.
- **Order — memory, not mouth:** recite the varṇamālā once with §9 open, once without; wherever
  you slip is your task-E risk. → l03 §15.

## 13. Examples

उदाहरणानि — two worked model answers before you start.

**Model dictation item (task B).** Audio says *himālaya*. You hear: a voiced breathing onset that
hums — ह, never dropped. Then short इ: the LEFT-hook mātrā from Chapter 2, so the hook is written
before the letter it follows in speech. Build it block by block: ह + ि = हि (hook on the left!),
then म + ा = मा, then ल, then य → **हिमालय**. Common wrong answers: इमालय (the onset ह dropped —
§12 row 6), हीमालय (short इ inflated to ई — Chapter 2's length discipline never retires).

**Model register sort (task D).** Card: "श and ष are the same sound." The reflex agrees — in
modern Hindi the two really have merged. But the claim is about *Sanskrit*, and it is checkable
in your own mouth: श is palatal /ɕ/, ष is retroflex /ʂ/ — put the tongue at ट's station, breathe,
and you produce a hiss English "sh" never makes. **Reject**, with the refutation performed, not
recited. Compare: "ह is traditionally grouped with the sibilants" — a real classification, the
ūṣman class, claiming nothing false about phonetics: **TRADITION box**, or FACT *about* the
tradition; both earn credit, and zero goes to calling ह itself a sibilant. Three claims, three
verdicts — sorting them is the whole skill.

## 15. Grammar

व्याकरणम् — the script laws restated, and the system declared closed.

> **[FACT · तथ्यम्]** **Law 1 — inherent vowel:** an unmarked consonant reads with अ.
> **Law 2 — mātrā:** any other vowel after a consonant is written as a sign. **Law 3 —
> mātrā geography:** the sign's position is fixed by the script, regardless of when the
> vowel is spoken. **Law 4 — the leaning signs:** ं and ः attach to the whole akṣara
> (ayogavāha). **Law 5 — the grid:** every stop is fully named by place × manner. **And
> now the closure:** the letter inventory is complete — 13 vowels + 2 ayogavāha + 33
> consonants. Every Sanskrit word you will ever read is spelled from this set.
> [REF:whitney1889 §§19–75] [REF:masica1991 ch.6]

unwritable by you) and the conjuncts (two owned letters welded — स्त, स्क), both in Chapter 5.
